## GS-204: Representation of the People Acts: salient features

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS2 · Polity · Institutions, law and politics

*Goal:* Explain Representation of the People Acts: salient features and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Constitutional basis for election law

Articles 327 and 328 let Parliament, and State legislatures where Parliament has not acted, make provision for all matters relating to elections, including electoral rolls and delimitation. Article 329 then restricts court interference: the validity of delimitation laws made under those articles cannot be questioned in any court, and an election to Parliament or a State legislature can be challenged only through an election petition presented to the authority and in the manner fixed by law. Article 326 fixes adult suffrage at 18 and allows disqualification by law on grounds such as non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime or corrupt practice.

### The 1950 Act: seats, officers and rolls

The Representation of the People Act, 1950 prepares the ground before any poll. Its long title covers allocating seats in the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies, delimiting constituencies, voter qualifications, electoral rolls and Rajya Sabha seats for Union territories. Section 3 points to the First Schedule for each State's Lok Sabha seats and SC/ST reservations, and each parliamentary constituency returns one member. Part IIA builds the roll machinery: a Chief Electoral Officer for each State, designated by the Election Commission after consulting the State, supervises roll work; District Election Officers coordinate it; Electoral Registration Officers prepare and revise each roll; and these officials count as on deputation to the Commission while on election duty. Part III bars non-citizens, persons declared of unsound mind by a court and those disqualified for electoral offences, and entitles every ordinarily resident adult of eighteen to one registration.

### The 1951 Act: conduct, disqualification and disputes

The 1951 Act runs from notification to dispute: administrative machinery, party registration (section 29A), nomination and scrutiny, polling and counting, declaration of assets, election expenses, corrupt practices (section 123), false affidavits (section 125A) and the ban on public meetings in the forty-eight hours before polls close (section 126). Election petitions are tried by High Courts, with appeal to the Supreme Court. Disqualification is the most-tested area. As printed in the older compilation cited, conviction under listed laws disqualifies for six years if only fined, or from conviction until six years after release if jailed; any other conviction with at least two years' sentence carries the same post-release bar. Further grounds include corrupt practices (section 8A), government contracts (section 9A) and failure to lodge expense accounts (section 10A). Confirm section 8 against a current official text.
