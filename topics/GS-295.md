## GS-295: Cell biology and microbiology

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Science · General science / technology

*Goal:* Explain Cell biology and microbiology and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Cells: prokaryotic and eukaryotic

Schleiden and Schwann proposed that plants and animals are made of cells and their products, and Virchow added that cells divide to form new cells. Cells with a membrane-bound nucleus are eukaryotic; those without are prokaryotic, represented by bacteria, blue-green algae, mycoplasma and PPLO. Eukaryotic cells contain organelles like ER, Golgi complex, lysosomes and mitochondria, whereas prokaryotes lack membrane-bound organelles except for ribosomes. Bacterial DNA lies naked without a nuclear membrane, and many bacteria also hold plasmids, which can carry antibiotic resistance. Prokaryotic ribosomes are 70S, formed from 50S and 30S subunits, and serve as the site of protein synthesis.

### Microbes in human welfare

Lactic acid bacteria turn milk into curd and raise its vitamin B12 content. Fleming's chance discovery of penicillin from a mould opened the antibiotic era. Cyclosporin A from the fungus Trichoderma polysporum suppresses immunity in organ-transplant patients, and statins from the yeast Monascus purpureus lower blood cholesterol. In sewage treatment, aerobic microbes consume organic matter and cut the biochemical oxygen demand; a higher BOD signals greater polluting potential. Anaerobic bacteria in sludge digesters and biogas plants produce methane-rich gas; methanogens like Methanobacterium are abundant in cattle dung, and IARI and KVIC developed India's biogas technology.

### Viruses: the HIV example

AIDS is caused by HIV, a retrovirus whose envelope encloses an RNA genome. It spreads through sexual contact, contaminated blood, shared needles and from mother to child, but not through casual touch. Inside the body the virus enters macrophages, where reverse transcriptase copies its RNA into DNA that integrates into the host genome and directs production of new virus particles. HIV then infects helper T lymphocytes and multiplies, progressively reducing their numbers. The weakened immune system can no longer resist infections that would normally be controlled, showing how the virus relies on infected host cells to produce new particles.

### Worked example

Classify: E. coli - prokaryote (70S ribosomes, plasmids possible); onion peel cell - eukaryote with cell wall; HIV - retrovirus that multiplies inside host cells using reverse transcriptase; Methanobacterium - prokaryote used in biogas. Use such classification in statement-based questions.

### Recall

- - **q:** Who stated that new cells arise from pre-existing cells?
  - **a:** Rudolf Virchow (1855).
- - **q:** What are the subunits of a prokaryotic ribosome?
  - **a:** 50S and 30S, forming 70S.
- - **q:** What enzyme lets HIV convert its RNA into DNA?
  - **a:** Reverse transcriptase.

### Common trap

Treating HIV like a bacterium that multiplies on its own: it enters host cells such as macrophages and helper T cells, where reverse transcriptase turns its RNA into DNA that directs the infected cell to make new virus.
