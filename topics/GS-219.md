## GS-219: Money, monetary aggregates and credit

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Basic macro and monetary

*Goal:* Explain Money, monetary aggregates and credit and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Functions of money and the demand for it

Barter needs a double coincidence of wants, which becomes prohibitively costly as an economy grows. Money solves this as a medium of exchange, and also serves as a unit of account in which all prices are expressed and as a store of value that does not perish. Its value as a store depends on price stability: a rising price level erodes purchasing power. People hold money mainly to make transactions, so money demand rises with income; because cash earns no interest, higher interest rates make holding money costlier and reduce the amount demanded. NCERT also notes the push toward digital transactions through Jan Dhan accounts, Aadhaar-enabled payments and e-wallets.

### Measures of money supply

Money supply is a stock: the money held by the public at a point in time. RBI publishes four measures. M1 is currency held by the public plus net demand deposits; 'net' means interbank deposits are excluded. M2 adds savings deposits with post office savings banks. M3 adds the net time deposits of commercial banks to M1, and M4 adds total post office deposits other than National Savings Certificates to M3. Liquidity falls from M1 to M4. M1 and M2 are classed as narrow money and M3 and M4 as broad money; M3 is the workhorse measure. Currency notes are fiat money and legal tender, while cheques on bank deposits can be refused.

### Credit creation and the multiplier in practice

Reserve money, the currency issued by the RBI and held by the public or banks, is the base on which banks create credit. A bank must keep a required share of deposits as reserves and can lend the remainder; that loan is redeposited, enabling further lending until required reserves absorb the original cash. The Economic Survey 2025-26 shows the mechanism in data: after the CRR cut, bankers' deposits with the RBI fell, so measured M0 growth dropped to 2.9% by 31 December 2025, yet M3 grew 12.1% and bank credit to the commercial sector 14.1%. The multiplier (M3/M0) rose to 6.21 from 5.70 a year earlier, which the Survey reads as improved financial intermediation by banks.

### Worked example

Reserve ratio 10%, initial cash deposit Rs 1,000, no currency leakage. Multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.10 = 10, so total deposits can reach Rs 10,000 and loans Rs 9,000. If the ratio is raised to 20%, the multiplier falls to 5 and deposits are capped at Rs 5,000.

### Recall

- - **q:** What distinguishes M3 from M1?
  - **a:** M3 adds net time deposits of commercial banks to M1.
- - **q:** What are the components of reserve money?
  - **a:** Currency in circulation, bankers' deposits with the RBI and other deposits with the RBI.
- - **q:** Why did M0 growth fall in 2025 even as policy eased?
  - **a:** The CRR cut reduced bankers' deposits with the RBI; CRR-adjusted M0 grew faster.

### Common trap

Calling M3 'narrow money' or assuming demand deposits are legal tender: cheques can be refused, so only currency is legal tender, and M3 is broad money.
