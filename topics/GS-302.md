## GS-302: Nanotechnology and materials

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Science · General science / technology

*Goal:* Explain why nanoscale size changes material behaviour and application.

### Small size, different properties

Nanotechnology concerns structures approximately 1–100 nanometres in size. A nanometre is one-billionth of a metre. As particles shrink, their surface area relative to volume rises, exposing more atoms to their surroundings. Quantum effects can also alter electronic and optical behaviour. Consequently the same chemical substance may behave differently in bulk and nanoscale forms.

### Match the property to the use

Large reactive surfaces can improve catalysts; engineered pores can support filtration; nanoparticle carriers can influence drug delivery. Applications depend on controlled size, shape and surface chemistry rather than smallness alone. Evaluate durability, manufacturing cost and safe disposal. Tiny particles may enter biological or environmental pathways differently from bulk materials, so useful performance does not automatically establish safe exposure.

### Worked example

Breaking a solid catalyst into smaller particles exposes more reaction sites per unit mass. Faster reaction may follow, but particles must remain stable: aggregation can reduce the surface advantage and complicate recovery.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why does surface-to-volume ratio increase as size falls?
  - **a:** Surface scales with length squared; volume with length cubed.
- - **q:** Are all nanoscale materials chemically identical?
  - **a:** No. Composition, structure and surface treatment differ.

### Common trap

Nanotechnology is a scale of engineering, not one substance with a single benefit or risk.
