## GS-083: Major folk and tribal dances

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS1 · Culture · Indian art, literature and architecture

*Goal:* Explain Major folk and tribal dances and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Tribal and folk: NCERT's distinction

Dance celebrates births, weddings, festivals, harvests and even the end of a field-worker's day. NCERT groups as tribal the ritual dances of communities living close to nature — Santhal dances, Kerala's Theyyam with tiger and lion masks, the Kalbelia snake-charmers' dance and the fire dances of Rajasthan and Gujarat. Folk dances, in its account, began once people farmed and settled, so they track the agricultural calendar and social events such as marriage, childbirth and death; examples are Bihu, Dandiya Raas, Garba, Bhangra and Chari. Both kinds are participatory, which marks them off from classical forms polished by trained artists and teachers.

### Signature forms from the regions

Kalbelias today live mainly in Jodhpur, Jaisalmer, Jalore and Barmer, and around Jaipur and Pushkar; their women's swirling dance is accompanied by the khanjari and poongi. Mizoram's Cheraw, counted among the oldest folk forms, turns bamboo staves into a moving floor pattern. In Sikkim, monks perform masked Lama dances with cymbals and great horns, rendering codified religious music for the welfare of Buddhism and the state. Kashmir's Rouf is a women's group dance in call-and-response song. Costume matters too: Bihu dancers wear Assamese mekhela chador and dhoti, Rajasthani dancers lehenga and chunri. These details are what statement-based questions usually test.

### Folk performance beyond dance

Many regional forms blend dance with theatre. Bhavai of Gujarat and Rajasthan began as a Navaratri offering to Goddess Amba; Maharashtra's Tamasha takes a Persian-derived name; Kashmir's Bhand Pather uses satire and parody; Swang of Haryana and Punjab tours during Holi and Dussehra; Jatra of Bengal and Odisha moves from place to place, now with social and historical themes. NCERT's Class VII text adds a caution: dances labelled 'folk' often share features thought typical of 'classical' ones, so the labels should not imply a hierarchy of value. Knowing community, occasion and accompaniment is the safest way to identify a form.
