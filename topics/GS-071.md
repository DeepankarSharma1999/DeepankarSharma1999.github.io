## GS-071: World War II origins and outcomes

Mains GS1 · World History · 18th–20th century

*Goal:* Explain World War II origins and outcomes and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Origins and course

The Second World War began barely twenty years after the First. The League of Nations, created to manage conflict after 1918, failed to prevent it. Japan's state-centred nationalism drove expansion in China and Asia, which merged into the global war after its attack on Pearl Harbor. The conflict ran for six years on many fronts — on land, at sea and in the air — between the Axis and the Allies. The German invasion of Russia proved a turning point, and the Soviet Union made huge sacrifices to defeat Nazi Germany. The war closed in Asia with atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki, whose necessity remains debated.

### Human and economic devastation

Death and destruction were enormous: at least 60 million killed directly or indirectly, about three per cent of the 1939 world population, with millions more injured. Unlike earlier wars, most deaths took place away from battlefields, and more civilians than soldiers died. Aerial bombing and sustained artillery destroyed cities, and vast parts of Europe and Asia were devastated, so reconstruction promised to be long and hard. Two influences shaped rebuilding: America's rise to economic, political and military leadership of the Western world, and the Soviet Union's rise, having transformed itself from a backward agrarian country into a world power while the capitalist world was mired in depression.

### Outcomes and exam angles

The victors built new institutions. The UN, founded in 1945 as successor to the League, gave permanent Security Council seats to the war's most powerful victors. The Bretton Woods conference of July 1944 created the IMF and World Bank and a fixed exchange-rate system pegged to the dollar. Defeated Japan was demilitarised under US occupation, adopted Article 9 renouncing war, held elections in 1946 in which women first voted, and saw land reform and attempts to break up the zaibatsu. Over the next two decades most Asian and African colonies won independence. Prelims: 1939–45; Axis versus Allies membership; Bretton Woods 1944. Mains: link failures of collective security to the post-war order.
