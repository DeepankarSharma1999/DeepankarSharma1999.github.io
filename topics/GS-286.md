## GS-286: Ozone layer and Montreal Protocol

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Climate and treaties

*Goal:* Explain Ozone layer and Montreal Protocol and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### How ozone forms and is lost

Ozone is a molecule of three oxygen atoms. At ground level it is poisonous, but high in the atmosphere it shields the surface from ultraviolet radiation that can cause skin cancer. It forms when energetic UV splits some O2 molecules into free oxygen atoms, which then join other O2 molecules. Ozone levels began dropping sharply in the 1980s, a change linked to synthetic CFCs used in refrigerants and fire extinguishers. In 1987 UNEP brokered an agreement to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels, and CFC-free refrigerators became mandatory. The Class 9 text adds that the layer is now slowly recovering, a mark of international scientific cooperation.

### The treaty regime and India's institutions

Concern about skin cancer, cataracts, immune suppression and lower crop yields led to the Vienna Convention in 1985 and the Montreal Protocol in 1987. Controlled ozone-depleting substances include CFCs, HCFCs, halons, carbon tetrachloride, methyl bromide and bromofluoromethane. MoEFCC's Ozone Cell is India's National Ozone Unit, and the ODS (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 implement phase-out, amended several times up to 2019. CFCs, CTC and halons have already been phased out for controlled uses. An empowered steering committee chaired by the Environment Secretary oversees implementation.

### HCFC phase-out, Kigali and cooling

India follows the accelerated HCFC schedule: 10% reduction by January 2015, 35% by 2020, 67.5% by 2025 and full phase-out of production and consumption by 2030, with a 2.5% annual servicing tail during 2030-2040. Import licences for HCFC-141b stopped from January 2020, and in 2024 India cut HCFC consumption by 50% against a 35% target. Manufacture of new HCFC-based equipment ended on 31 December 2024. India ratified the Kigali Amendment in September 2021, committing to cut HFCs in four steps from 2032. The India Cooling Action Plan, launched in March 2019 with a horizon to 2037-38, links refrigerant transition with cooling demand and efficiency.

### Worked example

Match: HCFC-141b - foam blowing, phased out by India first among developing Parties; HFCs - Kigali phase-down from 2032; CFCs - already phased out for controlled uses. Mains frame: science, treaty design (Vienna-Montreal-Kigali), India's schedule, ICAP for cooling demand.

### Recall

- - **q:** At what altitude is the stratospheric ozone layer?
  - **a:** About 10 to 50 km above the Earth's surface.
- - **q:** When will India completely phase out HCFC production and consumption?
  - **a:** By 2030, with a 2.5% servicing tail until 2040.
- - **q:** What are India's Kigali HFC reduction steps?
  - **a:** 10% in 2032, 20% in 2037, 30% in 2042 and 85% in 2047.

### Common trap

Merging the HCFC and HFC schedules: India's HCFC phase-out ends in 2030 (with a servicing tail), whereas the Kigali HFC phase-down only begins in 2032.
