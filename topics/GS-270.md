## GS-270: Biosphere reserves and Ramsar wetlands

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain Biosphere reserves and Ramsar wetlands and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Biosphere reserves: MAB and zonation

MoEFCC describes biosphere reserves as large landscapes of land, coastal or marine ecosystems, or a mix, designated within UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere (MAB) programme, which began in 1971; local communities are treated as an integral component. Each reserve is divided into three linked zones. The core is strictly protected for landscapes, ecosystems, species and genetic variation. The buffer surrounds it and hosts research, education and sustainable activities. The outermost transition zone supports economic and human development that is culturally and ecologically sustainable. The Ministry's Biodiversity Conservation scheme funds state management plans on a 60:40 basis (90:10 for north-eastern and Himalayan states), stressing alternative livelihoods in the buffer and transition zones so that pressure on the core falls.

### India's reserves and the UNESCO network

NCERT lists 18 biosphere reserves, beginning with Nilgiri (1986) across Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Karnataka, followed by Nanda Devi and Nokrek (1988) and Manas, Sunderban, Gulf of Mannar and Great Nicobar (1989); Panna (2011) is the newest in its table, which showed 12 in UNESCO's World Network. MoEFCC's 2025-26 report updates this: Cold Desert entered the World Network at the 2025 ICC-MAB meeting in China, giving India 13, the highest in South Asia. Proposals for Kanha, Pench and Bandhavgarh in Madhya Pradesh have gone to UNESCO, and five-year integrated management plans are being piloted in Nanda Devi, Pachmarhi, Dehang-Dibang and Agasthyamala.

### Ramsar sites and India's wetlands

The Ramsar Convention on Wetlands lists wetlands of international importance; NCERT cites Chilika Lake and Keoladeo National Park as protected waterfowl habitats. MoEFCC reports that 11 designations took India from 85 to 96 Ramsar sites by December 2025, and two more in January 2026 made 98, ranking India first in Asia and third worldwide. At COP15 in Victoria Falls, Zimbabwe, in July 2025, Indore and Udaipur became India's first accredited Wetland Cities, and India's resolution on sustainable lifestyles for wise use of wetlands was adopted. At home, wetlands are regulated under the Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules, 2017. NCERT puts wetland area at 3.9 million hectares, about 70 per cent paddy, and mangroves at 4,992 sq km.
