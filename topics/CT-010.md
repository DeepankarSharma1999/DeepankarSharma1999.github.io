### Bridge: Reading a percentage: the minimum notation

**Percent** means 'out of 100'. The symbol % is a shorthand for the fraction with denominator 100: 25% = 25/100 = 1/4 = 0.25. To turn a percent into a decimal, move the point two places left (40% = 0.40); to turn a decimal into a percent, move it two places right (0.07 = 7%).

The small word **of** between a percent and a quantity means multiply: 20% of 150 = 20/100 x 150 = 30. So '40% of the students' simply means 0.4 times the number of students.

Every percentage is taken **of something**. That something is the **base**. In '30% of the Indian students are girls' the base is the Indian students, not the whole class. Most mistakes in percentage questions come from using the wrong base, so before any calculation ask: percent of what?

A percentage can exceed 100. 150% of a number is 1.5 times it. A percentage can also be applied to a number that is itself the result of an earlier step; each step has its own base.

The CSAT prints decimals with a raised point (12·5%) and mixed fractions as 33 1/7%. They mean 12.5% and 33 + 1/7 percent. Rewrite them in a form you can calculate with before starting.

## The one idea behind all six

A percentage is a **fraction of a base**, and almost every error is a wrong base. The second idea is the **multiplier**: 'up 25%' means multiply by 1.25, 'down 20%' means multiply by 0.8. The third idea is to give an unknown whole a convenient value, usually **100**, when the stem gives only percentages.

### 2. Percent as a fraction: benchmarks and finding the whole

*Objective:* Convert between percent, fraction and decimal, recall the benchmark percentages, and find a whole from a given part.

## Percent, fraction, decimal

A percent is a fraction with denominator 100 (see bridge CT-010-B1). Learn the **benchmarks** as fractions, because they turn many items into one-line mental arithmetic. Every value below was checked by computer.

| Fraction | Percent | Fraction | Percent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 50 | 1/8 | 12.5 |
| 1/3 | 33 1/3 | 3/8 | 37.5 |
| 1/4 | 25 | 5/8 | 62.5 |
| 1/5 | 20 | 7/8 | 87.5 |
| 1/6 | 16 2/3 | 1/10 | 10 |
| 1/7 | about 14.29 | 1/12 | 8 1/3 |
| 1/9 | about 11.11 | 1/16 | 6.25 |

1/7 and 1/9 are shown rounded (Day 19 lesson CT-009 module 4); 1/3, 1/6 and 1/12 recur but are written as mixed numbers.

## Part, percent, whole

Three quantities are tied by **part = (percent / 100) x whole**. Given any two you can find the third.

- Part from whole: 35% of 300 = 0.35 x 300 = 105. *(Original example.)*
- Percent from part and whole: 105 out of 300 is 105 / 300 x 100 = 35%.
- **Whole from part and percent**: whole = part / (percent / 100).

## Finding the whole (family A)

**Unitary method.** If 80% of a race is 12 km, then 1% is 12 / 80 = 0.15 km and 100% is 15 km. Verified item 2019 Q16 asks exactly this; as printed the working gives **15 km** (option B; analysis, not the official key).

**Reverse step.** Verified item 2018 Q41: a student needs 40% to pass, scores 30 and fails by 30. The pass mark is 30 + 30 = 60 marks, and 60 is 40% of the maximum, so the maximum is 60 / 0.4 = **150** (option C by our working). Trap: treating 30 as the 40% mark gives 75, which is not an option, and mixing up which number is the part confuses the base.

**Check that the answer fits.** Verified item 2019 Q17: Raju has 9000, which is 75% of the price, so the price is 9000 / 0.75 = 12000. He borrows 2000, giving 11000, which is **less** than 12000. So option A (still not enough) holds, by our working. The wrong shortcut is to think 9000 plus 2000 clears the price.

Test every answer back into the stem: 80% of 15 is 12.

### 3. Percent change, more-than and less-than pairs, percentage points

*Objective:* Compute percent change on the correct base, convert between 'A is r% more than B' and 'B is s% less than A', and separate percentage from percentage points.

## Percent change

**Percent change = (new - old) / old x 100.** The base is always the **old** value, the one you start from. A rise from 80 to 100 is 20/80 = 25%; a fall from 100 to 80 is 20/100 = 20%. The same gap of 20 gives two different percents because the base differs.

## The pair rule (family B)

If A is r% more than B, then A = B x (1 + r/100). The reverse comparison has a different base (A), so B is **less** than A by s = 100r / (100 + r) percent. Checked values:

| A is more than B by | B is less than A by |
|---|---|
| 25% | 20% |
| 50% | 33 1/3% |
| 100% | 50% |
| 300% | 75% |

In the other direction, if B is d% less than A, A is 100d / (100 - d) percent more than B: 20% less gives 25% more, 40% less gives 66 2/3% more, 50% less gives 100% more. The two percents are not equal unless both are zero.

## Worked items

**2019 Q34** (verified): A scored 20 more than B and B scored 5% less than A. Then B = 0.95 A, so the gap is 5% of A = 20, giving A = 400 and **B = 380** (option B by our working). Trap: reading it as 'A is 5% more than B' gives B = 400, option C.

**2021 Q54** (verified): P scored 40 more than Q and Q scored 10% less than P. Q = 0.9 P, gap = 10% of P = 40, P = 400, **Q = 360** (option A by our working). Pattern: gap divided by the percent gives the **base** (the 'than' person), then subtract.

**2024 Q26** (verified): P is 20% lower than Q, Q is 20% lower than R. P = 0.8 Q and Q = 0.8 R, so P = 0.64 R. R / P = 1 / 0.64 = 1.5625, so R is **56.25% more** than P (option B by our working). The tempting answer 40% (adding 20 and 20) is not an option; 60.50 and 62.25 are nearby traps.

**2019 Q11** (verified): putting B on A raises the weight by 60%, so A + B = 1.6 A and B = 0.6 A. Removing B reduces the total 1.6 A by 0.6 A, which is 0.6 / 1.6 = **37.5%** of the total (option D by our working). The stem asks for the reduction relative to the **total**, not relative to A.

**2024 Q47** (verified): R = 1.2Q and P = 1.25R = 1.5Q, so 3.7Q = 3330, Q = 900 and **P = 1350** (option D by our working).

## Percentage versus percentage points

A rate that moves from 10% to 12% has risen by **2 percentage points** and by 2 / 10 = **20 percent** (relative). 'Percentage points' subtract two percents; 'percent' compares the change with the starting percent. When a stem compares two rates, ask whether it wants the difference or the relative change.

### 4. Successive and repeated percentage change

*Objective:* Combine successive changes by multiplying multipliers, derive and test a + b + ab/100, and explain why an increase and an equal decrease do not cancel.

## The multiplier method

A change of r% (rise positive, fall negative) multiplies the quantity by **1 + r/100**. Successive changes multiply their multipliers; the final percent change is the product minus 1, times 100. Percent changes are **not added**, because each step has a new base.

## Why a + b + ab/100

Start at 100. After a% the value is 100(1 + a/100). After a further b% it is 100(1 + a/100)(1 + b/100). Expand: (1 + a/100)(1 + b/100) = 1 + (a + b)/100 + ab/10000. So the net change is

$$ net % = a + b + ab/100 $$

with a fall entered as a negative number. This is an identity shown by expansion, also spot-checked numerically. Adding a + b alone is wrong whenever ab is not zero. Edge case: b = -100 sends the value to 0 whatever a is, and the formula gives -100.

## Worked items (family C)

**2021 Q78** (verified): -20% then +25%: 0.8 x 1.25 = 1.00, net **0%** (option A by our working). The formula gives -20 + 25 - 5 = 0.

**2022 Q20** (verified): +25%, -20%, +10%: 1.25 x 0.8 x 1.1 = 1.1, so **+10%** (option B by our working). Step 1 and 2 cancel, which is a pair worth remembering: +25% and -20% are inverses, as are +20% and -16 2/3% (master notes, Inverse pairs).

**Equal rise and fall.** Up k% then down k%: (1 + k/100)(1 - k/100) = 1 - k²/10000, a net change of **-k²/100 percent**. For k = 10 it is -1%, for k = 20 it is -4%, for k = 50 it is -25%. A rise and a fall of the same percent never cancel, because the fall has a bigger base.

**2025 Q10** (verified): the pair is applied twice, so q = p x (1 - k²/10000)² = p x (10000 - k²)² / 10⁸. Hence p(10⁴ - k²)² = q x 10⁸, which is option A by our working.

**2020 Q47** (verified): discounts of 10% and 20% then a 10% charge multiply to 0.9 x 0.8 x 1.1 = 0.792. The other orders multiply the same three factors, so all three options give 0.792 and **all are equally good** (option D by our working). Multiplication is order-free, so the order of percentage steps never matters. Edge case: a flat rupee discount is not a percentage, so order matters only for flat amounts (1000 minus 100 then +10% is 990; +10% then minus 100 is 1000).

**2017 Q12** (verified): one-third leaves each year, so two-thirds stay each year. After six years the share left is (2/3)⁶ = **64/729** (option D by our working). Trap: 32/243 is the five-year value, and 1 - 6/3 is nonsense.

**2024 Q27** (verified): dividing by 4 instead of multiplying by 4 turns 4x into x/4. As printed, the change in the result is (x/4 - 4x) / 4x = 1/16 - 1 = -15/16, a **93.75% decrease** (option D by our working, reading the base as the correct result 4x). The other base gives +1500%, not offered, which confirms the reading.

### 5. Price, quantity, expenditure, saving and variable comparisons

*Objective:* Solve items where a price change alters the quantity bought or the spending, treat saving as a difference, and compare two percentage expressions in variables.

## Price, quantity and expenditure (family D)

Always: **expenditure = price x quantity**. With a fixed budget, quantity moves opposite to price.

- Price up r%: quantity down 100r / (100 + r) percent.
- Price down d%: quantity up 100d / (100 - d) percent.

This is the pair rule of module 3 again. Checked: price up 10% gives 9.09% less (1/11); price up 25% gives 20% less; price down 20% gives 25% more.

**2020 Q70** (verified): a 25% rise lets a person buy 6 kg less for 1200. Quantity falls by 20% of the old quantity n, so 0.2n = 6 and n = 30 kg. The old price is 1200 / 30 = **40** (option B by our working). Test: new price 50, 1200 / 50 = 24 kg, which is 6 less than 30.

**2025 Q17** (verified): the price rises 10%, so the same spend buys 1/11 less fuel and the distance falls by 1/11. Distance 2200 km, so the reduction is 2200 / 11 = **200 km** (option B by our working).

## Saving is a difference

**2026 Q43** (verified): take salary 100. He saves 10, so spending is 90. The salary becomes 112 and spending becomes 99 (10% more than 90). Saving is now 112 - 99 = 13, a rise of 3 on 10: **+30%** (option B by our working). Trap: averaging 12% and 10% or using 12 - 10 = 2 points. A difference of two growing quantities does not grow at the average of the two rates.

## Comparing expressions in variables (family F)

**2017 Q30** (verified): P = 0.40A + 0.65B and Q = 0.50A + 0.50B with A > B. Then Q - P = 0.10A - 0.15B = 0.05(2A - 3B). The sign depends on 2A against 3B. With A = 10, B = 9, Q - P = -0.35 (P larger). With A = 10, B = 1, Q - P = +0.85 (Q larger). With A = 30, B = 20, they are equal. So no statement can be concluded with certainty: option D by our working. Method: write the difference and test two or three values.

**2022 Q60** (verified): 70% of x plus y gives 165% of y, so 0.7x = 0.65y and y = (14/13)x. Also 0.6x = 0.65z, so z = (12/13)x. Reading x as positive, z < x < y, which is option A by our working; with x negative every inequality flips and option C would follow.

### 6. Groups, sets, tables, mixtures and pie angles

*Objective:* Handle percentages of two or more groups by choosing a total of 100 or a variable, and convert a percentage share to a pie-chart angle.

## Choose a convenient total

When the stem gives only percentages, **set the whole to 100** (or to a number that makes every part whole). Fill a small table or write each group as a number. Convert back to a percent only at the end.

## Worked items (family E)

**2020 Q55** (verified): 40% of men and 30% of women are married, with one-to-one marriages. Married men equal married women. If M men and W women, 0.4M = 0.3W, so W = (4/3)M. The married persons number 0.4M + 0.4M = 0.8M. The adult total is M + (4/3)M = (7/3)M. Percentage = 0.8 / (7/3) = 2.4/7 = 12/35, which is **34 2/7%** (option C by our working, assuming all spouses lie within this adult population). Trap: averaging 40 and 30 to get 35.

**2021 Q9** (verified): class of 100. Indian students 60; girls 50 in all; 30% of Indian students are girls, so 18 Indian girls. Foreign students 40; foreign girls 50 - 18 = 32; foreign boys 40 - 32 = 8. Percentage of foreign students who are boys = 8 / 40 = **20%** (option D by our working). The base for the answer is the foreign students, not the class.

| | Boys | Girls | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indian | 42 | 18 | 60 |
| Foreign | 8 | 32 | 40 |
| Total | 50 | 50 | 100 |

**2024 Q28** (verified): 80% passed English so 20% failed it; 70% passed Hindi so 30% failed it; 15% failed both. Failed only one: (20 - 15) + (30 - 15) = **20%** (option B by our working). Consistency: passed both = 100 - (20 + 30 - 15) = 65%, which is possible.

**2022 Q64** (verified): votes cast are 80% of voters. X has 56% so Y has 44%; the margin is 12% of the votes cast, equal to 1440. Votes cast = 1440 / 0.12 = 12000, which is 80% of the voters, so voters = 12000 / 0.8 = **15000** (option A by our working). Two bases appear: votes cast, then voters.

**2017 Q37** (verified): the sample has 50% water. Take 3 units of milk; one-third is 1 unit, holding 0.5 unit of water. Add 1 unit of pure milk (an equal amount, read as equal to the one-third portion). Water 0.5 in 2 units is **25%** (option A by our working). The reading is stated, but any portion mixed with an equal amount of pure milk gives 25%.

## Pie angles

A full circle is 360°, so a share of s% has angle 3.6 s degrees. **2021 Q74** (verified): proteins 16% and water 70% leave 14% for the other dry elements. The stem says both proteins and the other dry elements correspond to p%. Read as the combined share, p = 16 + 14 = 30 and the angle is 0.30 x 360 = **108°** (option C by our working). If 'p' were meant for each, 16 would equal 14, which is impossible, so the combined reading is the only one that fits. Flag: read the stem as printed.

### 7. Method, timing, traps, ambiguity and when to guess

*Objective:* Apply a five-step routine to any percentage item, spot the standard traps, and decide with arithmetic whether a guess pays.

## The five-step routine (about 45 to 75 seconds, a practice target)

1. **Base.** Underline every percentage and write the base in words beside it.
2. **Family.** Name A to F (module 1) and the first move.
3. **Number.** Put a convenient value on the whole (100 for percent-only stems, otherwise a number that makes parts whole).
4. **Calculate** with multipliers or a small table; keep fractions exact until the end.
5. **Check.** Test the answer back into the stem, and ask whether its size is plausible (an up-and-down pair of the SAME percent k ends below the start; unequal pairs can end above it: +40/-25 = +5%).

## The eight standard traps

| Trap | Where it appears | Cure |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong base | 2019 Q11, 2021 Q9 | write the base in words |
| Adding successive percents | 2022 Q20, 2025 Q10 | multiply the multipliers |
| 'More' reversed to 'less' with the same number | 2019 Q34, 2021 Q54, 2024 Q26 | use 100r/(100+r) |
| Averaging unequal groups | 2020 Q55 | use weights or totals |
| Percentage points versus percent | module 3 | ask what is compared |
| Flat amounts treated as percents | module 4 edge case | order matters only for flat amounts |
| Averaging growth rates | 2026 Q43 | rebuild from 100 |
| Answer to the wrong quantity | 2019 Q11, 2019 Q17 | reread the last line of the stem |

## Weighted averages: a neat rule that fails

'The pass rate of two classes is the average of their rates' fails when the sizes differ: 20 students at 50% and 80 at 80% give 74%, not 65% (see the check). Use weights, as in 2020 Q55.

## Ambiguous stems

State the reading used, then solve. Four items in the verified record need one: 2017 Q37 (equal amount read as equal to the one-third portion), 2021 Q74 (both read as a combined share), 2022 Q60 (x read as positive) and 2024 Q27 (the change taken relative to the correct result).

## When a guess pays

A wrong answer costs 2.5 / 3 = 0.833 marks. A blind guess among four options has expected value 0.25 x 2.5 - 0.75 x 0.833 = 0.625 - 0.625 = 0. If you can eliminate two options, a guess between the remaining two is worth 0.5 x 2.5 - 0.5 x 0.833 = 1.25 - 0.417 = +0.833 marks on average. Elimination here is concrete: a net change after an equal rise and fall must be negative, when the part is less than 100% of the whole, the whole exceeds the part (a percent above 100 reverses this), and a percentage of a subgroup must lie between 0 and 100. Verify the penalty against the current notification.

### Notes: Definitions

- Percent: a fraction with denominator 100; 40% = 40/100 = 0.4. Per cent means out of a hundred.
- Base: the quantity a percentage is taken of. Naming the base is the first step of every item.
- Multiplier: the factor 1 + r/100 that applies a change of r% (negative r for a fall).
- Percent change: (new - old) / old x 100, on the old value as the base.
- Percentage point: the difference of two percents. A move from 10% to 12% is 2 points and a 20 percent relative rise.
- Successive change: two or more changes applied one after another, each on the new value.
- Reverse percentage: finding the original quantity from the changed quantity and the percent change.

### Notes: Formulae

- part = (percent / 100) x whole; whole = part / (percent / 100)
- new = old x (1 + r/100); percent change = (new - old)/old x 100
- A is r% more than B  =>  B is 100r/(100 + r)% less than A
- B is d% less than A  =>  A is 100d/(100 - d)% more than B
- Net change of successive a% and b%: a + b + ab/100 (fall entered negative); several steps: multiply the multipliers
- Equal rise and fall of k%: net change -k^2/100 percent
- Fixed spending: price up r% cuts quantity by 100r/(100 + r)%; price down d% raises it by 100d/(100 - d)%
- Pie angle = 3.6 x percent degrees

### Notes: Benchmark conversions (checked by computer)

- Fractions and their percents for fast mental arithmetic.

### Notes: Inverse pairs (equal start and end)

- A rise of r% is undone by the fall below; check each by multiplying.

### Notes: Edge cases tested against the rules in this lesson

- a + b + ab/100 equals the exact multiplier product (an identity, spot-checked numerically on whole pairs from -99 to 200); the plain sum a + b fails whenever ab is not zero.
- Order of percentage steps does not matter; for flat amounts it does (1000 minus 100 then +10% is 990, +10% then minus 100 is 1000).
- A 50% fall needs a 100% rise to undo it; a fall of 100% cannot be undone.
- Averaging rates fails with unequal groups (74% against 65%) and with differences of growing quantities (saving +30% against salary +12%, spending +10%).
- Percent more and percent less are equal only at zero (r = 0).

### Active recall

- **coreConcepts:**
  - Every percentage has a base; name it first.
  - A change of r% multiplies by 1 + r/100, and successive changes multiply.
  - More-than and less-than percentages use different bases, so they differ.
  - Percent items repeat six families: whole from part, pairs, successive change, price-quantity, groups, variable comparison.
- **essentialFacts:**
  - Net change of a% then b% is a + b + ab/100.
  - Equal rise and fall of k% gives -k^2/100 percent.
  - Price up r% cuts quantity at fixed spending by 100r/(100 + r)%.
  - 12.5% = 1/8, 37.5% = 3/8, 16 2/3% = 1/6, 33 1/3% = 1/3.
  - Pie angle = 3.6 x percent.
  - A wrong CSAT answer costs 0.833 marks (verify); a blind guess among four has expected value 0.
- **criticalDistinctions:**
  - Percent versus percentage points.
  - Percent more than versus percent less than (different bases).
  - Percent of the class versus percent of a subgroup.
  - A rise and a fall of the same percent do not cancel.
- **formulae:**
  - whole = part / (percent/100)
  - new = old x (1 + r/100)
  - a + b + ab/100
  - -k^2/100
  - 100r/(100 + r)
  - 100d/(100 - d)
- **traps:**
  - Adding successive percents.
  - Using the wrong base (class instead of subgroup).
  - Reversing a more/less pair with the same number.
  - Averaging percentages of unequal groups.
  - Treating a stem with a loose phrase as certain without stating the reading.
- **blankPrompts:**

  - - **q:** 10% to 12% is a rise of ____ percentage points.

- **shortQuestions:**
  - - **q:** Why do +20% and -20% not cancel?
    - **a:** The fall is taken on the larger value: 1.2 x 0.8 = 0.96, a net fall of 4%.

- **examinerTraps:**
  - Option sets built from the added-percent mistake (5% instead of 0%, 15% instead of 10%).
  - Pairs where the reversed base gives the second-best option (400 for 380).
  - Subgroup bases (Indian students, votes cast) that differ from the stated total.

- **distractorAnalysis:**
  - Adding percents gives the sum a + b, which usually differs from a + b + ab/100.
  - Using the wrong base gives a result that is a clean number, so it looks right.
  - Averaging gives 35% for the 40%/30% married item while the weighted answer is 34 2/7%.
  - When the percent is below 100, an answer smaller than the part shows you multiplied by the percent instead of dividing (12 x 0.8 = 9.6 instead of 12 / 0.8 = 15).
- **prelimsElimination:**
  - An up-and-down pair of the SAME percent k ends below the start (by k^2/100 percent); unequal pairs can end above, at or below it (+40/-25 = +5%, -20/+25 = 0). If no option is negative, check whether the steps cancel exactly.
  - When the part is less than 100% of the whole, the whole exceeds the part; a percent above 100 reverses this (60 is 150% of 40).
  - A subgroup percentage lies between 0 and 100, and the totals of a table must agree.
  - Try two or three numbers in a variable-comparison item; if the sign flips, the answer is that nothing is certain.
- **commonMistakes:**
  - Adding successive percents.
  - Using the wrong base.
  - Mixing percentage points and percent.
  - Calculating the wrong quantity at the end (total instead of reduction).
  - Rounding early.

#### Successive changes multiply: +25% then −25% (draft)

Module 4: percent changes multiply their multipliers. A rise and a fall of the same k percent never cancel, because the fall works on a bigger base; the net is -k^2/100 percent.

A horizontal number line with three marked points 93.75, 100 and 125. An arc above the line goes from 100 to 125 labelled times 1.25 (plus 25 percent). A longer arc below the line goes from 125 back to 93.75 labelled times 0.75 (minus 25 percent). The result line states 1.25 times 0.75 equals 0.9375, a net fall of 6.25 percent.

1. Draw a number line and mark 100, 125 and 93.75.
2. Draw an arc from 100 to 125 above the line and write × 1.25 (+25%).
3. Draw a second arc from 125 to 93.75 below the line and write × 0.75 (−25%).
4. Write the net multiplier 1.25 × 0.75 = 0.9375, so −6.25% (= −k²/100 with k = 25).

#### Percentage points against percent (draft)

Module 3: percentage points subtract two percents (12 - 10 = 2); percent compares that change with the starting percent (2/10 = 20 percent). Read the stem for which one it wants.

Two horizontal bars, an old rate of 10 percent and a new rate of 12 percent. The new bar has a dashed extra segment of 2 units. A bracket under the extra segment is labelled plus 2 percentage points, and the text below says the relative change is 2 divided by 10 times 100, which is 20 percent.

1. Draw a bar for the old rate (10%) and a slightly longer bar below it for the new rate (12%).
2. Mark the extra piece with a dashed outline and label it +2 percentage points (12 − 10).
3. Write the relative change: 2 ÷ 10 × 100 = 20 percent, with the old rate as the base.

#### More-than and less-than: same gap, different base (draft)

Module 3: the same gap of 25 is measured against two different bases, so A is 25 percent more than B while B is only 20 percent less than A.

Two vertical bars: B of height 100 and A of height 125. A dashed line from the top of B to A shows a gap of 25. Text says the gap is 25 percent of B so A is 25 percent more than B, and 20 percent of A so B is 20 percent less than A; the general rule is 100r over 100 plus r.

1. Draw bar B of height 100 and bar A of height 125 on a common baseline.
2. Extend a dashed line from the top of B across to A and mark the gap 25.
3. Write: gap ÷ B = 25% (A is more than B by 25%); gap ÷ A = 20% (B is less than A by 20%).
4. Write the general rule: B is less than A by 100r/(100 + r) percent.

### Memory skeleton

- Six item families: whole from part, more/less pairs, successive change, price-quantity-saving, groups and tables, variable comparison
- Base first: part = (percent/100) × whole, whole = part/(percent/100); give an unknown total the value 100
- Percent change on the old value; A r% more than B ⇒ B is 100r/(100 + r)% less; percent against percentage points
- Successive changes multiply: a + b + ab/100; equal rise and fall −k²/100; order free except for flat amounts
- Fixed spending: price up r% cuts quantity by 100r/(100 + r)%; saving is a difference, rebuild from 100
- Groups: total of 100, two-way table, inclusion-exclusion, weighted not averaged rates; pie angle 3.6 × percent
- Five-step routine: base, family, number, calculate, check; state the reading of a loose stem

### Last-week summary

- Name every base in words; whole = part/(percent/100) (105 is 35% of 300); a total of 100 for percent-only stems; test the answer back into the stem.
- r% more ⇒ 100r/(100 + r)% less (25 ↔ 20, 50 ↔ 33 1/3, 100 ↔ 50); d% less ⇒ 100d/(100 − d)% more; 10% to 12% is 2 points and 20 percent.
- Multiply multipliers: a + b + ab/100; ±k gives −k²/100 (±20: −4%); +25/−20 and +20/−16 2/3 cancel; order matters only for flat amounts (990 against 1000).
- Fixed spending: price +10% → quantity −1/11, +25% → −20%, −20% → +25%. Salary +12%, spending +10%, saving 10 of 100: saving +30%, not an average.
- Weighted, not averaged: 20 at 50% and 80 at 80% give 74%, not 65%; pie angle 3.6 × percent (25% = 90°); a subgroup percent lies between 0 and 100.
