## GS-285: Kyoto Protocol and carbon markets

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Climate and treaties

*Goal:* Explain Kyoto Protocol and carbon markets and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Kyoto: binding targets and flexibility mechanisms

NCERT records that the Kyoto Protocol, proclaimed in 1997, took effect in 2005 after 141 ratifications and required 35 industrialised countries to bring emissions by 2012 to 5 per cent below 1990. The IPCC glossary places its adoption at COP3 in Kyoto and its entry into force on 16 February 2005. To soften the economic cost of these commitments, the Protocol created three market-based flexibility mechanisms. Under Joint Implementation (Article 6), investors from Annex B countries jointly carry out projects that cut emissions or enhance sinks and share Emission Reduction Units. Under the Clean Development Mechanism (Article 12), they finance projects in non-Annex B developing countries and earn Certified Emission Reductions that count towards their own commitments. Emissions trading (Article 17) lets holders sell surplus permits under a cap.

### India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, 2023

The Ministry of Power notified the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme on 28 June 2023 under the Energy Conservation Act, 2001. One carbon credit represents one tonne of CO2 equivalent reduced, removed or avoided. A National Steering Committee, chaired by the Power Secretary with the Environment Secretary as co-chair, governs the Indian carbon market; the Bureau of Energy Efficiency is administrator, Grid Controller of India Limited is the registry, and CERC regulates trading on power exchanges. In the compliance mechanism, MoEFCC notifies greenhouse gas emission-intensity targets for obligated entities: those that beat their targets receive carbon credit certificates, while those that fall short must buy certificates to cover the gap. Non-obligated entities may buy certificates voluntarily.

### Article 6 deals and green credits

MoEFCC has a cell handling carbon markets and Paris Article 6. India and Japan signed a Memorandum of Cooperation on the Joint Credit Mechanism under Article 6.2 on 7 August 2025, and at the first Indo-Russia working group on climate change in September 2025 the two sides explored collaboration under Articles 6.2 and 6.4. Separately, the Green Credit Programme, launched at COP28 as part of the LiFE movement, rests on the Green Credit Rules, 2023, notified on 12 October 2023 under the Environment Protection Act, 1986. Green credits reward voluntary environment-positive actions, starting with tree plantation on degraded land, and the rules keep them independent of carbon credits under the CCTS. ICFRE administers the programme.
