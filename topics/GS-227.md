## GS-227: Trade deficits, tariffs and WTO basics

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Basic macro and monetary

*Goal:* Explain Trade deficits, tariffs and WTO basics and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Tariffs, quotas and why countries protect

NCERT's toy example captures the mechanics: a tax on imported toys raises their price in India, so imports fall and domestic toy-makers gain. Such an import tax is a trade barrier; a quota, which caps the quantity that may enter, is another. After Independence India used both, allowing mainly essential imports such as machinery, fertilisers and petroleum, to give infant industries room to grow — a strategy NCERT notes developed countries also used early on. From 1991 the policy changed: most barriers to trade and foreign investment were dismantled on the view that competition would raise quality and efficiency. Removing such government restrictions is what liberalisation means.

### GATT, the WTO and India's commitments

GATT, created in 1948 with 23 countries, gave way in 1995 to the World Trade Organisation, which administers multilateral agreements and seeks a rule-based regime in which members cannot impose arbitrary trade restrictions. Its agreements span goods and services and aim to cut tariff and non-tariff barriers and widen market access. India honoured its commitments by removing quantitative restrictions and lowering tariffs; QRs on manufactured consumer goods and farm products were fully lifted from April 2001. NCERT also records criticism: developed countries have kept trade barriers of their own, and while developing countries were pushed to open up, rich countries continued heavy farm support, raising questions about fairness.

### India's trade deficit and the tariff shock

The Economic Survey 2025-26 shows why the measure of 'trade deficit' matters. In FY25, total imports of goods and services were USD 919.9 billion, but because services earned a surplus, the combined trade deficit was USD 94.7 billion — far smaller than the merchandise deficit of USD 283.5 billion. In April–December 2025, the combined deficit was USD 96.6 billion against USD 88.4 billion a year earlier. The Survey adds that India faced an effective 50% tariff on exports to the US, among the highest on any country; in several labour-intensive sectors exports to the US fell year-on-year in April–November 2025 while exports to the world kept growing, which the Survey attributes to diversification. Diversification thus cushioned a bilateral tariff shock.

### Worked example

An imported toy costs Rs 400 landed; a domestic toy costs Rs 450. A 25% tariff raises the imported price to Rs 500, making the domestic toy cheaper and shifting purchases to it. A quota of 1,000 toys would instead cap the quantity directly, whatever the price — both are trade barriers, but only the tariff is a tax.

### Recall

- - **q:** When was the WTO founded and what did it replace?
  - **a:** 1995; it succeeded GATT (1948).
- - **q:** From when were India's QRs on consumer goods and farm products fully removed?
  - **a:** April 2001.
- - **q:** What was India's total trade deficit in FY25?
  - **a:** USD 94.7 billion (goods and services, Economic Survey 2025-26).

### Common trap

Assuming WTO membership guarantees free trade: NCERT notes developed countries kept large farm subsidies while developing members were pressed to cut barriers.
