## GS-207: UPSC and State PSCs

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS2 · Polity · Institutions, law and politics

*Goal:* Explain UPSC and State PSCs and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Why merit-based commissions

NCERT explains that the framers wanted a non-partisan, professional bureaucracy selected impartially on merit, so they entrusted recruitment of Union civil servants to the UPSC and created similar commissions for States, with fixed terms and removal only after enquiry. IAS and IPS officers selected through the UPSC form the backbone of State administration, while State services are filled through State PSCs. The Constitution also seeks social representativeness through reservations, and Article 320(4) separately provides that PSCs need not be consulted on the manner of making reservation provisions under Article 16(4) or on giving effect to Article 335.

### Composition, tenure and removal

The President appoints the Chairman and members of the UPSC and of Joint Commissions; the Governor appoints those of a State PSC. A member serves six years or until 65 for the UPSC and 62 for State or Joint Commissions, and cannot be reappointed. Removal for misbehaviour requires a Supreme Court inquiry on presidential reference; interest in government contracts is deemed misbehaviour. The President may remove a member directly for insolvency, outside paid employment or bodily or mental infirmity. Service conditions cannot be varied to a member's disadvantage, and expenses are charged on the Consolidated Fund, insulating PSCs from annual votes.

### Functions and the advisory nature of advice

PSCs conduct examinations for appointments, and the UPSC helps two or more States run joint recruitment for specialised services if they request it. They must be consulted on methods of recruitment, principles of appointment, promotion and transfer, disciplinary matters, legal-cost claims and injury pensions. Regulations can exempt specified matters from consultation, but these must be laid before the legislature for at least fourteen days. Additional functions can be conferred by law. Advice is not binding: annual reports, laid before Parliament or the State legislature, must explain any non-acceptance, which creates political accountability rather than legal compulsion.
