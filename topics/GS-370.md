## GS-370: Attitude structure, content and functions

Mains GS4 · Ethics · Attitude, aptitude and emotional intelligence

*Goal:* Explain Attitude structure, content and functions and use it to reason through the worked example.

### Concept and distinctions

Attitudes have cognitive, affective and behavioural components: beliefs, feelings and tendencies to act toward an object. These components may be inconsistent. Attitudes can help organize knowledge, express identity, defend self-image or serve practical interests.

### How to apply it

To change an attitude, diagnose its basis. Information may correct a false belief but leave fear or group loyalty untouched. Experience, trusted messengers and institutional incentives may be needed alongside evidence. A stated attitude also differs from actual behaviour under constraints. In an administrative setting, ask whether a service failure reflects prejudice, lack of resources or a rule, because the appropriate remedy differs.

### Worked example

A staff member may endorse inclusion intellectually yet avoid difficult applicants because of anxiety or workload.

### Recall

- - **q:** Name the three components of an attitude.
  - **a:** Cognitive beliefs, affective feelings and behavioural tendencies.

### Common trap

Assuming more information alone changes every attitude.
