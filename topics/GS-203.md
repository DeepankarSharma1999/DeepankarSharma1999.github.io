## GS-203: Election Commission: role and powers

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS2 · Polity · Institutions, law and politics

*Goal:* Explain Election Commission: role and powers and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Constitutional mandate and structure

Article 324 uses three strong words, superintendence, direction and control, over both electoral rolls and the conduct of elections. NCERT notes that these words give the Commission a decisive role in virtually everything concerning elections and that the Supreme Court has agreed with this reading. Each State has a Chief Electoral Officer to assist the Commission. The Commission can be single or multi-member: it was single-member until 1989, briefly expanded before that year's general election, and has stayed multi-member since 1993. The President may also appoint Regional Commissioners before general elections after consulting the Commission.

### Independence safeguards and their limits

The CEC can be removed only like a Supreme Court judge, and his service conditions cannot be varied to his disadvantage after appointment; other Commissioners and Regional Commissioners cannot be removed except on the CEC's recommendation. Article 324(2) leaves the method of appointment subject to parliamentary law. As printed in the NCERT Class XI text, Commissioners serve six years or until 65, are appointed on the Council of Ministers' advice, and critics proposed involving the Leader of Opposition and the Chief Justice; because Parliament may legislate on appointments, verify the statute in force rather than treating this description as current. NCERT also stresses that independence depends on assertive use of existing powers.

### Functions during the electoral process

NCERT lists the Commission's functions: supervising preparation of accurate voter lists; fixing the election schedule from notification to counting; postponing or cancelling polls where the atmosphere is vitiated; enforcing the Model Code of Conduct; ordering re-polls and recounts; and recognising parties and allotting symbols. Once the process begins, officers of the Union and State governments on election duty come under its control, and it can transfer them or act against partisan conduct. NCERT cites the mid-way postponement of the 1991 polls after Rajiv Gandhi's assassination and the deferral of the 2002 Gujarat Assembly election, which the Supreme Court upheld.

### Worked example

Statement set: (1) ECI conducts Vice-Presidential elections: correct (Art 324(1)); (2) ECI supervises Panchayat polls: incorrect, State Election Commissioners do (NCERT); (3) an Election Commissioner can be removed only like an SC judge: incorrect, removal needs the CEC's recommendation (Art 324(5) second proviso).

### Recall

- - **q:** Which elections fall under Article 324?
  - **a:** Parliament, State legislatures, President and Vice-President.
- - **q:** Since when has the ECI been continuously multi-member?
  - **a:** 1993 (per NCERT).
- - **q:** Who must recommend removal of an Election Commissioner?
  - **a:** The Chief Election Commissioner.

### Common trap

The CEC's removal protection does not extend equally to other Election Commissioners: they are removable on the CEC's recommendation, not through the judge-like procedure.
