## GS-299: Remote sensing and navigation systems

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Science · General science / technology

*Goal:* Explain Remote sensing and navigation systems and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### How remote sensing works

Human eyes and cameras respond to a small slice of radiation, but remote sensors record a much wider range emitted or reflected by any surface above absolute zero. The term arose in the early 1960s and refers to acquiring information about objects through a sensor not in physical contact with them. The stages run from the energy source (the sun, or artificial sources such as radar beams), through transmission, interaction with the surface and passage back through the atmosphere, to detection, conversion into images, information extraction and mapping. Carbon dioxide and water absorb some middle-infrared energy and dust scatters blue light, so that energy never reaches the sensor.

### Orbits, images and resolution

Remote sensing satellites fly near-polar sun-synchronous orbits at 700-900 km, while weather and telecom satellites sit at about 36,000 km in geostationary orbit. In a standard false colour composite, blue is given to green light, green to red light and red to near-infrared, which makes vegetation appear red. The sun-synchronous orbit repeats coverage at fixed intervals, called temporal resolution, enabling change detection, such as seasonal vegetation in the Himalaya or tsunami damage at Banda Aceh in 2004. Spatial resolution is the ability to separate closely spaced objects; spectral resolution is the ability to record different bands.

### India's navigation and radar systems

NavIC is India's independent regional navigation system for India and 1500 km beyond, intended for transport, aviation, maritime, surveying, timing and telecom users. Its base layer now has four satellites providing position, navigation and timing; NVS-02, launched in January 2025, missed its final orbit and offers limited service, though its indigenous atomic clock exceeded specification. GAGAN augments GPS for aviation, with ISRO building the space segment and AAI the ground segment. For imaging, NISAR, launched by GSLV in July 2025, carries L-band and S-band synthetic aperture radar, a radar system for microwave imaging developed jointly by ISRO and NASA.

### Worked example

Statement set: (1) In a standard FCC, healthy vegetation appears red - correct; (2) NavIC provides global coverage - incorrect, it is regional (India + 1500 km); (3) GAGAN is meant for civil aviation - correct. Mains frame: distinguish sensing (information about surfaces) from navigation (position and time), then show complementarity in mapping and disaster response.

### Recall

- - **q:** What is temporal resolution?
  - **a:** The revisit interval at which a satellite images the same area.
- - **q:** What is NavIC's service area?
  - **a:** India and up to 1500 km beyond the Indian mainland.
- - **q:** Which satellites form NavIC's current base layer?
  - **a:** IRNSS-1B, IRNSS-1F, IRNSS-1I and NVS-01.

### Common trap

Treating NavIC as a remote sensing system or as global; it is a regional navigation (PNT) system, distinct from imaging satellites.
