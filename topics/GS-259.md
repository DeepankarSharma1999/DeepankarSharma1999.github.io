## GS-259: Agricultural sustainability, soil and climate impacts

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Agriculture and food

*Goal:* Explain Agricultural sustainability, soil and climate impacts and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### How intensive farming degrades land

NCERT calls land degradation one of the most serious outcomes of faulty irrigation and development strategy, because it depletes soil fertility. In irrigated areas, alkalisation, salinisation and waterlogging have damaged large tracts, and heavy use of insecticides and pesticides has built toxic concentrations in soil profiles. Multiple cropping reduced fallow periods and pushed out leguminous crops, interrupting natural nitrogen fixation. Rain-fed humid and semi-arid areas suffer water and wind erosion, often human-induced. Groundwater depletion in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh, with rising salinity, completes the picture of resource stress.

### How climate change hits Indian farms

Government impact assessments cited in the Economic Survey 2023-24 project that, without adaptation, rainfed rice yields could drop 20 per cent by 2050 and 47 per cent by 2080, irrigated rice 3.5 and 5 per cent, and wheat 19.3 and 40 per cent. Research cited in the Economic Survey 2024-25 ties severe rainfall shortfalls to large yield losses, finds heat stress doing growing damage, and estimates that 2°C of warming with 7 per cent more rain by 2099 could lower productivity by 8–12 per cent; droughts and heatwaves do more harm than floods or cold waves. The CACP traced below-average rice yields in several states to unseasonal rain, heat stress and dry spells, and pulse output fell in 15 of 27 El Niño years between 1951 and 2024 (Economic Survey 2025-26). Indo-Gangetic and rice-dominant states face higher risk.

### Policy response: resilience, soils and organic farming

The National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture, one of the NAPCC missions, seeks climate resilience through location-specific integrated farming, soil and moisture conservation, water efficiency, nutrient management, agro-advisories and climate-resilient crop, livestock and fish varieties. Over 25.55 crore Soil Health Cards had been issued by November 2025, yet cheap urea pushed the N:P:K ratio to about 10.9:4.1:1 in 2023-24, adding nitrate leaching to soil damage. PKVY and MOVCDNER promote organic farming, and the National Mission on Natural Farming had enrolled 15.79 lakh farmers (Economic Survey 2025-26). NCERT notes organic farming saves on inputs and uses more labour but begins with lower yields and shorter shelf life. ICAR's 2024 index scores Indian agriculture at 0.49, only moderately sustainable. Protect soil, water and biodiversity rather than reject modern inputs.

### Worked example

Mains frame — 'Climate change and agricultural sustainability': Impacts — projected yield falls (rainfed rice −20 per cent by 2050; wheat −19.3 per cent by 2050), heat stress, El Niño hits pulses; Stressors — salinity, groundwater decline, N:P:K 10.9:4.1:1 in 2023-24; Measures — NMSA, irrigation and micro-irrigation, soil health cards, balanced fertiliser, crop diversification, organic and natural farming; Trade-offs — initial yield loss, marketing; Conclusion.

### Recall

- - **q:** Projected 2050 yield fall for rainfed rice without adaptation?
  - **a:** About 20 per cent (47 per cent by 2080), per the Economic Survey 2023-24.
- - **q:** Which extreme events hurt Indian farm productivity most, per studies cited in the Economic Survey 2024-25?
  - **a:** Droughts and heatwaves, more than floods or cold waves.
- - **q:** NMSA belongs to which national plan?
  - **a:** The National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC).
- - **q:** Main form of land degradation in irrigated areas?
  - **a:** Salinisation/alkalisation and waterlogging of soils.

### Common trap

Assuming degradation is mainly a dryland problem: NCERT stresses that the situation is particularly alarming in irrigated areas, where salinity, alkalinity and waterlogging are the main forms.
