## GS-177: Cabinet and cabinet committees

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS2 · Polity · Union government

### Bridge: Committee, council, cabinet: three words, three bodies

In everyday speech 'cabinet' and 'council' are used loosely, but UPSC uses them precisely. The **Council of Ministers** is the whole body of ministers (Cabinet ministers, ministers of state and deputy ministers: GS-176 module 7). The **Cabinet** is the senior group within the Council that takes the main decisions. A **Cabinet committee** is a smaller group of ministers, picked by the Prime Minister, that handles a subject area on behalf of the Cabinet. A simple picture is three circles: the biggest is the Council, inside it is the Cabinet, and the committees are overlapping groups drawn from the Cabinet, with a few non-Cabinet ministers allowed. A **committee of Parliament** is something else: it is a group of members of Parliament that scrutinises the government. Do not mix the two: a Cabinet committee is part of the executive, a parliamentary committee belongs to the legislature (standard; verify with the parliament lessons).

### Bridge: Extra-constitutional, convention and Rules of Business

An institution's authority can come from different places. Some bodies are written into the Constitution, such as the Council of Ministers in Articles 74 and 75. Others are called **extra-constitutional**: they are not mentioned in the Constitution but exist by practice, rules or convention. A **convention** is an accepted practice that is followed although not written as law; the Cabinet's role in practice is largely a matter of convention. The **Rules of Business** are rules of the government itself that say how the executive carries out its work; Laxmikanth says they provide for Cabinet committees. Extra-constitutional does not mean illegal or unconstitutional: it means the Constitution is silent and the arrangement rests on rules and practice. Knowing this prevents a typical error: saying that a Cabinet committee is 'constitutional because it is part of the Cabinet'. It is extra-constitutional in the sense given in Laxmikanth's first feature.

### 1. Orientation: why a layer of committees sits under the Cabinet

*Objective:* Say what a Cabinet committee is, why it exists and where it sits between ministers and the Cabinet.

## The problem the committees solve

The Union government takes decisions on defence, the economy, foreign policy, appointments and the business of Parliament, all at the same time. If every file had to be argued by the whole Cabinet in one weekly meeting, the Cabinet would be overloaded and slow. Laxmikanth explains that Cabinet committees are an organisational device to **reduce the enormous workload** of the Cabinet. They allow in-depth examination of policy issues and effective coordination, and rest on **division of labour** and **effective delegation**. Think of a hospital: the full medical board does not examine every patient; specialist teams do and report to the board.

## Where they sit

Three layers are in play, and GS-176 has already taught the first two (the Council of Ministers, the Cabinet and the Kitchen Cabinet; see GS-176 module 9 and its master-note table). This lesson adds the third:

1. The **Council of Ministers**: the whole body of ministers (Articles 74-75).
2. The **Cabinet**: the senior part of the Council that takes the decisions.
3. **Cabinet committees**: smaller groups of ministers set up by the Prime Minister to prepare, and often to take, decisions for the Cabinet.

## What you will not find here

GS-176 teaches collective responsibility, the 15 per cent size cap and the Cabinet against the Council. They are not repeated. This lesson spends its words on what the committees are, the list and functions, the Cabinet Secretariat and PMO in outline, the way a decision moves, the Appointments Committee and how UPSC tests the idea (the 2017 Prelims question on the principle of the Cabinet system and the 2024 Mains question on the Cabinet and parliamentary supremacy).

### 2. Features of Cabinet committees

*Objective:* State the eight features of Cabinet committees and the difference between standing and ad hoc committees.

## Not in the Constitution

Laxmikanth's first feature: Cabinet committees are **extra-constitutional**. The Constitution does not mention them. However, the **Rules of Business** provide for their establishment. So they exist because of rules and practice, not because of an Article (bridge module B2 explains the vocabulary).

## Standing and ad hoc

Two types exist: **standing** committees are permanent; **ad hoc** committees are temporary, set up from time to time to deal with special problems and disbanded after the task is complete. Laxmikanth's example (note 1): the **Emergency Committee** set up in 1962 after the Chinese invasion.

## Who sets them up

The **Prime Minister** sets them up according to the needs of the time. Their number, names and composition therefore vary from time to time. Module 3 shows the list changing over 1994, 2013 and 2019.

## Membership

- A committee has **three to eight members**.
- It usually includes only **Cabinet ministers**, but **non-Cabinet ministers are not debarred**.
- It includes the ministers in charge of the subjects covered and also **other senior ministers**.

## Who chairs

Committees are **mostly headed by the Prime Minister**. Sometimes other Cabinet ministers, particularly the **Home Minister** or the **Finance Minister**, chair them. But if the Prime Minister is a member of a committee, he **invariably presides** over it. (The statement about the Home Minister reflects Laxmikanth's text; which minister chairs a given committee changes with the government, so verify current chairs.)

## What they do

They sort out issues and **formulate proposals** for the Cabinet, and they also **take decisions**. But the **Cabinet can review** their decisions. This is the answer to the worry that committees weaken the Cabinet: the final authority stays with the Cabinet.

## One-line feature list

1. Extra-constitutional; provided for by the Rules of Business.
2. Standing or ad hoc.
3. Set up by the Prime Minister.
4. Three to eight members.
5. Cabinet ministers, with non-Cabinet ministers not debarred.
6. Include the subject ministers and other senior ministers.
7. Mostly chaired by the Prime Minister.
8. Take decisions, reviewable by the Cabinet; reduce workload.

## Two quick rule tests

Test 'Cabinet committees only recommend': false, because they also take decisions. Test 'Only Cabinet ministers can be members': false as a rule, because non-Cabinet ministers are not debarred, though usually only Cabinet ministers are members.

### 3. The list of committees, the four important ones and Groups of Ministers

*Objective:* Recall the committee lists in 1994, 2013 and 2019 and the functions and chairs of the four important committees.

## The list changes with the government

Laxmikanth gives three snapshots, which show how the PM adjusts the committees to the time:

| Year | Number | Committees named (summary) |
|---|---|---|
| 1994 | 13 | Political Affairs; Natural Calamities; Parliamentary Affairs; Appointments; Accommodation; Foreign Investment; Drug Abuse Control; Prices; Minority Welfare; Economic Affairs; Trade and Investment; Expenditure; Infrastructure |
| 2013 | 10 | Economic Affairs; Prices; Political Affairs; Appointments; Security; WTO Matters; Investment; UIDAI-related issues; Parliamentary Affairs; Accommodation |
| 2019 | 8 | Political Affairs; Economic Affairs; Appointments; Security; Parliamentary Affairs; Accommodation; Investment and Growth; Employment and Skill Development |

Checks: from 13 in 1994 to 8 in 2019 is a fall of 5; from 10 in 2013 to 8 in 2019 is a fall of 2. Five committees (Political Affairs, Economic Affairs, Appointments, Parliamentary Affairs and Accommodation) appear in all three lists. **After 2019**: committees are reconstituted when a new government forms, so the present list must be checked from current official sources (verify; not in the extract).

## The four important committees

Laxmikanth names four as the more important:

1. **Political Affairs Committee**: all policy matters in domestic and foreign affairs; the most powerful, often called the **'Super-Cabinet'**.
2. **Economic Affairs Committee**: directs and coordinates government activity in the economic sphere.
3. **Appointments Committee**: decides all higher-level appointments in the Central Secretariat, public enterprises, banks and financial institutions (module 6).
4. **Parliamentary Affairs Committee**: looks after the progress of government business in Parliament.

The first three are chaired by the **Prime Minister** and the last by the **Home Minister** (Laxmikanth; the chair of the Parliamentary Affairs Committee has varied between governments, verify). The Security Committee, named in the 2013 and 2019 lists, is not described in the extract; its remit (national security and defence matters) is standard knowledge to verify. Standard knowledge (verify): the Security Committee is commonly called the Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS), which takes senior security appointments and defence and national-security decisions, and the Economic Affairs Committee is commonly called the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA), which clears major economic proposals.

## Groups of Ministers (GoMs)

Besides committees, several **Groups of Ministers** are formed to look into issues. Some are empowered to **take decisions on behalf of the Cabinet**; others only **recommend**. GoMs are **ad hoc** bodies, with the ministers heading the concerned ministries inducted, and they are **disbanded** when the advice is crystallised.

The Second Administrative Reforms Commission (2005-09, headed by Veerappa Moily) observed that a **large number** of GoMs meant many could not meet regularly, causing **delays**. It recommended **selective but effective** use of GoMs, with a **clear mandate and prescribed time limits**, and wanted them empowered to decide on behalf of the Cabinet.

## Committee against GoM

A Cabinet committee is formed by the Prime Minister, may be standing or ad hoc, and has a defined subject. A GoM is always ad hoc and issue-specific. This contrast is a typical statement-type trap.

### 4. Cabinet Secretariat, Cabinet Secretary, Rules of Business and the PMO (outline)

*Objective:* Describe in outline the staff machinery that serves the Cabinet and its committees, flagging what rests on standard knowledge.

## What the source gives and what it does not

Laxmikanth chapter 21 says only that the **Rules of Business** provide for the establishment of Cabinet committees. The **Cabinet Secretariat** and the **Prime Minister's Office** are in separate chapters not in the extract. Everything below is therefore **standard knowledge to verify** against Laxmikanth chapters on those bodies or official sources.

## Rules of Business

The Rules are rules made under the Constitution for how the Union government's business is transacted, including who allocates subjects to ministries and how files move. In standard textbooks they are the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules and (Allocation of Business) Rules, made under Article 77(3) (the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961 and (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961; standard knowledge, verify). Article 77(1)-(2) adds that executive action of the Government of India is expressed to be taken in the President's name and authenticated as the rules provide (standard knowledge; verify). The Cabinet committees' basis lies in these Rules, not in Part V of the Constitution.

## The Cabinet Secretariat

The Cabinet Secretariat is the office that serves the **Cabinet and its committees**. In outline: it prepares the agenda, circulates the notes, records the decisions and follows up on their implementation. It works under the **direct charge of the Prime Minister** (verify).

## The Cabinet Secretary

The **Cabinet Secretary** is the senior-most civil servant of the Union and heads the Cabinet Secretariat. Standard descriptions give him the task of servicing the Cabinet and its committees, coordinating ministries and presiding over meetings of senior secretaries (verify). He is not a minister and takes no political decisions; he is the administrative support of the decision-making process.

## The Prime Minister's Office (PMO)

For the PMO and the Cabinet Secretariat in outline, see GS-176 module 9 (one-line recap: the PMO is the PM's own staff agency, headed by the Principal Secretary to the Prime Minister). What is new here: the Cabinet Secretary also chairs the Committee of Secretaries, the senior-secretary forum that prepares inter-ministry issues for decision (standard knowledge; verify).

## Cabinet Secretariat against PMO

| Point | Cabinet Secretariat | PMO |
|---|---|---|
| Serves | The Cabinet and its committees as a whole | The Prime Minister personally |
| Head | Cabinet Secretary | Principal Secretary to the PM |
| Nature of work | Agenda, notes, minutes, coordination, follow-up | Assisting the PM in the PM's tasks |

These descriptions are standard and require verification; do not quote them as Laxmikanth's words.

## Why it matters for the exam

A Mains answer on Cabinet effectiveness often mentions the Secretariat (coordination, record of decisions) and the PMO (concentration of power around the Prime Minister). The Kitchen Cabinet's informal influence (GS-176 module 9) is the other side of the same concern.

### 5. How the Cabinet decides: collective decision and a worked flow

*Objective:* Trace one decision from a ministry to the Cabinet and explain 'collective decision' and 'sense of the Cabinet'.

## Decision by collective discussion

The Cabinet is the highest decision-making authority of the Union (GS-176 module 9 lists its roles). Its decisions are **collective**: they bind all ministers, even those who disagreed in the meeting (GS-176 module 8 on collective responsibility). NCERT chapter 4 states the principle as solidarity of the Cabinet: a minister who does not agree with a decision must accept it or resign.

## 'Sense of the Cabinet'

In standard accounts, Cabinet discussions normally end without a formal vote. The Prime Minister, presiding, sums up the **sense of the meeting**, and that summary is recorded as the decision (standard knowledge; not in the extract; verify). The expression helps explain why a decision is regarded as the Cabinet's as a whole rather than of a majority.

## A worked flow (invented illustration)

Dates and the proposal are invented to show the logic; the real procedure should be verified against the Rules of Business.

Suppose the Ministry of Rural Development wants a scheme with a large outlay.

1. **Ministry drafts** a note with the purpose, the cost and the legal basis.
2. **Consultation**: other ministries, notably Finance, give their views.
3. **Cabinet Secretariat** circulates the note and places it before the right committee (here, the **Economic Affairs Committee**, since the scheme is in the economic sphere according to Laxmikanth's description).
4. **Committee examines** it. Laxmikanth says committees can take decisions; the decision may be final or referred up to the full Cabinet.
5. **Cabinet** can review the committee's decision.
6. **Record and follow-up**: the Cabinet Secretariat circulates the decision (standard; verify) and the ministry implements it.
7. **Parliament**: the Council of Ministers remains accountable to the Lok Sabha for the decision (GS-176 module 8).

## Why committees decide some matters

The idea is a balance: they take decisions to save the Cabinet's time, but the Cabinet's power to review keeps final authority intact.

## A cautionary note on who decides

Because the Prime Minister chairs the most important committees and sets up all of them, the committee system also **strengthens the Prime Minister's control**. That is the point at which the 2024 Mains question (module 7) begins.

### 6. The Appointments Committee of the Cabinet

*Objective:* State what the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet decides and who chairs it, flagging what is standard knowledge.

## What the source says

Laxmikanth names the **Appointments Committee of the Cabinet** among the four important committees. It **decides all higher-level appointments** in the **Central Secretariat, public enterprises, banks and financial institutions**. It is **chaired by the Prime Minister**. It appears in all three lists: in 1994, in 2013 and in the 2019 list, so it is among the most durable committees.

## Why a Cabinet committee decides appointments

Higher posts need a decision by the political executive that is both quick and accountable. Rather than take every name to the full Cabinet, the matter goes to a small committee that the Prime Minister leads, and the Cabinet retains its power to review committee decisions (module 2).

## Standard knowledge to verify

The extract does not say who the other members are. In standard accounts the Appointments Committee consists of the Prime Minister and the Home Minister, and approves appointments of senior civil servants and heads of some public bodies, with its own staff support from the Department of Personnel and Training (verify; the exact membership and remit should be checked against current official sources). It is **not** the same body as the Collegium for judges, and the committee's powers are limited to the matters assigned by the Government of India.

## Typical exam use

- Statement tests: 'Appointments Committee is chaired by the Prime Minister' (consistent with Laxmikanth).
- Match tests: Appointments Committee with higher-level appointments in the Central Secretariat, public enterprises, banks and financial institutions.
- Trap: confusing the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet with the committee that selects the CBI Director or other bodies (those are statutory bodies; verify separately).

## One-line recall

The **Appointments Committee**: Prime-Minister-chaired; higher-level appointments; Central Secretariat, public enterprises, banks, financial institutions.

## The vocabulary table

| Point | Council of Ministers | Cabinet | Cabinet committee |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basis | Articles 74 and 75 of the Constitution | Role by convention; the word 'Cabinet' is defined in Article 352 (GS-176) | Extra-constitutional; the Rules of Business provide for them |

## Thinking aloud: 2017 Prelims Q99

The question asks for the principle underlying the Cabinet form of government. Step 1: the principle taught in GS-176 module 8 is **collective responsibility** of the government to the legislature and hence the people. Step 2: an option that speaks only of speed (module 2's workload) describes the function of committees, not the principle. Step 3: an option about minimising criticism or strengthening the head of the government describes a result or a risk, not the principle. On this analysis the option on collective responsibility fits; this is analysis, not the official key.

## Thinking aloud: 2024 Mains Q3 (10 marks, 'Elucidate')

The statement says the growth of the Cabinet system has marginalised parliamentary supremacy. 'Elucidate' means explain with reasons and examples. A balanced frame:

- **Meaning**: parliamentary supremacy, with the executive accountable to Parliament (GS-176 module 8).
- **Support for the claim**: power sits in the Cabinet, its committees (which take decisions), the Prime Minister's office and the Kitchen Cabinet; legislators who support the government follow party discipline; Ramsay Muir's 'dictatorship of the Cabinet' (GS-176); ordinances (GS-174).
- **Against the claim**: the Council is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha and falls if the House withdraws confidence; question hour and committees of Parliament; coalition governments and a strong opposition; judicial review of executive action.
- **Conclusion**: the Cabinet leads in practice, but Parliament retains its accountability role; reform means stronger committees and scrutiny.

The extract does not discuss parliamentary committees, so the argument about Parliament's own tools is standard knowledge to verify.

## Mains frame in short

Part 1 what the committees are; part 2 the case for and against dominance; part 3 a conclusion with a reform idea, such as time-bound Group of Ministers mandates (Second ARC).
