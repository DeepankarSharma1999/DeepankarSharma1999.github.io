## GS-368: Ethics versus law, morality and conscience

Mains GS4 · Ethics · Ethics and human interface

*Goal:* Explain Ethics versus law, morality and conscience and use it to reason through the worked example.

### Concept and distinctions

Law establishes enforceable public rules; morality concerns judgments about right and wrong; conscience is an individual's reflective sense of obligation. Their conclusions can diverge. A sincere conscience may be mistaken, while a legal rule may be ethically contested.

### How to apply it

Public service requires reasoned engagement with conflict rather than automatic obedience or automatic personal defiance. Examine the rule's purpose, constitutional limits, authorized discretion and available review or escalation channels. Record concerns and seek lawful remedies where possible. Ethical responsibility includes the consequences of action and inaction, but private conviction alone cannot justify arbitrary treatment of citizens under delegated public authority.

### Worked example

An officer uneasy about a discriminatory instruction should document the concern and use lawful review channels rather than secretly alter unrelated cases.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why is conscience not a sufficient public decision rule by itself?
  - **a:** Personal conviction can be mistaken and inconsistent; official choices require publicly defensible reasons and lawful authority.

### Common trap

Equating legality, morality and conscience as identical concepts.
