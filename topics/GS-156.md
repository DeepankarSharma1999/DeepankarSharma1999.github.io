## GS-156: Social empowerment and inclusion

Mains GS1 + Prelims GS1 · Society · Indian society

*Goal:* Explain Social empowerment and inclusion and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### State initiatives against caste discrimination

Schedules listing castes and tribes entitled to special treatment were drawn up in 1935 and continued after Independence, with programmes extended to OBCs from the early 1990s. Reservation, the most important measure, sets aside seats in legislatures, government employment and educational institutions. A long chain of laws addresses discrimination: the 1850 Caste Disabilities Removal Act, used to admit Dalits to government schools; Article 17 of the Constitution abolishing untouchability; the 1989 Prevention of Atrocities Act strengthening punishment for violence and humiliation against Dalits and adivasis; and the 93rd Amendment for OBC reservation in higher education. NCERT draws a pointed conclusion: the very need to legislate repeatedly shows that law alone cannot end a social practice, since atrocities and discrimination continue to be reported.

### Self-assertion: Dalits, OBCs and adivasis

No group is merely a victim. Dalit assertion runs from Phule, Iyotheedas, Periyar and Ambedkar to organisations such as the Bahujan Samaj Party and Karnataka's Dalit Sangharsh Samiti, alongside rich Dalit literature in Marathi, Kannada, Tamil, Telugu and Hindi. OBCs, defined by the Constitution as socially and educationally backward classes, are internally diverse and include members of non-Hindu religions. The Kalelkar report of 1953 was sidelined, the issue became regional, and only the 1990 decision to implement the Mandal report made it national, enabling OBCs to turn numbers into influence. NCERT notes a gap between landed upper OBCs and poorer lower OBCs, and that urban OBCs are far worse off, closer to SCs and STs than to upper castes. Adivasi movements, concentrated in contiguous areas, could win States such as Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh.

### Disability, gender and grassroots democracy

NCERT argues people are disabled less by impairment than by a society designed without their needs in mind, citing inaccessible buildings that cascade into exclusion from education and work. Popular attitudes in India often treat disability as fate or past karma and the disabled as victims, labelled with pity. Disability and poverty reinforce each other through malnutrition, weak immunisation and unsafe housing. Census 2011 expanded disability enumeration to eight categories. Political empowerment has also widened through decentralisation: the 73rd Amendment created a constitutionally protected three-tier Panchayati Raj with mandatory five-yearly elections and reserved seats for SCs, STs and women. The Supreme Court has expanded Article 21's right to life to include livelihood, health, shelter, education and dignity, and read equal pay for equal work into Article 14.

### Worked example

Q: 'Legal safeguards are necessary but not sufficient for social empowerment.' Discuss. Frame: list safeguards (1935 schedules, reservations, Article 17, PoA Act 1989, 93rd Amendment, 73rd Amendment); evidence of limits (continuing atrocities; repeated legislation); role of self-organisation (Dalit literature and parties, Mandal-era OBC politics, adivasi statehood); disability's social model; judicial expansion of Articles 21 and 14; conclude with awareness campaigns and institutional accountability.

### Recall

- - **q:** What did the 93rd Constitutional Amendment (2005) provide?
  - **a:** Reservation for OBCs in institutions of higher education; it came into force on 23 January 2006.
- - **q:** When was the Mandal Commission report implemented by the Centre?
  - **a:** In 1990.
- - **q:** How many disability types did Census 2011 cover versus 2001?
  - **a:** Eight in 2011, compared with five in 2001.

### Common trap

Assuming the term 'Dalit' was coined by Ambedkar. NCERT says he neither coined nor frequently used it; it gained currency in the early 1970s, notably through the Dalit Panthers in western India.
