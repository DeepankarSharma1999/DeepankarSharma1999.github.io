## GS-133: Primary sector location and agriculture

Mains GS1 + Prelims GS1 · Geography · Human/economic/world

*Goal:* Explain Primary sector location and agriculture and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Activities that follow the environment

Primary activities - gathering, pastoralism, fishing, forestry, farming and mining - are carried out by 'red-collar' workers outdoors. Gathering survives in harsh environments: high latitudes such as northern Canada, northern Eurasia and southern Chile, and low latitudes such as the Amazon Basin, tropical Africa and interior Southeast Asia; some gathering is now commercial (chicle, quinine, cork). Nomadic herding spans a core belt from Atlantic North Africa through Arabia to Mongolia and Central China, plus the Eurasian tundra and pockets in south-west Africa and Madagascar. Transhumance moves herds vertically, as Gujjars, Bakarwals, Gaddis and Bhotiyas do in the Himalaya. Political boundaries and settlement schemes are shrinking nomadic space.

### Farming systems and their regions

Subsistence systems include shifting cultivation in tropical forests and intensive farming in densely peopled monsoon Asia, dominated by wet paddy or, where paddy is impractical, wheat, soyabean, barley and sorghum. Europeans introduced plantations - large estates, heavy capital, single crops, cheap labour and transport to export markets - such as British tea in India and Sri Lanka and rubber in Malaysia. Mid-latitude semi-arid interiors carry mechanised commercial grain farms. Mixed farming in north-western Europe and eastern North America balances crops with livestock and fodder rotations. Mediterranean agriculture specialises in citrus, grapes, olives and figs, selling winter produce to European and North American markets. Market gardening and truck farming cluster near cities with high-income consumers.

### Access and markets: dairying, ranching, mining

Some primary activities are tied less to land quality than to markets and capital. Dairying is capital- and labour-intensive with no off-season, so it sits near urban and industrial centres; refrigeration, pasteurisation and better transport have extended storage. Commercial ranching on fenced, permanent ranches matches herd size to pasture carrying capacity and exports meat, wool and hides, notably from New Zealand, Australia, Argentina, Uruguay and the USA. Mining depends on the size, grade and mode of occurrence of deposits as well as demand and costs; open-cast working is cheap and rapid, while shaft mining is costly and risky. NCERT adds that high labour costs are pushing developed economies out of mining, and several African and a few South American and Asian countries draw over half their earnings from minerals.

### Worked example

Question: 'Why is market gardening practised near urban areas?' Frame: high-value perishable crops (vegetables, fruit, flowers); need for daily access to high-income urban consumers; small farms with heavy inputs (irrigation, HYV seed, fertiliser, greenhouses); good transport links; examples - north-west Europe, north-eastern USA, Netherlands tulips; extend to truck farming defined by overnight driving distance.

### Recall

- - **q:** What are the local names of shifting cultivation in north-east India, Mexico and Indonesia?
  - **a:** Jhuming, milpa and ladang respectively.
- - **q:** Name three regions of extensive commercial grain farming.
  - **a:** Any three of: Eurasian steppes, North American Prairies, Pampas, Velds, Australian Downs, Canterbury Plains.
- - **q:** In which country did co-operative farming succeed so well that almost every farmer is a member?
  - **a:** Denmark.

### Common trap

Mixing up the two productivity measures: extensive grain farming has low yield per acre but high yield per worker, while intensive paddy farming is the reverse.
