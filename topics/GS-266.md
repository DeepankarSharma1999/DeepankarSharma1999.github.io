## GS-266: Biodiversity types, hotspots and endemism

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain Biodiversity types, hotspots and endemism and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Three levels of biodiversity

Biodiversity covers variation at all levels of biological organisation. Genetic diversity is variation within a species, such as differing reserpine potency in Rauwolfia vomitoria across Himalayan ranges, India's 50,000-plus rice strains and 1,000 mango varieties. Species diversity is the variety of species in an area — the Western Ghats hold more amphibian species than the Eastern Ghats. Ecosystem diversity reflects the range of habitats: India's deserts, rainforests, mangroves, coral reefs, wetlands and alpine meadows give it more ecosystem diversity than Norway. Genetic variety is essential for healthy breeding populations.

### Hotspots, megadiversity and endemism

Conservationists picked biodiversity hotspots for maximum protection: regions of very high species richness and high endemism, meaning species confined to that region. The list grew from 25 to 34. Three cover India: Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, and Himalaya. Together hotspots occupy less than 2 per cent of land yet shelter a very large share of species. Megadiversity countries are a separate idea — twelve mostly tropical nations, including India, Brazil, Indonesia and Australia, holding a large share of world species. Madagascar shows extreme endemism, with about 85 per cent of species unique to it.

### Why biodiversity is being lost

Current extinction rates are 100 to 1,000 times pre-human rates, and amphibians appear particularly vulnerable. NCERT's 'evil quartet' names the causes. Habitat loss and fragmentation is the most important; tropical rainforests have shrunk from over 14 per cent of land surface to no more than 6 per cent. Over-exploitation drove Steller's sea cow and passenger pigeon to extinction. Alien species invasions, like the Nile perch in Lake Victoria, eliminate natives. Co-extinctions follow when an obligate partner disappears. Conservation arguments are narrowly utilitarian, broadly utilitarian through ecosystem services, and ethical.

### Worked example

Prelims check: 'India has three of the 34 hotspots: Western Ghats–Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Himalaya' — correct. 'Hotspots are defined only by high species richness' — incorrect; endemism and threat are also central.

### Recall

- - **q:** Name the three levels of biodiversity.
  - **a:** Genetic, species, ecosystem.
- - **q:** Which hotspots cover India?
  - **a:** Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Himalaya.
- - **q:** What are the four causes in the 'evil quartet'?
  - **a:** Habitat loss and fragmentation, over-exploitation, alien species invasions, co-extinctions.

### Common trap

Equating hotspots with species richness alone: NCERT's definition needs high richness plus high endemism, and hotspots are also regions of accelerated habitat loss.
