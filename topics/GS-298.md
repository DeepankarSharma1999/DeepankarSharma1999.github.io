## GS-298: Space missions, launch vehicles and satellites

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Science · General science / technology

*Goal:* Explain Space missions, launch vehicles and satellites and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Orbital physics and orbit choice

Earth satellites follow Kepler's laws: the square of the period is proportional to the cube of orbital radius, so a near-surface orbit takes about 85 minutes while geostationary satellites at nearly 36,000 km keep pace with Earth's 24-hour spin. Escape speed is about 11.2 km/s from Earth and about 2.3 km/s from the Moon, and a bound satellite always has negative total energy. Orbit follows purpose. Remote sensing satellites such as the IRS series use near-polar sun-synchronous orbits at 700-900 km, circling about 14 times a day with fine resolution, whereas weather and communication satellites such as INSAT sit in geostationary orbit, viewing about one-third of the globe at coarser resolution.

### India's launch vehicles

PSLV, GSLV and LVM3 are operational, and SSLV carries up to 500 kg to low orbits. PSLV-C61 carrying EOS-09 failed in May 2025 because of a third-stage anomaly. GSLV, with a cryogenic upper stage able to put about 2000 kg into geosynchronous transfer orbit, placed NASA-ISRO's NISAR in a sun-synchronous polar orbit on 30 July 2025. LVM3 combines S200 strap-ons, an L110 core and a C25 cryogenic stage; on 2 November 2025 it launched CMS-03, the heaviest Indian communication satellite, and restarted its C25 stage in space for the first time. A human-rated HLVM3 is qualified for the first uncrewed Gaganyaan flight, and a reusable Next Generation Launch Vehicle is under development.

### Space missions and the road to 2047

Chandrayaan-3, launched by LVM3 on 14 July 2023, soft-landed at the Moon's southern high latitudes on 23 August 2023; its Vikram lander made the first in-situ temperature profile there, and the Pragyan rover measured volatiles. The Chandrayaan-2 orbiter still works from a 100 km polar orbit. Aditya-L1, India's solar observatory launched on PSLV-C57 in September 2023, entered a halo orbit around L1 on 6 January 2024, and XPoSat studies X-ray polarisation. SpaDeX satellites docked on 16 January 2025. Gaganyaan aims to send humans to low Earth orbit on an Indian rocket and return them safely, with uncrewed G1-G3 flights before crewed H1. Space Vision 2047 targets a Bharatiya Antariksh Station by 2035 and an Indian Moon landing by 2040, with Chandrayaan-4 planned to return lunar samples.
