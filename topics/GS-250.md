## GS-250: MSP and agricultural price policy

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Agriculture and food

*Goal:* Explain MSP and agricultural price policy and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### What MSP is meant to do

Minimum support price is a price floor declared by the government for important crops. NCERT presents it as one of several measures — alongside remunerative and procurement prices — introduced to stop speculators and middlemen from exploiting farmers. Historically, farmers lacked price information and storage and were forced into distress sales at low prices, often with faulty weighing. A declared floor price is meant to reduce that risk. During the Green Revolution, the marketed surplus of rice and wheat sold by farmers allowed the government to procure enough grain to build reserves for times of shortage, linking price support directly with national food security.

### How MSP is fixed and which crops it covers

The Centre announces MSPs for 22 mandated crops. Fourteen are kharif crops, among them paddy, jowar, bajra, ragi, maize, tur, moong, urad, cotton, groundnut and soyabean; six are rabi crops, namely wheat, barley, gram, masur, rapeseed-mustard and safflower; and two, jute and copra, are commercial crops (Economic Survey 2025-26). The pricing rule dates from the Union Budget 2018-19: the support price must equal at least 150 per cent of production cost, so each mandated crop now carries a margin of 50 per cent or more over the all-India weighted average cost. The Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices prepares season-wise Price Policy reports that compare yields across crops and states. Because farmers factor MSP into sowing decisions, the announced price also shapes the cropping pattern, not just farm income.

### Procurement, limits and the reform debate

A support price bites only where grain is bought at it. FCI is the nodal agency for procurement, storage and distribution, and the Centre also runs a decentralised procurement scheme. In KMS 2023-24, 489.20 LMT of rice was bought from 98.26 lakh farmers, and wheat procurement for RMS 2024-25 had reached 263.33 LMT by 24 May 2024 (Economic Survey 2023-24). NCERT reminds us that private traders still dominate rural markets, so a declared price does not reach every seller. The Economic Survey 2025-26 adds that rice and wheat procurement has outrun food-security needs; rather than changing MSP, it suggests spending stock savings on voluntary diversification, price-deficiency payments and assured offtake. The repealed 2020 marketing laws show how contested reform is. Ask three things: is a price announced, is there procurement at it, and can farmers reach the procurement point?

### Worked example

Mains frame (benefits vs limits of MSP): Design — 22 mandated crops, at least 1.5 times the weighted average cost since 2018-19. Benefits — floor against middlemen, grain for buffer stock and PDS, signal for sowing. Limits — effective mainly where procurement is large (paddy, wheat); private trade still dominates rural markets; excess rice-wheat stocks. Way forward — wider procurement access, price-deficiency payments and diversification incentives funded from stock savings (Economic Survey 2025-26).

### Recall

- - **q:** How many crops have a mandated MSP and how are they split?
  - **a:** 22: 14 kharif, 6 rabi and 2 commercial crops (jute, copra).
- - **q:** What cost rule has governed MSP since 2018-19?
  - **a:** At least 1.5 times (a 50 per cent margin over) the all-India weighted average cost of production.
- - **q:** Which agency is nodal for procurement at MSP?
  - **a:** The Food Corporation of India, alongside decentralised procurement.
- - **q:** Which three instruments does NCERT link for food policy?
  - **a:** MSP, FCI buffer stocks of wheat and rice, and PDS distribution of foodgrains and sugar.
- - **q:** What happened to the 2020 farm-marketing laws?
  - **a:** They were repealed after opposition from sections of farmers.

### Common trap

Assuming announcement equals assurance: MSP is a declared price, and NCERT records that private trade still predominates in farm markets despite this intervention, so an announced price does not by itself reach every seller.
