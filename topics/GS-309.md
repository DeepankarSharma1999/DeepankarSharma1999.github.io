## GS-309: RTI implementation and limitations

Mains GS2 · Governance · Policy implementation and delivery

*Goal:* Use the RTI framework to distinguish access, exemptions and appeals.

### Ask for existing information

The Right to Information Act, 2005 enables citizens to seek information held by or under the control of public authorities. A request can seek records, documents or inspection; it does not normally require an officer to create a new explanation or conduct research. Proactive disclosure under Section 4 can reduce the need for repeated individual applications.

### Balance access with protected interests

The ordinary response period is thirty days; information concerning life or liberty has a forty-eight-hour standard. Exemptions protect specified interests, but the Act also contains public-interest and severability principles. An applicant may use the statutory appeal route when access is refused or delayed. Specific requests with dates, office names and document descriptions are easier to process than broad accusations.

### Worked example

Instead of asking “Why is my village ignored?”, request the sanctioned road list, allocated amounts and inspection records for a stated year. These records allow residents to test the factual basis of the grievance.

### Recall

- - **q:** Must a public authority create a new opinion for an RTI reply?
  - **a:** Normally no; access concerns information already held or controlled.
- - **q:** Why does proactive disclosure matter?
  - **a:** Frequently needed information becomes available without individual applications.

### Common trap

RTI is an information-access mechanism; obtaining records does not itself resolve every service grievance.
