## GS-086: Major schools of miniature painting

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS1 · Culture · Indian art, literature and architecture

*Goal:* Explain Major schools of miniature painting and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### The Mughal atelier

Mughal painting developed from the sixteenth century to the mid-nineteenth as a workshop art of calligraphers, painters, gilders and binders, made largely for royal viewing. Babur's memoirs discuss the Herat master Bihzad. Humayun, exiled at Shah Tahmasp's Safavid court, returned with Mir Sayyid Ali and Abd us Samad. Akbar employed more than a hundred Persian and Indian artists, continued the Hamzanama — large cloth paintings held up while a narrator recited — and commissioned Persian translations of Sanskrit texts such as the Razmnama. European prints entered the atelier, as in Basawan's Madonna and Child. Jahangir preferred fewer, finer works by single masters, fine studies of flora, fauna and portraits, and albums with gilded margins.

### Rajasthani schools

The Rajasthani schools flourished in the kingdoms and thikanas of Mewar, Bundi, Kota, Jaipur, Bikaner, Kishangarh, Marwar, Malwa and Sirohi, mostly between the sixteenth and early nineteenth centuries. Coomaraswamy's 1916 'Rajput' label tried to separate them from Mughal work, but specific categories such as Rajasthani and Pahari are now used. Styles differ in fine or bold execution, palette, treatment of architecture and nature, and narrative mode. Themes reflect Vaishnava bhakti: Jayadeva's Gita Govinda, Bhanu Datta's Rasamanjari, Keshav Das's Rasikapriya and Kavipriya (with its Baramasa), Bihari's Satsai, ragamala albums of ragas and raginis, bardic romances like Dhola-Maru, the epics, and court life, hunts and festivals.

### Pahari schools

Pahari ('of the hills') painting spans Basohli, Guler, Kangra, Kullu, Chamba, Mankot, Nurpur, Mandi, Bilaspur and Jammu from the seventeenth to the nineteenth century. It began with Basohli's bold, flamboyant idiom and matured, through a Guler or pre-Kangra phase, into the refined Kangra style. Because dated works and colophons are scarce and the hill states shared artists, NCERT notes that these centres do not form fully independent schools. Mughal and Rajasthani influence probably reached the hills through provincial Mughal works and marriage ties with Rajasthani courts. Basohli's hallmarks — warm yellow grounds, stylised trees, raised white for pearls and beetle wings for emeralds — make it easy to distinguish from Kangra.
