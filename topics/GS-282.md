## GS-282: UNFCCC, COP and Paris Agreement

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Climate and treaties

*Goal:* Explain UNFCCC, COP and Paris Agreement and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Origins, objective and the Paris goal

The UNFCCC was adopted in May 1992, opened for signature at that year's Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro and came into force in March 1994; it had 197 Parties by September 2020. Its ultimate objective is to keep greenhouse gas concentrations at a level that stops dangerous human interference with the climate system. Two later treaties carry its provisions forward: the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement. Paris was adopted at COP21 in December 2015 and entered into force on 4 November 2016. One of its goals is to hold the rise in global average temperature well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels while pursuing efforts to keep it to 1.5°C; it also seeks to strengthen countries' ability to cope with climate impacts. Under Article 4.2, every Party must prepare, communicate and maintain successive NDCs.

### India's machinery and COP28

Within MoEFCC, the Climate Change Division represents India at the UNFCCC, under its Kyoto Protocol and Paris Agreement, including the annual COP. It is supported by cells for adaptation, national communications, climate finance and carbon markets under Paris Article 6, and at home it coordinates the NAPCC with its nine missions and state action plans and formulates the NDC. COP28 in Dubai (30 November to 13 December 2023) produced the outcome of the first Global Stocktake, intended to raise ambition before 2030 in a nationally determined way, and agreed to operationalise new funding arrangements, including a Fund, for loss and damage in vulnerable developing countries.

### COP30 and India's negotiating principles

COP30 in Belém in November 2025 came when most Paris rulebook items were operational; the Brazilian presidency focused on finance, the Global Goal on Adaptation, the mitigation work programme and just transition. India acted as LMDC spokesperson and BASIC chair and helped secure a two-year work programme on climate finance, including Article 9.1. In its national statement India urged developed countries to reach net zero well before current target dates and to provide new, additional and concessional finance, and sought affordable technologies free of restrictive intellectual property barriers. It argued that net zero should be a global objective around 2050 rather than a uniform national deadline, citing its own 2070 goal, and resisted top-down use of the Global Stocktake, stressing equity and national circumstances.

### Worked example

Statement set: (1) The Paris Agreement aims to hold warming well below 2°C and pursue 1.5°C - correct; (2) COP28 operationalised a loss and damage fund - correct; (3) The Global Stocktake assigns national targets - incorrect, because action remains nationally determined. Mains frame: UNFCCC objective, Paris cycle (NDC-transparency-stocktake), India's equity-based stance.

### Recall

- - **q:** What is the temperature goal of the Paris Agreement?
  - **a:** Holding warming well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels and pursuing efforts to limit it to 1.5°C.
- - **q:** When was the UNFCCC adopted and when did it enter into force?
  - **a:** Adopted May 1992 (opened for signature at Rio, 1992); in force March 1994.
- - **q:** What were two major COP28 outcomes?
  - **a:** The first Global Stocktake outcome and operationalisation of the loss and damage fund.
- - **q:** Which coalition did India speak for at COP30?
  - **a:** The Like-Minded Developing Countries (LMDC); India also chaired BASIC.

### Common trap

Believing the Paris Agreement sets binding national emission targets imposed from above; contributions are nationally determined, and India resisted top-down use of the Global Stocktake.
