## GS-218: CPI, WPI and inflation dynamics

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Basic macro and monetary

*Goal:* Explain CPI, WPI and inflation dynamics and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### What each index measures

A consumer price index, also called a cost-of-living index, shows how much more a household must spend today to buy the basket it bought in the base period. India publishes several CPIs (for industrial workers, agricultural labourers, rural labourers, and rural, urban and combined series on 2012 = 100); the combined CPI is the one the RBI watches. Its weights come from household consumption surveys, with food and beverages carrying a large share. The wholesale price index instead records prices at which goods change hands in bulk, has no consumer reference group and leaves out services. Because they cover different items at different stages, CPI and WPI inflation can diverge sharply: in 2023-24 average CPI inflation was 5.36% while WPI inflation was −0.73% (Economic Survey 2025-26).

### Uses: deflating, wages and purchasing power

Index numbers are policy tools, not just statistics. NCERT notes that CPI-type indices feed into wage bargaining, income and price policy, rent control and taxation, while WPI is used to strip price effects out of aggregates such as national income and capital formation. CPI also converts money incomes into real terms: dividing a money wage by the cost-of-living index and multiplying by 100 gives the wage in base-period purchasing power, and the inverse of the index shows what one rupee is now worth. Inflation mainly lowers the value of money; if it becomes large enough, money may stop serving as a medium of exchange and unit of account.

### Recent dynamics: headline, food and core

The Economic Survey 2025-26 records a steep disinflation: average CPI inflation fell from 6.7% in 2022-23 to 4.6% in 2024-25 and 1.7% in April–December 2025, with October 2025 at 0.3%, the lowest in the 2012 = 100 series. Food prices drove the fall, helped by good harvests. Core inflation, which removes food and fuel, behaved differently, rising to 4.62% by December 2025; the Survey attributes much of this to record gold and silver prices, and finds core excluding precious metals was declining. WPI-based inflation stayed below CPI inflation over these years. The Survey also quotes a rating agency noting inflation sat near the lower bound of the RBI's 2–6% target range.

### Worked example

A worker's money wage is Rs 30,000 and the cost-of-living index (base = 100) is 150. Real wage = 30,000 ÷ 150 × 100 = Rs 20,000 in base-year rupees. To keep the base-year standard of living from a base-year wage of Rs 20,000, pay must be raised by 50% to Rs 30,000.

### Recall

- - **q:** Which index does the RBI use as its main measure of consumer prices?
  - **a:** The All-India Combined CPI (base 2012 = 100).
- - **q:** What does core inflation exclude?
  - **a:** Volatile food and fuel components.
- - **q:** Why did the Economic Survey 2025-26 say core inflation looked sticky?
  - **a:** Sharp rises in gold and silver prices; excluding precious metals, core was falling.

### Common trap

Saying the RBI targets WPI inflation: the inflation target is defined on CPI, while WPI covers only goods and is also used to remove price effects from aggregates such as national income.
