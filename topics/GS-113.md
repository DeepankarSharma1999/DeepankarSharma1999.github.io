### Bridge: Reading height, slope and gradient on a relief map

A relief description uses three small ideas, so fix them before module 1.

**Height above mean sea level.** Every height in NCERT (Anaimudi 2,695 m, the plain at 50-150 m) is measured upward from the average level of the sea. Sea level is zero. A place at 150 m is 150 m above the sea, not 150 m above its neighbour.

**Slope and gradient.** The slope of land is how much it tilts. The **gradient** is the number that measures the tilt: the height lost divided by the horizontal distance. If a river falls 100 m over 100 km, its gradient is 1 m per km, which is gentle; a mountain stream that falls 100 m over 1 km has a gradient of 100 m per km, which is very steep. NCERT tells you Peninsular river valleys have low gradients and Himalayan valleys are cut by fast rivers (kegy102 p3).

**Reading direction on a map.** North is at the top unless the map shows an arrow. 'Rises from west to east' means the high side is in the west. When NCERT says the Peninsular plateau slopes west to east, it means the high ground lies near the Western Ghats and the land falls towards the Bay of Bengal.

### 1. Orientation: India's relief on one page

*Objective:* Name the three geological divisions and the six physiographic divisions of India and say in one line what each looks like.

## Two ways to cut the same land

NCERT (kegy102, PDF pp. 2-3) divides India in two ways. The first is by **geological structure** (what the rocks are and how old the land is). The second is by **physiography**, which NCERT defines as "the outcome of structure, process and the stage of development". In plain words, physiography is the visible shape of the land that results from three things: what the rock is, what forces have worked on it, and how long they have worked.

## The three geological divisions

1. **The Peninsular Block**: a very old, rigid, stable mass of gneiss and granite.
2. **The Himalayas and other Peninsular mountains** (NCERT's own heading): young, weak and flexible mountains that are still changing.
3. **The Indo-Ganga-Brahmaputra Plain**: a trough filled by river sediment.

## The six physiographic divisions

| No. | Division | One-line picture |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Northern and North-eastern Mountains | The Himalayas and the north-eastern hills: parallel ranges, deep gorges |
| 2 | The Northern Plain | Flat alluvial land of the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra |
| 3 | The Peninsular Plateau | An irregular triangle of old, stable table-land |
| 4 | The Indian Desert | Dunes and low rain north-west of the Aravali hills |
| 5 | The Coastal Plains | A narrow western strip and a broad eastern strip |
| 6 | The Islands | Bay of Bengal group and Arabian Sea group |

## The big contrast to carry

North: a vast expanse of rugged mountains. South: stable, dissected table-land with scarps. Between them: the vast north Indian plain (kegy102 p3). Almost every Prelims map question on this topic is a question about which of these six divisions a feature belongs to.

Later lessons go deep: the Western and Eastern Ghats are in Day 17 lesson GS-116, rivers in Day 17 lesson GS-117, neighbouring countries and India's position in Day 19 lesson GS-132. The Himalayan ranges, passes and drainage are Day 22 lesson GS-114, and the northern plains and Peninsular plateau in detail are Day 23 lesson GS-115. Do not look for them here.

### 2. Foundation: relief words and how the Indian plate built the land

*Objective:* Define the words used in every relief answer and explain in four steps how the northward-moving Indian plate shaped India.

## Words first

- **Relief**: the ups and downs of the land surface (mountains, plateaus, plains).
- **Structure**: the type and arrangement of rocks underneath.
- **Endogenic forces**: forces from inside the earth (plate movement, faulting). **Exogenic forces**: forces on the surface (rivers, wind, ice, weathering). NCERT says the land has been shaped mainly by these two working together (kegy102 p2).
- **Plateau**: a raised, fairly flat area. **Plain**: a low, level or gently undulating area. **Mountain**: a high, steep landform. A **hill** is lower than a mountain.
- **Gneiss and granite**: very ancient hard crystalline rocks.
- **Relict or residual mountain**: a mountain that is the worn-down remnant of a much higher old range (the Aravali hills are NCERT's example).
- **Rift valley**: a long, narrow valley that sinks between two faults; **block faulting** means the land breaks into blocks that rise or sink.
- **Alluvium**: sand, silt and clay laid down by rivers.
- **Delta**: the fan-shaped land built where a river meets the sea.
- **Submerged coast**: land that has sunk below the sea. **Emergent coast**: land (or sea floor) that has risen out of the sea.
- **Gradient**: how steeply the land falls (height lost per distance). A steep gradient makes a river fast; a low gradient makes it slow.

## How the Indian plate built India (kegy102 p2)

1. Earth is about **4,600 million years old** (NCERT: current estimation).
2. Millions of years ago the Indian plate lay south of the equator; NCERT says it was much larger and the Australian plate was once part of it.
3. It broke into parts. The Australian plate moved in a south-eastern direction, and the Indian plate moved north.
4. The northward movement continues and has large effects on the physical environment of the subcontinent. The Himalayas and the plain at their feet are the main results (modules 3 and 4).

Day 18 lesson GS-096 and Day 19 lesson GS-097 teach the plate mechanism; link back to them if the picture is hazy.

## One number to carry

NCERT's hazards chapter (kegy106 p4) gives the plate's speed as about **1 cm a year** towards the north and north-east. Most modern estimates are higher, about 4-5 cm a year (verify), and Day 19 lesson GS-097 works out about 5.8 cm a year as a long-run average. Quote the figure with its source. Even a few centimetres a year, kept up for tens of millions of years, adds up to hundreds or thousands of kilometres.

## Two small checks for later

NCERT calls the whole southern mass "the Peninsula" and speaks of a "block". Remember the word **block**: it means the old rigid core, not any one hill. And "Himalayan" does not mean "Himachal"; Himachal Pradesh is one state in a long mountain system.


### 3. Core: the three geological divisions

*Objective:* Describe the Peninsular Block, the Himalaya with the other mountains, and the Indo-Ganga-Brahmaputra Plain by rock, age and landforms.

## 1. The Peninsular Block (kegy102 pp. 2-3)

**Where.** NCERT draws its northern boundary as an irregular line: from **Kachchh**, along the western side of the **Aravali** range near Delhi, then roughly parallel to the **Yamuna and the Ganga** as far as the **Rajmahal Hills** and the Ganga delta. The Karbi Anglong and Meghalaya plateau in the north-east and Rajasthan in the west are also extensions of the block; a **Malda fault** in West Bengal separates the north-eastern parts from the Chotanagpur plateau. In Rajasthan, the desert and desert-like features lie over the block.

**What it is made of.** A great complex of **very ancient gneisses and granites**. Since the **Cambrian period** the Peninsula has stood like a rigid block. Two exceptions: part of the **western coast is submerged** beneath the sea, and some parts have changed through tectonic activity without disturbing the original base.

**What has happened to it.** As part of the Indo-Australian Plate it has seen vertical movements and block faulting. NCERT's examples: the **rift valleys of the Narmada, Tapi and Mahanadi** and the **Satpura block mountains**.

**Typical landforms.** Mostly **relict and residual mountains**: Aravali, Nallamala, Javadi, Veliconda, Palkonda and Mahendragiri hills. River valleys are **shallow with low gradients**. Most east-flowing rivers make **deltas** before entering the Bay of Bengal: the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri.

## 2. The Himalayas and other Peninsular mountains

These are **young, weak and flexible**, unlike the rigid Peninsular Block. They are still under the push and pull of internal and surface forces, so they develop **faults, folds and thrust planes**. They are tectonic in origin and are cut by fast, youthful rivers, which make **gorges, V-shaped valleys, rapids and waterfalls**.

## 3. The Indo-Ganga-Brahmaputra Plain

Formed by the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra. Originally a **geo-synclinal depression** (a long trough). NCERT says it reached its maximum development during the **third phase of Himalayan mountain formation, about 64 million years ago**, and has since been filled by sediments from Himalayan and Peninsular rivers. The alluvium is **1,000-2,000 m** deep. *Reading note:* NCERT says "average depth" here (p3) but "maximum depth" for the same range on p5. Treat 1,000-2,000 m as a rough range, and check the wording before using it in an answer. The 64-million-year figure is the book's; compare it with the dating in Day 19 lesson GS-097 and verify.

## Comparison table

| Feature | Peninsular Block | Himalaya and other mountains | Plain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age and stability | Very old, rigid | Young, weak, flexible | Young fill |
| Main material | Gneiss, granite | Folded and faulted rocks | River alluvium |
| Rivers | Shallow, low gradient valleys | Fast, youthful, gorges | Meandering, depositional |
| Typical landform | Relict hills, rift valleys | Gorges, V-valleys, rapids | Flat plain, deltas |

## Using it in a Prelims question

The 2026 Prelims item on the Peninsular Block (module 8) asks which features belong to this block. The two ideas to use: **submergence of the western coast** and **relict ranges such as Veliconda and Mahendragiri**. Deep V-shaped valleys belong to the young mountains, not to the block.


### 4. Core: the northern mountains and the Northern Plain

*Objective:* Describe the north and north-eastern mountains and the three belts of the Northern Plain with the figures NCERT gives.

## 1. The Northern and North-eastern Mountains (kegy102 p5)

They consist of the **Himalayas** and the **North-eastern hills**. The Himalayas are a series of **parallel ranges**; NCERT's sentence lumps the ranges loosely (it says the Greater Himalayan range includes the Great Himalayas and the Shiwalik); the standard division is three parallel ranges, the Greater Himalaya (Himadri), the Lesser Himalaya (Himachal) and the Shiwalik (outer), taught in Day 22 lesson GS-114 (verify). Their direction changes along the arc:

- **North-west India:** north-west to south-east.
- **Darjiling and Sikkim:** east-west.
- **Arunachal Pradesh:** NCERT prints "south-west to north-west". This reads like a slip; maps show a south-west to north-east trend there, so check before quoting.
- **Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram:** north-south.

NCERT gives about **2,500 km** east to west and **160-400 km** north to south, attached to the Great Himalayan (central axial) range; these figures describe the Himalayan system as a whole, not only the axial range (flagged in Day 19 lessons GS-112 and GS-132; check). NCERT's key sentence: the Himalayas are a physical barrier and also a **climatic, drainage and cultural divide**. Details of the ranges, passes and rivers wait for Day 22 lesson GS-114.

## 2. The Northern Plain (kegy102 pp. 5-6)

Built by alluvium from the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra. About **3,200 km** from east to west, **150-300 km** wide, alluvium up to **1,000-2,000 m** deep. A general elevation of **50-150 m** above sea level, "otherwise a featureless plain".

### The belts from north to south

| Belt | Width | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Bhabar** | 8-10 km | Narrow belt along the Shiwalik foothills where the slope breaks; streams drop boulders and rocks and sometimes disappear |
| **Tarai** | 10-20 km | Streams re-emerge without clear channels; marshy, swampy, thick natural vegetation, varied wildlife |
| **Bhangar** | (no width given) | Older alluvium |
| **Khadar** | (no width given) | Newer alluvium |

Bhangar and Khadar are the two parts of the **alluvial plains** south of the Tarai; they show the mature stage of river landforms: sand bars, meanders, ox-bow lakes, braided channels.

## Three more facts the extract gives

1. The **Brahmaputra plains** are known for riverine islands and sand bars; many areas suffer **periodic floods and shifting courses**.
2. The river mouths build some of the largest deltas of the world; the example is the **Sunderbans**.
3. **Haryana and Delhi** form a **water divide** between the Indus and Ganga systems. The Brahmaputra flows from the north-east to the south-west, then turns almost **90 degrees southward at Dhubri** before entering Bangladesh.

The plains have fertile alluvial soil supporting wheat, rice, sugarcane and jute and hence a large population.

## Common mix-ups

- The Bhabar is where rivers **sink** (porous boulders); the Tarai is where they **re-emerge** (marsh). They are not the same belt.
- Older alluvium is **Bhangar**, newer is **Khadar**. Khadar is the newer deposit (NCERT p5), the part rivers are still adding to in floods (standard reasoning).

### 5. Core: the Peninsular Plateau and its three groups

*Objective:* Describe the Peninsular Plateau, its outer limits, and its three groups (Deccan, Central Highlands, North-eastern Plateau) with the heights NCERT gives.

## The plateau as a whole (kegy102 p6)

The Peninsular Plateau is an **irregular triangle** rising from about **150 m above the river plains** to **600-900 m**. NCERT marks its outer extent: the **Delhi ridge** (an extension of the Aravali) in the north-west, the **Rajmahal hills** in the east, the **Gir range** in the west and the **Cardamom hills** in the south. Its north-eastern extension is the **Shillong and Karbi Anglong plateau**.

It is made of a series of plateaus (Hazaribagh, Palamu, Ranchi, Malwa, Coimbatore, Karnataka, and others). It is "one of the oldest and the most stable landmass of India". The general slope is **west to east**, which the flow of the rivers proves. Typical features: **tors, block mountains, rift valleys, spurs, bare rocky structures, hummocky hills and wall-like quartzite dykes** (natural water-storage sites). The west and north-west have **black soil**. The plateau has seen repeated uplift and submergence with faulting; the **Bhima fault** is noted for repeated seismic activity. The north-west has **ravines and gorges**, for example the Chambal, Bhind and Morena ravines.

NCERT divides it into three groups.

## (i) The Deccan Plateau

Bordered by the **Western Ghats** (west), **Eastern Ghats** (east) and the **Satpura, Maikal range and Mahadeo hills** (north).

- **Western Ghats**: locally **Sahyadri** in Maharashtra, **Nilgiri hills** in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and **Anaimalai and Cardamom hills** in Kerala. Higher and more continuous than the Eastern Ghats; average elevation about **1,500 m**, rising from north to south. Highest peak of the Peninsular plateau: **Anaimudi, 2,695 m**, on the Anaimalai hills; next, **Dodabetta, 2,637 m**, on the Nilgiri hills. Most Peninsular rivers rise here.
- **Eastern Ghats**: discontinuous, low, highly eroded by the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri. Ranges include the Javadi hills, Palconda range, Nallamala hills and Mahendragiri hills.
- The two Ghats **meet at the Nilgiri hills**. Full treatment: Day 17 lesson GS-116.

## (ii) The Central Highlands

Bounded on the west by the **Aravali**. General elevation **700-1,000 m**, sloping to the **north and north-east**. The **Satpura range**, a series of scarped plateaus at **600-900 m**, is described as the northernmost boundary of the Deccan plateau and a "classic example" of relict mountains. Most **Yamuna tributaries** rise in the **Vindhyan and Kaimur** ranges; **Banas**, from the Aravalli, is the only significant tributary of the Chambal that rises in the west. The Rajmahal hills are an eastern extension, with the mineral-rich **Chotanagpur plateau** south of them. The plateau reaches **Jaisalmer**, where it is covered by dunes. Metamorphic rocks (marble, slate, gneiss) occur.

*Reading note:* NCERT describes the Satpura in the Central Highlands section but also as the northern edge of the Deccan. Keep both statements; they describe one range from two sides.

## (iii) The North-eastern Plateau

An extension of the Peninsular plateau. NCERT's account: the northeast push of the Indian plate during Himalayan building created a huge **fault between the Rajmahal hills and the Meghalaya plateau**, later filled by river deposits. Today the **Meghalaya and Karbi Anglong plateaus stand detached** from the main block. Meghalaya has three parts, the **Garo, Khasi and Jaintia Hills**, named after tribal groups. Mineral-rich (coal, iron ore, sillimanite, limestone, uranium); heavy south-west monsoon rain gives a highly eroded surface; **Cherrapunji** shows bare rock.

## Quick map-check

Most major Peninsular rivers flow from west to east; the exceptions are the **Narmada and Tapi**, which flow west through rift valleys (kegy103 p7). That is Day 17 lesson GS-117's territory.


### 6. Core: the Indian Desert, the Coastal Plains and the Islands

*Objective:* Describe the last three divisions: the desert, the two coastal plains and the two island groups, with the figures and place names NCERT gives.

## The Indian Desert (kegy102 pp. 7-8)

Lies **north-west of the Aravali hills**; **Marusthali**. Undulating land dotted with **longitudinal dunes and barchans** (crescent-shaped dunes). Rainfall **below 150 mm** a year, so arid climate and thin vegetation. NCERT says that in the Mesozoic era this region was under the sea, and cites the **Wood Fossils Park at Aakal** and marine deposits near **Brahmsar** (near Jaisalmer); wood fossils about 180 million years old. The underlying rock is an extension of the Peninsular plateau, but surface forms are made by **physical weathering and wind**: mushroom rocks, shifting dunes, oases (mostly in the south). The northern part slopes towards **Sindh**, the southern towards the **Rann of Kachchh**. Rivers are mostly **ephemeral**; the **Luni** is the main one. Some streams vanish into inland drainage ending in a lake or **playa**; their brackish water is the source of salt.

## The Coastal Plains (kegy102 p8)

| Feature | Western coastal plain | Eastern coastal plain |
|---|---|---|
| Type | **Submerged** coast | **Emergent** coast |
| Width | Narrow; narrower in the middle, broader towards north and south | Broader |
| Rivers | **No deltas** | Well-developed deltas: Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri |
| Ports | Many natural ports and harbours | Fewer ports; continental shelf extends up to **500 km** |
| Named parts | Kachchh and Kathiawar (Gujarat), Konkan (Maharashtra), Goan coast, Malabar (Karnataka and Kerala per NCERT) | (not subdivided in the extract) |

Western ports named: **Kandla, Mazagaon, JLN Port Navha Sheva, Marmagao, Mangalore, Cochin**. The Malabar coast has **kayals** (backwaters), used for fishing, inland navigation and tourism; the Nehru Trophy Vallamkali (boat race) is held at Punnamada Kayal, Kerala. NCERT says Dwaraka, once part of the mainland, lies under water. *Naming note:* the NCERT sentence does not name the Karnataka stretch; it is commonly called the Kanara coast (standard; verify).

## The Islands (kegy102 p9)

| Feature | Bay of Bengal group | Arabian Sea group |
|---|---|---|
| Names | **Andaman** (north), **Nicobar** (south) | **Lakshadweep and Minicoy** |
| Extent | About **572** islands/islets; roughly **6-14 degrees N, 92-94 degrees E** | Roughly **8-12 degrees N, 71-74 degrees E**; **220-440 km** off the Kerala coast |
| Separated by | **Ten Degree Channel** (Andaman from Nicobar) | **Nine Degree Channel** separates Minicoy from the main Lakshadweep group (standard; NCERT p9 places it between Amini and Canannore, which Day 17 lesson GS-130 treats as a misprint for the Eleven Degree Channel; see also GS-132; verify) |
| Origin | Believed to be elevated parts of submarine mountains; some smaller islands volcanic | Built entirely of **coral** |
| Other | **Barren Island**, the only active volcano in India, is here | About **36** islands, **11** inhabited |

Peaks (as printed): Saddle Peak (North Andaman) 738 m, Mount Diavolo (Middle Andaman) 515 m, Mount Koyob (South Andaman) 460 m, Mount Thuiller (Great Nicobar) 642 m. Rain is **convectional**; vegetation is of the **equatorial** type. The 26 December 2004 tsunami struck these islands (kegy102 p9, kegy106 p8).

*Source slip to check:* NCERT says Minicoy is the largest island with an area of **453 sq km**. That is not plausible for a group of coral islets whose total land area is only a few tens of sq km (standard; verify). Do not memorise 453.


### 7. Master: how relief shapes India, and India as a subcontinent

*Objective:* Link relief to climate, rivers, minerals, ports and settlement, and answer 'Why is India a subcontinent?' with evidence.

## Why India is called a subcontinent (kegy101 p5)

NCERT's reasoning: India is **bounded by the Himalayas in the north, the Hindukush and Sulaiman ranges in the north-west, the Purvachal hills in the north-east and the Indian Ocean in the south**, forming "a great geographic entity known as the Indian subcontinent". It includes **Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh and India**. The Himalayas, with other ranges, acted as a formidable barrier; except for a few passes (**Khyber, Bolan, Shipkila, Nathula, Bomdila**) crossing was difficult, which helped create a unique **regional identity**. Add physical diversity (module 1) and the monsoon unity (Day 20 lesson GS-119) and you have a full answer to the 2021 Mains question. A subcontinent is commonly defined as a large, distinct part of a continent marked off by physical boundaries (standard; the NCERT book glossary is not in the extracts; verify).

## Six links to carry

1. **Relief and climate.** The Himalayas shield India from cold northern winds and trap the monsoon (kegy104 p3). The windward side of the Western Ghats and Assam gets heavy rain; the southern plateau on the leeward side stays dry. The Indian desert is arid. See Day 21 lesson GS-120.
2. **Relief and rivers.** The plateau slopes **west to east**, so most Peninsular rivers flow to the Bay of Bengal and form deltas; the Narmada and Tapi flow west through rift valleys (kegy103 p7). Haryana and Delhi are a water divide between the Indus and the Ganga.
3. **Relief and minerals.** Chotanagpur and Meghalaya plateaus are mineral-rich (coal, iron ore, sillimanite, limestone, uranium).
4. **Relief and soils/crops.** Alluvial plains: wheat, rice, sugarcane, jute. Black soil: west and north-west of the plateau.
5. **Relief and ports.** The submerged west coast has natural harbours; the emergent east coast, with a shelf up to 500 km wide, has fewer.
6. **Relief and hazards.** Young mountains: faults, folds, earthquakes and landslides; stable plateau: fewer, but note the Bhima fault.

## 2026 Prelims Q34: features of the Peninsular Block

Statement 1: submergence of parts of the western coast due to tectonic activity. NCERT: part of the western coast of the Peninsula is submerged beneath the sea (module 3). ### Notes: Definitions

- Physiography: the outcome of structure, process and stage of development (NCERT).
- Peninsular Block: the old, rigid core of gneiss and granite south of the Aravali-Yamuna-Ganga line.
- Relict (residual) mountain: a worn-down remnant of an old range, for example Aravali, Nallamala, Veliconda.
- Bhabar: the narrow belt of boulders at the foot of the Shiwalik where streams sink. Tarai: the marshy belt where they re-emerge.
- Bhangar: older alluvium. Khadar: newer alluvium (NCERT).
- Submerged coast: sunk under the sea. Emergent coast: risen from the sea.
- Subcontinent: a large, distinct part of a continent marked off by physical boundaries (standard definition, not taken from the extracts; verify).

### Memory skeleton

- Physiography = structure + process + stage; the Indian plate moved north and built the Himalaya and the plain
- Three geological divisions: Peninsular Block, Himalayas and other mountains, Indo-Ganga-Brahmaputra Plain
- Peninsular Block: boundary Kachchh-Aravali-Yamuna-Ganga-Rajmahal; gneiss and granite, rigid since the Cambrian
- Six physiographic divisions: mountains, plain, plateau, desert, coastal plains, islands
- Northern Plain belts north to south: Bhabar, Tarai, Bhangar, Khadar
- Peninsular Plateau, Indian Desert, coasts and islands in one line each
- Relief to consequence: climate, rivers, ports, minerals, settlement

### Last-week summary

- 3 geological divisions (old rigid block, young flexible mountains, filled trough) and 6 physiographic divisions; the Himalaya is a climatic, drainage and cultural divide.
- Block boundary: Kachchh, west flank of the Aravali near Delhi, parallel to Yamuna-Ganga, Rajmahal Hills; Malda fault cuts off Meghalaya-Karbi Anglong from Chotanagpur.
- Northern Plain about 3,200 km long, 150-300 km wide, 50-150 m high; Bhabar 8-10 km (streams sink), Tarai 10-20 km (re-emerge, marsh); Bhangar old, Khadar new; Haryana-Delhi divides Indus from Ganga.
- Plateau 150 m above plains to 600-900 m, Delhi ridge-Rajmahal-Gir-Cardamom; desert north-west of the Aravali, under 150 mm, Luni; peaks in GS-116, coasts and islands in GS-130.
