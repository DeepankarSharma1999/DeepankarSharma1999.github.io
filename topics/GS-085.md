## GS-085: Classical theatre and puppetry

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS1 · Culture · Indian art, literature and architecture

*Goal:* Explain Classical theatre and puppetry and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Natyashastra and Sanskrit drama

Drama in India likely grew from ritual dances in which a community slowly divided into performers and spectators. The Natyashastra, ascribed to Bharata, codified this practice: it gathers recitation, song, gesture and emotion, distinguishes natya, nritya and nritta, and treats abhinaya as both entertaining and educating. Sanskrit plays used a sutradhara to link events. About 35 classical plays are counted, from Ashvaghosha's Kushan-era fragment to Bhasa, Kalidasa, Shudraka, Harsha, Vishakhadatta's Mudrarakshasa and Bhavabhuti. Royal patronage and an elite audience made this theatre vulnerable when courts and Sanskrit declined, while Kutiyattam in Kerala kept its structure alive by absorbing local culture.

### Folk theatre and the modern stage

Popular languages produced their own theatres. Tamil literature mentions kuttu, surviving in rural therukoothu, and the south's lasting contribution was dance-drama: Bhagavata Mela, Yakshagana, Kuchipudi and Kathakali. NCERT also lists Bhavai (Gujarat and Rajasthan), Tamasha (Maharashtra), Bhand Pather (Kashmir), Nautanki (Kanpur, Lucknow, Hathras), Swang (Haryana and Punjab), Raslila (Uttar Pradesh) and Jatra (Bengal and Odisha). Under colonial rule English theatre shaped urban stages; Girish Chandra Ghosh and Bharatendu Harishchandra mixed entertainment, nationalism and reform, and Pestonji Framji's company launched Parsi theatre around 1870. IPTA later transformed modern theatre, with Sangeet Natak Akademi and the National School of Drama as state institutions.

### Puppetry: string, glove, rod and shadow

Puppet forms are best learnt by type. Shadow theatre, tied to particular communities, includes Chamadyacha Bahulya (Maharashtra), Tolu Bommalata (Andhra Pradesh), Togalu Gombeyata (Karnataka), Tolpava Kuthu (Kerala) and Ravanachhaya (Odisha). Sangeet Natak Akademi's 2018-19 festivals add the other families. String puppets came from Rajasthan (Kathputli), Maharashtra (Kalasutri Bahulya), Odisha (Kundhei), Assam, Manipur, Tripura and West Bengal. Glove puppets were shown by Beni Putul groups of West Bengal, a Kerala troupe and a glove putul team from Uttar Pradesh, and West Bengal's Putul Nach used rods. Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Odisha sent combined string-and-rod troupes. Shadow-play narratives, NCERT adds, come mainly from the epics, Puranas and local tales.
