## GS-067: World War I origins and outcomes

Mains GS1 · World History · 18th–20th century

*Goal:* Explain World War I origins and outcomes and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Origins: nationalism, imperialism and the Balkans

After 1871 the Balkans — a Slav-inhabited region spanning present-day states such as Serbia, Bulgaria, Greece, Romania, Albania and Bosnia-Herzegovina — became Europe's most serious source of nationalist tension. Much of it lay under the weakening Ottoman Empire, whose reforms failed to stop subject nationalities breaking away. Slav groups justified independence by claiming a lost past freedom, and the new Balkan states competed jealously for territory. Russia, Germany, England and Austria-Hungary each tried to block the others and extend their own influence, amid wider rivalry over trade, colonies and naval and military power. This chain of rivalry led to a series of regional wars and finally to the First World War.

### The war as an industrial and social upheaval

The war pitted the Allies against the Central Powers and was fought mainly in Europe, though its effects were global. Industrial nations harnessed mass production to destroy their enemies, and millions of soldiers were moved by ship and train to the front. Most of those killed or maimed were working-age men, so Europe's workforce shrank and household incomes fell. Industry switched to war goods, and women took jobs previously reserved for men. Economic links among leading powers snapped; Britain financed the war with American loans and ended with large external debts, while industries grew in India and Japan during Britain's preoccupation.

### Outcomes and exam angles

Post-war Europe struggled. When the war boom ended, production fell and governments cut spending; in 1921 one British worker in five was unemployed. Wartime expansion of wheat in Canada, America and Australia, followed by revived eastern European output, created a glut that hurt farmers. Politically, the war encouraged states to build an international body to manage conflict, giving rise to the League of Nations, which later failed to stop the next world war. Meanwhile anti-imperial nationalisms spread among colonised peoples. Prelims: alliance membership, 1914–18 dates, first industrial war. Mains: link causes (nationalism plus imperial rivalry) to consequences (debt shift to the US, labour losses, institutional experiments).

### Worked example

Question: 'The fusion of nationalism and imperialism brought Europe to catastrophe in 1914.' Explain, and assess the war's economic consequences. Frame: (1) Narrowing of nationalism after 1871 and great-power manipulation of subject nationalities. (2) The Balkans: Ottoman decline, rival Slav states, Russia–Germany–England–Austria-Hungary competition. (3) Consequences: 9 million dead, workforce loss, US becomes creditor, British debt and unemployment, agricultural glut. (4) Institutional outcome: League of Nations. Conclude by linking these to interwar instability.

### Recall

- - **q:** Name the Central Powers in the First World War.
  - **a:** Germany, Austria-Hungary and Ottoman Turkey.
- - **q:** Why was the Balkans explosive before 1914?
  - **a:** Romantic nationalism among Slav peoples, Ottoman disintegration, rivalry among Balkan states and competition among Russia, Germany, England and Austria-Hungary.
- - **q:** How did the war change the US's financial position?
  - **a:** Heavy British borrowing made the US an international creditor instead of a debtor.

### Common trap

Mixing up First and Second World War line-ups: in NCERT's account Ottoman Turkey and Austria-Hungary fought with Germany as Central Powers in 1914–18, while Russia fought on the Allied side; the label 'Axis Powers' belongs to the Second World War.
