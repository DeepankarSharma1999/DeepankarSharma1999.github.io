## GS-261: Food chains, webs and ecological pyramids

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain Food chains, webs and ecological pyramids and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Grazing and detritus chains

A grazing food chain starts with living plants, for example grass eaten by a goat eaten by a human. A detritus food chain begins with dead organic matter and is run by decomposers, mainly fungi and bacteria, also called saprotrophs, which secrete enzymes to break down dead material and absorb the products. Death of any organism starts the detritus chain. In water, grazing chains carry most energy; on land, more energy passes through detritus. The two chains connect because some detritus organisms are eaten by grazing-chain animals, and omnivores like crows and cockroaches link them into food webs.

### Energy loss and the 10 per cent law

Energy fixed by producers is either passed to a consumer or lost when the organism dies and becomes detritus. At each step, much is dissipated as heat, consistent with the second law of thermodynamics. The 10 per cent law states that roughly a tenth of energy reaches the next level, which is why grazing chains rarely exceed a few levels — producer, herbivore, primary carnivore, secondary carnivore. NCERT's energy pyramid also shows producers converting only about 1 per cent of available sunlight into net primary productivity. NCERT asks whether detritus chains face a similar limit.

### Ecological pyramids and their exceptions

Pyramids express number, biomass or energy at each trophic level, with producers at the base. In most ecosystems all three are upright. Exceptions exist: one large tree with many insects and birds produces an inverted number pyramid; in the sea, small phytoplankton standing crop supports larger zooplankton and fish biomass, inverting the biomass pyramid. The energy pyramid cannot invert. NCERT lists limits of pyramids: they ignore species at multiple levels, assume simple chains rather than webs, and give no place to saprophytes despite their vital role.
