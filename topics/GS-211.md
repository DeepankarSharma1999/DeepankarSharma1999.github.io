## GS-211: RTI and Information Commissions

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS2 · Polity · Institutions, law and politics

*Goal:* Explain RTI and Information Commissions and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Scope and obligations

The Act's preamble links transparency to an informed citizenry, curbing corruption and holding governments accountable, while acknowledging the need to harmonise disclosure with other public interests. 'Public authority' includes bodies created by the Constitution or by law and bodies owned, controlled or substantially financed by government, including substantially funded NGOs. 'Information' is broadly defined, covering records, e-mails, opinions, contracts and electronic data. Section 4 obliges authorities to catalogue records and publish details of their functions, decision procedures, budgets, subsidy beneficiaries and officers, and to give reasons for administrative and quasi-judicial decisions to affected persons, so that citizens need to file fewer requests.

### Requests, exemptions and appeals

A citizen applies in writing or electronically to the Public Information Officer, paying the prescribed fee (waived for people below the poverty line), without stating reasons. The officer must reply within thirty days, or forty-eight hours for life-and-liberty matters. Section 8 exempts categories such as information harming sovereignty or security, contempt of court, breach of legislative privilege, trade secrets, fiduciary information, cabinet papers until decisions are complete, and unwarranted invasions of privacy, but public interest can override, and most information older than twenty years must be given. Partial disclosure through severance is allowed. Aggrieved applicants file a first appeal, then a second appeal to the Information Commission.

### Information Commissions and enforcement

The Central Information Commission consists of a Chief Information Commissioner and up to ten Information Commissioners, appointed by the President on a recommending committee made up of the Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha and a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister; State commissions mirror this design. Commissions hear complaints and appeals with civil-court powers, may examine any record, require authorities to improve compliance, award compensation and impose penalties. The Act's overriding clause trumps the Official Secrets Act, and courts are barred from entertaining suits against orders except through the Act's appeal route. Commission decisions are binding, and commissions report annually on implementation. As originally enacted in 2005, sections 13 and 16 fixed a five-year term with an age cap of 65 and no reappointment, and tied salaries to those of the Election Commission; the RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019 changed these so that tenure and salary are now as prescribed by the Central Government.
