## GS-289: Indian climate missions and policy tools

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Climate and treaties

*Goal:* Explain Indian climate missions and policy tools and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### NAPCC as the umbrella

The NAPCC is India's overarching framework for both adaptation and mitigation. Its national missions address solar energy, enhanced energy efficiency, water, agriculture, the Himalayan ecosystem, sustainable habitat, a Green India, strategic climate knowledge and human health, and a National Mission on Sustainable Transport is under development. Each mission is institutionalised under a nodal ministry. Several, such as sustainable agriculture, water, habitat, Himalayan ecosystem, forestry and health, carry an adaptation emphasis. Beyond the missions, the government runs schemes across water, agriculture, forests, energy, mobility and housing to build resilience in priority areas.

### State Action Plans and coordination

MoEFCC's Climate Change Division coordinates the NAPCC and monitors its nine sectoral missions, and is revising the plan. It also guides states in preparing State Action Plans on Climate Change so that climate goals enter sub-national development strategies. SAPCCs are aligned with the NAPCC and emphasise region-specific measures to cut emissions, build resilience and advance sustainable development. Thirty-four States have prepared plans; in 2025 the Ministry approved revised plans for Maharashtra, Meghalaya and Chandigarh, taking the number of revised SAPCCs to 16. A proposal for a State Climate Forum is also being conceptualised.

### Delivery tools: NAFCC and ICAP

The National Adaptation Fund for Climate Change, set up in 2015, finances adaptation projects in vulnerable States and UTs in project mode. An evaluation in 2025 credited completed projects with climate-resilient farming, community outreach and better climate data through GIS mapping. On the mitigation and ozone side, the India Cooling Action Plan, launched in March 2019, gives an integrated twenty-year vision of cooling across sectors, covering lower cooling demand, refrigerant transition, energy efficiency and better technology, and recommends synergies with existing schemes. Ozone and HFC regulation, including HCFC phase-out and the Kigali Amendment, provides the legal backbone for the refrigerant transition.
