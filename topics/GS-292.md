## GS-292: Human physiology and nutrition basics

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Science · General science / technology

*Goal:* Explain Human physiology and nutrition basics and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Nutrients and a balanced diet

NCERT calls nutrients the food components that supply energy, support growth and repair, guard against disease and keep body functions going; the major ones are carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins and minerals. Carbohydrates from cereals, potato or banana and fats from ghee, oils, nuts and seeds are energy-giving foods, fat also acting as stored energy. Proteins from pulses, beans, milk, paneer, eggs and fish are body-building foods for growth and repair. Vitamins and minerals, needed only in small amounts, are protective nutrients. Dietary fibre, or roughage, gives no nutrients but helps clear undigested food, and water is also essential. A balanced diet provides every nutrient plus roughage and water in proper amounts, and needs differ with age, gender, activity and health. Junk foods carry many calories from sugar and fat but little else.

### Deficiency diseases

NCERT's chart pairs missing nutrients with disorders: vitamin A with poor vision and night blindness, vitamin B1 with beriberi, vitamin C with scurvy (bleeding gums and slow wound healing), vitamin D with rickets (soft, bent bones), calcium with weak bones and tooth decay, iodine with goitre (a swelling at the front of the neck) and iron with anaemia. In 1746 James Lind saw sailors with scurvy recover after eating lemons and oranges. In the 1960s goitre was common in the Himalayan region and northern plains because local soil lacked iodine, and iodised salt visibly reduced it. High-heat cooking can destroy vitamin C, washing cut or peeled produce can remove some vitamins, and sunlight lets the body make vitamin D.

### Digestion, respiration and circulation

Digestion is completed in the small intestine. In the stomach, hydrochloric acid gives pepsin its acidic medium and mucus protects the lining; then bile emulsifies fats and neutralises the acid, pancreatic trypsin and lipase act on proteins and fats, and villi absorb the products. Cells split glucose into pyruvate; with oxygen it yields carbon dioxide, water and far more energy, but in oxygen-starved muscle it becomes lactic acid and causes cramps. The energy is stored as ATP. Haemoglobin carries oxygen, while carbon dioxide travels mostly dissolved in blood. The four-chambered heart keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood apart, and blood passes through the heart twice in each circuit of the body (double circulation), and normal blood pressure is about 120/80 mm Hg.

### Worked example

Match the deficiency: bleeding gums and slow wound healing - vitamin C (scurvy); soft, bent bones - vitamin D (rickets); swelling at the front of the neck - iodine (goitre); weakness and breathlessness - iron (anaemia). Statement check: 'Pepsin acts best in an alkaline medium' - incorrect, stomach acid provides the acidic medium pepsin needs.

### Recall

- - **q:** Which deficiency causes goitre, and how was it reduced in India?
  - **a:** Iodine deficiency; supplementing common salt with iodine (iodised salt).
- - **q:** What is a balanced diet?
  - **a:** A diet with all essential nutrients, roughage and water in the right amounts for proper growth and development.
- - **q:** Which vitamin's deficiency causes rickets?
  - **a:** Vitamin D.
- - **q:** Which organ completes digestion of carbohydrates, proteins and fats?
  - **a:** The small intestine.
- - **q:** What is normal blood pressure?
  - **a:** About 120 mm Hg systolic and 80 mm Hg diastolic.

### Common trap

Assuming vitamins and minerals give energy or that roughage is a nutrient: vitamins and minerals are protective nutrients needed in small amounts, carbohydrates and fats are the energy-giving foods, and roughage supplies no nutrients.
