## GS-248: Irrigation types and water efficiency

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Agriculture and food

*Goal:* Explain Irrigation types and water efficiency and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Why India irrigates

Rainfall in India is concentrated in a few monsoon months and is uneven across space; the north-west and Deccan plateau are drought prone and even wetter states like Bihar and West Bengal suffer monsoon breaks. Crops such as rice, sugarcane and jute need far more water than rain can reliably supply. Irrigation therefore enables multiple cropping, raises yields above rain-fed levels and supplies the steady moisture that high-yielding varieties require. That is why Green Revolution success clustered in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh. Yet only about a third of cultivated land is irrigated, leaving most farmers exposed to erratic monsoons, droughts and floods.

### Protective, productive, dryland and wetland

NCERT classifies farming by moisture source into irrigated and rain-fed (barani) systems. Protective irrigation treats water as a supplement to rainfall and tries to wet as large an area as possible against moisture stress. Productive irrigation aims at high productivity, so it applies more water per hectare. Rain-fed farming splits into dryland farming, mostly below 75 cm of rain, where hardy crops like ragi, bajra, moong, gram and guar are grown with moisture conservation and rainwater harvesting, and wetland farming, where rain exceeds crop needs, flood and erosion risks are high, and water-hungry crops like rice, jute and sugarcane dominate.

### Efficiency, groundwater stress and policy response

Groundwater irrigation has been the engine of north-western agriculture, but heavy pumping has caused falling water tables, salinity in soils and chemical contamination. PMKSY, launched in 2015-16, seeks assured protective irrigation for all farms, integration of source, distribution and use, better on-farm water-use efficiency through water-saving technology, and watershed-based development of rain-fed areas. Watershed management works by checking runoff and recharging aquifers through percolation tanks and recharge wells, and succeeds mainly where communities participate, as in Ralegan Siddhi, which also banned water-intensive sugarcane.
