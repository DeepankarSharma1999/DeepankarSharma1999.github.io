## GS-322: Senior citizens and vulnerable groups

Mains GS2 · Social Justice · Rights, welfare and services

*Goal:* Identify the different risks faced by older people and other vulnerable groups.

### Vulnerability is multidimensional

Older persons may face income insecurity, chronic illness, isolation, abuse or inaccessible housing. Age alone does not define dependence: health, gender, assets and social support matter. Other vulnerable groups may face homelessness, migration-related exclusion or weak documentation. A useful assessment identifies the actual risk rather than treating a broad category as a uniform population.

### Combine security with autonomy

Pensions, affordable care, accessible transport, legal support and community services address different needs. Delivery should preserve informed choice and dignity. Assisted applications and home visits may be needed for people with limited mobility, but safeguards should prevent coercion or appropriation of benefits. Coordinate referrals so that a person is not repeatedly sent between offices.

### Worked example

An older widow receives a pension but cannot travel to collect medicines. A local health referral and accessible transport solve a different barrier from income support; both are needed for effective protection.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why is an income transfer sometimes insufficient?
  - **a:** Care, mobility, safety and social support needs may remain.
- - **q:** What does autonomy require?
  - **a:** Respecting the person’s informed choices and control over support.

### Common trap

Do not assume every elderly person is dependent or that family presence guarantees safety.
