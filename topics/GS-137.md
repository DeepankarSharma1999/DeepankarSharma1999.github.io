## GS-137: Population pyramids and demographic transition

Mains GS1 + Prelims GS1 · Geography · Human/economic/world

*Goal:* Explain Population pyramids and demographic transition and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Measuring change: rates and components

Growth is the change in a population between two dates, positive or negative. Natural growth is births minus deaths; actual growth also adds in-migrants and subtracts out-migrants. Crude birth rate counts live births per thousand people in a year, and crude death rate counts deaths per thousand, both using mid-year population. Mortality reflects an area's demographic structure, social advancement and economic development. The Class VIII text adds a simple balance rule: when birth rate exceeds death rate the population grows, when they are equal it is steady, and when deaths exceed births it shrinks. World population reached one billion in 1804, three billion in 1959 and six billion in 1999.

### Demographic transition in three stages

Transition theory describes and helps predict a region's population as society shifts from rural, agrarian and illiterate toward urban, industrial and literate. In the first stage families have many children to offset deaths from epidemics and uncertain food, life expectancy is short, technology is basic, and growth is slow; two centuries ago every country was here. In the second stage, better sanitation and health cut deaths while fertility stays high before declining, so the gap produces rapid net growth. In the last stage, both rates are low, people are urban and literate and consciously limit family size, and growth is slow or nil. Countries today sit at different stages.

### Reading pyramids, and India's place

An age-sex pyramid divides the population into age groups and shows the male and female percentage in each. The base reflects births, the top reflects survival to old age, and the bars below 15 and above 65 show young and elderly dependents relative to the working ages. Kenya's shape - wide base, rapid narrowing - signals high births and deaths; India's broad younger bands show more infants surviving, implying a growing workforce; Japan's narrow base shows low births and longer lives. India's own record matches the transition: a stagnant phase to 1921, steady growth to 1951, explosion from 1951 to 1981, and slowing since. Its large adolescent share is both an asset and a vulnerability.
