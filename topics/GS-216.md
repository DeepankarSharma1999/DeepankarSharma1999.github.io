## GS-216: GDP, GVA and growth accounting

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Basic macro and monetary

*Goal:* Explain GDP, GVA and growth accounting and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Value added and why it avoids double counting

A good can pass through several hands before it reaches a final user. If national output were measured by adding every firm's sales, the value of raw cotton would be counted again inside yarn, again inside cloth and again inside a shirt. The value-added method fixes this: each firm contributes only the difference between what it produces and what it buys from other producers as intermediate inputs. Adding these contributions for all producers in a year gives gross domestic product. If the wear and tear on machines and buildings (depreciation) is also deducted, the result is net value added, a measure that leaves out the capital worn out during the year.

### Factor cost, basic prices and market prices

India once highlighted GDP at factor cost, which values output only by payments to labour, land, capital and enterprise. Since the CSO revision of January 2015, the official series presents GVA at basic prices and treats GDP at market prices as the main number. The distinction rests on two kinds of taxes. Production taxes such as land revenue or stamp duty are paid regardless of how much is produced; product taxes such as excise or customs are levied per unit sold. Basic prices sit between the two ends: factor cost plus net production taxes gives basic prices, and basic prices plus net product taxes (product taxes minus product subsidies) give market prices.

### Limits of GDP as a welfare measure

Higher real GDP usually means more goods and services, but NCERT flags three reasons it can mislead. First, distribution: aggregate output can rise while most people's incomes fall, if the gains go to a small group. Second, non-monetary activity: unpaid domestic work and barter exchanges in remote areas are not recorded, so measured GDP understates productive activity. Third, externalities: a refinery's value added is counted, but the harm its river pollution does to fishermen and water users is not charged to it, so negative spillovers make GDP overstate well-being, while positive spillovers make it understate well-being. For Mains, pair these limits with real (not nominal) GDP and per-capita figures.
