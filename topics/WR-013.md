## WR-013: अपठित गद्यांश: मुख्य भाव

Hindi Paper A (qualifying) · Writing/Language · Reading and writing

### Bridge: Devanagari reading vocabulary: the small words that steer a passage

In Hindi prose, a handful of small words tell you how one sentence relates to the one before it. If you learn to notice them, you can find the author's real point even when the passage is long or difficult. These words are called **संयोजक शब्द** (connectives: words that join sentences or ideas).

| Kind of signal | Common Hindi words | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| विरोध / विपरीत सूचक (contrast) | परंतु, किंतु, लेकिन, मगर, पर, तथापि, फिर भी, जबकि, हालाँकि | The sentence that follows often carries the author's own point; what came before may be a common belief or a concession |
| कारण सूचक (reason) | क्योंकि, चूँकि, इसलिए कि | A reason is coming |
| निष्कर्ष सूचक (conclusion) | अतः, इसलिए, अतएव, फलस्वरूप, सारांश यह है कि, संक्षेप में, वस्तुतः, निश्चय ही | The author is wrapping up or stating the point firmly |
| जोड़ने वाले (addition) | और, तथा, एवं, साथ ही, इसके अतिरिक्त | One more point of the same kind |
| उदाहरण सूचक (example) | जैसे, यथा, उदाहरण के लिए | What follows is only an illustration, not the main idea |

**How to use the table.** Circle these words with your pencil on the first reading. After every विरोध word, stop and ask: which side is the author on? After every निष्कर्ष word, ask: is this the point the whole passage has been building towards? Words after जैसे are examples, so they usually should not become your main idea.

Remember that these are clues, not laws. A passage can state its main idea in plain words with no signal word at all.

### Bridge: What is a gist (सार) and why do we write one?

A **gist** (सार; भावार्थ is close to it) is the shortest honest statement of what a passage is saying; the central idea (मुख्य भाव) is its core claim. 'Honest' means that a person who has not read the passage should be able to read your gist and understand the author's main message correctly, without being misled and without anything you have invented.

A gist is different from a **summary** in length and purpose. A summary (संक्षेपण, a topic of its own that you will study later as WR-016) keeps the main points in order and is written to a stated length. A gist is smaller still: usually one or two sentences. Both must be in your own words.

A good gist answers three silent questions: What is the author talking about? What does the author claim about it? Why or to what end? For example, a passage may be about water. The claim may be that water is being wasted. The end may be that every citizen must conserve it. The gist joins the three: 'Water is being wasted, and conserving it is the duty of every citizen.'

Why does UPSC test this? The paper's stated aim, in the Plan of Examination, is to test whether the candidate can read serious discursive prose (prose that reasons and explains) and express ideas clearly and correctly. Writing a gist is the smallest complete exercise of that: you must understand first and then express.

### 1. What is an unseen passage (अपठित गद्यांश) in the UPSC Hindi paper?

*Objective:* Explain in your own words what अपठित गद्यांश means, where it sits in the UPSC scheme, and whether the Hindi qualifying paper applies to you.

## The words first

**अपठित** means 'not previously read' (अ = not, पठित = read), so it means *unseen*. **गद्यांश** is a passage (अंश = portion) of **गद्य** (prose, ordinary running writing, as opposed to **पद्य**, poetry). An **अपठित गद्यांश** is therefore a prose passage that you see for the first time in the examination hall and then answer questions on. You cannot memorise it in advance. You can only train the skill of reading it.

## Where it sits in the examination

The Civil Services (Main) Examination has nine papers that are written. Two of them are language papers: **Paper A** (one Indian language chosen from those in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution; 300 marks) and **Paper B** (English; 300 marks). This is stated in the Plan of Examination (registry entry Plan-9). Plan-10 adds the rules that matter most:

- The papers are of matriculation (about Class X) standard.
- They are of *qualifying nature*; the marks are not counted for ranking.
- Your Essay, General Studies and Optional papers are taken into account only if you obtain at least 25% in the Indian language paper and 25% in English.

The official syllabus clause for this topic is **Mains Hindi Paper A-1**, whose exact wording is: 'comprehension of given passages.' Plan-15 states the aim of the language papers in one line: to test the candidate's ability to read and understand serious discursive prose and to express ideas clearly and correctly.

## Does this apply to you?

**Do not assume.** Paper A is in the Indian language *you choose* from the Eighth Schedule, and some candidates are exempted from it. The registry entries I read (Plan-9, Plan-10, Plan-15) state the structure above but do not reproduce the exemption wording, so check that wording in the current notification for your own case. For this learner, the project brief fixes Hindi as the qualifying language, so these lessons treat Hindi Paper A as applicable. A different candidate would adapt the same skills to their own language.

## What this lesson covers

Comprehension questions can ask for the central idea, a detail, the meaning of a statement or an inference. This topic (WR-013) concentrates on the **मुख्य भाव**, the central idea. Later topics deepen the other parts: WR-014 (तर्क और निष्कर्ष, Day 9), WR-015 (तथ्य एवं आशय, Day 14) and WR-016 (संक्षेपण, Day 20). The official papers you will practise on are in the official-publication record; in the verified set for this topic each comprehension question is recorded as 12 marks and asks a specific question after a passage.

Do not worry about the qualifying risk yet. Module 8 gives a safe-pass plan. Today, learn to read in the right order.

### 2. मुख्य भाव, विषय, शीर्षक and सार: five words that are not the same

*Objective:* Distinguish central idea (मुख्य भाव), topic (विषय), title (शीर्षक), gist (सार) and intended meaning (आशय) and apply a three-part test to a candidate main idea.

Many wrong answers in a comprehension exercise come from mixing these terms. Learn them as a set.

| Term | Meaning in English | Form it takes | Example for a passage on water |
|---|---|---|---|
| **विषय** | Topic: what the passage is *about* | A noun or a phrase | जल |
| **मुख्य भाव / केंद्रीय भाव** | Central idea: what the author *says* about the topic | A full statement with a claim | जल व्यर्थ हो रहा है और उसे बचाना हर नागरिक का कर्तव्य है |
| **शीर्षक** | Title: a short, catchy label for the passage | A short phrase, not a sentence | जल-संरक्षण: सबका दायित्व |
| **सार** | Gist: the passage compressed | One to three sentences | The central idea plus the key reason |
| **आशय / तात्पर्य / अभिप्राय** | Intended meaning of a statement or of the author | Explanation of what is meant beyond the surface words | What the author really wants the reader to understand or do |

**The key lesson:** a topic names the subject; a central idea *makes a claim about* the subject. 'पर्यावरण' is a topic. 'पर्यावरण और जीवन एक-दूसरे पर निर्भर हैं, इसलिए प्रकृति का संतुलन बचाना आवश्यक है' is a central idea.

## The three-part test for a candidate main idea

Before you write, test your sentence:

1. **Does it cover the whole passage?** If it only fits one paragraph, it is a detail.
2. **Is it a claim, not a bare noun?** If it has no verb and no judgement, it is a topic.
3. **Is it the author's idea, not yours?** Do not add opinions, facts or examples that the passage does not contain.

## A worked illustration with an official passage

Take the passage on books in the 2017 paper (record upsc-2017-hindi-qualifying-a-q2-a). Its विषय is 'पुस्तकें'. A weak main idea is 'पुस्तकों का महत्त्व' (that is a topic with a word added). A stronger one is: books preserve and pass on human experience, guide and refine the mind and society, and so their value is beyond comparison. Check it against the test: it covers the beginning (food for the mind), the middle (guidance, delight, ideas) and the final lines (passed to the next generation), it has a claim, and every part comes from the passage.

**Title versus central idea:** a शीर्षक may be metaphorical ('ज्ञान का अक्षय भंडार'), but a central idea must be literal and explicit. If a question asks for a title, give a short phrase that reflects the whole passage; if it asks for the main idea, write a sentence.

### 3. A reading strategy and where main-idea clues usually sit

*Objective:* Apply a three-pass reading routine and use sentence-level and paragraph-level clues (first sentence, last sentence, contrast and conclusion words) to locate the main idea.

## The three-pass routine

Reading an unseen passage word by word and hoping to remember it is the slow, tiring way. Use three passes instead.

1. **Pass 0, the question (30 seconds).** In the papers you will practise on, the question is printed after the passage. Read it first anyway. If it asks 'आशय स्पष्ट कीजिए' you must look for the author's meaning; if it asks 'क्या है' or 'कौन' you are hunting for a definition or a detail.
2. **Pass 1, the skim (1 to 2 minutes).** Read the first sentence of each paragraph and the last sentence of the passage. Circle the contrast and conclusion words (परंतु, किंतु, अतः, वस्तुतः, सारांश यह है कि).
3. **Pass 2, the careful read.** Read the full passage once, at normal speed, and after each paragraph write a **short label (one to three words)** in the margin (for example 'समस्या', 'कारण', 'उपाय'). Do not copy sentences.

After Pass 2, cover the passage and say the main idea aloud in one sentence. If you cannot, read the labels again.

## Sentence-level and paragraph-level clues

- **प्रथम वाक्य (first sentence):** often announces the topic or even the claim. In the 2020 environment passage the very first sentence states the idea that the question later asks you to explain.
- **अंतिम वाक्य (last sentence):** often concludes. In the 2017 books passage the closing line sums up the importance of books; in the 2019 sea-farming passage a sentence beginning with 'इसलिए' (near the end of the first paragraph) says what humans must now do.
- **विरोधी संयोजक:** after 'परंतु', 'किंतु' or 'लेकिन' the author usually moves to what he or she actually wants to say. The 2019 passage first says humans became farmers on land and then, with 'लेकिन', turns to the sea where he is still largely a hunter.
- **निष्कर्ष शब्द:** 'अतः', 'सारांश यह है कि', 'संक्षेप में'. The 2026 passage uses 'सारांश यह है कि' to state exactly what the author counts as true enthusiasm.
- **Repeated key words:** a word that returns in every paragraph (for example पर्यावरण) is probably what the passage is about.
- **Examples:** words after 'जैसे' or a list of names are usually proof, not the main idea.

## When the clues fail

These are tendencies, not laws. In the 2024 literature passage the central line about the true writer comes in the middle of a long paragraph, after other ideas; you find it only by reading all of it. In the 2021 passage on kingship the later part shows a *change* (the king's show and splendour grew). A first-sentence-only reader would miss it. So: use the clues to form a hypothesis, and then confirm it against the whole passage before you write.

### 4. The author's stance, tone (भाषा-शैली) and purpose

*Objective:* Identify the author's attitude, tone and purpose from word choice, and separate the author's own view from views the author merely reports.

The main idea is not only *what* is said but *how strongly and why*. Questions such as 'लेखक का आशय क्या है?' need you to read attitude.

## Purpose (उद्देश्य)

Ask what the author is trying to do to the reader:

- **सूचना देना** (inform): states facts, definitions, history.
- **समझाना / व्याख्या करना** (explain): clarifies a concept.
- **पक्ष रखना / तर्क देना** (argue): defends a position.
- **चेतावनी देना** (warn): points to a danger.
- **प्रेरित करना** (inspire or urge): asks the reader to act.

## Tone (स्वर) and language style (भाषा-शैली)

Typical tone words are: गंभीर (serious), चिंतापूर्ण (worried), आशावादी (hopeful), आलोचनात्मक (critical), उपदेशात्मक (advisory), व्यंग्यात्मक (ironic), तटस्थ (neutral). Language style can be **वर्णनात्मक** (descriptive), **विवेचनात्मक** (analytical), **भावात्मक** (emotional) or **सूत्रात्मक** (short and aphoristic).

## Reading tone from words

In the 2020 environment passage the author says 'आश्चर्य होता है' about human exploitation of resources and warns that a certain day 'दूर नहीं लगता'. Words of surprise, judgement and warning tell you the tone is worried and critical, and the purpose is to warn. Contrast this with the 2025 biodiversity passage, which mostly states facts and classifications in a measured way; the tone is informative and neutral, with a concern underneath.

## Author's view versus reported view

Passages often *report* other people's views. In the 2021 kingship passage the opening lines say what 'प्राचीन धार्मिक ग्रंथों के अनुसार' was believed, and later what Kautilya said. These are views attributed to sources. If a question asks 'राजा और राज्य की प्राचीन अवधारणा क्या है?', your answer must say 'ancient texts and Kautilya regard...' and not present them as your own claim. Attribution words to notice: 'के अनुसार', 'माना गया है', 'कहा गया', 'कथन है कि'.

## Correcting a common belief

In the 2023 labour passage the author says people 'आमतौर पर' think a worker is someone who is paid by an employer, and then says that this is not so ('पर ऐसा नहीं है'). The author's own view is the corrected one. When you meet a sentence that quotes what 'we usually think', slow down: the turn is coming.

## A short routine

Before you write, finish this sentence in your head: 'The author wants the reader to ... because ...'. If you can finish it, you have the stance and the purpose.

### 5. Writing the answer: own words, word limit, clean Hindi

*Objective:* Write a short main-idea answer in your own Hindi within a stated word limit without copying whole sentences from the passage.

## The answer-building recipe

Use one fixed pattern so that your thinking is not rebuilt every time.

**Claim + reason (+ consequence).** Example frame: 'लेखक का मत है कि ... , क्योंकि ... । इसलिए ... ।' Fill the three slots from your margin labels.

1. Decide what the **question** asks (idea, meaning, detail, definition). Answer *that*.
2. Write the **claim** first, in one sentence, so that a tired examiner finds the point at once.
3. Add one **reason or support** from the passage, in your own words.
4. If words remain, add one **consequence or implication** (what follows).
5. Stop. Count the words.

## Own words, not copying

Copying full sentences shows memory, not understanding, and it often brings in irrelevant material. Three safe techniques:

- **Change the structure, not just the words.** The passage may say 'यदि हम पानी बचाएँ तो भविष्य सुरक्षित होगा'; you can write 'जल-संरक्षण से भावी पीढ़ियाँ सुरक्षित रहेंगी'.
- **Use your margin labels.** Write from your labels, not by looking at the sentences.
- **Short key phrases are acceptable** (a technical term such as 'मत्स्य न्याय' or a key word such as 'सह-अस्तित्व'); full sentences are not.

## The word limit

The question paper states the limit; treat it as binding and practise with it. I do not claim any official rule on how much is deducted for exceeding it. The safest habit is to stay at or slightly under the limit and to count every time during practice. In Hindi, count each written word separately (a hyphenated pair such as 'जल-संरक्षण' is usually counted as one; check the instructions in the paper). Train your eye: a handwritten line holds roughly 8 to 10 Hindi words, so 40 words is about four or five lines.

## Clean Hindi (शुद्ध वर्तनी)

Even a correct idea loses if the Hindi is careless. Watch these:

| Check | Correct | Common slip |
|---|---|---|
| की (of) and कि (that) | 'यह कहा गया कि जल कम है' | using की for कि |
| है (singular) and हैं (plural) | 'नदियाँ सूख रही हैं' | 'नदियाँ सूख रही है' |
| matra and conjunct | उन्नति, परिस्थिति, श्रेष्ठ | उन्नती, परिस्थती, श्रेष्ट |
| nukta and bindu | ज़रूरी, ख़तरा, संघर्ष | careless dropping of marks |
| punctuation | end a sentence with '।' | using a full stop '.' throughout (check what the paper uses and be consistent) |

**Check at the end:** read your answer once for spelling, once for meaning. Two extra minutes here is cheap.

### 6. Master layer: harder passages, unknown words and common failure patterns

*Objective:* Handle literary or abstract passages, unfamiliar words and typical failure patterns without losing the main idea.

This module is a deeper layer: do it after the first pass and again before Day 77 (the timed Hindi paper).

## Two kinds of passage

**Expository passages** (informative, such as the 2019 sea-farming, 2022 colonial records, 2023 labour, 2025 biodiversity passages) have a clear structure: a problem, causes, examples, solutions. Headings can be guessed from the labels you wrote.

**Literary or reflective passages** (such as the 2024 and 2026 passages on literature and on enthusiasm) are denser: long sentences, abstract nouns (उत्साह, धृति, साहस), and ideas built by contrast. Read slower, but keep to the routine. Underline the pairs being contrasted (प्रचार versus साहित्य; साहस versus उत्साह). The main idea in such a passage is often the *distinction* itself.

## Unknown words

You will meet words you do not know. Do not stop.

1. **Look at the neighbours.** Words near it (and the contrast words) usually reveal if the unknown word is positive or negative.
2. **Break it into parts.** Many Hindi words are built from a prefix (उपसर्ग), a root and a suffix (प्रत्यय), and some are compounds: 'अविवेकपूर्ण' = अ (not, a prefix) + विवेक (discernment, the root) + पूर्ण (full of; a compound element, not a प्रत्यय), so 'without judgement'. Prefix and suffix practice is a later topic, WR-022.
3. **Guess the sign.** If you only know that the word is negative, you can still write a correct main idea.
4. **Return later.** Never spend more than ten seconds on one word.

## Typical failure patterns

- **Topic as answer:** writing the subject, not the claim.
- **Detail as answer:** choosing the most vivid example because it was easy to remember.
- **Copying a paragraph:** giving a long lift from the passage.
- **Adding outside knowledge:** for example writing facts about Kautilya that the passage does not contain.
- **Reversing the author:** treating a reported or rejected view as the author's own.
- **Over-long preamble:** three lines of introduction before the claim.

## When the first/last sentence rule misleads

If the first sentence only sets a scene, if the last sentence is an example, or if the key sentence is hidden in the middle, the rule fails. This is why Pass 2 and the margin labels are important. Trust the whole passage over the habit.

## Reading speed

Do not try to read faster until you can state a main idea correctly. Speed comes from not re-reading, and not re-reading comes from labelling as you go.

### 7. Exam layer: five worked analyses of official passages

*Objective:* Analyse how five official Hindi qualifying (A) questions test the main idea, a detail or a meaning, and decide an approach for each.

The verified records for this topic come from the official Hindi qualifying (A) papers of 2017 to 2026 (18 records, 9 papers). Below is analysis only: I quote short phrases and I do **not** assert any official model answer or marking scheme. Open the official papers to read the full passages.

## 1. 2017 Q2(a): 'पुस्तकों को बेजोड़ क्यों माना गया है?'

The opening line calls books the best food for the mind. The passage then gives reasons: books preserve human achievement, guide people, entertain in a deep sense, spread ideas that change society, and carry knowledge to the next generation. The question is 'why', so it wants *reasons*. **Approach:** group the reasons into three or four clusters (preserve and pass on knowledge; guide and uplift; delight; social change) and write a claim sentence plus the clusters. **Trap:** listing every sentence, or treating 'मनोरंजन' as only light fun, when the author redefines it.

## 2. 2020 Q2(a): 'इस कथन का आशय स्पष्ट कीजिए कि जीवन और पर्यावरण एक-दूसरे से संबद्ध हैं'

The statement quoted in the question is the passage's first sentence. 'आशय' asks for meaning, not repetition. **Approach:** unpack it: living beings are products of their environment; body and mind are shaped by it; so both must coexist; harm to one harms the other (the passage's later paragraphs describe human-caused imbalance and its diseases). **Trap:** restating the sentence in different words. The same passage carried four narrower questions in the project record (2020 Q2(b) to (e): the relation of environment and nature, how nature builds balance, the crisis from imbalance, the diseases from pollution). They are detail questions; the same reading serves both, and WR-015 returns to them.

## 3. 2021 Q2(a): 'राजा और राज्य की प्राचीन अवधारणा क्या है?'

The passage reports ancient views: the king as the soul of the state and representative of divine power, and Kautilya's account that the people, tired of 'मत्स्य न्याय', chose a king who took a share of income and promised their welfare. Only later does it note that power and show grew. **Approach:** answer the 'ancient concept' with attribution ('के अनुसार'), and keep the later change out or mention it in one clause. **Trap:** mixing the later change into the ancient concept. This passage also had four sibling questions (2021 Q2(b) to (e)), including the official question "'मत्स्य न्याय' को अन्याय कहने का क्या अभिप्राय है?" and one asking what 'मानवता का साम्राज्य' means.

> **Mid-module stopping point:** you can pause here, after analyses 1 to 3, and return later for analyses 4 and 5. Note which analysis you finished.

## 4. 2023 Q2(a): 'श्रमिक कौन होता है?'

A detail-type question, but the passage warns against the common belief that only paid people are workers. **Approach:** use the author's corrective idea: anyone engaged in economic activity, including the self-employed and helpers, and those temporarily absent; add that economists argue that unpaid women's work should count. **Trap:** the popular definition.

## 5. 2026 Q2(a): 'उत्साह में साहस की भूमिका को स्पष्ट कीजिए'

A reflective passage that separates साहस (the capacity to bear pain or loss) from उत्साह (a joyful readiness to act despite pain). The summarising sentence 'सारांश यह है कि' states that उत्साह is seen in joyful effort, not in passive endurance, and the passage links साहस and धृति as parts of it. **Approach:** state that साहस is a necessary element but not sufficient; उत्साह needs the joyful effort too; illustrate with one example in your own words (such as giving). **Trap:** treating the two words as synonyms.

## Also available for practice

2019 (sea farming; its question asks how future problems of sea farming can be solved), 2022 (colonial records and maps), 2024 (the true writer, literature versus propaganda) and 2025 (biodiversity and its valuation). Use the same method and compare yourself with the rubric in module 8.

### 8. Practice routine, self-evaluation and the safe-pass plan

*Objective:* Run a repeatable practice routine on original passages, score yourself with a four-part rubric, and plan a safe pass of the qualifying paper without over-investing.

## Practice routine (use the 'language' tasks in this lesson)

1. Set a timer: 6 minutes for one passage with a main-idea question and a title question.
2. Do the three-pass reading and write the answer within the stated limit.
3. Open the model gist. Compare, do not copy. The model is a sample by Mission 90 and is not an official answer.
4. Score with the rubric below and write down the reason for each lost point.
5. Repeat the same passage after seven days (see delayed retention).

## Self-evaluation rubric (a study aid, not an official marking scheme)

Score each line 0 (absent), 1 (partial) or 2 (good).

| Criterion | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| भाव की सटीकता (accuracy of idea) | The claim covers the whole passage and matches the author's view |
| अपनी भाषा (own words) | No full sentence lifted; structure re-built |
| शब्द-सीमा (word limit) | At or under the stated limit |
| शुद्ध वर्तनी (clean Hindi) | Matras, कि/की, है/हैं, punctuation correct |

A total of 7 or 8 out of 8 on three passages in a row is a good sign for the first pass. It is evidence about your practice, not a prediction of the exam result.

## Safe-pass plan for the qualifying paper

Paper A is 300 marks, and 25% is the minimum (Plan-10). That is 25% of 300, i.e. 75 marks. The marks do not count for rank. So the aim is **to pass safely, not to score highly**:

- Give Hindi a small, regular daily slot (for example 30 to 40 minutes on days when GS or Physics is heavy), not whole days.
- Accuracy first: a clear, correct main idea earns more than a long, loose one.

### Notes: Key terms: topic, central idea, title, gist, intended meaning

- विषय (topic) is what the passage is about; it is a noun or phrase.
- मुख्य भाव / केंद्रीय भाव (central idea) is the claim the author makes about the topic; it is a full statement.
- शीर्षक (title) is a short, apt label for the passage; it may be figurative.
- सार (gist) is the passage compressed to one or two sentences in your own words.
- आशय / तात्पर्य / अभिप्राय is the meaning intended behind a statement or by the author.

### Notes: The reading routine

- Pass 0: read the question first (30 seconds).
- Pass 1: skim first sentences, last sentence and circle contrast and conclusion words (1 to 2 minutes).
- Pass 2: read once at normal speed, writing a short one-to-three-word label per paragraph.
- Cover the passage and say the main idea aloud in one sentence.
- Write claim + reason (+ consequence) in your own words, count words, check spelling.

### Notes: Author stance, purpose and tone vocabulary

- Purpose: सूचना देना, समझाना, पक्ष रखना, चेतावनी देना, प्रेरित करना.
- Tone: गंभीर, चिंतापूर्ण, आशावादी, आलोचनात्मक, उपदेशात्मक, व्यंग्यात्मक, तटस्थ.
- Style: वर्णनात्मक, विवेचनात्मक, भावात्मक, सूत्रात्मक.
- Attribution words that mark a reported view: के अनुसार, माना गया है, कहा गया, कथन है कि.

### Notes: Exceptions: when the first/last sentence rule misleads

- A scene-setting first sentence may not state the idea (look for the contrast word).
- A last sentence may be an example or an extra detail, not the conclusion.
- In literary passages the key sentence may be hidden in the middle of a long paragraph (as in the official 2024 passage).
- A passage may contain no signal words at all; then rely on repeated key words and your margin labels.

### Notes: Confused pairs

- विषय versus मुख्य भाव: a topic names the subject; a central idea makes a claim.
- शीर्षक versus सार: a title is a label; a gist is a sentence.
- आशय versus शब्दार्थ: आशय is the meaning in context; शब्दार्थ is the dictionary meaning of a word.
- साहस versus उत्साह (2026 passage): साहस is the capacity to bear pain; उत्साह needs the joyful readiness to act on top of it.
- The author's view versus a reported view: check 'के अनुसार' and 'आमतौर पर'.

### Practice

**Q.** Expand अपठित गद्यांश into its parts and explain what makes a passage 'unseen'.

**A.** अपठित = अ + पठित (not read before); गद्यांश = गद्य (prose) + अंश (portion). The passage is new to you in the exam, so you cannot prepare it; you can only prepare the skill.

**Q.** What is the official syllabus wording for this clause, and what are its marks and qualifying rule?

**A.** 'comprehension of given passages.' (Mains Hindi Paper A-1). Paper A is 300 marks, matriculation standard, qualifying only; 25% is needed for the other papers to be evaluated (Plan-9, Plan-10).

**Q.** Give one example each of a विषय and a मुख्य भाव for a passage on books.

**A.** विषय: पुस्तकें. मुख्य भाव: पुस्तकें मानव-अनुभव को सुरक्षित रखकर पीढ़ी-दर-पीढ़ी पहुँचाती हैं और मन तथा समाज का मार्गदर्शन करती हैं, इसलिए उनका महत्त्व अतुलनीय है।

**Q.** Name three connectives that signal contrast and three that signal conclusion.

**A.** Contrast: परंतु, किंतु, लेकिन (also मगर, तथापि). Conclusion: अतः, इसलिए, सारांश यह है कि (also अतएव, संक्षेप में).

**Q.** Why is 'पर्यावरण' not a valid main idea but 'पर्यावरण और जीवन परस्पर निर्भर हैं' is?

**A.** The first only names the topic; the second makes a claim about it. A main idea is a statement the author makes about the topic.

**Q.** Why is the first-sentence rule only a tendency and not a law? Give a case from the official papers.

**A.** Some passages open with a scene or a reported belief, and the key sentence sits later: for example the 2024 literature passage places its line about the true writer in the middle of a long paragraph. So use the first sentence as a hypothesis and confirm it against the whole passage.

**Q.** How does attributing a view ('के अनुसार') protect you in an answer?

**A.** It shows that you understand the view is the source's (or a reported belief), not your own claim or the author's final position, so you are not scored as having misread the author.

**Q.** What is the difference between a gist and a summary?

**A.** A gist is the shortest statement of the central idea (one or two sentences); a summary (संक्षेपण) keeps the main points in order to a stated length. Both are in your own words.

**Q.** Apply the three-part test to this candidate main idea for the 2021 kingship passage: 'कौटिल्य और राजा'. Does it pass? Improve it.

**A.** It fails: it is a topic (no claim) and does not cover the whole passage. An improved version: ancient texts and Kautilya treat the king as a protector chosen or ordained to give security and welfare in return for a share of income, though later the king's show and power grew.

**Q.** A question asks 'श्रमिक कौन होता है?' and your first instinct is 'वह जिसे वेतन मिले'. What does the passage tell you to do?

**A.** Reject the instinct. The author presents it as the common belief and rejects it ('पर ऐसा नहीं है'); write that anyone engaged in economic activity, including the self-employed and helpers, is a worker.

**Q.** Suppose you must answer 'लेखक का आशय स्पष्ट कीजिए' in 40 words. Describe your five steps.

**A.** Read the question; skim first/last sentences and contrast words; read once and label paragraphs; write claim + reason (+ consequence) from labels in own words; count words and check spelling.
