## GS-393: Ethical case study: stakeholder mapping

Mains GS4 · Ethics · Thinkers, administration and probity

*Goal:* Explain Ethical case study: stakeholder mapping and use it to reason through the worked example.

### Concept and distinctions

Stakeholder mapping identifies who is affected, who has power and who may be overlooked. Include direct and indirect effects, present and future interests, and people unable to speak easily. A stakeholder's influence is not the same as the moral weight of their claim.

### How to apply it

Start with the decision and its likely consequences, then identify rights, responsibilities and vulnerabilities. Separate factual uncertainty from value conflict: missing information may require inquiry before choosing. Record which interests are legitimate and which demands would violate others' rights. Mapping should guide the response, not become a decorative list. Pay particular attention to absent groups whose harms may be less visible than those of organized or powerful participants.

### Worked example

A dam decision affects displaced households, downstream users, project workers, taxpayers and future maintenance authorities.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why must stakeholder analysis include less powerful groups?
  - **a:** Their rights and harms matter even when they lack the resources to influence the decision publicly.

### Common trap

Ranking ethical importance solely by political influence.
