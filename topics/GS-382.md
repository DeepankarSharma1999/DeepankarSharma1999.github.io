## GS-382: Public service ethics and institutional values

Mains GS4 · Ethics · Thinkers, administration and probity

*Goal:* Explain Public service ethics and institutional values and use it to reason through the worked example.

### Concept and distinctions

Public-service ethics begins from delegated authority: officials exercise powers and spend resources entrusted to them for public purposes. The philosophical basis of probity is that public office is a trust, not private property. Equal citizenship, dignity and the rule of law justify limits on arbitrary advantage.

### How to apply it

A social-contract perspective links authority to legitimate public justification; duty-based reasoning requires fair procedures and respect for persons; consequential reasoning considers welfare and institutional trust; virtue ethics stresses honesty and practical wisdom. These foundations explain disclosure, impartial decisions and accountability. Probity demands more than avoiding prosecution: it requires conduct that can withstand informed public scrutiny, while protecting legitimate confidentiality and individual rights.

### Worked example

An officer discloses a financial interest and recuses from procurement because entrusted authority must not serve private gain.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why is probity justified even where no bribery is proved?
  - **a:** Public office is a trust; conflicts and arbitrary advantage can undermine impartiality and legitimate public confidence without a completed bribe.

### Common trap

Defining probity only as absence of a criminal conviction.
