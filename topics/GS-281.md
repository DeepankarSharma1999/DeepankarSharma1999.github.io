## GS-281: Mitigation versus adaptation

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Climate and treaties

*Goal:* Explain Mitigation versus adaptation and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Mitigation: cutting emissions and growing sinks

India reports that its emission intensity of GDP declined 36% from 2005 to 2020, showing growth decoupling from emissions. Non-fossil capacity rose from about 30% in 2014 to 51.93% by December 2025, and the 50% NDC goal was met in June 2025. Solar capacity climbed from about 2.63 GW (March 2014) to roughly 135.80 GW (December 2025). India ranks fourth in renewable installed capacity, fourth in wind and third in solar. On sinks, forest and tree carbon stock implies an additional 2.29 billion tonnes CO2e since 2005. These are mitigation outcomes because they reduce or offset emissions rather than reduce vulnerability.

### Adaptation: reducing vulnerability

The National Adaptation Fund for Climate Change, created in 2015, funds projects in vulnerable States and UTs in project mode; 30 projects in 27 States/UTs have been sanctioned. A third-party evaluation in August 2025 credited completed projects with awareness campaigns, crops able to withstand drought and salinity, integrated farming systems, sustainable fish culture, support for tribal, women and marginalised groups, GIS-based climate information and training. Under NAPCC the national missions with an adaptation focus cover sustainable agriculture, water, sustainable habitat, the Himalayan ecosystem, forestry and human health, while SAPCCs translate national strategy into state-specific actions for both adaptation and mitigation.

### Why the balance matters in negotiations

At COP30 in Belém, India argued for moving beyond a narrowly mitigation-focused framework towards adaptation and means of implementation, especially finance and technology. Parties agreed to pursue at least a tripling of adaptation finance by 2035 relative to 2025 and adopted 59 indicators for the Global Goal on Adaptation that are voluntary and add no reporting burden, which India welcomed as preserving national flexibility. India also pressed developed countries to reach net zero much earlier and to provide new, additional and concessional finance. The underlying principle is that the mitigation burden should not shift to those least responsible.

### Worked example

Classify: (a) solar capacity rising to ~135.8 GW - mitigation; (b) saline-tolerant crops under NAFCC - adaptation; (c) Green India plantation - mitigation with adaptation co-benefits. Mains frame: definitions, Indian examples, finance gap, and the COP30 adaptation-finance outcome.

### Recall

- - **q:** Give one Indian adaptation financing instrument.
  - **a:** The National Adaptation Fund for Climate Change (2015).
- - **q:** What adaptation finance goal was agreed at COP30?
  - **a:** Efforts to at least triple adaptation finance by 2035 relative to 2025 levels.
- - **q:** How much did India's emission intensity fall between 2005 and 2020?
  - **a:** By 36 percent.

### Common trap

Classifying afforestation only as adaptation; it is mitigation because forests act as carbon sinks, though it can also bring adaptation co-benefits.
