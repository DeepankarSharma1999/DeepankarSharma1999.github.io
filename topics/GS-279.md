## GS-279: Greenhouse gases and radiative forcing

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Climate and treaties

*Goal:* Explain Greenhouse gases and radiative forcing and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### The heat budget and albedo

Taking incoming solar energy at the top of the atmosphere as 100 units, roughly 35 are sent back to space before reaching the surface, mostly from cloud tops and some from snow and ice; this reflected share is the planet's albedo. Of the 65 units absorbed, 14 stay in the atmosphere and 51 reach the surface. The surface returns 51 units as terrestrial radiation: 17 escape directly and 34 are captured by the atmosphere. The atmosphere then radiates 48 units to space, so 17 plus 48 equals the 65 received. The solar constant is about 1.4 kW per square metre, but clear-sky insolation at the ground is about 1 kW per square metre, and snow reflects far more than dark soil or ocean water.

### How greenhouse gases act

The atmosphere lets most incoming short-wave sunlight through but absorbs much of the long-wave radiation emitted upward by the ground; gases doing this are greenhouse gases. NCERT names CO2, CFCs, methane, nitrous oxide and ozone as the main ones; NO and CO matter indirectly because they react with GHGs. How strongly a gas warms depends on how much its concentration rises, how long it stays aloft and which wavelengths it absorbs. CFCs are very effective; ozone absorbs ultraviolet in the stratosphere but traps terrestrial radiation when present in the lower troposphere. Longer-lived gases mean slower recovery of the system after emissions change.

### Radiative forcing: the IPCC measure

The IPCC defines radiative forcing as the change in net radiative flux, downward minus upward and measured in watts per square metre, produced when an external driver changes, for example CO2 concentration, volcanic aerosols or the Sun's output. It is called instantaneous when stratospheric temperatures are not allowed to adjust, stratospherically adjusted when they are, and effective radiative forcing once tropospheric adjustments are also included. AR6 Working Group I (2021) put human-caused forcing at 2.72 W m-2 in 2019 relative to 1750, driven mainly by greenhouse gases and partly cancelled by aerosol cooling, 0.43 W m-2 higher than the AR5 estimate. Average 2019 concentrations were 410 ppm CO2, 1866 ppb methane and 332 ppb nitrous oxide. Global Warming Potential uses forcing too: it compares a unit mass of a gas with CO2 over a chosen time horizon.

### Worked example

If melting snow is replaced by dark ocean water, albedo falls and more sunlight is absorbed, because low-albedo surfaces absorb more than snow. Distinguish this surface effect from radiative forcing, which the IPCC ties to changes in external drivers such as CO2 concentration; AR6 puts the human-caused total at 2.72 W m-2 in 2019 relative to 1750.

### Recall

- - **q:** What is the Earth's albedo in the NCERT heat budget?
  - **a:** About 35 units out of 100 units of insolation.
- - **q:** How does the IPCC define radiative forcing?
  - **a:** The change in net downward-minus-upward radiative flux (W m-2) due to a change in an external driver of climate change, such as CO2 concentration, volcanic aerosols or solar output.
- - **q:** What was human-caused radiative forcing in 2019 relative to 1750 according to AR6 WG I?
  - **a:** 2.72 [1.96 to 3.48] W m-2.
- - **q:** Which three factors decide a GHG molecule's effectiveness?
  - **a:** Increase in concentration, lifetime in the atmosphere, and wavelength of radiation absorbed.

### Common trap

Saying the greenhouse effect is entirely harmful: without it the Earth would be too cold for life; the concern is the enhanced effect from excess emissions.
