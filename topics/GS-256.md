## GS-256: E-NAM and digital farm technology

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Agriculture and food

*Goal:* Explain E-NAM and digital farm technology and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### The information problem in farm markets

Farmers historically sold cheaply because they did not know prices in other markets and had no storage to wait, while traders cheated through faulty weighing and manipulated accounts. Government answered with regulated market yards run by Agricultural Produce Marketing Committees. Physical access is still thin. An average mandi serves about 434.48 sq km, whereas the five-km radius suggested by the National Commission on Farmers (2006) implies roughly 80 sq km (Economic Survey 2023-24). Long trips and delays in reaching formal markets push small farmers towards intermediaries. That gap is what e-NAM, launched in 2016 according to NCERT, set out to narrow.

### How e-NAM works and how far it reaches

Started in April 2016, e-NAM is a nationwide electronic platform that links existing APMC mandis rather than replacing them. Each mandi receives free software and ₹75 lakh for hardware, quality-assaying equipment and facilities for cleaning, grading, sorting and packaging, so lots can be assayed and sold through online bidding. By 31 December 2025 roughly 1.79 crore farmers and 4,698 FPOs had registered, across 1,522 mandis in 23 states and 4 UTs (Economic Survey 2025-26). Evaluations cited in the Economic Survey 2023-24 report better price realisation, and about 66 per cent of surveyed farmers judged quality testing transparent. Weak awareness, low trust and difficulty setting up assaying labs persist. The 2020 scheme for 10,000 FPOs, budgeted at ₹6,860 crore to 2027-28, is meant to strengthen collective marketing.

### Wider digital agriculture and its limits

e-NAM is one piece of a larger digital push. The Digital Agriculture Mission, approved in September 2024, is building digital public infrastructure such as AgriStack and the Krishi Decision Support System, plus soil fertility maps. Crop insurance now uses YES-TECH remote-sensing yield estimates and WINDS weather stations, and the Kisan Rin Portal (2023) flagged ₹1,080.88 crore of duplicate or excess interest-subvention claims. NCERT's Farmer's Portal and earlier radio and TV weather bulletins show the longer history of farm information services. Yet NCERT warns that IT is a tool, not a catalyst: roads, warehouses and cold storage still matter, and private traders dominate many markets. Pair each digital initiative with the physical and institutional reforms that make it work.
