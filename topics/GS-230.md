## GS-230: Financial inclusion and digital payments

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Basic macro and monetary

*Goal:* Explain Financial inclusion and digital payments and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Why inclusion matters for an economy

Money's basic functions — medium of exchange, unit of account and store of value — are only fully available to people who can hold and move it safely. NCERT describes a move toward fewer cash transactions, where payments travel as digital information between parties, and links it to government efforts for financial inclusion: Jan Dhan accounts, Aadhaar-enabled payment systems, e-wallets and the National Financial Switch. Widespread mobile and smartphone use made such inclusion feasible at scale. For an exam answer, frame inclusion as a ladder: first an account, then regular use for payments and savings, then access to affordable credit, insurance and pensions, delivered with adequate protection for the user.

### Schemes that built access and credit

The Economic Survey 2025-26 notes that account ownership among adults more than doubled from 35% in 2011 to 89% in 2021, with narrower gaps between rich and poor and between men and women. PMJDY, started in 2014, provided the base, with 55.02 crore accounts by March 2025. Credit schemes built on it: Stand-Up India loans of ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore for SC, ST and women entrepreneurs; PM SVANidhi collateral-free working capital for street vendors; and PMMY, whose tiers let borrowers graduate from Shishu to larger Kishore, Tarun and Tarun Plus loans. Over a decade, Shishu's share of PMMY accounts fell from 92% to 63%, indicating borrowers moving up.

### Digital rails and measuring inclusion

The Survey describes digital public infrastructure as the second driver. The account aggregator framework passes verified bank and GST data to lenders, and UPI creates transaction trails that help assess first-time borrowers; research it cites finds credit expanded without higher default rates. Field surveys show UPI used across many purposes, yet over 90% of users still use cash, and access frictions mirror wider digital divides. Progress is tracked by the RBI's Financial Inclusion Index, built from 97 indicators covering banking, investment, insurance, postal and pension services across access, usage and quality sub-indices. Its composite value climbed from 64.2 in March 2024 to 67.0 in March 2025.

### Worked example

Answer frame (150 words): define inclusion as access → usage → quality. Evidence: account ownership 35% → 89% (2011–2021); PMJDY 55.02 crore accounts (March 2025); FI Index 64.2 → 67.0 (March 2024 → March 2025). Mechanism: UPI and account aggregators create data trails that unlock credit (PMMY graduation). Trade-offs: digital divide, cash dependence, need for consumer protection. Conclusion: shift focus from opening accounts to deepening use and credit.

### Recall

- - **q:** What are the three dimensions of the RBI's FI Index?
  - **a:** Access, usage and quality.
- - **q:** Name the four PMMY loan tiers.
  - **a:** Shishu, Kishore, Tarun and Tarun Plus.
- - **q:** How many PMJDY accounts existed by March 2025?
  - **a:** 55.02 crore (Economic Survey 2025-26).

### Common trap

Equating account ownership with inclusion: the FI Index explicitly weighs usage and quality, and the Survey stresses that effective use of accounts, not their number, determines outcomes.
