## GS-284: IPCC reporting basics

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Climate and treaties

*Goal:* Explain IPCC reporting basics and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### What the IPCC is and how it works

Created in 1988 by the World Meteorological Organization and the UN Environment Programme, the IPCC is the international body that assesses climate-change science so that policymakers receive regular assessments of its scientific basis, impacts, future risks, and adaptation and mitigation options. These assessments underpin negotiations under the UNFCCC, but they are policy-relevant rather than policy-prescriptive: they set out projections, risks and implications of choices without telling governments what to do. Membership is open to all WMO and UN member countries and stood at 195. Member-state representatives meet in plenary to take major decisions, and an elected Bureau advises on scientific and management matters. The IPCC does no research of its own; volunteer lead authors assess published literature through repeated drafting and expert review.

### Working groups and assessment cycles

Authors are organised in Working Group I on the physical science basis, Working Group II on impacts, adaptation and vulnerability, and Working Group III on mitigation, alongside a Task Force on National Greenhouse Gas Inventories. An Assessment Report usually has one volume per working group plus a Synthesis Report; Special Reports take up a single issue, and Methodology Reports guide inventory preparation under the UNFCCC. In the sixth cycle (AR6), the Working Group I report built on the 2013 AR5 contribution and three Special Reports of 2018-2019 on 1.5°C warming, land, and the ocean and cryosphere. MoEFCC notes that the Working Group III report in Climate Change 2022 acknowledged Mission LiFE, which India launched on 20 October 2022.

### From assessment to planning and reporting

IPCC scenarios reach sub-national planning. Lakshadweep's draft State Action Plan on Climate Change modelled futures under all IPCC Representative Concentration Pathway scenarios up to 2100 because the islands are a small, exposed system, and set priorities such as freshwater security, coastal ecosystem protection and disaster risk reduction. National reporting runs on a separate track: India released its Third National Communication to the UNFCCC on 9 December 2023, with energy at 75.81% of anthropogenic emissions. For biodiversity, governments set up IPBES in April 2012 as the counterpart of the IPCC at the science-policy interface.
