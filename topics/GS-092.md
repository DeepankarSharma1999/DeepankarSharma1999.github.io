## GS-092: UNESCO heritage and intangible heritage

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS1 · Culture · Indian art, literature and architecture

*Goal:* Explain UNESCO heritage and intangible heritage and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### World Heritage Sites: places

The World Heritage List records places of outstanding value. PIB's August 2024 annexure gives India 43 entries with state and inscription year. The first batch in 1983 was Agra Fort, Ajanta, Ellora and the Taj Mahal; Mahabalipuram and Konark followed in 1984. National parks and natural areas include Kaziranga, Keoladeo and Manas (1985), Sundarbans (1987), Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers, the Western Ghats (2012), the Great Himalayan National Park (2014) and Khangchendzonga (2016). Some entries are serial, such as the Mountain Railways (Darjeeling 1999, Nilgiri 2005, Kalka-Shimla 2008) and the six Hill Forts of Rajasthan (2013). Recent additions are Dholavira and Ramappa (2021), Santiniketan and the Hoysala temples (2023) and Moidams (2024).

### Intangible heritage: living practices

Intangible heritage means traditions handed down and still practised: oral expressions, performing arts, rituals, festivals, and knowledge and craft skills. Under the 2003 Convention an intergovernmental committee meets yearly to examine nominations. The Ministry of Culture's 2024 backgrounder counts 15 Indian elements, all on the Representative List: Vedic chanting, Ramlila and Kutiyattam (2008); Ramman of Garhwal (2009); Chhau, Kalbelia and Mudiyettu (2010); Buddhist chanting of Ladakh (2012); Manipur's Sankirtana (2013); the Thathera brass and copper craft of Jandiala Guru (2014); Yoga and Nowruz (2016); Kumbh Mela (2017); Durga Puja in Kolkata (2021); and Garba (2023). In India, Sangeet Natak Akademi is the nodal agency, preparing dossiers on the advice of its expert body ABICHU.

### Safeguarding and other UNESCO programmes

Recognition starts a safeguarding effort rather than ending it. SNA's Kutiyattam project ran for about a decade before the 2001 proclamation, and its Kutiyattam Kendra (2007) supports Kerala institutions such as Margi and the Ammannur Chachu Chakyar gurukulam in training, performance and documentation. Two other UNESCO systems should be kept apart. The Creative Cities Network, begun in 2004, links cities using creativity for sustainable urban development; SNA proposed Mumbai and Kochi. Biosphere reserves belong to the Man and the Biosphere programme of 1971, with core, buffer and transition zones; the Environment Ministry is nodal, and its 2025-26 report records Cold Desert's inclusion, taking India to 13 reserves in the World Network.
