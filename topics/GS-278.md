## GS-278: Forests, afforestation and REDD+

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain Forests, afforestation and REDD+ and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### What REDD+ covers and why India engages

ICFRE's 2018 report expands REDD+ as reducing emissions from deforestation and forest degradation together with conservation, sustainable forest management and enhancement of forest carbon stocks in developing countries. India argues it has been a REDD+ frontrunner in UNFCCC talks because conservation-oriented policies made its forests a net CO2 sink. It has filed a National Forest Reference Level and released a National REDD+ Strategy in 2018, aiming to attract results-based finance. The strategy supports the NDC goal of an additional 2.5 to 3 billion tonnes of CO2 sink through extra forest and tree cover by 2030, and recognises the role of local communities and tribal people.

### Delivering REDD+ through forest management

Working plans are the main instrument of scientific forest management, and the National Working Plan Code, 2014 lists REDD+ implementation among its objectives, treating it as a division-level mechanism for sustainable management and carbon stock enhancement. About 30% of forest area is managed through over 1,18,213 Joint Forest Management Committees, which offer a route to integrate REDD+ with community forestry. Pilot work in Mizoram showed that analysing drivers of deforestation can yield strategies that also raise community incomes. REDD+ is expected to strengthen safeguards through stakeholder participation, but needs tested benefit-sharing and MRV systems for carbon benefits.

### Measuring forests and the carbon sink

The Forest Survey of India maps forest cover wall-to-wall every two years by interpreting satellite imagery; 18 cycles have been completed from 1987 to 2023, and work for ISFR 2025 is under way. ISFR 2023 recorded a gain of 156.41 sq km in forest cover and 1,289.40 sq km in tree cover over ISFR 2021, a combined increase of 1,445.81 sq km. On the climate side, the same assessment puts total forest and tree carbon stock at 30.43 billion tonnes of CO2 equivalent, implying an additional sink of 2.29 billion tonnes relative to 2005, which the Ministry presents as substantial progress towards the 2.5-3.0 billion tonne NDC target.
