## GS-153: Communalism and social harmony

Mains GS1 + Prelims GS1 · Society · Indian society

*Goal:* Explain Communalism and social harmony and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Defining communalism precisely

In English, 'communal' simply refers to a community as opposed to an individual. In South Asian usage, communalism means aggressive chauvinism rooted in religious identity: one's own group is seen as the only legitimate one and others as inferior or hostile. NCERT emphasises that it is a political ideology, not a religious practice; the defining feature is a political identity built on religion and a readiness to condemn or attack those outside it. Communalism also claims that religion trumps everything else, so that rich and poor, different castes and different political views within a faith are treated as a single homogeneous bloc. This flattening of internal diversity is central to how communal mobilisation works.

### History: conflict and coexistence

India has experienced communal riots since before Independence, partly encouraged by the colonial policy of divide and rule. NCERT adds two balancing points: inter-community conflicts also existed before colonial rule, and colonialism cannot be blamed for riots after 1947. Equally, India has a long tradition of pluralism ranging from peaceful coexistence to syncretism, visible in Bhakti and Sufi devotional songs; Kabir's verses rejecting the idea that God is confined to temples, mosques, pilgrimages or idols are a lasting symbol of this blend. The Class 11 Political Theory text lists cases of targeted violence and displacement against Sikhs in 1984, Kashmiri Pandits in the Kashmir valley and victims of the 2002 post-Godhra riots, observing that each denied citizens basic freedoms on account of religious identity.

### Types of domination and the path to harmony

Political Theory frames the problem as religious domination in two forms. Inter-religious domination occurs when members of one community are victimised because of their faith. Intra-religious domination occurs within a religion, as when Dalits have been barred from Hindu temples, women are excluded from places of worship or dissenting sects are persecuted. Secularism opposes both and is not hostile to religion as such. Education and individual acts of solidarity, such as members of one faith protecting another during a riot, help reduce prejudice, but NCERT argues they cannot eliminate discrimination alone. Because modern states wield enormous power, how the state behaves is decisive: it must not be run by religious authorities and must protect all citizens' freedom and dignity equally to sustain social harmony.
