## GS-235: Planning and NITI Aayog

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Growth, public finance and infrastructure

*Goal:* Explain Planning and NITI Aayog and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Why India planned

After independence, leaders sought a middle path between capitalism and Soviet-style socialism: a strong public sector alongside private property and democracy. A plan, in NCERT's description, specifies how national resources will be used to reach stated goals within a fixed period; India borrowed the five-year format from the Soviet Union. The Planning Commission, chaired by the Prime Minister, began work in 1950. Indian plans did not try to fix output of every good, as the Soviet Union had attempted; they concentrated on sectors where the state played a commanding role, such as power and irrigation. The Second Plan, shaped by P.C. Mahalanobis, is regarded as the start of planning in its full sense.

### Four goals and their tensions

Growth means raising the economy's capacity to produce, tracked by a steady rise in GDP. Modernisation covers new technology but also social change, such as women working outside the home. Self-reliance, stressed in the first seven plans, meant avoiding imports of goods India could produce, especially food, to protect sovereignty. Equity requires that the poor share in prosperity, with basic needs met and wealth inequality reduced. NCERT warns that the goals can collide: labour-saving technology may advance modernisation while hurting employment. Because resources are limited, each plan had to choose which goal to emphasise. This framework is useful for evaluating any development strategy, including present ones.

### NITI Aayog: from plan to think tank

The 2015 resolution argued that one-size-fits-all central planning could create needless friction because States want a decisive say in growth strategy. It therefore asked for an institution serving as the government's think tank, offering strategic and technical advice to both the Centre and the States, and replacing the earlier one-way policy flow with continuing partnership. NITI Aayog's mandate includes a shared national development vision, cooperative federalism, village-level planning aggregated upward, attention to groups at risk of being left behind, and monitoring of programmes. Its Governing Council brings together all Chief Ministers and Lt. Governors under the Prime Minister. For Mains, weigh whether an advisory, partnership-based think tank coordinates the Centre and States better than the Planning Commission's five-year plans did.

### Worked example

Compare in a table: Planning Commission (1950 Cabinet resolution; centre-to-state plans; five-year plans with growth, modernisation, self-reliance, equity) versus NITI Aayog (1 January 2015 resolution; think tank; Governing Council of all CMs/LGs; Regional Councils; village plans aggregated upward; monitoring and evaluation). Conclude with one strength and one limitation of each.

### Recall

- - **q:** When and how was the Planning Commission set up?
  - **a:** 15 March 1950, by a Cabinet resolution, with the Prime Minister as Chairperson.
- - **q:** Who sits on NITI Aayog's Governing Council?
  - **a:** The Chief Ministers of all States and Lt. Governors of Union Territories, chaired by the Prime Minister.
- - **q:** Name the four goals of India's five-year plans.
  - **a:** Growth, modernisation, self-reliance and equity.

### Common trap

Calling NITI Aayog (or the Planning Commission) a constitutional or statutory body: both were created by executive Cabinet resolutions.
