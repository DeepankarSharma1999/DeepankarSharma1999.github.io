## GS-277: Wetlands, mangroves, coral reefs

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain Wetlands, mangroves, coral reefs and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Wetland diversity and mangrove ecology

India has a wide variety of wetland habitats, of which roughly 70 per cent is paddy land, with a total wetland area of 3.9 million hectares in the NCERT account. The eight categories range from Deccan reservoirs and southern west-coast lagoons, the saline flats of Rajasthan, Gujarat and the Gulf of Kachchh, and east-coast deltas such as Chilika, to Gangetic marshes, Brahmaputra floodplains, Kashmir and Ladakh lakes, and the mangroves of the Andaman and Nicobar arc. Mangroves grow in salt marshes, tidal creeks, mudflats and estuaries, are made of salt-tolerant species, and are under encroachment pressure, which makes conservation necessary.

### Ramsar record and the mangrove-coral scheme

MoEFCC's Wetlands Division runs the National Plan for Conservation of Aquatic Ecosystems and Ramsar obligations. India added eleven sites in 2025, moving from 85 to 96, and reached 98 in January 2026. At COP15 in Victoria Falls (July 2025) India's resolution on sustainable lifestyles for wise use of wetlands was adopted, and Indore and Udaipur became India's first accredited Wetland Cities. Separately, a central sector scheme for conservation and management of mangroves and coral reefs funds coastal state forest departments in a 60:40 ratio for eco-restoration, awareness and livelihoods. It identifies 38 mangrove sites, among them the Sunderbans, Bhitarkanika, Coringa and Pichavaram, and four coral reef sites: the Gulf of Kachchh, Gulf of Mannar, Andaman and Nicobar, and Lakshadweep.

### Coral reef ecology and bleaching

NCERT describes a coral as a tiny marine polyp that secretes calcium and lives in colonies, chiefly in warm, shallow seawater; these colonies build reefs. The IPCC glossary describes a reef as an underwater ecosystem framed by structure-building stony corals. Warm-water reefs lie in shallow, mostly tropical seas, and their coral animals contain algae that need light and fairly steady temperatures; cold-water reefs are found worldwide, usually 50-500 m deep. Living corals often grow on older dead calcium carbonate. Bleaching is the loss of coral colour when the symbiotic algae within coral cells, called zooxanthellae, or their pigments are lost. Both reef types support rich fish and other biodiversity and are rated especially vulnerable to climate change. An atoll is a low tropical island of coral reefs encircling a central depression, often a lagoon.

### Worked example

Prelims check: 'India has the largest number of Ramsar sites in the world.' False: the 2025-26 report places India first in Asia and third globally (98 sites by January 2026). Mains frame: ecosystem services, legal tools (Ramsar, Wetlands Rules 2017, mangrove-coral scheme), recent gains (Wetland Cities), and threats such as encroachment.

### Recall

- - **q:** Which two Indian cities were first accredited as Ramsar Wetland Cities?
  - **a:** Indore and Udaipur.
- - **q:** What is coral bleaching?
  - **a:** Loss of coral pigmentation through loss of the intracellular symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae) and/or their pigments.
- - **q:** Name India's four coral reef sites under the central scheme.
  - **a:** Gulf of Kachchh, Gulf of Mannar, Andaman and Nicobar, and Lakshadweep.
- - **q:** What share of world mangroves does India have according to NCERT?
  - **a:** About 7 per cent (4,992 sq km).

### Common trap

Assuming Ramsar designation is a ban on use; the Convention promotes conservation and 'wise use', and India's own COP15 resolution was on sustainable lifestyles for wise use of wetlands.
