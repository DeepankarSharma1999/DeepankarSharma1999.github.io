## GS-257: Animal husbandry and rural livelihoods

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Agriculture and food

*Goal:* Explain Animal husbandry and rural livelihoods and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Why animal husbandry matters

Indian farming households commonly practise mixed crop–livestock farming, keeping cattle, goats and fowl. Livestock gives more stable income, food security, transport, fuel and nutrition without disrupting food crops. NCERT counts over 70 million small and marginal farmers and landless labourers who depend on livestock for alternate livelihoods, with many women employed too. Diversification matters because kharif dominates farm employment, and in poorly irrigated areas rabi-season work is scarce; allied activities fill this gap and reduce the risk of relying only on crops.

### Dairy and Operation Flood

Milk output rose about twelve-fold between 1951 and 2021, which NCERT attributes mainly to Operation Flood, also called the White Revolution in Class 10. Under this model, farmers pool milk, which is graded by quality, processed and marketed to cities through cooperatives, assuring a fair price. Gujarat is the model state, emulated by others; major milk-producing states include Gujarat, MP, UP, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Punjab and Rajasthan. Meat, eggs and wool are emerging diversification avenues. The 2019 livestock profile shows poultry with 61 per cent share and about 303 million cattle including 110 million buffaloes.

### Fisheries and constraints

Inland fisheries supply about three-quarters of fish production value and marine fisheries the rest; fish output is about 1.5 per cent of GDP. Leading states include West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala, Gujarat, Maharashtra and TN. Fishing communities face underemployment, low earnings, illiteracy and indebtedness; women make up about 60 per cent of export-marketing and 40 per cent of internal-marketing workforce, and need credit through cooperatives and SHGs. Over-fishing and pollution need regulation. For livestock, NCERT prescribes better breeds, improved veterinary care and credit for small farmers and landless labourers.

### Worked example

Mains frame — 'Allied activities as income stabilisers': risk diversification (seasonal unemployment), dairy success via cooperatives (12-fold milk rise 1951–2021), fisheries (inland 75%), women's employment, constraints (low productivity, veterinary care, credit), way forward.

### Recall

- - **q:** Which livestock category has the largest share in India per NCERT?
  - **a:** Poultry, at 61 per cent.
- - **q:** What explains the twelve-fold rise in milk output?
  - **a:** Mainly Operation Flood's cooperative pooling, grading, processing and marketing.
- - **q:** Inland vs marine share of fish production value?
  - **a:** About 75 per cent inland, 25 per cent marine.

### Common trap

Assuming large livestock numbers mean high productivity: NCERT notes that livestock productivity is low compared with other countries and needs better breeds, veterinary care and credit.
