## GS-344: Hazard, disaster, vulnerability and resilience

Mains GS3 · Disaster Management · Risk management

*Goal:* Distinguish a hazard from the social conditions that turn it into disaster.

### Four connected concepts

A hazard is a potentially damaging event or process, such as an earthquake or flood. Exposure places people and assets in its path. Vulnerability describes their susceptibility to harm; capacity describes resources and abilities that reduce loss. Disaster risk therefore depends on more than the physical event. Similar rainfall can produce very different outcomes in two settlements.

### Resilience is a process

Resilience includes the ability to withstand, adapt to and recover from disruption while protecting essential functions. Strong buildings, diversified livelihoods, accessible warnings and trusted local institutions contribute in different ways. A simple risk expression multiplying hazard, exposure and vulnerability is a learning aid, not a universal numerical law. Explain the causal pathway rather than relying on a formula alone.

### Worked example

Heavy rain affects two villages. One has homes in a floodplain and no evacuation route; the other has raised shelters and practiced warnings. The hazard is similar, but exposure, vulnerability and capacity change the losses.

### Recall

- - **q:** Can a hazard occur without becoming a disaster?
  - **a:** Yes, if exposure and vulnerability are sufficiently low or capacity prevents severe disruption.
- - **q:** Does resilience mean returning to every old practice?
  - **a:** No. Recovery may require adapting to reduce future risk.

### Common trap

Disaster loss is not determined by natural forces alone.
