## GS-291: Renewable energy and energy transition

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Climate and treaties

*Goal:* Explain Renewable energy and energy transition and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Resource base and capacity milestones

Peak clear-sky insolation at the ground is about 1 kW per square metre, and India's tropical and sub-tropical location gives abundant sunshine. Solar capacity rose from roughly 2.63 GW in March 2014 to about 135.80 GW in December 2025, and India ranks fourth in the world for renewable installed capacity, fourth for wind and third for solar. Non-fossil sources made up 51.93% of installed capacity in December 2025, against about 30% in 2014; the first NDC's 40% target had already been exceeded, with the share at 43.82% in October 2023, and the updated NDC's 50% goal in June 2025. During 2025-26, up to 31 December 2025, 38.61 GW of renewable capacity was added, including 30.16 GW of solar and 4.47 GW of wind.

### Technologies and policy instruments

The Economic Survey 2025-26 groups India's clean-energy tools by technology. In solar, more than 9.75 lakh standalone pumps were installed under PM-KUSUM Component B by December 2025, PM Surya Ghar had added 8 GW of rooftop capacity, 55 solar parks with 39,973 MW sanctioned had been approved, and a PLI scheme backs high-efficiency PV modules. Wind capacity stood at 54.51 GW in December 2025, fourth highest in the world, with viability gap funding for offshore wind. The National Green Hydrogen Mission aims at 5 MMT of green hydrogen a year by 2030 and has designated hubs at the Deendayal, V.O. Chidambaranar and Paradip ports. The Nuclear Energy Mission targets 100 GWe by 2047, including small modular reactors, and the bio-energy programme covers biomass, biogas and waste-to-energy.

### Storage, grid integration and the climate frame

Solar and wind are variable and material-intensive, so storage is a central constraint. The Central Electricity Authority estimates that India will need about 336 GWh of storage by 2029-30 and 411 GWh by 2031-32. Support includes waiving inter-state transmission charges for co-located battery and pumped storage projects until June 2028, two viability gap funding schemes for about 43 GWh of battery storage, an ₹18,100 crore PLI for 50 GWh of advanced chemistry cells, and an advisory to co-locate storage equal to at least 10% of solar capacity. India frames the transition through equity and common but differentiated responsibilities: the updated NDC's non-fossil goal is linked to technology transfer and low-cost international finance. Because targets use installed capacity, actual generation must be tracked separately.

### Worked example

Statement: 'India has achieved 50% of electricity generation from renewable sources.' Incorrect on two counts: the milestone concerns installed capacity, and it counts all non-fossil sources including nuclear and hydro. Correct version: non-fossil sources reached 50% of installed capacity in June 2025.

### Recall

- - **q:** What was the non-fossil share of installed capacity in December 2025?
  - **a:** 51.93%.
- - **q:** What is the green hydrogen production target of the National Green Hydrogen Mission?
  - **a:** 5 MMT of green hydrogen per year by 2030.
- - **q:** How much energy storage does CEA estimate India needs by 2031-32?
  - **a:** About 411 GWh (336 GWh by 2029-30).
- - **q:** What is India's global rank in solar installed capacity?
  - **a:** Third.

### Common trap

Equating 'non-fossil' with 'renewable': non-fossil capacity also includes nuclear and hydro.
