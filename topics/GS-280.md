## GS-280: Climate change evidence and impacts

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Climate and treaties

*Goal:* Explain Climate change evidence and impacts and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Natural variability in the record

Geological and historical sources show that climate change is continuous. Glacial landforms, lake sediments and tree rings record warm, cold, wet and dry phases. Archaeology suggests the Rajasthan desert was wet and cool around 8,000 BC, and Europe had a Little Ice Age from 1550 to about 1850. NCERT groups causes into astronomical ones, such as sunspot cycles and Milankovitch changes in orbit, wobble and axial tilt, and terrestrial ones such as volcanic eruptions that load the air with aerosols and briefly cool the planet. The most important human influence is the rising concentration of greenhouse gases.

### Instrumental evidence of warming

The average near-surface air temperature of the world is about 14°C. A warming trend was clear in the 20th century, concentrated in 1901-44 and 1977-99, each adding about 0.4°C, with slight cooling between. By 2000 the global mean stood about 0.6°C above the late 19th century value. NCERT notes that sea level can rise through melting glaciers and ice caps and through thermal expansion of seawater, threatening coasts and islands. It also warns that once warming sets in, reversing it is difficult and the effects will not be uniform across regions.

### Impacts through Earth's cycles

A warmer atmosphere holds more moisture, producing heavier rain in some places, such as intensified monsoons, and drought elsewhere. Glacier melt first adds river flow and eventually raises sea level, threatening cities like Mumbai and Chennai. Intense bursts of rain increase run-off and erosion and reduce groundwater recharge. In the carbon cycle, burning fossil fuels returns long-stored carbon to the air quickly; CO2 rose about 35% since 1960. Extra CO2 also acidifies seawater, harming plankton and corals, and a warmer ocean becomes a weaker sink. Deforestation reduces transpiration and local rainfall and alters surface albedo.
