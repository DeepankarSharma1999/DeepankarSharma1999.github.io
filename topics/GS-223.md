## GS-223: CRR, SLR and financial stability

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Basic macro and monetary

*Goal:* Explain CRR, SLR and financial stability and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Reserve requirements as a brake on credit

Banks can expand deposits by lending, so a legal floor on reserves prevents runaway credit. NCERT defines the cash reserve ratio as the percentage of deposits a bank must keep as reserves, and notes a separate requirement to hold liquid assets for the short term — the statutory liquidity ratio. Take a 20% reserve ratio: a bank with Rs 100 of cash can keep lending until deposits reach Rs 500, at which point Rs 100 just meets the requirement. If the RBI raises the ratio to 25%, the same reserves support only Rs 400 of deposits, so loans must shrink. Changing the reserve ratio is therefore a direct quantitative lever on money supply.

### Using CRR in both directions

The Economic Survey 2022-23 shows CRR used for tightening: when inflation rose in 2022, the RBI raised the CRR by 50 bps in May alongside a 40-bp increase in policy rates, pulling about ₹87,000 crore of primary liquidity out of banks. The Economic Survey 2025-26 shows the reverse. With inflation low, the RBI announced a 100-bp CRR cut to 3.0% of net demand and time liabilities, implemented in stages from September to November 2025, and expected to release roughly ₹2.5 lakh crore. Including an earlier move, CRR fell by a cumulative 125 bps from December 2024 to November 2025, reinforcing the 100-bp repo cut.

### Reading the data after a CRR change

A CRR cut lowers the balances banks must keep at the RBI, and those balances are part of reserve money. So in the Survey's data, M0 growth slowed to 2.9% by 31 December 2025 compared with 4.9% a year earlier, even though policy was easing. Adjusted for the first-round effect of the CRR cut, M0 growth was actually 9.4%, up from 6.2%. Meanwhile broad money growth rose to 12.1%, which the Survey reads as banks using the released liquidity to lend. For exam answers, link CRR to both liquidity (immediate funds) and stability (a reserve buffer), and treat SLR as a separate requirement to hold liquid assets for the short term.
