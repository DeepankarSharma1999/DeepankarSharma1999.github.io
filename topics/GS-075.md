## GS-075: Capitalism, socialism and their social impact

Mains GS1 · World History · 18th–20th century

*Goal:* Explain Capitalism, socialism and their social impact and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Two answers to the same three questions

Every society must decide what goods to produce, whether to use more labour or more machinery, and how output should be shared. A market or capitalist economy answers through supply and demand: profitable goods get made, cheaper inputs are preferred, and goods go to those who can pay. A socialist economy lets the government decide based on social need, giving little weight to individual consumer wishes, and aims to distribute according to need — for example, free health care for all. In strict socialism the state owns everything. Mixed economies, the most common type, let markets supply what they do well and the state provide essentials markets neglect.

### Social impact: the Soviet case and its unwinding

The USSR attempted to abolish private property and build an equal society. It guaranteed minimum living standards, subsidised welfare and created a large industrial and energy base, but its single party controlled every institution, free speech was absent, and consumer goods lagged the West. After 1991, shock therapy imposed rapid privatisation, free trade and the replacement of collective farms with private farming. The results were severe: industries sold off cheaply in what was called 'the largest garage sale in history', inflation ran so high that people lost their savings, food security collapsed, subsidies were withdrawn, a mafia gained economic control, and the old relative equality gave way to wide inequality between people and regions.

### India's choice and exam angles

At independence India saw two models: liberal capitalism in Europe and the US, and Soviet socialism. Many leaders — communists, socialists and Nehru within the Congress — admired Soviet planning, and few backed American-style capitalism. Yet Nehru rejected total state ownership as incompatible with democracy, choosing a socialist-leaning mixed economy with a strong public sector, private property and planning. Political vocabulary followed: 'Left' favours state control and regulation; 'Right' trusts free competition and limited intervention. Prelims: purchasing power versus need-based distribution; definition of mixed economy. Mains: compare welfare gains and freedom costs under Soviet socialism, the market's neglect of the poor, and the social costs of abrupt transition.
