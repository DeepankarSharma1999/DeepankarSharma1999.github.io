## GS-154: Regionalism, federal cohesion

Mains GS1 + Prelims GS1 · Society · Indian society

*Goal:* Explain Regionalism, federal cohesion and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Roots of regional sentiment

Regionalism draws on India's diverse languages, cultures, tribes and religions, but diversity alone is not enough. It becomes politically potent when these markers are concentrated in particular territories and when people feel their region has been neglected or deprived. At Independence, India inherited large multilingual presidencies such as Madras, Bombay and Calcutta. Strong popular agitations soon forced their reorganisation into ethno-linguistic States. Later, in 2000, statehood for Chhattisgarh, Uttaranchal and Jharkhand rested on a mix of tribal identity, language, regional deprivation and ecology rather than language alone. NCERT's activity on State origins encourages classifying States by the criteria used for their formation and notes ongoing demands such as the Vidarbha movement, showing that regional claims continue to evolve.

### Federal institutions that accommodate

Creating States is not enough; they must be viable, relatively autonomous units. India achieves this through constitutional lists that assign some subjects exclusively to the Union, some to the States and others to both through the Concurrent List. State legislatures elect the Rajya Sabha, giving regions a voice in Parliament. Fiscal sharing is handled by a Finance Commission constituted every five years to divide tax revenues between the Centre and States. Until 2017, each Five Year Plan included detailed State Plans prepared by State Planning Commissions, and today the GST Council includes State members. In NCERT's account these provisions keep States viable as relatively autonomous units, so regional aspirations can be negotiated within the federal structure.

### Tribal regionalism: Jharkhand and the North-East

The Social Movements chapter shows how regional identity forms. In south Bihar, missionary-spread literacy let adivasis document their history and customs, building a shared Jharkhandi consciousness; educated adivasis entering government jobs formed a middle-class leadership that lobbied for a separate State. Common resentment against dikus, outsider traders and moneylenders who gained from mining and industry while adivasi land was alienated, fuelled collective action. Issues included land acquisition for irrigation projects and firing ranges and nationalisation of forest produce. In the North-East, hill tribes conscious of distinct identity were uneasy about incorporation into Assam's administration; NCERT, citing Nongbri, notes an earlier secessionist tendency has shifted toward seeking autonomy within the Constitution. Forest and land alienation link tribal movements across regions.

### Worked example

Q: 'Regionalism is a threat to national integration.' Critically analyse. Frame: define regionalism and its roots (concentrated identity + deprivation); examples (linguistic reorganisation; 2000 States; Jharkhand's anti-diku mobilisation; North-East shift from secession to autonomy); federal accommodation (lists, Rajya Sabha, Finance Commission, GST Council); risks (inter-State disputes); conclude that accommodation converts regional assertion into constitutional bargaining.

### Recall

- - **q:** Name two factors NCERT says turn diversity into regionalism.
  - **a:** Geographical concentration of identity markers and a sense of regional deprivation.
- - **q:** Which body decides Centre–State tax sharing and how often is it set up?
  - **a:** The Finance Commission, every five years.
- - **q:** Who were the 'dikus' in the Jharkhand movement?
  - **a:** Migrant traders and moneylenders seen as outsiders who grabbed the region's wealth.

### Common trap

Treating all regionalism as separatism. NCERT presents federalism as the means of accommodating regional sentiment, argues that linguistic States strengthened rather than weakened unity, and records the North-East's shift from secessionist tendencies to seeking autonomy within the Constitution.
