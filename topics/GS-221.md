## GS-221: Repo, reverse repo, SDF and liquidity concepts

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Basic macro and monetary

*Goal:* Explain Repo, reverse repo, SDF and liquidity concepts and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Repo and reverse repo as temporary OMOs

Open market operations change bank reserves by buying or selling government securities. Outright purchases or sales are permanent. A repurchase agreement is different: the RBI buys securities now and the contract fixes when and at what price they will be sold back, so the cash injection is temporary and the implicit interest is the repo rate. The mirror operation, a reverse repo, has the RBI selling securities with a promise to buy them back, temporarily draining money at the reverse repo rate. NCERT notes that these operations, at overnight, 7-day, 14-day and other tenors, have become the RBI's main policy instrument, alongside the bank rate at which it lends to banks.

### The 2022 corridor redesign

When inflation crossed the upper tolerance limit in 2022, the RBI began tightening. At its April 2022 meeting the MPC introduced the Standing Deposit Facility at 3.75%. Banks could now deposit surplus funds with the RBI without the RBI having to hand over government securities as collateral, making absorption collateral-free; the window is overnight, though longer tenors can be used if needed. The SDF replaced the fixed reverse repo rate as the floor of the liquidity adjustment facility corridor. With the Marginal Standing Facility kept 25 bps above repo, the corridor became symmetric and 50 bps wide. Between May and December 2022, repo, SDF, MSF and bank rate each rose 225 bps.

### Reading liquidity in 2025-26

Net LAF shows whether banks are net borrowers from or depositors with the RBI. The Economic Survey 2025-26 reports surplus liquidity: net LAF averaged about ₹1.89 lakh crore in surplus in FY26 up to 8 January 2026, versus ₹1,605 crore in FY25. Consistent with this, banks borrowed less under the MSF and parked more under the SDF, whose average deployment rose to ₹1.74 lakh crore. The surplus came partly from OMO purchases worth ₹2.39 lakh crore in April–May 2025 and further OMOs plus a dollar–rupee swap in December. With ample liquidity, the call rate stayed slightly below the repo rate, helping the 2025 rate cuts reach lending rates.

### Worked example

Assuming the symmetric 50-bp corridor described in ES 2022-23 still applies, and a repo rate of 5.25% (December 2025, ES 2025-26): MSF = 5.25 + 0.25 = 5.50% (ceiling) and SDF = 5.25 − 0.25 = 5.00% (floor). A call rate of 5.17% lies inside the corridor, 8 bps below repo — the FY26 average gap the Survey reports.

### Recall

- - **q:** When and at what rate was the SDF introduced?
  - **a:** April 2022, at 3.75%.
- - **q:** Which rate forms the floor of the LAF corridor now?
  - **a:** The SDF rate (it replaced the reverse repo rate).
- - **q:** What does a surplus in net LAF indicate?
  - **a:** Banks are on net placing funds with the RBI, i.e. system liquidity is in surplus.

### Common trap

Calling the reverse repo rate the floor of today's corridor: since April 2022 the SDF rate is the floor, and SDF needs no collateral, unlike reverse repo.
