## GS-275: Pollution: water, soil, plastic and noise

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain Pollution: water, soil, plastic and noise and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Water: measuring and ranking river pollution

The national water quality network spans rivers, lakes, ponds, tanks and groundwater, including 2260 river locations and 1271 groundwater points. River Ganga is watched in real time at 40 stations for parameters such as pH, BOD, DO, nitrate and turbidity. Bathing suitability uses five primary criteria, among them BOD not above 3 mg/L and dissolved oxygen of at least 5 mg/L. Locations exceeding the BOD limit are labelled polluted, and continuous runs of them become Polluted River Stretches graded into five priority classes from Priority V (3-6 mg/L) to Priority I (above 30 mg/L). The count fell from 351 stretches in 2018 to 311 in 2022 and 296 in 2025.

### Noise: zone-wise limits and sources

The Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000, made under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, express limits in dB(A) Leq, a time-averaged sound level weighted to match human hearing. Day and night limits are 75 and 70 for industrial areas, 65 and 55 for commercial areas, 55 and 45 for residential areas, and 50 and 40 in silence zones, with day counted from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. States must sort areas into these four categories, and at least 100 metres around hospitals, educational institutions and courts can be notified as a silence zone. A loudspeaker needs written permission from the authority. NCERT notes that traffic is the largest nuisance and that noise weakens with distance from its source. CPCB and state boards run 82 monitoring stations across ten metro cities.

### Land pollution and plastic

NCERT treats land pollution as its own category. Typical pollutants are pathogens from human and animal excreta, garbage and the vectors it breeds, residues of pesticides and fertilisers, alkalinity, fluorides and radioactive material; the causes are dumping of untreated industrial waste and heavy use of farm chemicals. Such chemicals can seep through soil to groundwater, and fertiliser nitrates reaching lakes feed algal blooms that strip oxygen, the process called eutrophication. For plastic, the Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016 underpin a ban on identified single-use items from 1 July 2022 and on carry bags below 120 microns from 31 December 2022, enforced by task forces in all 36 States/UTs. Extended Producer Responsibility holds producers, importers and brand owners accountable for packaging, with an obligation of about 8.3 million tonnes for 2024-25.

### Worked example

A river sample shows BOD 12 mg/L at two consecutive locations. It is a Polluted River Stretch in Priority III (10-20 mg/L). Answer frame for Mains: indicator, ranking system, State action plans reviewed by the River Rejuvenation Committee, and source control (sewage, industry, fertiliser run-off).

### Recall

- - **q:** What BOD level marks a river location as polluted?
  - **a:** BOD above 3 mg/L.
- - **q:** What are the day and night noise limits for a residential area?
  - **a:** 55 dB(A) Leq by day and 45 dB(A) Leq by night.
- - **q:** Name two pollutants NCERT lists under land pollution.
  - **a:** Any two of: pesticide and fertiliser residues, garbage, pathogens from excreta, fluorides, alkalinity, radioactive substances.
- - **q:** From when are carry bags below 120 microns prohibited?
  - **a:** 31 December 2022.

### Common trap

Reversing the BOD and DO criteria: a location is polluted when BOD exceeds 3 mg/L, whereas bathing quality needs DO of at least 5 mg/L.
