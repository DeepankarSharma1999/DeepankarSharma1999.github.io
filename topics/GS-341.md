## GS-341: UN system and multilateral organisations

Mains GS2 + Prelims GS1 · International Relations · India and world

*Goal:* Match major UN organs and agencies to their powers.

### Separate the principal organs

The UN has six principal organs: General Assembly, Security Council, Economic and Social Council, Trusteeship Council, International Court of Justice and Secretariat. The General Assembly provides universal member-state representation. The Security Council has primary responsibility for international peace and security. The International Court of Justice is a court for legal questions within its jurisdiction, not a global police force.

### The wider system has specialized roles

Programmes, funds and specialized agencies perform different tasks, such as health, children’s welfare or development. A recommendation, a legally binding obligation and an operational project are different outputs. Institutions depend on mandates, resources and state cooperation. For India, debates over representation and Security Council reform concern legitimacy as well as the ability to respond to contemporary problems.

### Worked example

An epidemic may involve WHO technical guidance, national health authorities and other agencies’ logistics. Calling every action a “UN order” obscures who has the power and responsibility to implement it.

### Recall

- - **q:** Which organ is the UN’s principal judicial organ?
  - **a:** The International Court of Justice.
- - **q:** Why distinguish organs from specialized agencies?
  - **a:** Their legal status, mandates and functions differ.

### Common trap

Do not confuse the ICJ with the International Criminal Court or assume every UN resolution has identical legal force.
