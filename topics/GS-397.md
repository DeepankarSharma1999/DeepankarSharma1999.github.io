## GS-397: Environmental Impact Assessment: stages and appraisal

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain Environmental Impact Assessment: stages and appraisal and use it to reason through the worked example.

### Concept and distinctions

Environmental Impact Assessment identifies likely project effects before a decision, using scope, baseline conditions, alternatives and mitigation. Appraisal examines whether evidence and safeguards are adequate. The process informs decision-making; it does not make every approved project harmless.

### How to apply it

Good baseline work captures seasonal variation and affected ecosystems, not only convenient survey dates. Cumulative impacts may arise from several projects even when each seems limited alone. Meaningful participation can reveal local dependence on water, forests or access routes. Approval conditions need measurable monitoring and enforceable responsibility. Distinguish predicted impacts from observed performance and revise management when evidence changes. Legal applicability and detailed procedures require the current official notification and project category.

### Worked example

A dry-season survey alone may miss a wetland's monsoon extent and underestimate a road's hydrological impact.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why does EIA need seasonal and cumulative analysis?
  - **a:** One-time or project-isolated surveys can miss important variation and combined effects.

### Common trap

Treating project clearance as certification of zero environmental harm.
