## GS-396: Technology missions in agriculture (oilseeds, pulses, horticulture)

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Agriculture and food

*Goal:* Explain Technology missions in agriculture (oilseeds, pulses, horticulture) and use it to reason through the worked example.

### Concept and distinctions

Agricultural technology missions coordinate research, inputs, extension and market support around a defined production problem. They address bottlenecks that a single intervention cannot solve. Missions concerning oilseeds, pulses or horticulture differ in crop biology, supply chains and market needs.

### How to apply it

Evaluate the mechanism rather than memorize a mission label. Better seed requires suitable agronomy, water, farmer knowledge and incentives; higher output also needs storage and marketing. Compare additional production, productivity, resilience and farmer returns, noting whether gains persist after support ends. A mission can improve domestic supply without eliminating every import, because demand and trade incentives also change. Verify current scheme names and provisions separately from this enduring design principle.

### Worked example

A pulse programme links improved varieties with extension and procurement access instead of distributing seed alone.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why can seed distribution alone fail to meet a technology mission's objective?
  - **a:** Water, agronomy, knowledge, incentives and marketing may remain binding constraints.

### Common trap

Assuming a mission's announcement proves its production targets were achieved.
