## GS-306: Technology indigenisation and applications

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Science · General science / technology

*Goal:* Assess technological self-reliance through capabilities rather than labels.

### Indigenisation has several layers

Domestic assembly, domestic design and domestic control over critical components are different achievements. Indigenisation develops the ability to understand, produce, maintain and improve technology within the country. A product assembled locally may still depend on imported chips, specialist materials, software or testing equipment. Map these dependencies before describing a system as fully self-reliant.

### Build a learning ecosystem

Research funding, skilled workers, patient procurement, standards and competitive suppliers turn prototypes into dependable products. Technology transfer can accelerate learning when recipients acquire know-how rather than only finished equipment. Complete isolation can raise cost and slow innovation; selective international collaboration can strengthen domestic capability. Evaluate reliability, lifecycle support, intellectual property and strategic supply risks together.

### Worked example

A domestically assembled medical device fails when an imported sensor becomes unavailable. Developing an interchangeable sensor, local calibration facilities and maintenance skills reduces vulnerability more effectively than changing the product label.

### Recall

- - **q:** Is local assembly the same as domestic design?
  - **a:** No. Design authority and component dependence may remain external.
- - **q:** Why examine lifecycle support?
  - **a:** Equipment needs spares, upgrades and repair after purchase.

### Common trap

Self-reliance means capability and resilience; it need not mean producing every input alone.
