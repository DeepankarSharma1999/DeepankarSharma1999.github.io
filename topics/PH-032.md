### Bridge: Energy, momentum, work and the units eV, MeV, MeV/c and u

Momentum p is mass times velocity; it is a vector and total momentum of an isolated system stays constant. Kinetic energy is the work done to bring a body from rest to its speed; work is force times displacement along the force, W = ∫ F dx. Joules are awkward for particles, so use the electron-volt: 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, the energy an electron gains across 1 volt. 1 MeV = 10⁶ eV. In relativity, energies are written in MeV, momenta as pc in MeV (so p is in MeV/c) and masses as m₀c² in MeV (mass in MeV/c²). Useful values: electron m₀c² = 0.511 MeV, proton 938.3 MeV, atomic mass unit 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c² (this uses c = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s; with c = 3 × 10⁸ it becomes about 933, or 933.75 if 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J are also used). Scientific notation: 9 × 10¹⁶ means 9 followed by 16 zeros. Always convert once, at the start, and keep one unit system through the problem.

### Bridge: Binomial expansion, substitution and integration by parts

Binomial expansion: (1 − x)^(−1/2) = 1 + x/2 + 3x²/8 + 5x³/16 + … for small x. With x = β² this gives γ ≈ 1 + β²/2, and γ − 1 ≈ β²/2 when β is small (PH-028-B1 uses it too). Substitution: to integrate v dv/√(1 − v²/c²), let s = 1 − v²/c²; then ds = −2v dv/c², so the integral becomes a simple power of s. Integration by parts: ∫ v dp = v p − ∫ p dv, which you can check by differentiating the right side. Differentiating an equation such as E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴ with respect to p, with m₀ constant, gives 2E dE/dp = 2pc². A calculus note: these are the only tools needed; every step is written out in the modules.

### 2. Core: relativistic momentum and variation of mass, derived

*Objective:* Derive m = γm₀ and p = γm₀v from conservation of momentum in a collision seen from two frames.

## Why Newton's momentum has to change

Keep one law: total momentum is conserved in every inertial frame. Upadhyaya §6.13 (PDF 351 to 353) shows that with p = m₀u the law holds in one frame of an elastic collision and fails when the same collision is seen from another frame; Mathur §3.7.7 (PDF 160 to 163) reaches the same point. The fix: let the mass depend on speed, p = m(u) u. The derivation below uses the transverse rule of PH-030 module 4.

## Set-up and assumptions

Two identical balls A and B, each of rest mass m₀. S′ moves at +v along +x of S, and the origins coincide at t = t′ = 0.

- A is at rest in S except for a small speed w along y. In S, A moves along the y axis: u_A = (0, +w).
- B is at rest in S′ except for a speed w along −y′: u′_B = (0, −w).
- They meet at the instant the origins coincide, collide elastically, and by mirror symmetry each reverses its y velocity in the frame where it moves only along y: A: +w → −w in S; B: −w → +w in S′.
- Assumed: momentum is m(u)·u with the mass depending only on the speed u, and momentum is conserved in S.

## Step 1: B as seen in S

From the inverse velocity rule (PH-030 module 4) with u′ₓ = 0: uₓ = v and u_y = u′_y/γ. So before the collision B is (v, −w/γ) and after it (v, +w/γ). The x velocities do not change in the collision, so x momentum is conserved trivially.

## Step 2: conservation of y momentum in S

Let m_A = m(w) and m_B = m(speed of B).

- Before: m_A w + m_B(−w/γ).
- After: m_A(−w) + m_B(+w/γ).

Setting before = after gives 2m_A w − 2m_B w/γ = 0, so

$$ m_B = γ m_A $$

## Step 3: let w → 0

A then moves very slowly, so m_A → m₀. B's speed in S tends to v. Hence

$$ m(v) = γ m₀ = m₀/√(1 − v²/c²),   p = m v = γ m₀ v $$

Both books give this result (Upadhyaya §6.13 eq. (7) and (8), PDF 352 to 353; Mathur §3.7.7, PDF 161 to 163). Mathur also gives a proper-time route (his first argument): p_y = m₀(Δy/Δτ) and Δt = γΔτ give m = m₀(dt/dτ) = γm₀.

## Checks and limits

- Dimensions: γ is a pure number, so p has the units of m₀v.
- v ≪ c: γ ≈ 1 + β²/2, so p → m₀v (Newton) with a correction of order β².
- Numbers: m/m₀ = 1.005 (β = 0.1), 1.155 (0.5), 1.667 (0.8), 2.294 (0.9), 7.089 (0.99), 22.37 (0.999). As β → 1 the momentum grows without bound, so no finite force reaches c.
- Mathur Example 36: m = 2m₀ gives β = √3/2 = 0.866. Example 37: a proton at 0.9c has m = 2.294 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ = 3.83 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and p = 1.034 × 10⁻¹⁸ kg m/s (the source prints 3.83 × 10⁻²⁴ g and 10.34 × 10⁻¹⁴ g cm/s, the same values).

## Force

Newton's second law survives as F = dp/dt (Mathur §3.9, PDF 165), which is m a + v dm/dt, not m a, because m changes with speed.

### 3. Core: kinetic energy T = (γ − 1)m₀c² and the low-speed limit

*Objective:* Derive the relativistic kinetic energy from the work-energy theorem with every integration step, then prove T → ½m₀v² for v ≪ c.

## Definition used

The kinetic energy is the work done on a particle starting from rest to reach speed v (Mathur §3.9 and Upadhyaya §6.14, PDF 165 to 167 and 353 to 354). Force is F = dp/dt with p = γm₀v from module 2. Motion along one line; work is positive when F and displacement agree.

## Step 1: write the work as ∫ v dp

dT = F dx = (dp/dt) dx = (dx/dt) dp = v dp, so T = ∫₀^v v dp.

## Step 2: integrate by parts

∫ v dp = v p − ∫ p dv. With p = γm₀v:

$$ T = γ m₀ v² − m₀ ∫₀^v v dv/√(1 − v²/c²) $$

## Step 3: the remaining integral

Put s = 1 − v²/c². Then ds = −2v dv/c², so v dv = −(c²/2) ds and

∫ v dv/√s = −(c²/2) ∫ s^(−1/2) ds = −c² √s.

At v = 0, s = 1; at the top, s = 1 − β². So the integral is −c²(√(1 − β²) − 1) = c²(1 − 1/γ).

## Step 4: assemble

T = γm₀v² − m₀c²(1 − 1/γ) = γm₀c²β² − m₀c² + m₀c²/γ.

Now m₀c²/γ = γm₀c²(1 − β²), so the terms in γm₀c² add to γm₀c²[β² + 1 − β²] = γm₀c². Hence

$$ T = γ m₀ c² − m₀ c² = (m − m₀)c² = (γ − 1) m₀ c² $$

Total energy E = T + m₀c² = γm₀c² = mc²; rest energy E₀ = m₀c².

## Shorter route (check it matches)

From m²(c² − v²) = m₀²c², differentiate: 2mc² dm − 2mv² dm − 2m²v dv = 0, so (c² − v²) dm = m v dv. Then v dp = v(m dv + v dm) = m v dv + v² dm = c² dm. So dT = c² dm and T = (m − m₀)c².

## Limit v ≪ c (the 2022 task)

γ = (1 − β²)^(−1/2) = 1 + β²/2 + 3β⁴/8 + … (binomial, bridge B2). Then

$$ T = m₀c²(β²/2 + 3β⁴/8 + …) = ½ m₀ v² + 3m₀v⁴/(8c²) + … → ½ m₀ v² $$

Numbers: β = 0.1 gives exact (γ − 1) = 0.005038 and ½β² = 0.005 (0.75 per cent low); β = 0.5 gives 0.1547 against 0.125 (19 per cent low). A sympy check of the integral and series agreed.

## A trap in the sources

Mathur (PDF 167, top) says ½mv² gives the true kinetic energy even with relativistic m; the printed sentence reads as 'does'. It cannot be right and his own exercise 42 says 'does not': at β = 0.8, ½γm₀v² = 0.533 m₀c², while T = 0.667 m₀c².

## Checks

Dimensions: m₀c² is joules, γ − 1 pure. T → 0 as v → 0. As v → c, T → ∞: infinite work for speed c.

### 4. Core: E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴, massless particles and v = pc²/E

*Objective:* Prove the energy-momentum relation, deduce dE/dp = v, E = pc and v = pc²/E, and use p²c² = T² + 2Tm₀c².

## Proof

From modules 2 and 3: p = γm₀v and E = γm₀c². Then

E² − p²c² = γ²m₀²c⁴ − γ²m₀²v²c² = γ²m₀²c⁴(1 − β²) = m₀²c⁴, because γ²(1 − β²) = 1.

$$ E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴ $$

The sources reach it by writing γ²v² = γ²c² − c² (Mathur §3.12, PDF 169 to 170; Upadhyaya, PDF 355 to 356); Mathur's text prints v³ where v² is meant (a typo). The right side is the same in every frame (m₀ and c are invariant); the four-vector reading of this is to come on Day 14 (PH-033, not yet written).

## Speed from E and p

Divide p = γm₀v by E = γm₀c²: p/E = v/c², so

$$ v = pc²/E $$

## dE/dp = v (the 2020 task)

Differentiate E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴ with m₀ constant: 2E dE = 2pc² dp, so dE/dp = pc²/E = v.

## The kinetic-energy form

With E = T + m₀c²: (T + m₀c²)² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴ gives

$$ p²c² = T² + 2T m₀c² $$

This is Mathur Example 42(i) and Upadhyaya Example 17. It lets you get p from T with no γ at all.

## Limits

- T ≪ m₀c²: p²c² ≈ 2Tm₀c², so T = p²/(2m₀), the Newtonian result.
- T ≫ m₀c²: p²c² ≈ T², so E ≈ pc (ultra-relativistic, particle behaves like light).

## Massless particles

If m₀ = 0, E = pc and v = pc²/E = c (Mathur §3.13, PDF 170). A photon of frequency ν has E = hν and p = hν/c. The product γm₀ is 0 × ∞ for m₀ = 0, so for photons E and p are the starting quantities, not γ and m₀. Neutrinos are treated as massless in these problems (a modern caution: they have a tiny nonzero mass; verify).

## Worked check: electron with T = m₀c²

pc = √(T² + 2Tm₀c²) = m₀c²√3 = 0.885 MeV (m₀c² = 0.511 MeV); E = 1.022 MeV; v/c = pc/E = 0.866, the value from γ = 2 in module 3. The two routes agree.

## Transformation (used in module 6)

Mathur §3.10 (PDF 168 to 169): p′ₓ = γ(pₓ − vE/c²), p′_y = p_y, E′ = γ(E − v pₓ), with the inverse E = γ(E′ + v p′ₓ).

## Dimensional check

pc has units of energy (kg m/s × m/s = J), as does m₀c². Every term in E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴ is J².

### 5. Core: what E = mc² means, mass defect, binding energy and Q-value

*Objective:* State what mass-energy equivalence does and does not claim, and compute energy released from mass differences.

## The claim

Total energy E = γm₀c² and rest energy E₀ = m₀c². Both books conclude that energy added to a body adds to its inertia, Δm = ΔE/c² (Mathur PDF 166 to 168; Upadhyaya §6.14 to 6.15, PDF 354 to 355). The energy 'locked' in a kilogram is 9 × 10¹⁶ J, which is about 25 × 10⁹ kWh.

## What it does not say

- It does not say mass vanishes and 'turns into' something else. The total energy and the total momentum of an isolated system are conserved.
- It does not say a moving body gets heavier in some second 'substance'. m = γm₀ is a bookkeeping of E/c².
- It does not claim every energy change is visible: a compressed spring or hot body does gain mass, W/c² or Q/c², too small to see. Heating 1 kg of water by 1 K (4186 J) adds 4.65 × 10⁻¹⁴ kg.

## Conservation of E follows from conservation of p

Mathur §3.11 (PDF 169): if momentum is conserved in S and S′, apply p′ₓ = γ(pₓ − vE/c²) to each particle. The difference between the two conservation statements leaves (v/c²)(E_before − E_after) = 0, so E_before = E_after. Energy conservation and momentum conservation therefore hold together in all inertial frames.

## Mass defect, Q and binding energy

- Q-value of a reaction: Q = (Σm₀,initial − Σm₀,final)c² = Σ T_final − Σ T_initial.
- Binding energy of a nucleus: B = (Σ masses of separate nucleons − nuclear mass)c². Mass defect Δm = B/c² (Mathur PDF 168 and Example 45, PDF 175 to 176; Upadhyaya PDF 355).
- Sun: helium formation from four protons and two electrons has Δm ≈ 0.0448 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, giving about 25 MeV per helium (Mathur's masses, recomputed 25.2 MeV; Upadhyaya agrees). Upadhyaya's 3.8 × 10²⁶ W sun gives 3.8 × 10²⁶/c² = 4.2 × 10⁹ kg/s, the book's 4 × 10⁹ kg/s.
- Upadhyaya's Li + ²H → 2 ⁴He: Δm = 0.02381 u gives 0.02381 × 931.5 = 22.2 MeV, matching the observed 22.2 MeV.
- Deuteron: standard masses (verify) m_p + m_n − m_d give 2.224 MeV. Mathur's rounded masses give 2.25 MeV, because the difference 0.004 × 10⁻²⁴ g keeps about one significant figure.
- Neutron decay: Q = (m_n − m_p − m_e)c² = 0.782 MeV (standard masses, verify). The sources treat the products as proton and electron only; real decay also emits an antineutrino, which shares this energy.
- U-235 fission, 200 MeV out of 235 × 931.5 MeV: 0.091 per cent of the rest energy (Mathur exercise 57, the printed 0.091 per cent is correct).

## Units

1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; m_e c² = 0.511 MeV; 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c² (this needs c = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s, 1.6605 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; with c = 3 × 10⁸, 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J you get 933.75, which is why exam answers vary in the third digit).

### 6. Core: two-body decay at rest, pion to muon plus neutrino, and decay in flight

*Objective:* Derive the energies, momenta and speeds of the products of M → m₁ + m₂ at rest and solve the 2018, 2024 and 2026 pion tasks.

## Set-up

A parent of rest mass M at rest decays into products 1 and 2. Conservation of momentum (total zero) gives equal and opposite momenta, magnitude p. Conservation of energy gives E₁ + E₂ = Mc². Each product obeys Eᵢ² = p²c² + mᵢ²c⁴ (module 4).

## Algebra

E₂ = Mc² − E₁, and E₁² − m₁²c⁴ = E₂² − m₂²c⁴ (both equal p²c²). Substitute:

E₁² − m₁²c⁴ = M²c⁴ − 2Mc²E₁ + E₁² − m₂²c⁴, so

$$ E₁ = (M² + m₁² − m₂²)c²/(2M),   E₂ = (M² + m₂² − m₁²)c²/(2M) $$

For m₂ = 0 (neutrino, with m₁ = m for the muon):

$$ E_ν = pc = (M² − m²)c²/(2M),   E_μ = (M² + m²)c²/(2M) $$
$$ T_μ = (M − m)²c²/(2M),   β_μ = pc/E_μ = (M² − m²)/(M² + m²) $$

Check: T_μ + E_ν = (M − m)c² = Q, the full mass difference.

## The 2024 task: M = 273 mₑ, m = 207 mₑ, mₑc² = 0.511 MeV

- T_μ = 66²/(2 × 273) = 7.978 mₑc² = 4.08 MeV.
- E_ν = (273² − 207²)/546 = 31 680/546 = 58.02 mₑc² = 29.65 MeV.
- Sum 66.00 mₑc² = 33.73 MeV = Q. β_μ = 31 680/117 378 = 0.2699.

## The 2018 and 2026 tasks

The verified text of the 2018 task gives no masses, and the verified 2026 text only labels the particles (π, μ, ν) with no mass values. With standard tabulated masses (pion 139.57, muon 105.66 MeV/c²; verify): T_μ = 4.12 MeV, E_ν = 29.79 MeV, β_μ = 0.2714, v ≈ 8.1 × 10⁷ m/s. With 273 and 207 you get β = 0.270. The two data sets differ in the third digit only. State the masses you use.

## Trap: using energy alone

Writing T_μ = Q = 33.7 MeV assumes the neutrino takes no energy. It carries momentum p, hence energy pc ≈ 29.7 MeV. Momentum conservation is not optional.

## Limits and a second example

m → 0: E₁ = E₂ = Mc²/2. So a neutral particle at rest decaying to two photons gives photons of Mc²/2 each, back to back; π⁰ at 134.98 MeV/c² gives 67.49 MeV each (standard mass, verify). m → M: E_ν → 0. M < m₁ + m₂: no decay (the square root in pc is imaginary).

## Decay in flight

If the pion moves with γ = 2 in the lab, use E = γ(E* + β p*c cosθ*) from module 4 (Mathur PDF 168 to 169), with E* = p*c = 29.65 MeV the neutrino energy in the pion frame. Forward (θ* = 0): γ(1 + β)E* = 2 × 1.866 × 29.65 = 110.7 MeV. Backward: γ(1 − β)E* = 7.94 MeV. Check: the two sum to 2γE* = 118.6 MeV. Other angles lie between.

### 7. Core: relativistic coalescence, photon emission with recoil

*Objective:* Find mass and speed after two particles stick together (2021 and 2025 tasks), and the true photon energy and recoil of an emitting atom.

## Coalescence: general result

Particle 1 (rest mass m₁, speed u₁, γ₁) hits particle 2 (rest mass m₂) at rest and they stick. Conserve p and E (module 5):

- E = (γ₁m₁ + m₂)c²,  p = γ₁m₁u₁.
- The final particle has rest mass M, so by module 4 M²c⁴ = E² − p²c².

M²c⁴ = c⁴[γ₁²m₁² + 2γ₁m₁m₂ + m₂² − γ₁²β₁²m₁²] = c⁴[m₁² + m₂² + 2γ₁m₁m₂]

$$ M² = m₁² + m₂² + 2γ₁m₁m₂,   V = pc²/E = γ₁m₁u₁/(γ₁m₁ + m₂) $$

This agrees with Mathur exercise 56 for u₂ = 0. Note M > m₁ + m₂: the kinetic energy that disappears reappears as rest mass.

## The 2025 task: m₁ = 1 kg at 0.9c, m₂ = 2 kg

γ₁ = 2.2942. E = (2.2942 + 2)c² = 4.2942c² (kg units with c = 1), p = 2.2942 × 0.9 = 2.0647. M² = 1 + 4 + 4 × 2.2942 = 14.177, so M = 3.765 kg. V = 2.0647/4.2942 = 0.4808c. Check: M²c⁴ = 4.2942² − 2.0647² = 18.440 − 4.263 = 14.177.

## The 2021 task: proton 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg at 0.8c, nucleus 5.0 × 10⁻²⁶ kg (rest mass, at rest)

- m = γm₀ = 1.6667 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ = 2.783 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.
- p = m v = 2.783 × 10⁻²⁷ × 0.8 × 3 × 10⁸ = 6.68 × 10⁻¹⁹ kg m/s.
- Total relativistic mass before: m + 5.0 × 10⁻²⁶ = 5.2783 × 10⁻²⁶ kg. V = p/(total mass) = 6.68 × 10⁻¹⁹/5.2783 × 10⁻²⁶ = 1.266 × 10⁷ m/s = 0.0422c.
- Rest mass after: 5.274 × 10⁻²⁶ kg, above 5.167 × 10⁻²⁶ kg, the sum of the rest masses.

A Newtonian shortcut (momentum m₀v divided by the sum of rest masses) gives 7.76 × 10⁶ m/s, which is 39 per cent too low.

## Photon emission with recoil

An atom of rest mass M_i, at rest, emits a photon and ends with rest mass M_f; ΔE = (M_i − M_f)c². Momentum: the atom recoils with p = E_γ/c. Energy: M_ic² = E_γ + √(E_γ² + M_f²c⁴). Rearranging, (M_ic² − E_γ)² = E_γ² + M_f²c⁴ gives M_i²c⁴ − 2M_ic²E_γ = M_f²c⁴, so

$$ E_γ = (M_i² − M_f²)c²/(2M_i) = ΔE(1 − ΔE/(2M_ic²)) $$

The recoil kinetic energy is ΔE − E_γ = ΔE²/(2M_ic²). Mathur Example 43 states the loss of internal energy is hν(1 + hν/2mc²), the same to first order.

Numbers: hydrogen, ΔE = 10.2 eV, M_ic² = 938.8 MeV: recoil 5.5 × 10⁻⁸ eV. A 14 keV photon from a nucleus of mass 57 u: p = E/c = 7.48 × 10⁻²⁴ kg m/s (Mathur exercise 54a prints 7.5 × 10⁻⁹ g cm/s; the correct unit conversion is 7.5 × 10⁻¹⁹ g cm/s) and recoil energy 1.85 × 10⁻³ eV.

### 8. Core: threshold energy, pair production, annihilation and Compton scattering

*Objective:* Use E and p conservation to find thresholds, explain why pair production needs a nucleus, and derive the Compton formulae (2026 Q3a).

## Annihilation

An electron and positron at rest annihilate. The initial momentum is 0, so a single photon (p = E/c ≠ 0) is impossible; two photons go back to back, each with E = m₀c² = 0.511 MeV and wavelength λ = h/(m₀c) = 2.43 × 10⁻¹² m = 0.0243 Å (Mathur exercises 50 and 51; his answer 0.024 Å agrees).

## Pair production needs a third body

A photon (E, p = E/c) in empty space cannot turn into e⁺e⁻. In the frame where the pair has zero total momentum (it exists: boost along their total momentum), the photon would also need zero momentum; but a photon has p = E/c ≠ 0 in every frame (it travels at c in all of them, module 4). So momentum conservation fails. Near a nucleus of mass M the nucleus absorbs momentum (Mathur PDF 167: pair production happens in the intense field near a nucleus). Threshold: the nucleus is initially at rest and, at minimum energy, the three products move off together with one common velocity (c = 1 units),

(E + M)² − E² = (M + 2m)², so 2EM + M² = M² + 4Mm + 4m², giving

$$ E_th = 2mc²(1 + m/M) $$

This is 1.0226 MeV for a proton target and 1.0220 MeV for a 12 u nucleus; the nucleus mass barely matters, so Mathur's 'at least 1.02 MeV' is right. The step 'E² − p²c² is the same in all frames, for the total of several particles' is standard content, formalised on Day 14 (PH-033, not yet written); verify.

## Threshold for particle creation

Beam of energy E on a target of mass m at rest, producing total rest mass ΣM. At threshold the products move off together with one common velocity (at rest in the centre-of-momentum frame), and (E + m)² − p² = 2m² + 2Em (c = 1). Setting this to (ΣM)²: for p + p → p + p + p + p̄ (ΣM = 4m_p): 16m_p² = 2m_p² + 2Em_p, so E = 7m_p, T_th = 6m_p c² = 5.63 GeV (standard result, verify; m_p c² = 938.3 MeV). A photon producing a proton-antiproton pair near a nucleus of mass M needs 2m_pc²(1 + m_p/M): at least 2m_pc² = 1.88 GeV for a heavy nucleus, and 3.75 GeV on a free proton (standard result, verify). Mathur PDF 167 prints 1.08 GeV, a slip.

## Compton scattering (the 2026 task)

A photon (energy E, momentum E/c) scatters through θ off a free electron at rest (rest energy mc²). Afterwards the photon has E′ and the electron momentum p_e and energy E_e. Take the x axis along the incoming photon.

Momentum: p_e = (E/c, 0) − (E′cosθ/c, E′sinθ/c), so p_e²c² = E² + E′² − 2EE′cosθ.
Energy: E + mc² = E′ + E_e, and E_e² = p_e²c² + m²c⁴.

Square the energy equation: (E − E′ + mc²)² = E² + E′² − 2EE′cosθ + m²c⁴. Expand the left: E² + E′² + m²c⁴ − 2EE′ + 2mc²(E − E′). Cancel:

2mc²(E − E′) = 2EE′(1 − cosθ), so E − E′ = EE′(1 − cosθ)/(mc²), and

$$ E′ = E/[1 + (E/mc²)(1 − cosθ)],   T = E − E′ = (E²/mc²)(1 − cosθ)/[1 + (E/mc²)(1 − cosθ)] $$

These are the two results demanded. Checks: θ = 0 gives E′ = E, T = 0. For E = 0.511 MeV: θ = 90° gives E′ = 0.2555 MeV, T = 0.2555 MeV; θ = 180° gives E′ = 0.1703 MeV, T = 0.3407 MeV. Taking 1/E′ − 1/E gives the wavelength shift Δλ = (h/mc)(1 − cosθ) (standard, verify).

### Notes: Definitions

- Rest mass m₀: mass in the particle's own rest frame; invariant.
- Relativistic mass m = γm₀; E/c². Modern usage keeps only m₀ as 'mass'.
- Kinetic energy T = E − m₀c² = (γ − 1)m₀c²; rest energy E₀ = m₀c²; total energy E = γm₀c².
- Q-value: (Σ initial rest masses − Σ final rest masses)c²; positive means kinetic energy released.
- Binding energy: energy equivalent of the mass defect of a nucleus.
- Threshold energy: minimum beam energy that conserves E and p while producing the required rest mass.

### Notes: Key equations

- m = γm₀, p = γm₀v, E = γm₀c², T = (γ − 1)m₀c².
- E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴; p²c² = T² + 2Tm₀c²; v = pc²/E; dE/dp = v.
- Massless: E = pc, v = c; photon E = hν, p = hν/c.
- Low speed: T ≈ ½m₀v² + 3m₀v⁴/(8c²).
- Decay at rest M → m + massless: E_ν = (M² − m²)c²/2M, T = (M − m)²c²/2M, β = (M² − m²)/(M² + m²).
- Coalescence on a target at rest: M² = m₁² + m₂² + 2γ₁m₁m₂; V = γ₁m₁u₁/(γ₁m₁ + m₂).
- Compton: E′ = E/[1 + (E/mc²)(1 − cosθ)], T = E − E′.
- Pair production on a nucleus: E_th = 2mc²(1 + m/M).

### Notes: Derivation skeletons

- Mass variation: two identical balls, one at rest in S, the other at rest in S′; u_y of B in S is u′_y/γ; y-momentum balance gives m_B = γm_A; w → 0 gives m = γm₀.
- Kinetic energy: T = ∫v dp; parts; s = 1 − v²/c²; assemble γm₀c² − m₀c². Short route: v dp = c² dm.
- E–p relation: γ²(1 − β²) = 1 in E² − p²c²; differentiate for dE/dp = v.
- Decay at rest: equal p, E₁ + E₂ = Mc², subtract E_i² − m_i²c⁴ equations.
- Compton: square the energy equation, substitute p_e²c² from the momentum triangle, cancel.

### Notes: Confused concepts

- m₀ against m: rest mass is invariant, m = γm₀ is frame-dependent.

- Kinetic energy is not ½mv² with relativistic m.
- Photon: massless, E = pc, but it still has momentum.

### Notes: Exceptions and limits

- A single photon cannot produce a pair in empty space (momentum).

### Memory skeleton

- Keep momentum conservation in all frames: p = m(u)u
- Two-frame collision, u_y = u′_y/γ, w → 0: m = γm₀, p = γm₀v
- T = ∫v dp, by parts: T = (γ − 1)m₀c²; low speed ½m₀v²
- E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴; v = pc²/E; dE/dp = v; p²c² = T² + 2Tm₀c²
- Massless: E = pc, v = c
- E = mc² meaning: Q-value, mass defect, binding energy
- Decay at rest, coalescence, threshold, Compton (applications)

### Last-week summary

- Mass variation: A at rest in S with +w along y, B at rest in S′; y-momentum balance in S gives m_B = γm_A; w → 0 gives m = γm₀.
- T = ∫v dp = vp − ∫p dv, s = 1 − v²/c²: T = (γ − 1)m₀c²; short route v dp = c² dm; low speed ½m₀v² + 3m₀v⁴/(8c²).
- E² − p²c² = γ²m₀²c⁴(1 − β²) = m₀²c⁴; v = pc²/E; dE/dp = v; p²c² = T² + 2Tm₀c²; m₀ = 0 gives E = pc.
- T = m₀c² (or m = 2m₀): γ = 2, β = 0.866. Q = (Σm₀ initial − Σm₀ final)c²; B = (Σ nucleon masses − nuclear mass)c².
