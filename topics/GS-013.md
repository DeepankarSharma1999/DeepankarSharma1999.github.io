## GS-013: Ancient Indian education and philosophical schools

Prelims GS I (Mains culture cross-links) · History · Ancient India

*Goal:* Explain Ancient Indian education and philosophical schools and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Core mechanism

Indian intellectual traditions include Vedic learning, Buddhist monasteries and competing philosophical schools. Distinguish a school's account of knowledge from its account of reality and liberation.

### Exam-facing synthesis

Build the topic around three defensible links. Learning occurred in households, teacher-student lineages, monasteries, courts and major centres of scholarship. Philosophical schools disagreed about valid knowledge, the self, reality, action and liberation. Buddhist and Brahmanical institutions interacted with patrons and long-distance intellectual networks. For objective questions, classify each statement by process, chronology or scope before eliminating it. For a written answer or worked solution, state the governing idea, apply it to a concrete example, and finish with the limitation: Do not turn a diverse, changing intellectual landscape into one uniform ancient education system.

### Worked example

Buddhist monastic centres such as Nalanda combined learning with institutional patronage.
