## GS-355: External state and non-state threats

Mains GS3 + Prelims GS1 · Security · Internal security

*Goal:* Explain External state and non-state threats and use it to reason through the worked example.

### Concept and distinctions

External state actors may influence security through direct pressure, covert support or cyber operations. Non-state actors include armed groups, criminal networks and other organizations operating outside state structures. Their actions can overlap, but external origin and state control are separate factual questions.

### How to apply it

Analyse capabilities, incentives, support networks and evidence of attribution before selecting a response. Border measures, diplomacy, financial controls and cyber resilience address different channels. Not every transnational threat is military, and cooperation with foreign institutions can be as important as deterrence. Uncertain attribution requires proportionate conclusions rather than confident claims based on suspicion alone.

### Worked example

A cross-border criminal network may exploit weak coordination even without proven direction by a foreign government.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why distinguish external origin from state sponsorship?
  - **a:** Activity crossing a border does not by itself establish that a foreign state directed or controlled it.

### Common trap

Assuming every external threat has the same sponsor or requires the same response.
