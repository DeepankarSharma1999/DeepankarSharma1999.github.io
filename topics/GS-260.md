## GS-260: Ecosystem structure and trophic levels

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain Ecosystem structure and trophic levels and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Components and structure

NCERT defines an ecosystem as a functional unit of nature in which organisms interact with each other and with their physical surroundings, varying in size from a pond to an ocean; some ecologists treat the whole biosphere as one global ecosystem. The abiotic part includes air, water, soil and dissolved substances, plus regulating factors like sunlight, temperature and day length. The biotic part has producers, consumers and decomposers. Two structural features describe an ecosystem: species composition, found by identifying and counting species, and stratification, the vertical layering of plants from tall trees to ground herbs.

### The pond as a model

A pond shows all functions in miniature. Its abiotic component is water with dissolved inorganic and organic substances and the bottom soil. Autotrophs include phytoplankton, algae and floating, submerged and marginal plants. Consumers are zooplankton and free-swimming and bottom-dwelling animals. Decomposers — fungi, bacteria and flagellates — are concentrated at the bottom. Autotrophs convert inorganic material into organic matter using solar energy; heterotrophs consume them; decomposers mineralise dead matter so nutrients return for reuse. Energy moves one way, to higher trophic levels, and is lost as heat.

### Trophic levels

Organisms are placed according to their source of food. Green plants are producers at the first level; herbivores, the primary consumers, at the second; carnivores, the secondary consumers, at the third, with tertiary consumers above. Each level holds a standing crop, the living biomass or number per unit area at a given time, best measured as dry weight. Energy decreases at each successive level. Because a trophic level is a role rather than a species label, omnivores and humans feed at several levels, and calculations must include all organisms at a level.

### Worked example

Statement check: 'Zooplankton form the second trophic level in a lake' — correct, since phytoplankton are producers (first level). 'Energy and nutrients both cycle in ecosystems' — incorrect: energy flows one way and is lost as heat; nutrients cycle.

### Recall

- - **q:** Name the two structural features of an ecosystem.
  - **a:** Species composition and stratification.
- - **q:** Name the four functional aspects of an ecosystem.
  - **a:** Productivity, decomposition, energy flow, nutrient cycling.
- - **q:** Why is a trophic level called functional?
  - **a:** Because one species may occupy different levels depending on what it eats.

### Common trap

Treating trophic level as a fixed attribute of a species; NCERT stresses that one species can occupy more than one trophic level at the same time.
