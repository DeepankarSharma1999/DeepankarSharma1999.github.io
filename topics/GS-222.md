## GS-222: Commercial banks, NBFCs and regulation

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Basic macro and monetary

*Goal:* Explain Commercial banks, NBFCs and regulation and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### What banks do

Commercial banks collect deposits from the public and lend part of them to borrowers, earning the spread between lending and deposit rates. NCERT's goldsmith story shows the logic: because all depositors rarely withdraw together, a custodian can lend part of what it holds, and the loans come back as fresh deposits. This is how banks create money. The limit is regulatory: the RBI requires a share of deposits to be held as reserves, which stops any bank from over-lending. Banks also need the RBI as lender of last resort when they run short of funds. Their balance sheet logic — deposits as liabilities, loans and reserves as assets, net worth as the buffer — underpins later prudential ratios.

### Health of scheduled commercial banks

The Economic Survey 2025-26 describes intermediaries as the channel through which policy rates, liquidity and credit decisions reach households and firms. For banks it reports strong buffers: CRAR of 17.2% in September 2025 and gross and net NPA ratios at multi-decadal and record lows. Sector-wise, industry GNPA fell to 1.9% and services to 1.8% by September 2025, but agriculture remained high at 6.0% and its share of total GNPA rose. The slippage ratio, which captures fresh NPAs as a share of standard loans, improved from 7.1% in FY18 to 1.4% in FY25, while recovery rates through IBC and SARFAESI improved in FY25.

### NBFCs in the system

The Economic Survey 2022-23 notes the rising weight of non-banking financial companies: their credit has grown as a share of GDP and relative to bank credit, and they absorbed the pandemic shock with policy support. Their asset quality recovered, with GNPA falling from 7.2% in June 2021 to 5.9% in September 2022, and their CRAR was 27.4% in end-September 2022, well above the 15% minimum. An RBI stress test of 152 NBFCs found 8% would breach the minimum capital requirement in the baseline scenario, rising to 13% under severe stress. NBFC lending is concentrated in industry, followed by retail, services and agriculture, making their health relevant to credit transmission and systemic risk.
