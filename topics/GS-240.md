## GS-240: Agriculture versus manufacturing and services

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Growth, public finance and infrastructure

*Goal:* Explain Agriculture versus manufacturing and services and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Three sectors and their interdependence

Growing cotton or rearing dairy animals depends on natural processes, so it is primary activity. Spinning that cotton into yarn or making bricks from earth is secondary, because a manufacturing step creates the product. Transport, storage, banking, trade and communication, along with teachers, doctors and IT services, form the tertiary or service sector. The sectors depend on one another: a sugar mill closes if farmers stop supplying cane, and farmers suffer if fertiliser or pump prices rise. To measure output, values rather than quantities are added, and only final goods are counted, since the price of biscuits already includes the flour and wheat used. India now reports each sector's contribution as Gross Value Added.

### Output shifted, jobs lagged

Developed countries moved from primary to secondary and then to tertiary dominance over more than a century. NCERT shows that between 1977-78 and 2017-18 India's tertiary output grew most and became the largest sector. Reasons include demand for basic public services, services that support growing agriculture and industry, rising incomes creating demand for eating out, tourism and private schooling, and new ICT-based services. Employment did not follow: industrial output rose more than nine times but industrial employment only about three times, and services output about 14 times against roughly five times for jobs. By 2017-18 more than half of workers remained in the primary sector producing only about a sixth of GVA, which NCERT reads as disguised unemployment.

### Current picture

NCERT's 2023-24 workforce data show progress but persistence: the primary sector still employs about 46 per cent of workers, against roughly 74 per cent in 1972-73, while secondary and tertiary shares have grown to about 24 and 30 per cent. The urban workforce is mainly in services, while about 60 per cent of rural workers depend on agriculture and allied activity. The Economic Survey 2025-26 notes that services now contribute more than half of GVA, have grown about 7–8 per cent a year with low volatility, and attract the most FDI. It also observes that India became service-led at a far lower per capita income than peers that followed manufacturing-led paths; for Mains, ask what this means for absorbing less-skilled workers leaving farms.
