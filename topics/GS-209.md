## GS-209: Human Rights Commissions

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS2 · Polity · Institutions, law and politics

*Goal:* Explain Human Rights Commissions and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Composition and appointment

The NHRC, headquartered in Delhi, is constituted by the Central Government under section 3. After the 2019 amendment the Chairperson may be a former Chief Justice of India or a former Supreme Court judge. Other members are a serving or former Supreme Court judge, a serving or former High Court Chief Justice, and three experts on human rights, at least one being a woman. Chairpersons of national commissions for SCs, STs, women, minorities, backward classes and child rights, and the Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities, are deemed members for most functions. A six-member committee chaired by the Prime Minister recommends appointments, which the President makes by warrant.

### Functions and powers

Under section 12 the Commission inquires, on its own motion, on a petition or on a court's direction, into violations or negligence in preventing violations by public servants; intervenes in court proceedings with the court's approval; visits jails and similar institutions; reviews constitutional and legal safeguards; studies treaties; promotes research and human-rights literacy; and supports NGOs. While inquiring it has civil-court powers, can require information, enter premises to seize documents, and use central or State investigative agencies with government concurrence. After inquiry it may recommend compensation, prosecution or interim relief, or approach the Supreme Court or High Court; governments must comment within a month.

### State commissions and jurisdictional limits

A State Government may constitute a State Human Rights Commission, chaired by a person who has been a High Court Chief Justice or judge, with one member who is or was a High Court judge or a District Judge of seven years' standing, and one human-rights expert; the Governor appoints them. A State commission inquires only into matters relatable to the State and Concurrent Lists. Section 36 bars the NHRC from matters pending before a State or other statutory commission (section 21(5) imposes a similar bar on State commissions) and stops both from inquiring into violations more than one year old. Armed-forces cases follow a separate route in which the Centre reports and must state action taken within three months. NHRC annual and special reports are laid before Parliament or the State legislature with action-taken memoranda.
