## GS-243: FDI, FPI and external investment

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Growth, public finance and infrastructure

*Goal:* Explain FDI, FPI and external investment and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### How MNCs invest

NCERT defines a multinational corporation as one that owns or controls production in more than one country. MNCs locate where markets are close, labour of the right skill is cheap and other inputs are reliable, and they look for favourable government policies. They may set up joint production with local firms, providing money and newer technology, or, most commonly, buy up local companies and expand, as Cargill did with Parakh Foods. They also control production by placing orders with many small producers who make garments, footwear or sports goods sold under the MNC's brand. Spending by MNCs on land, buildings and machines is foreign investment, made in the expectation that these assets will earn profits.

### Where foreign investment sits in the balance of payments

The capital account records international purchases and sales of assets such as shares, bonds and government debt. A foreigner buying shares of an Indian company is a credit; an Indian buying a foreign firm is a debit. Its components include foreign direct investment, foreign institutional investment, external commercial borrowing and external assistance. A country with a current account deficit must finance it by selling assets or borrowing abroad, so the current and capital accounts together balance, with the RBI using reserves to cover any remaining gap. NCERT's sample table breaks foreign investment into components and shows FDI on its own line, a reminder that direct and portfolio flows should not be lumped together.

### Recent trends and volatility

The Economic Survey 2025-26 reports gross FDI inflows of USD 81.0 billion in FY25, about 60 per cent of equity inflows going to services, software and hardware, trading, non-conventional energy, construction and autos. Yet net FDI has been modest because repatriation and outward investment by Indian firms have grown; the Survey reads rising repatriation as a sign that investors earn returns here, and cites a CareEdge estimate of about 7.3 per cent average return on inward FDI for 2014-23. FPI behaves differently: flows swing between inflows and outflows with global financial cycles, equity dominating good years and debt reacting to interest rates. The VRR and FAR routes have helped steady debt flows.
