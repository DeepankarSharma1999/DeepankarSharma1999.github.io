## GS-139: Resource conflict and sustainable use

Mains GS1 + Prelims GS1 · Geography · Human/economic/world

*Goal:* Explain Resource conflict and sustainable use and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Why water becomes contested

About 71% of the earth's surface is water, yet freshwater is only about 3%, and its availability varies across space and seasons; NCERT notes that disputes over sharing and control are spreading among communities, regions and states. India's position is tight: a small share of world water must serve a large share of people, and only around 60% of surface and replenishable groundwater can be put to beneficial use. Rainfall is concentrated in the monsoon and in certain basins; south Indian rivers like the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri are already heavily harnessed. Because agriculture dominates demand, choices about crops and irrigation shape competition with industry and growing domestic needs.

### Over-use and pollution as hidden conflicts

Green Revolution states depend on irrigation for over 85% of their net sown area, and wells and tubewells supply about 76% of irrigated area in Punjab and 51% in Haryana, drawing down groundwater. The costs spill onto others: falling water tables, salinity, and toxic fluoride or arsenic in drinking water. Pollution adds a second layer, as agricultural, domestic and industrial effluents reduce usable supply; NCERT names the Yamuna between Delhi and Etawah as the most polluted stretch, with the Sabarmati, Gomti, Musi and others severely affected. Laws such as the Water Act 1974 and Environment Protection Act 1986 have been weakly implemented, so downstream users and ecosystems bear the burden.

### Tools for sustainable sharing

NCERT's prescription mixes technology, community and policy. Watershed management conserves land, water, plants and people together and succeeds where communities participate - Haryali, Neeru-Meeru in Andhra Pradesh, Arvary Pani Sansad in Alwar, and Ralegan Siddhi, where villagers banned water-intensive sugarcane. Rainwater harvesting, recycling and reuse stretch supplies. The National Water Policy 2012 treats water as an economic good only after drinking water, sanitation, food security and ecosystem needs are met, and calls for a framework law for inter-state rivers. Atal Bhujal Yojana works in 8,220 water-stressed gram panchayats (Ministry of Jal Shakti, 2022-23). The Indira Gandhi Canal case adds measures such as avoiding water-intensive crops, lining channels and warabandi.
