## GS-132: Location-based map practice: neighbouring countries

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS1 · Geography · Indian geography

### 1. Orientation: the neighbours around India on one page

*Objective:* See all of India's neighbours arranged around it on a compass sketch and know the order of the drill.

## The skill

A map-practice question gives a name and asks where it is. The method is always the same: first the **side** of India (north, north-east, east, south-east, south, south-west, west, north-west), then the **State or Union Territory** that touches it, then the **physical feature** between them (mountain, river, strait or sea). Day 18 lesson GS-131 (Location-based map practice: protected areas) taught a landscape-and-state habit; here the same habit is applied to neighbouring countries.

## What the extracts give

NCERT (Class XI, chapter 1) says India lies in the south-central part of the continent of Asia, bordering the Indian Ocean and its two arms, the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea. It calls Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh and India together the Indian subcontinent, bounded by the Himalayas in the north, the Hindukush and Sulaiman ranges in the north-west, the Purvachal hills in the north-east and the Indian Ocean in the south. Sri Lanka and the Maldives are the two island neighbours. An exercise lists China, Myanmar, Bangladesh and Pakistan as land-frontier options, and the river map in the drainage chapter carries the label Afghanistan.

## What the extracts do not give

They do not say on which side each neighbour lies, which States touch which neighbour, border lengths or capitals. This lesson supplies the sides and the State lists as standard knowledge flagged **verify**, and gives no border lengths or capitals.

### 2. Foundation: the grid, directions and map words

*Objective:* Define the map words in this lesson and use eight compass directions correctly.

## Words to own

- **Latitude:** an angle measured north or south of the equator (0 degrees) along a **parallel**. **Longitude:** an angle measured east or west of the prime meridian along a **meridian**. A degree has 60 minutes, written with a prime: 6 degrees 45 minutes is 6 degrees and 45 minutes (6.75 degrees). Day 17 lesson GS-095 teaches the grid.
- **Boundary and frontier:** the line where one country's territory ends. A **land boundary** runs over land or rivers; a **maritime boundary** runs through the sea.
- **Territorial limit:** how far a country's sea territory reaches. NCERT says India's extends 12 nautical miles from the coast.
- **Nautical mile:** a sea distance unit, 1.852 km (kegy101 prints 'about 1.8 km (1.852 km)'). A statute mile is the ordinary land mile, about 1.609 km.
- **Gulf:** a large inlet of the sea partly enclosed by land. **Strait:** a narrow passage joining two seas. **Channel:** a stretch of water joining two larger water bodies, often between islands or an island and a coast; strait and channel overlap in usage. NCERT asks the learner to tell a gulf from a strait; the definitions here are standard.
- **Pass:** a gap in a mountain range that people can cross. **Peninsula:** land surrounded by water on three sides. **Subcontinent:** a large, distinct part of a continent; NCERT's Indian subcontinent includes Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh and India.
- **Standard meridian:** the meridian chosen for a country's clock time (module 3).

## Eight directions

North (N), north-east (NE), east (E), south-east (SE), south (S), south-west (SW), west (W), north-west (NW). On a map north is up, east is right. A country is said to lie, for example, to the **east** of India if it touches the eastern side of the Indian boundary; for island countries the direction is that of the sea route.

## A drawing habit

Draw a plain box for India's mainland, then an arrow for each neighbour. Write the direction word next to the arrow before you name the country. Questions get easy when you can say, for example, that Bhutan lies on the northern side, east of Nepal, and that the Bay of Bengal lies to the east of the peninsula.

## Rule of three

For every new place, say three things: side, State and feature. Example format: 'Palk Strait: south-east, Tamil Nadu coast, strait to Sri Lanka.' If you cannot supply one of the three, mark it for an atlas visit.

### 3. Core: position, extent, size and the standard meridian

*Objective:* State India's southern limit, spans, size, coastline and standard meridian and explain the time difference.

## Position (NCERT Class XI, chapter 1)

The mainland extends from **Kashmir in the north to Kanniyakumari in the south** and from **Arunachal Pradesh in the east to Gujarat in the west**. The southern boundary extends to **6 degrees 45 minutes N** in the Bay of Bengal (the southern tip of the Nicobar group), and India's sea territory reaches **12 nautical miles** from the coast. Use 22.2 km for 12 nautical miles (12 x 1.852); the printed 21.9 km is a unit slip recorded in the master notes.

## Extent (recap)

NCERT gives **3,214 km from north to south and 2,933 km from east to west**, though both spans are about 30 degrees. The reason (meridians converge, parallels do not) is taught in Day 17 lesson GS-095, "Core: longitude and time, standard time and IST". Keep only what the drill needs: the mainland values 8 degrees 4 minutes N to 37 degrees 6 minutes N and 68 degrees 7 minutes E to 97 degrees 25 minutes E (standard; verify). Check: 37 deg 6 min minus 8 deg 4 min is 29 deg 2 min; 97 deg 25 min minus 68 deg 7 min is 29 deg 18 min. One option in the exercise, 6 deg 45 min N to 37 deg 6 min N, is the legitimate whole-country span including the Nicobar Islands; it is wrong only as the mainland span. The gap between 6 deg 45 min and 8 deg 4 min is 1 deg 19 min.

## Time (recap)

India uses one standard meridian, **82 degrees 30 minutes E**, so Indian Standard Time is **5 hours 30 minutes ahead of Greenwich Mean Time** (82.5 / 15 = 5.5), although the east and west differ by nearly two hours of sun time. The derivation is in GS-095.

## Neighbours' clocks (standard; verify)

India's neighbours do not share its clock. Typical offsets from Greenwich (verify before relying on them): Pakistan +5, Sri Lanka +5:30, Nepal +5:45, Bangladesh +6, Bhutan +6, Myanmar +6:30. The eastward order of the clocks follows the eastward order of the countries, which is one more way to remember that Myanmar lies furthest east, and that Nepal's odd quarter-hour offset sits between India and Bangladesh. NCERT's exercise asks whether India needs more than one standard time; module 8 gives an answer frame.

## Size and coast

India's area is **3.28 million sq km**, 2.4 per cent of the world's land and the **seventh largest** country. The coastline is **6,100 km** on the mainland and **7,517 km** including the island groups; the measurement and the 21.9/22.2 km slip are in Day 17 lesson GS-130, "Foundation: measuring India's sea edge, coastline numbers and the maritime location".

NCERT says India lies in the south-central part of Asia, with the Indian Ocean to the south and its two arms, the Bay of Bengal on the east and the Arabian Sea on the west. This maritime location gives the peninsular part links to neighbouring regions by sea and air routes.

### 7. Master: the Himalayan wall, the other barriers and the passes

*Objective:* Link each side of India to its boundary range and name the passes NCERT lists.

## The barriers around the subcontinent

NCERT (kegy101) says India is bounded by the **Himalayas in the north**, the **Hindukush and Sulaiman ranges in the north-west**, the **Purvachal hills in the north-east** and the **Indian Ocean in the south**, forming the Indian subcontinent. These mountains acted as a formidable physical barrier in the past and contributed to the unique regional identity of the subcontinent. The Himalayas, according to the physiography chapter (kegy102), are also a **climatic, drainage and cultural divide** and stand almost like a long wall between the subcontinent and the Central and East Asian countries.

## Passes NCERT names

NCERT says that except for a few mountain passes such as the **Khyber, the Bolan, the Shipkila, the Nathula, the Bomdila** and others, it was difficult to cross the barrier. The extracts do not locate them. The standard locations (verify with an atlas) are:

| Pass | Location | Side of India |
|---|---|---|
| Khyber | North-west, in the Pakistan-Afghanistan region | North-west |
| Bolan | North-west, in Pakistan | North-west |
| Shipki La | Himachal Pradesh and Tibet; the Satluj passes through it (kegy103) | North |
| Nathu La | Sikkim and Tibet | North |
| Bomdila | Arunachal Pradesh | North-east |

The extract also places the source of the Beas near the Rohtang Pass (kegy103).

## Orientation of the Himalayan ranges (kegy102)

The Himalayas are a series of parallel ranges. Their general orientation is **north-west to south-east** in the north-western part of India, **east-west** in the Darjiling and Sikkim region and **north-south** in Nagaland, Manipur and Mizoram. The Great Himalayan range, also known as the central axial range, is approximately 2,500 km long from east to west and its width varies between 160 and 400 km from north to south (as NCERT prints it; the figures describe the Himalayan system as a whole, not one range; verify). A good drawing habit is to draw the arc: it bends from the north-west, runs east, then turns south along the Purvachal.

## Other divides that help the map

- The **Delhi ridge, the Aravalis and the Sahyadris** separate the Arabian Sea drainage from the Bay of Bengal drainage (kegy103).
- The **Great Indian Desert** lies north-west of the Aravali hills; its northern part slopes towards Sindh and its southern part towards the Rann of Kachchh (kegy102). This is the western frontier region.
- **Peninsular India** extends into the Indian Ocean, with the Western Ghats on the west and the Eastern Ghats on the east meeting at the Nilgiri hills.
- The **Purvachal** hills in the north-east separate India from Myanmar on the map's eastern side.

## Memory frame

North wall: Himalayas. North-west gate: Khyber and Bolan. North passes: Shipki La and Nathu La. North-east: Bomdila and the Purvachal. South: the Indian Ocean. This five-line frame feeds the compass sketch.

### 8. Exam: the two 2026 questions and a location elimination method

*Objective:* Solve State-adjacency and river-bridge items by the five-step method and frame a Mains answer on one standard time.

## The five-step elimination method

1. **Side:** place the country or place on a side of India. 2. **Feature:** attach the range, river or channel. 3. **State:** name the States that touch it. 4. **Count:** check counts (for example how many States touch a neighbour). 5. **Test the words:** only, all, longest, highest and not each need a separate check.

## The 2026 question on State adjacency (Q26)

It asks which statements about Indian States are **not correct**: 1. Uttar Pradesh shares its boundary with the highest number of other Indian States; 2. Rajasthan shares the longest international border among all Indian States; 3. Sikkim is the only State that shares its boundary with just one other Indian State. The options are 1 only, 1 and 2, 2 and 3, 3 only.

Analysis (standard facts, verify): statement 1 matches the count in module 5 (eight States and Delhi), so it is correct and should not be marked. Statement 3 fails because Meghalaya also touches only one State (Assam); the positive reason is the Meghalaya fact, not the absence of a source. Statement 2 needs a border-length comparison, which the extracts do not give. The stem says States, so the Union Territories Ladakh and Jammu and Kashmir are not counted; West Bengal touches three countries and its Bangladesh frontier alone is commonly reported as longer than Rajasthan's Pakistan frontier (verify). On that reading statement 2 is also not correct, and the pattern is 2 and 3. The statement-1 check eliminates the options that contain 1, leaving two options that depend on statement 2. This is analysis only, not an official key.

## The 2026 question on river bridges (Q61)

It lists three statements: 1. Maitri Setu over the Feni river connects Ramgarh in India with Sabroom in Bangladesh; 2. the Jhulaghat suspension bridge connects India with Myanmar; 3. the Mechi bridge connects Panitanki Bypass in India with Kakarvitta in Nepal.

Analysis (verify): the module 5 river facts help. The Kali (Sarda) river forms the Indo-Nepal border (module 4). Jhulaghat is on the Kali (Mahakali) in Pithoragarh district, Uttarakhand, facing Nepal's Baitadi district (standard; verify). Uttarakhand touches China and Nepal, not Myanmar (module 5 table), so statement 2 fails. Sabroom is in Tripura, India, and Ramgarh is on the Bangladesh side, so statement 1 reverses the two ends. Panitanki in West Bengal touches Nepal and Kakarvitta is across the Mechi river in Nepal, which supports statement 3. The reasoning points to the option 3 only. Analysis only, never an official key.

## One standard time: Mains frame

NCERT asks whether India needs more than one standard time. Frame in 150 words: (a) facts, 30 degrees of longitude and nearly two hours; 82 deg 30 min E; (b) arguments for more zones, daylight use in the north-east and energy saving (standard); (c) arguments against, administrative simplicity, railway and communication timetables and national unity (standard); (d) a balanced conclusion.

### Core concepts

- India lies in south-central Asia with the Indian Ocean to the south and the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea as its arms.
