## GS-068: Russian Revolution and communism

Mains GS1 · World History · 18th–20th century

*Goal:* Explain Russian Revolution and communism and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### The revolution and the Soviet system

Sociologists classify the Bolshevik revolution, which removed the Tsar and created a communist state, as a revolutionary social movement: one that seeks to transform social relations radically by seizing state power. The USSR that emerged attempted, on an unprecedented scale, to abolish private property and design a society around equality. Its builders gave primacy to the state and the party. Only the communist party was allowed, the economy was centrally planned, and land and productive assets were state-owned. The state guaranteed a minimum standard of living and subsidised health, education and childcare. Over time, however, the system became bureaucratic and authoritarian, and the lack of democracy and free speech stifled citizens.

### Leaders and phases

Lenin, a practitioner of Marxism, founded the Bolshevik party, led the 1917 revolution and guided the new state through its hardest years until 1924. Stalin, his successor, consolidated the Union between 1924 and 1953: he launched rapid industrialisation and forced collectivisation of farming, is credited with the Soviet victory in the Second World War, and is blamed for the Great Terror and the elimination of party rivals. After the Second World War the east European countries liberated by the Soviet army were remodelled on Soviet lines, forming the socialist bloc held together by the Warsaw Pact. The USSR comprised fifteen republics, but Russia dominated in practice.

### Global influence and exam angles

Russia's success had worldwide appeal. Lenin and Trotsky founded the Comintern in 1918 to support communist parties abroad, though it was dissolved in 1943 after becoming an instrument of Soviet interests. In China the Communist Party, founded in 1921, at first followed orthodox Marxist expectations of an urban workers' revolution, but Mao Zedong built his movement on peasants, land redistribution and rural soviets, winning power in 1949 under the banner of a 'New Democracy' allying all classes. Prelims: Lenin 1917–24; Stalin 1924–53; Comintern 1918–43; CCP 1921. Mains: egalitarian goals versus authoritarian practice; communism's adaptation from urban to peasant revolution.

### Worked example

Question: 'The Russian Revolution sought equality but produced an authoritarian state.' Examine. Frame: (1) Goals: abolish private property, egalitarian society, state planning. (2) Achievements: guaranteed minimum living standards, subsidised welfare, rapid industrialisation under Stalin. (3) Costs: one-party control, Great Terror, forced collectivisation, lack of free speech, Russian dominance over other republics. (4) Global effects: Comintern, CCP 1921, Maoist adaptation. Conclude with the tension between egalitarian ideals and concentrated party-state power.

### Recall

- - **q:** What kind of social movement was the Bolshevik revolution, in sociological terms?
  - **a:** A revolutionary movement — aiming at radical transformation of social relations by capturing state power.
- - **q:** When was the Comintern founded and dissolved?
  - **a:** Founded in March 1918 by Lenin and Trotsky; dissolved in 1943.
- - **q:** Name two policies associated with Stalin.
  - **a:** Rapid industrialisation and forcible collectivisation of agriculture.

### Common trap

Reading Marx's 'dictatorship of the proletariat' literally. NCERT explains that Marx used it to mean a revolutionary working-class government replacing the repressive government of the propertied class, not a dictatorship in today's sense.
