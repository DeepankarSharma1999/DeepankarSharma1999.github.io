## GS-001: Sources and archaeological evidence

Prelims GS I (Mains culture cross-links) · History · Ancient India

*Goal:* Explain Sources and archaeological evidence and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Core mechanism

Archaeology studies material remains; inscriptions supply recorded claims, while coins reveal issuers and exchange. Date, provenance and corroboration determine what a source can establish.

### Exam-facing synthesis

Build the topic around three defensible links. Material remains answer questions about production and daily life; texts and inscriptions preserve different voices. A source proves only what its date, context and survival permit; corroboration reduces overclaiming. Coins, inscriptions, structures, plant remains and ceramics require different methods of interpretation. For objective questions, classify each statement by process, chronology or scope before eliminating it. For a written answer or worked solution, state the governing idea, apply it to a concrete example, and finish with the limitation: Do not treat a literary statement as a direct photograph of society or equate absence of evidence with evidence of absence.

### Worked example

An inscription naming a ruler supports chronology; it does not independently prove every royal boast.
