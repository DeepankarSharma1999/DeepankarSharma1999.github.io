## GS-332: India–Nepal and Bhutan relations

Mains GS2 + Prelims GS1 · International Relations · India and world

*Goal:* Compare India’s distinct relationships with Nepal and Bhutan.

### Start with geography and historical frameworks

Nepal and Bhutan are Himalayan neighbours whose transport and energy links are closely connected to India. India–Nepal relations include an open-border tradition and the 1950 Treaty of Peace and Friendship. India and Bhutan revised their friendship treaty in 2007. These historical frameworks should not erase either neighbour’s sovereignty or differences in domestic priorities.

### Hydropower links opportunity with negotiation

Hydropower can connect investment, export revenue and regional electricity supply. Roads, rail links and transmission infrastructure also support trade and people-to-people movement. Negotiations involve financing, project delays, environmental effects and perceptions of dependence. With Nepal, boundary and political sensitivities require careful diplomacy; with Bhutan, cooperation must also respect development and ecological priorities.

### Worked example

A hydropower project can supply electricity to India and revenue to a Himalayan partner. Its lasting value depends on realistic costs, reliable purchases, local consultation and equitable sharing of environmental and financial risks.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why not treat Nepal and Bhutan as one foreign-policy case?
  - **a:** Their institutions, agreements and priorities differ.
- - **q:** What makes hydropower cooperation more than engineering?
  - **a:** Financing, markets, environment and sovereign decisions are also involved.

### Common trap

Neighbourhood cooperation should not be described as a right to control a smaller state’s choices.
