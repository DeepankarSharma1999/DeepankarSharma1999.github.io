### Bridge: Reading a world map: the grid, the equator and 'between' words

Map questions use three kinds of words, and each has a rule.

**1. The grid.** Latitude lines (parallels) run east-west and tell how far north or south of the **equator** (0 degrees) a place is; longitude lines (meridians) run north-south. The equator crosses South America, Africa and the East Indies; a lake in the northern hemisphere has a north latitude, one in the southern hemisphere a south latitude (Day 17 lesson GS-095). To test whether the equator passes through a water body, check whether it lies on both sides of 0 degrees.

**2. Facing words.** A country *borders a sea* only if part of its own coastline touches that sea. A country that is merely near the sea, or is reached by a river, does not border it. Landlocked countries have no sea border.

**3. 'Between' words.** A **strait** is a narrow water passage joining two larger waters and lying between two land masses; a **canal** is a human-made strait; an **isthmus** is a narrow strip of land joining two larger lands and separating two waters. When you meet any of them, ask: which two waters, which two lands?

**A worked check.** The Palk Strait lies between India's Tamil Nadu coast and Sri Lanka (Leong p138 names Palk Strait as the strait that separates Sri Lanka from the mainland). The two lands are India and Sri Lanka; the two waters are the Bay of Bengal (north-east) and Palk Bay (south-west). Palk Bay is closed on the south by Adam's Bridge shoals, and the Gulf of Mannar lies beyond that line (standard; verify; the wider gulf to the south, as in Day 19 lesson GS-132). The same two-lands, two-waters habit works for every strait in this lesson.

### 1. Orientation: the world map on one page

*Objective:* Say in one minute how much of the earth is ocean, how many oceans there are, and what kinds of question this topic produces.

## The big picture

- **Oceans cover more than 70 per cent of the earth.** Leong (p145) gives 364 million sq km of ocean in a total of 510 million sq km; 364 / 510 = 0.714, so about 71 per cent. NCERT (kegy212 p3) prints about 91 per cent of the planet's water in the oceans; standard references give about 97 per cent (book figure; verify).
- **Five oceans** (Leong p145 and NCERT p3): the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern and Arctic. By far the largest is the **Pacific**, nearly one-third of the world's surface (Leong). Seas, bays and gulfs are parts of these oceans.
- **The ocean floor is not flat.** A major portion lies 3-6 km below sea level and has the world's largest mountain ranges, deepest trenches and largest plains (NCERT p3).
- **Islands** are either pieces of continent cut off by the sea (continental) or built in mid-ocean by volcanoes and corals (oceanic) (Leong pp138-139).
- **Enclosed seas** such as the Red Sea and the Baltic show extreme salinity because they are cut off from the open ocean (Leong, NCERT).
- **India** has six physiographic divisions, including coastal plains and islands (NCERT kegy102 pp4-9), and sits at the centre of the Indian Ocean (Day 19 lesson GS-132).

### 2. Foundation: map words and the four-question method

*Objective:* Define the landform and water-body words used in this topic and apply a four-question method to any place name.

## Water words

- **Ocean**: one of the five great bodies of water. **Sea**: a smaller part of an ocean, often partly enclosed by land (Mediterranean, Black, Red). **Gulf**: a large inlet partly enclosed by land. **Bay**: a smaller inlet. (Day 19 lesson GS-132 gives the same definitions for India.)
- **Strait**: a narrow water passage joining two larger waters between two land masses. **Channel**: a passage between an island and a mainland or between islands; the words overlap. **Canal**: a human-made waterway.
- **Lagoon**: shallow water between a reef or bar and the shore. **Atoll**: a ring of coral enclosing a lagoon (NCERT p5).

## Land words

- **Continent**: a very large land mass. **Island**: land surrounded by water on all sides (Leong p138). **Archipelago**: a group of islands. **Peninsula**: land surrounded by water on three sides. **Isthmus**: a narrow strip of land joining two larger lands and separating two waters.
- **Mountain range**, **plateau** and **plain**; **fold mountain** (made by folding of rocks), **block mountain** (a block lifted or left standing between faults).
- **Basin**: a low area drained by a river or filled by water; a river basin is the whole area a river drains.

## The four-question method

For every place named in an exam, ask in order:

1. **Name**: what kind of thing is it (sea, strait, lake, region, range)?
2. **Position**: which continent, which latitude band, which side of which country?
3. **Neighbours**: which countries, seas or ranges touch it (walk around it in one direction)?
4. **Link**: what does it join or separate, and what flows into or out of it?

**Worked example.** Black Sea. Name: a sea. Position: between south-east Europe and Asia Minor (Turkey), north of the Mediterranean. Neighbours: Turkey, Bulgaria, Romania, Ukraine, Russia and Georgia (standard; verify). Link: reached from the Mediterranean through the Dardanelles, the Sea of Marmara and the Bosporus (standard; verify); it receives enormous fresh water from rivers and so has very low salinity (NCERT kegy212 p7).


### 3. Core: the relief of the ocean floor

*Objective:* Describe the four divisions and the minor features of the ocean floor with depths, and note where NCERT and Leong differ.

## Four divisions (NCERT kegy212 pp3-4; Leong pp146-148)

| Division | NCERT | Leong |
|---|---|---|
| **Continental shelf**: the shallow margin of a continent | Average gradient 1 degree or less; average width about 80 km; depth from about 30 m to 600 m; Siberian shelf in the Arctic, the largest, about 1,500 km wide; almost absent off Chile and west Sumatra | Average depth about 180 m; width generally 32-160 km; Siberian shelf about 1,200 km; about 7 per cent of the ocean area; gradient about 1 in 500 |
| **Continental slope**: the steep link to the ocean basin | Gradient 2-5 degrees; depth 200 to 3,000 m; canyons and trenches | Steeper than the shelf; to about 3,000 m |
| **Deep-sea plain**: the flattest, smoothest region | 3,000-6,000 m; fine clay and silt | Undulating abyssal plain 2-3 km below sea level, about two-thirds of the floor, with plateaus, ridges and islands |
| **Oceanic deeps / trenches** | Steep-sided narrow basins, 3-5 km deeper than the surrounding floor, along island arcs and at the base of continental slopes, with active volcanoes and earthquakes; 57 deeps explored: 32 Pacific, 19 Atlantic, 6 Indian | Up to 9,000 m or more; most are near the continents, mainly in the Pacific; Mariana Trench near Guam more than 10,900 m; Mindanao Deep 10,700 m; Tonga 9,450 m (a dated figure: standard references give about 10,800 m for the Tonga Trench, usually ranked second deepest; verify); Japanese Trench 8,500 m |

Check: 32 + 19 + 6 = 57, so the NCERT counts add up. **Choose the NCERT figures for the exam; remember that Leong differs (1,200 against 1,500 km for the Siberian shelf).**

## Minor features (NCERT pp4-5)

- **Mid-oceanic ridge**: two chains of mountains separated by a large depression; peaks as high as 2,500 m, some reaching the surface (Iceland is part of the mid-Atlantic ridge).
- **Seamount**: a pointed volcanic mountain that does not reach the surface, 3,000-4,500 m tall (Emperor seamount, an extension of the Hawaiian Islands).
- **Guyot**: a flat-topped seamount that shows gradual subsidence; more than 10,000 seamounts and guyots in the Pacific.
- **Submarine canyon**: deep valleys cutting across shelves and slopes, often off large river mouths (Hudson Canyon).

## Why it matters

The shelf's shallow water lets sunlight reach plankton, so shelves are the richest fishing grounds (Grand Banks, North Sea, Sunda Shelf; Leong p147). Shelf sediments are the source of fossil fuels (NCERT). Great ports such as Southampton, Hamburg and Rotterdam lie on shelves (Leong). Trenches matter for plate movement (Day 19 lesson GS-097).

## Surface temperature (NCERT p6)

The average surface temperature is about **27 degrees C** (an equatorial value; the hemispheric averages below are 19 and 16 degrees C) and falls from the equator towards the poles, generally by about 0.5 degrees C per degree of latitude: about 22 degrees C at 20 degrees, 14 degrees C at 40 degrees and near 0 degrees C at the poles. The 0.5 rule is rough: NCERT's own sample values imply about 0.25-0.4 degrees C per degree. The highest temperature is not at the equator but slightly north of it. The northern hemisphere's oceans average about 19 degrees C and the southern about 16 degrees C, because the north has more land touching the ocean (NCERT p5 on land and water).

**Slip check.** Leong (p150) gives about 21 degrees C at the equator and 13 degrees C at 45 degrees; NCERT gives about 27 degrees C. Learn NCERT's and treat Leong's as a variant.

## Layers (NCERT pp5-6)

A warm top layer about 500 m thick at 20-25 degrees C (the thickness varies, and NCERT's thermocline depths overlap it); below it the **thermocline** (500-1,000 m thick, beginning around 100-400 m down), where temperature falls fast; then a very cold layer to the floor. About 90 per cent of the water lies below the thermocline, near 0 degrees C. Polar waters have one cold layer only.

## Four controls (NCERT p5)

Latitude; unequal land and water; prevailing wind (offshore winds drive warm surface water away and cold water upwells); ocean currents (the Gulf Stream and North Atlantic Drift warm, keeping the Norwegian coast ice-free at 60-70 degrees N; the Labrador Current cools; Leong).

Ocean-current causes and patterns are in Day 17 lesson GS-102 (section 'Core: ocean currents, their causes, types and circulation').


### 5. Core: islands, coral reefs and the straits that cut islands off

*Objective:* Classify islands as continental or oceanic, name the strait that separates each continental example in Leong and state the coral-reef conditions.

## Two big classes of island (Leong pp138-139)

**Continental islands** were once part of the mainland and are now detached by subsidence or a rise in sea level, so their rocks, plants and animals resemble those of the mainland. They appear as:

- **Individual islands**, each cut off by a named strait:

| Island | Separated from the mainland by |
|---|---|
| Newfoundland | Strait of Belle Isle |
| Madagascar | Mozambique Channel |
| Sri Lanka | Palk Strait |
| Tasmania | Bass Strait |
| Formosa (Taiwan) | Formosa Strait |

- **Archipelagoes** (island groups): the British Isles, the Balearic Islands of the Mediterranean, the islands of the Aegean Sea.
- **Festoons or island arcs**: looped island chains marking the continuation of mountain ranges: the East Indies, Aleutian, Ryukyu, Kurile islands and other arcs of the Pacific coasts.

**Oceanic islands** lie in mid-ocean, unconnected with any mainland, with their own flora and fauna (the Galapagos have many unique species).

- **Volcanic** islands: tops of volcanic cones. Mauna Loa in Hawaii rises 4,169 m above sea level from a floor 5,490 m below, so the mountain is about 4,169 + 5,490 = 9,659 m tall from base to summit. Other volcanic examples: Galapagos, Azores, Ascension, St Helena, Madeira, the Canaries, Mauritius, Reunion, South Sandwich Islands, Bouvet.
- **Coral** islands: low islands built by coral animals: Marshall, Gilbert (now Kiribati) and Ellice (now Tuvalu) in the Pacific, Bermuda (Atlantic), Laccadives (now Lakshadweep) and Maldives (Indian Ocean); modern names: verify.

## Where corals grow (Leong pp140-141)

1. Water **not colder than 20 degrees C**, so reefs lie in tropical and sub-tropical seas; cold upwelling currents explain why reefs are generally absent on the **western coasts of continents**, while a warm current such as the Gulf Stream lets corals grow far north of the West Indies.
2. Depth **not more than 30 fathoms (about 55 m)**, because sunlight is needed by the algae on which the polyps depend; shallow water under 30 m is ideal.
3. **Saline, clear water**, free of sediment, so reefs avoid muddy river mouths; the outer, wave-washed edge of a reef grows best.

## Three reef types

- **Fringing reef**: a platform close to the shore, sometimes cut off by a shallow lagoon; completely absent where a stream enters.
- **Barrier reef**: separated from the coast by a wide, deep channel or lagoon, with narrow gaps for ships. The Great Barrier Reef off Queensland is 1,930 km long in Leong (standard references give about 2,300 km; verify), with a channel 160 km wide in places and over 60 m deep.
- **Atoll**: circular, enclosing a shallow lagoon with no land in the centre. Suvadiva in the Maldives has a lagoon over 64 km across.

Darwin's subsidence theory and Daly's glacial-control theory are in Day 17 lesson GS-102 (section 'Core: islands and coral reefs as coastal and oceanic landforms').

**For India:** the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep are treated in module 9 and in Day 17 lesson GS-130.


### 6. Core: enclosed seas, salinity and temperature

*Objective:* Explain why some seas are very salty and others fresh, using evaporation, river input and water mixing, and place the NCERT and Leong figures.

## Three causes (NCERT kegy212 pp6-8; Leong pp149-150)

Salinity is grams of salt per 1,000 grams of seawater, expressed in parts per thousand (per mille). The causes are in Day 17 lesson GS-109; for this map lesson remember the shorthand: **high evaporation and low fresh water give salty seas; large river input, melting ice and heavy rain give fresher seas; enclosed seas that mix poorly with the ocean keep their extremes.** Open-ocean salinity lies between 33 and 37 per mille (NCERT); the average is about 35 (Leong 35.2).

| Sea | What the books say | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Red Sea** | NCERT: as high as 41 per mille, land-locked. Leong: average 39 per mille; temperature 29-38 degrees C, the highest water temperature in the book | Much surface evaporation; no perennial river reaches it, only seasonal wadis (standard; verify) |
| **Baltic Sea** | Low: about 7 per mille (Leong) | Large river water, melting ice, cold climate |
| **Black Sea** | Very low (NCERT) | Enormous fresh water from rivers |
| **Mediterranean Sea** | High (NCERT); high, often over 37 (Leong) | High evaporation; poor mixing; the less saline Atlantic water flows in at the surface and denser water flows out below (Leong p152) |
| **North Sea** | Higher than its latitude suggests | Saline North Atlantic Drift (NCERT) |
| **Persian Gulf, Caspian** | Leong lists them with the Red Sea and Mediterranean as partly enclosed seas with poor mixing | Poor mixing, high evaporation |
| **Dead Sea, Lake Van, Great Salt Lake** | NCERT: Dead Sea 238, Lake Van 330, Great Salt Lake 220 per mille. Leong: Dead Sea 250, Lake Van 330 | Inland drainage; people float easily (book figures; see slip check) |

**Slip check.** The two books disagree on the Dead Sea (238 or 250 per mille) and the Red Sea (41 or 39); if a question quotes the books, use "about 240-250" and "about 40". Both books' Dead Sea and Lake Van figures look doubtful: standard references give the Dead Sea about 340 per mille and Lake Van far lower, in the low tens (verify). Leong also prints **180 per mille for the Caspian Sea**; standard references give about 12 (verify), because large rivers such as the Volga feed it. Do not memorise 180 or 330.

## Seas that have shrunk (standard; verify)

The 2018 Prelims item asked which of the Aral Sea, Black Sea and Lake Baikal "has shrunk immensely or dried up in the recent past due to human activities". The extracts do not name the Aral Sea. Standard knowledge (verify): it shrank after the rivers feeding it were diverted for irrigation; the Black Sea is a large open sea and Lake Baikal a deep freshwater lake.

## Isthmuses

An **isthmus** is a narrow neck of land. The Isthmus of Suez joins Africa and Asia; the Isthmus of Panama joins North and South America; the Isthmus of Kra in Thailand is the narrow part of the Malay peninsula where a canal has long been proposed but, as of writing, not built (verify).

## Why they matter for trade (the 2022 Mains theme)

1. **Short cuts.** Suez avoids the voyage round Africa; Panama avoids the voyage round South America (standard; verify).
2. **Chokepoints.** Because ships must pass through a narrow place, a large share of trade is concentrated there; closing, blocking or attacking it raises freight and insurance costs.
3. **Energy flows.** Hormuz is the exit for Persian Gulf oil and gas; the Sumed pipeline in Egypt, running from the Gulf of Suez on the Red Sea to the Mediterranean coast, offers a route that bypasses the canal (standard; verify).
4. **Law and fragility.** Navigation through straits is governed by the Law of the Sea (module 10); dependence on one canal makes supply lines fragile, and the alternatives (pipelines, longer routes) cost more.

## Method: 'which ships must pass through this?'

Find the sea a country faces, then the passage by which it reaches the ocean: Persian Gulf ports, Hormuz; Black Sea ports, the Bosporus and Dardanelles; Mediterranean to Indian Ocean, Suez and Bab-el-Mandeb unless the ship goes round Africa.

### 8. Core: the world's mountain systems, their alignment and their effect on weather

*Objective:* State the alignment, type and weather effect of the main ranges and explain the rule that links alignment to rainfall.

**Source status.** The extracts support: the Himalayan wall (NCERT kegy102 p5), the Western Ghats and parallel ranges on the western coasts of the Americas (Leong p135), the rift valleys and mountains of the Peninsula (kegy102). The table below is standard knowledge: **verify on an atlas**.

## The ranges

| Range | Where | Alignment | Type | Weather effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Andes** | Western South America | North-south along the Pacific coast | Young fold | Westerly belt in the south: wet west side, dry rain shadow east (Patagonia) |
| **Rockies** | Western North America | North-south | Young fold and uplift | Pacific-facing slopes wet, interior drier; Chinook, a warm dry lee wind, on the eastern slopes (Day 19 lesson GS-104, module 9) |
| **Appalachians** | Eastern North America | North-east to south-west | Old fold | Low barrier; modest effect |
| **Alps** | South-central Europe | Arcs west to east | Young fold | Barrier between the Mediterranean and north Europe; Foehn on the lee side (Day 19 lesson GS-104) |
| **Himalaya** | Between the subcontinent and Central Asia | West-east wall; NW-SE in the north-west, E-W in Darjiling and Sikkim, SW-NE in Arunachal Pradesh, N-S in Nagaland, Manipur and Mizoram (NCERT kegy102 p3, which misprints the Arunachal trend as 'southwest to the northwest') | Young fold | Shield from cold northern winds; traps monsoon (NCERT) |
| **Urals** | Russia | North-south | Old fold | Divide between Europe and Asia |
| **Western Ghats** | West coast of India | North-south | Edge of the Peninsula | Windward 250-400 cm; rain shadow east (Day 20 lesson GS-119) |
| **Vosges** | France, Europe | North-south along the Rhine rift | Block (horst), not fold | Local effects only (verify) |
| **Great Dividing Range** | Eastern Australia | North-south | Old uplifted | Wet coast, drier interior |

## The rule: alignment and wind

1. **Ranges across the wind** (north-south ranges in the westerly belt; the Western Ghats in the monsoon) force moist air up the **windward** side, which gets rain, and leave a **rain shadow** on the lee side.
2. **Ranges along a latitude** (the Himalaya, the Alps) act as barriers to north-south air movement: they stop cold air going south and trap warm moist air.

**Edge cases.** Rule 1 fails if the wind runs along the range (the monsoon break on the west coast, Day 20 lesson GS-119); rule 2 fails at gaps and passes. In the tropical Andes rule 1 reverses: the easterly trades make the eastern (Amazon) slopes wet, while the Pacific coast of Peru and northern Chile (Atacama) is dry, helped by the cold Peru (Humboldt) current (standard; verify).

## Fold or block?

Fold mountains are made by compression of rock layers (Day 19 lesson GS-097). Block mountains are blocks left high between faults; examples in the extracts are the Satpura block mountains of the Narmada-Tapi rift (kegy102 p3). The Vosges and the Black Forest flank the Rhine rift in Europe (standard; verify).


### 9. Core: India's physiography - the six divisions, the coasts and the islands

*Objective:* Name India's three geological divisions and six physiographic divisions with NCERT's figures, and place the coasts and island groups.

## Three geological blocks (NCERT kegy102 pp2-3)

The **Peninsular Block** (very ancient gneisses and granites, rigid since the Cambrian period, relict mountains such as the Aravali), the **Himalayas and other Peninsular mountains** (young, weak, flexible, with faults, folds and thrust plains; fast youthful rivers cut gorges and V-shaped valleys) and the **Indo-Ganga-Brahmaputra Plain** (a former depression filled by sediment; alluvium 1,000-2,000 m deep).

## Six physiographic divisions (pp4-9)

| Division | Key NCERT facts |
|---|---|
| **Northern and North-eastern Mountains** | Himalaya: parallel ranges; the Greater Himalayan range about 2,500 km long and 160-400 km wide (NCERT's wording; the figures describe the whole system, check); a physical barrier and a climatic, drainage and cultural divide |
| **Northern Plains** | About 3,200 km east to west, 150-300 km wide; Bhabar (8-10 km, rivers vanish), Tarai (10-20 km, marshy), Bhangar (old alluvium), Khadar (new alluvium); 50-150 m above sea level |
| **Peninsular Plateau** | 600-900 m; Deccan Plateau between the Western and Eastern Ghats; Central Highlands; North-eastern plateau (Meghalaya, Karbi Anglong); Anaimudi (2,695 m) is its highest peak, Dodabetta (2,637 m) next; the two Ghats meet at the Nilgiri hills |
| **Indian Desert** | North-west of the Aravali; rainfall below 150 mm; Luni river in the south; barchans and longitudinal dunes |
| **Coastal Plains** | West: narrow, submerged, no deltas, natural ports (Kandla, Mazagaon, JLN Port, Marmagao, Mangalore, Cochin); East: broader, emergent, deltas of the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri, shelf up to 500 km (NCERT) |

## Method 1: walk the coast

To list countries on a sea, start at one end and walk around the shore in one direction. For the Mediterranean (2017 Prelims): from Spain along the north shore through France, Monaco, Italy, the Balkan coast (Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia, Montenegro, Albania), Greece and Turkey, then south through Syria, Lebanon, Israel and along the African shore through Egypt, Libya, Tunisia, Algeria and Morocco (standard; verify). Jordan and Iraq do not appear: Jordan's short coast is on the Gulf of Aqaba at the Red Sea; Iraq's is on the Persian Gulf. So of Jordan, Iraq, Lebanon and Syria, only Lebanon and Syria border the Mediterranean.

## Method 2: pair a region with its country and reject by contradiction

Learn each region name with one anchor fact: Catalonia (north-east Spain), Crimea (a peninsula in the Black Sea, linked in the news to Ukraine and Russia), Mindanao (island of the Philippines), Oromia and Amhara (regions of Ethiopia), Anatolia (the Asian part of Turkey), Cabo Delgado (northern Mozambique), Mallorca (Spanish island), Normandy (northern France), Sardinia (Italian island) (all standard; verify). A pair fails if the region is clearly in a different country.

## Method 3: count by listing neighbours

For 'how many share a border with Ukraine' list Ukraine's neighbours first (Belarus, Russia, Poland, Slovakia, Hungary, Romania, Moldova; standard, verify) and then tick the options; do the same for Afghanistan and for the North Sea. The risk is a country that is near but not adjacent (Bulgaria is on the Black Sea but not on Ukraine's border).

## Method 4: three numbers for the Law of the Sea (2022)

Territorial sea up to 12 nautical miles from the baseline; Exclusive Economic Zone not beyond 200 nautical miles from the baseline; ships of all states have the right of innocent passage through the territorial sea (standard; check the Convention text).

## Method 5: the equator test

The equator crosses Lake Victoria; Lake Tanganyika lies south of it, the Tonle Sap in Cambodia north of it and the Patos Lagoon in southern Brazil south of it (standard; verify).

## Negative marking: when is a guess worth it?

A wrong Prelims answer costs one-third of 2 marks, 0.67. A blind guess among four options: 0.25 x 2 - 0.75 x 0.67 = 0.5 - 0.5 = 0. After safely eliminating two options, a guess between the other two gives 0.5 x 2 - 0.5 x 0.67 = 1.00 - 0.33 = 0.67 marks on average, which is positive. Eliminate by contradiction first.

### Notes: Definitions to know cold

- Ocean: one of the five great water bodies; sea, gulf and bay are parts of oceans (NCERT, Leong).
- Continental shelf: the shallow margin of a continent; slope: the steep link to the basin; deep-sea plain: the flat basin floor; deeps or trenches: the deepest narrow basins.
- Mid-oceanic ridge, seamount, guyot, submarine canyon, atoll: the minor relief features (NCERT p4-5).
- Continental island: formerly part of the mainland; oceanic island: volcanic or coral, unconnected with a mainland (Leong).
- Strait: narrow passage between two lands joining two waters; canal: human-made; isthmus: narrow neck of land joining two lands and separating two waters.
- Fold mountain: made by compression of rock layers; block mountain: a block left high between faults.

### Notes: Causes of salinity and temperature differences

- High evaporation with few rivers gives high salinity: Red Sea, Mediterranean, Arabian Sea.
- Large river water, melting ice and heavy rain give low salinity: Baltic, Black Sea, Bay of Bengal, Arctic waters.
- Poor mixing in enclosed seas keeps extremes; free mixing in open oceans keeps salinity near 35 per mille.
- The warm North Atlantic Drift raises the North Sea's salinity and keeps the Norwegian coast ice-free; the cold Labrador Current icebinds eastern Canadian ports for about half the year (Leong).
- Cold upwelling currents explain why coral reefs are generally absent on the western coasts of continents.

### Notes: Confused pairs

- Strait versus canal: Gibraltar, Hormuz, Malacca are natural; Suez and Panama are canals.
- Isthmus versus strait: land joining two lands versus water joining two waters.
- Black Sea (very low salinity) versus Caspian (large enclosed inland sea; Leong's 180 per mille is suspect).
- Volcanic island versus coral island: high cones (Hawaii, Mauritius) versus low reef islands (Maldives, Lakshadweep).
- Nine, Ten and Eleven Degree Channels: Ten separates Andaman from Nicobar; for Lakshadweep see Day 17 lesson GS-130.
- Ocean (five) versus sea: seas are parts of oceans.
