## GS-378: Emotional intelligence concepts and models

Mains GS4 · Ethics · Attitude, aptitude and emotional intelligence

*Goal:* Explain Emotional intelligence concepts and models and use it to reason through the worked example.

### Concept and distinctions

Emotional intelligence concerns recognizing, understanding and managing emotions in oneself and interactions with others. Common models distinguish self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy and social skills. Emotional control means responding deliberately, not denying that emotions exist.

### How to apply it

Emotions carry information but are not automatically reliable verdicts. Anger may signal perceived unfairness while also narrowing attention. An emotionally intelligent response names the feeling, checks the evidence and chooses an action suited to the responsibility. Assessments of EI differ by model, so avoid presenting one list as a universally agreed scientific taxonomy. Administrative value lies in better judgment and relationships, not merely appearing calm.

### Worked example

An officer notices irritation during a complaint, pauses, and checks whether a genuine service failure explains the citizen's anger.

### Recall

- - **q:** How does self-regulation differ from suppressing emotion?
  - **a:** It recognizes the emotion and chooses a deliberate response rather than pretending the emotion is absent.

### Common trap

Assuming emotional intelligence means always agreeing or remaining visibly cheerful.
