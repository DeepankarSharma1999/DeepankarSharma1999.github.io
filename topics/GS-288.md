## GS-288: CBD, CITES, Ramsar

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Climate and treaties

*Goal:* Explain CBD, CITES, Ramsar and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### CBD, Nagoya and India's law

The CBD, one of the key agreements of the 1992 Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro, has three aims: conserve biological diversity, use its components sustainably, and share benefits from genetic resources fairly. India ratified it in February 1994 and passed the Biological Diversity Act in 2002 (amended 2023), with rules in 2004 replaced by the Biological Diversity Rules, 2024. The Nagoya Protocol on access and benefit sharing was adopted in 2010 and is implemented through the same Act. Implementation runs through the National Biodiversity Authority, State Biodiversity Boards and local Biodiversity Management Committees, which have documented 2.72 lakh People's Biodiversity Registers. India accounts for 57.35% of internationally recognised certificates of compliance published under Nagoya.

### Ramsar: wetlands of international importance

Ramsar work in MoEFCC sits with the Wetlands Division, alongside the National Plan for Conservation of Aquatic Ecosystems and the Wetlands Rules. In 2025 India designated eleven new sites, rising from 85 to 96, and reached 98 in January 2026, ranking first in Asia and third worldwide. At the Ramsar COP15 held at Victoria Falls in July 2025, India's draft resolution on sustainable lifestyles for wise use of wetlands was adopted, Indore and Udaipur became accredited Wetland Cities, and India won a seat on the Standing Committee for 2026-2028, its third term since 1999-2002.

### CITES: appendices, permits and India's law

CITES was signed at Washington DC on 3 March 1973 and amended at Bonn on 22 June 1979. It regulates cross-border trade, meaning export, re-export, import and introduction from the sea, in species listed in its Appendices I, II and III. India's Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 incorporates these Appendices as Schedule IV and implements the Convention through Chapter VB. The Centre designates a Management Authority, an officer not below Additional Director General of Forests, to issue permits and certificates and submit reports, and Scientific Authorities that advise it and track Appendix II exports so that species stay well above the level that would justify Appendix I listing. Exports of Appendix I or II specimens need a prior export permit. In November 2023 India was removed from the Review of Significant Trade for Red Sanders and placed in Category 1 of the National Legislation Programme.
