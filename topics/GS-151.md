## GS-151: Poverty and deprivation

Mains GS1 + Prelims GS1 · Society · Indian society

*Goal:* Explain Poverty and deprivation and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Two ways of measuring poverty

Consumption-based poverty lines count people whose monthly spending falls below a threshold; NCERT's table for 2011–12 uses Rs 816 per person per month in rural areas and Rs 1,000 in urban areas. Multidimensional measures instead ask whether people face several deprivations at once. NITI Aayog's National MPI covers three dimensions, health, education and standard of living, through 12 indicators including nutrition, child and adolescent mortality, maternal health, years of schooling, school attendance, cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets and bank accounts. Its headline number combines the headcount ratio (how many are poor) with intensity (how deprived the poor are). The 2023 edition estimates poverty for States and 707 districts, enabling targeted action rather than a single national figure.

### What recent data show

Between NFHS-4 (2015–16) and NFHS-5 (2019–21), the multidimensionally poor share fell from 24.85% to 14.96%, a drop of 9.89 percentage points that NITI equates to about 135.5 million people. The MPI value nearly halved, from 0.117 to 0.066, and the report says India is on course to meet SDG target 1.2 well before 2030. Rural areas improved fastest, but the gap remains wide: 19.28% of rural residents were multidimensionally poor in 2019–21 versus 5.27% of urban residents. Bihar saw the fastest absolute reduction, from 51.89% to 33.76%, and Uttar Pradesh recorded the largest number exiting poverty, 3.43 crore. All 12 indicators improved; sanitation and cooking fuel deprivations fell most, while nutrition and schooling gains also contributed.

### Poverty as structured deprivation

NCERT's tables show poverty is socially patterned. In 2011–12 nearly half of rural Scheduled Tribe members and almost a third of rural Scheduled Castes were below the poverty line, compared with about a quarter of the rural population overall; urban poverty was lower but SC, ST and Muslim groups again exceeded the average. Although rich and poor are found in every caste, the macro link between caste and economic status persists. On hunger, Amartya Sen's analysis shows famines can occur through a 'failure of entitlements', when people cannot buy or obtain food, even without falling output; MGNREGA is cited as a state response to rural hunger.
