## GS-380: Indian ethical thinkers and their ideas

Mains GS4 · Ethics · Thinkers, administration and probity

*Goal:* Explain Indian ethical thinkers and their ideas and use it to reason through the worked example.

### Concept and distinctions

Indian ethical thought includes diverse approaches to duty, non-violence, compassion, self-discipline and social justice. Buddhist and Jain traditions, the Bhagavad Gita, Gandhi and Ambedkar address different questions and cannot be reduced to one doctrine. Identify a thinker's relevant argument before applying it.

### How to apply it

Gandhi emphasizes the relationship between means and ends; Ambedkar connects liberty, equality and fraternity with institutional and social transformation. Jain ahimsa highlights restraint from harm, while Buddhist ethics relates conduct to suffering and its cessation. Compare what each approach illuminates and where administrative duties create tensions. Use accurate paraphrases when an exact quotation has not been checked against its source.

### Worked example

A policy justified by welfare goals still requires scrutiny of its means, applying Gandhi's concern with ethical method.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why should thinkers be compared through arguments rather than slogans?
  - **a:** Their concepts address different problems and have distinct assumptions, implications and limits.

### Common trap

Inventing a quotation or attributing every ethical idea to the same thinker.
