## GS-253: Food Corporation and buffer stocks

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Agriculture and food

*Goal:* Explain Food Corporation and buffer stocks and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Purpose of a buffer stock

A buffer stock is grain held in reserve to smooth supply between good and bad years. In India the Green Revolution made it possible: once farmers sold surplus rice and wheat, government could procure and store grain to be released when shortage struck. NCERT describes FCI's buffer stocks of wheat and rice as one of three linked instruments — the others being MSP and the PDS. Stocks therefore connect the farm gate, through procurement, with the ration shop, through distribution.

### FCI's mandate and the procurement-to-release cycle

Food management pursues three aims: buy grain from farmers at remunerative prices, supply it affordably to consumers, especially vulnerable groups, and keep buffer stocks for food security and price stability. Its two price instruments are procurement at MSP and the Central Issue Price charged for distribution, and FCI is the nodal body for procurement, storage and distribution (Economic Survey 2023-24). A decentralised procurement scheme helps keep central-pool wheat and rice adequate; by mid-2024 combined stocks exceeded 600 LMT. On the release side, the Open Market Sale of staple grains is used to stabilise prices (Economic Survey 2025-26). Every step costs money. The economic cost, made up of pooled grain cost, procurement incidentals and distribution cost, was ₹3,931.34 per quintal for rice and ₹2,709.59 for wheat in 2023-24 (RE).

### Excess stocks, storage and reform

The Economic Survey 2025-26 warns that rice and wheat procurement has grown faster than food-security needs, so buffer stocks stay high and storage, handling, interest and rotation costs climb. It proposes channelling those savings into voluntary crop diversification, with procurement volumes and buffer norms adjusting automatically to keep food security intact. Storage remains the weak link NCERT identified, with more than a tenth of farm produce lost for want of it. Recent responses include steel silos built under PPP, hub-and-spoke silos with rail sidings, Flospan mobile storage units and sensor-based smart warehouses piloted with the World Food Programme (Economic Survey 2024-25), plus godowns at PACS under the decentralised grain storage plan. Good answers weigh farmer support and price stability against fiscal cost.
