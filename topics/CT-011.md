## CT-011: Ratio, proportion and variation

Prelims CSAT (qualifying) · CSAT · Quantitative aptitude

### Bridge: Reading a ratio: the minimum notation

A **ratio** compares two quantities of the same kind by division. The notation **3 : 5** is read 'three to five' and means the first quantity is 3 parts when the second is 5 parts. It is the same as the fraction 3/5 for calculation, but it speaks about a pair, not about one number.

Three habits prevent most errors.

1. **Order matters.** 3 : 5 and 5 : 3 are different. 'Boys to girls' puts boys first.
2. **Same unit.** Compare 30 minutes with 2 hours only after converting: 30 : 120 = 1 : 4.
3. **A ratio has no unit.** 3 : 5 of money and 3 : 5 of weight are both just 3 : 5.

A ratio does not change if both terms are multiplied or divided by the same non-zero number: 6 : 10 = 3 : 5 = 12 : 20. These are **equivalent ratios**. A ratio is in **simplest form** when its terms have no common factor (use the divisibility tests of Day 17 lesson CT-008 module 4 to cancel).

A **part** is one unit of the ratio. If two people share money in the ratio 3 : 5, there are 3 + 5 = 8 equal parts in all. The first person's share is 3/8 of the money, the second's 5/8. To turn a ratio into a share, always add the parts first.

Two quantities are in **proportion** when two ratios are equal, written a : b = c : d, or a/b = c/d. Cross-multiplying gives a x d = b x c (the cross-multiplication of Day 19 lesson CT-009 modules 2 to 4).

## The one idea behind all five

A ratio fixes **proportions, not amounts**, so you must find **one real quantity** (a total, a saving, a common term) to turn parts into numbers. The second idea is the **constant k**: when two quantities move together, y/x (or x y) stays fixed and k carries information from the given case to the asked case.

### 2. Ratio: simplifying, sharing, chains and fractional ratios

*Objective:* Share a sum in a ratio, clear fractions inside a ratio, join two ratios into a chain, and handle a ratio of points on a line.

## Sharing a sum

If an amount is shared in the ratio a : b : c, add the parts a + b + c, find the value of one part (amount divided by the sum) and multiply. Original example: 4,200 in 2 : 5 has 7 parts of 600, so **1,200 and 3,000**.

## Fractional ratios (2020 Q57)

A ratio of fractions is cleared by multiplying every term by the LCM of the denominators. The verified 2020 item shares 2,500 in 1/2 : 3/4 : 5/6. LCM of 2, 4, 6 is 12, so the terms become 6 : 9 : 10. That is 25 parts, 100 per part, shares **600, 900, 1,000**, and the gap between largest and smallest is **400**.

## Chains

When a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5, the common term b has different values (3 and 4). Make it equal using the LCM, 12: a : b = 8 : 12 and b : c = 12 : 15, so **a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15**, and a : c = 8 : 15. Merging as 2 : 3 : 5 is the typical mistake.

## Ratio, fraction and percent

Boys : girls = 3 : 5 means boys are 3/8 of the class (37.5%), but 3/5 = 60% **of the girls**. The base differs; add the parts first.

## Ratios on a line (2021 Q63)

P, Q, R lie on a straight line with PQ : QR = 3 : 5. Put Q at 0. Text figure for the possible orders:

    Order 1:  P ---3--- Q ------5------ R      PR = 8,  PQ : PR = 3 : 8
    Order 2:  Q ---3--- P --2-- R              P between Q and R: PR = 2,  PQ : PR = 3 : 2
    Impossible:  P ----- R --- Q               R between P and Q would make PQ longer than QR

R cannot lie between P and Q (PQ would be longer than QR, but PQ : QR = 3 : 5). So there are **two** values, n = 2: draw before you divide.

## A ratio read from data (2018 Q13)

The verified 2018 item reads a ratio of two import series from a data display not in the project's text record, so no number is computed. Method: total each series over the months asked, divide the first total by the second, and compare with the options (1 : 1, 1.2 : 1, 1.4 : 1, 1.6 : 1).

### 3. Proportion, the common-term trick and continued proportion

*Objective:* Compare two ratios by making a shared term equal, solve ratio-plus-condition items with one unknown, and handle X : Y = Y : Z.

## The common-term trick (2026 Q58)

When two ratios each hold one **shared quantity**, rewrite both so that quantity has the same value. The verified 2026 item: male : female is 13 : 10 in company A and 7 : 5 in company B, and both have the same number of women. Let each have F women. A has 13F/10 men, so A has 23F/10 workers. B has 7F/5 = 14F/10 men and 12F/5 workers in all. Ratio A : B = (23/10) / (12/5) = **23 : 24**. Original example: pens : pencils is 3 : 4 in box P and 5 : 6 in box Q, with the same number of pencils. Use 12 pencils (the LCM of 4 and 6): P has 9 pens, Q has 10, so pens P : Q = **9 : 10**.

## A ratio plus a condition: find the real amount

A ratio is never enough alone; an actual number fixes the scale. Pattern: **write the parts, turn the condition into an equation, solve, then add**.

**2017 Q74** (verified): incomes 4 : 3, expenses 3 : 2, savings 6,000 each. Write incomes 4k and 3k, expenses 3e and 2e, so 4k - 3e = 6,000 and 3k - 2e = 6,000. Multiply the first by 2 and the second by 3: 8k - 6e = 12,000 and 9k - 6e = 18,000. Subtract: k = 6,000, so incomes 24,000 and 18,000, total **42,000**. Check: e = 6,000, expenses 18,000 and 12,000, savings 6,000 each.

## A ratio and an overall percent (2021 Q30)

Marks are in the ratio 5 : 6 : 7 : 8 : 9 : 10 over six papers of equal maximum M, with overall 60%. Total scored = 0.6 x 6M = 3.6M over 45 parts, so one part is 0.08M. The papers score 0.40M, 0.48M, 0.56M, 0.64M, 0.72M, 0.80M; the first three are below 0.60M. The answer is **3**.

## Continued proportion (2025 Q75)

X : Y = Y : Z means Y squared = X Z (Y is the **geometric mean**). The verified item adds X + Y + Z = 37 runs. Runs are whole numbers, and a search of the positive whole-number triples with sum 37 and Y squared = X Z finds only **(9, 12, 16)** and **(16, 12, 9)** (12 squared = 144 = 9 x 16). In one X < Y < Z, in the other X > Y > Z; the stem does not choose, so the order is **not determined**. A ratio does not tell you which term is the larger.

### 4. Direct, inverse and joint variation, and exchange chains

*Objective:* Write a variation statement as an equation with a constant k, find k from one case and use it for another, and solve exchange and scaling items.

## The three forms

Two quantities **vary directly** if their quotient stays fixed: y = k x. Double x and y doubles. They **vary inversely** if their product stays fixed: y = k / x. Double x and y halves. Joint variation combines them: y = k x / z means y varies directly with x and inversely with z. The letter k is the **constant of variation**. Method: **write the equation, find k from the given case, use k for the asked case.**

## 2026 Q30 (verified)

y is directly proportional to x squared, so y = c x squared. From y = 50 and x = 5: 50 = c x 25, c = 2. For y = 98: x squared = 49, so x = 7 (positive values). Also x = k / z: 5 = k / (7/25) gives k = 7/5, so z = k / x = (7/5) / 7 = **1/5**.

## Scaling (2020 Q38)

Lengths times s give areas times s squared and volumes times s cubed. Side of Q is twice that of P, so y = 8x; mass n = 2m. Then v = 2m / 8x = u / 4, so **u = 4v**.

## Exchange chains (2018 Q68 and 2024 Q36)

A lift holds 18 adults **or** 30 children, so they exchange at 18 adults = 30 children, that is 1 adult = 5/3 children. Twelve adults use 12/18 = 2/3 of the capacity, leaving 1/3, which is **10 children**. 

In 2024 Q36, 6 boys = 7 girls = 3 men = 4 women in weight. The weight of 4 women is 4 x 63 = 252 kg, so 6 boys weigh 252 kg and one boy averages 252 / 6 = **42 kg**. Trap: 63 is for one woman, not the group.

## Rate x time = work: an inverse pair (2025 Q9)

Distance, work or runs equal rate x time, so for a fixed total, rate and time vary inversely. In the verified 2025 item, Team Y's time is 10% less than 20 overs: 18 overs. Rate = N / 18. A rate 50% higher is 1.5 N / 18 = N / 12, and N / 12 x 12 = N. The second condition **repeats** the first and fixes nothing, so N **cannot be determined** (option (d)). Always check whether a condition is new information.

### 5. Rates in clock problems: strikes, gaining and losing clocks

*Objective:* Separate gaps from strikes in clock-strike items and convert a gaining or losing clock's reading to the correct time using a ratio of rates.

## Strikes and gaps (2017 Q33)

A clock that strikes n times has **n - 1 gaps** between the strikes. If the stem gives the time for the whole sequence, the usual reading is: time = gaps x length of one gap. The verified 2017 item: 5 strikes take 12 seconds, so 4 gaps take 12 s and one gap is 3 s. Ten strikes have 9 gaps, so **27 s**.

That number is **not among the four printed options** (20, 24, 28, 30). Two other readings: time proportional to the number of strikes gives 12 / 5 x 10 = 24 s, which is option (b); counting each strike and each gap as one equal unit (5 + 4 = 9 units for 12 s) gives 12 / 9 x 19 = 25.33 s, also not an option. So this stem is ambiguous or carries a printed slip.

**Bracket check.** Let each strike last s and each gap g, both at least 0. Then 5s + 4g = 12, so g is at most 3, and ten strikes take 10s + 9g = 2(5s + 4g) + g = 24 + g, between 24 s (gaps take no time) and 27 s (strikes take no time). Of the printed options only (b), 24, lies in that range, and the gap method's 27 s is its top end. This is analysis, not the official key: state the reading used and **verify against the official key**.

## A clock that gains or loses (2019 Q49)

The key idea: the clock's reading and the real time are **two quantities in a fixed ratio**. If a clock gains 10 minutes in every 24 hours, then in 1,440 real minutes the clock shows 1,450. So clock time : real time = 1,450 : 1,440 = 145 : 144.

The verified item: set right at 8:00 a.m. Monday, the clock shows 6:00 p.m. Wednesday. The clock reading has advanced by 2 days 10 hours = 58 hours = 3,480 minutes. Real time elapsed = 3,480 x 1,440 / 1,450 = **3,456 minutes = 57 hours 36 minutes**. Adding to 8:00 a.m. Monday gives 8:00 a.m. Wednesday plus 9 hours 36 minutes = **5:36 p.m. Wednesday**.

## A clock that loses

If a clock loses g minutes per 24 real hours, clock : real = (1,440 - g) : 1,440, so real = clock x 1,440 / (1,440 - g). Original: loses 16 minutes a day gives 1,424 : 1,440 = 89 : 90, so 89 hours of clock reading is 90 hours of real time. A losing clock's correct time is later than the time it shows.

### 6. Mixtures: ingredient tracking, replacement and alligation

*Objective:* Track one ingredient through a mixing or replacement process, use the retention factor for repeated replacement, and apply alligation to two mixtures.

## Track one ingredient

In any mixture item, follow **one ingredient** (water, copper, the first liquid); the other is the total minus it. 

**2017 Q37** (verified): milk with 50% water; one-third of it is added to an equal amount of pure milk. Take 3 units of the sample. One-third is 1 unit holding 0.5 unit water. Adding 1 unit of pure milk gives 0.5 unit water in 2 units: **25%** (option (a)). The stem's 'equal amount' is read as equal to the one-third portion; other readings give 12.5% or 16 2/3%, neither offered. Day 20 lesson CT-010 also works this item.

## Repeated replacement

Draw r litres from a container of V litres, replace by the other liquid, and the original liquid left is multiplied by (1 - r/V), because a uniform mixture loses the same fraction of every ingredient. After n rounds the original liquid left is **V (1 - r/V) to the power n**.

| Item | V | r | n | Original left | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 Q68 | 20 | 4 | 2 | 20 x 0.8 x 0.8 = 12.8 | 7.2 |
| 2026 Q79 | 10 | 1 | 3 | 10 x 0.9 x 0.9 x 0.9 = 7.29 | 2.71 |

For 2020 Q68 the ratio is 12.8 : 7.2 = **16 : 9** (option (c)). For 2026 Q79 the other liquid is 2.71 litres, **27.1%**, approximately 27 (option (b)). Trap: subtracting 4 + 4 from 20.

## Two containers (2022 Q69)

X has 100 ml milk, Y has 100 ml water. 20 ml of milk goes X to Y, then 20 ml of the mixed Y comes back. Y then holds 120 ml (20 milk, 100 water); the 20 ml returned is one-sixth of it, with 3.33 ml milk and 16.67 ml water. X has 83.33 ml milk in 100 ml (m = 5/6); Y has 83.33 ml water in 100 ml (n = 5/6). So **m = n** (option (a)): the milk missing from X is exactly the water missing from Y.

## Alligation

Mixing strengths s1 < s2 into strength m needs the weaker and stronger in the ratio **(s2 - m) : (m - s1)**. Line figure:

    P (20%) ------------------ R (50%) ------ Q (60%)
              distance 30            distance 10      amounts P : Q = 10 : 30 = 1 : 3

**2026 Q65** (verified): R needs equal copper and zinc, so 50% copper; P is 20% and Q 60%. P : Q = (60 - 50) : (50 - 20) = **1 : 3** (option (a)). Check: 1 part P and 3 parts Q have copper (0.2 + 3 x 0.6) / 4 = 0.5.

### 7. Partnership: profit shared by capital x time

*Objective:* Share a profit among partners in the ratio of capital x time, including fractional capitals and a given difference of shares.

## The rule

Each partner's **investment unit** is capital x time (for example rupee-months). Profit is shared in the ratio of these units.

- Equal time: the profit ratio equals the capital ratio.
- Equal capital: the profit ratio equals the time ratio.
- Both differ: multiply, then compare.

Reason: profit scales with rupees x months.

## 2024 Q25 (verified)

P puts in 14,000 more than Q, for 8 months against Q's 10. Of a 2,000 profit P's share is 400 more than Q's. Shares sum to 2,000 with a difference of 400, so P = 1,200 and Q = 800, ratio **3 : 2**. Let Q's capital be q; P's is q + 14,000. Then 8 (q + 14,000) : 10 q = 3 : 2, so 16 (q + 14,000) = 30 q, and 14 q = 224,000, q = 16,000. P's capital is **30,000** (option (a)). Check: 30,000 x 8 = 240,000 and 16,000 x 10 = 160,000, which are in the ratio 3 : 2.

## 2026 Q78 (verified): fractions of capital and time

Let the total capital be 1 and the total duration be 1. A: capital 1/3, time 1/3, unit 1/9. B: capital 1/4, time 1/4, unit 1/16. C: the remaining capital 1 - 1/3 - 1/4 = 5/12 for the whole time, unit 5/12. Write over 144: A = 16, B = 9, C = 60, total 85. C gets 60/85 x 17,000 = 200 x 60 = **12,000** (option (a)). Trap: using capitals alone gives C 5/12 of the profit, 7,083.

## Original worked example

A invests 20,000 for 12 months and B invests 25,000 for 6 months. Units: 240,000 and 150,000, ratio 8 : 5 after dividing by 30,000. A profit of 13,000 splits into 13 parts of 1,000: A **8,000**, B **5,000**.

## Late joining

If B joins 4 months into a 12-month year, B's time is 8 months. Write the units as a table: partner, capital, months, product. When the stem gives a **difference** of shares (2024 Q25), turn it into the ratio of shares first.

### 8. Method, timing, traps, ambiguity and when to guess

*Objective:* Choose a first move for each of the five families, state the reading used when a stem is ambiguous, and apply the marking arithmetic to a guess.

## A six-step routine (practice target 60 to 90 seconds)

1. **Name the family** (A to E) in five seconds.
2. **Name the real quantity** that turns parts into numbers: a total, a common term, a saving, a time.
3. **Choose the form:** table of parts, y = k x, retention factor, or capital x time.
4. **Calculate** with simple numbers; keep fractions to the end.
5. **Test the answer** back into the stem.
6. **Match the option** and note any guess.

## Traps in the verified and original items

- **Order in a chain (original example):** merging 2 : 3 and 4 : 5 as 2 : 3 : 5 (module 2).
- **Common term not made equal** (module 3).
- **One ratio, many orders:** X : Y = Y : Z does not fix which is largest (2025 Q75); 'r < p + q' does not make r the smallest (2021 Q70, with p, q, r positive: p > q + r makes p the largest, so statement 1 holds; p = 1, q = 5, r = 3 breaks statement 2).
- **A condition that repeats the data** (2025 Q9).
- **Two geometric orders** (2021 Q63: n = 2).

## Ambiguous stems

Several stems need a stated reading: 2017 Q33 (gap reading 27 s is not an option; any non-negative reading lies between 24 s and 27 s, and only (b), 24, is printed in that range), 2018 Q13 (data display absent from the record), 2025 Q75 (whole runs), 2021 Q70 (positive amounts). Solve as printed, name the reading, and assert no official key.

## Marking arithmetic and guessing

Each item carries 2.5 marks and a wrong answer costs one-third of that, **0.833** (verify the current notification). With four options and no elimination a blind guess has an expected value of 0.25 x 2.5 - 0.75 x 0.833 = 0.625 - 0.625 = **0**. With one option eliminated, three remain: (1/3) x 2.5 - (2/3) x 0.833 = 0.833 - 0.556 = **+0.278**. So guess only after eliminating at least one option, and record the guess in the error log (see Day 12 lesson CT-006 module 5).

## Elimination that works here

In a sharing item every share is below the total and the largest ratio term gets the most. After two rounds of replacement less of the original liquid is left than after one. A gaining clock's correct time is earlier than the time it shows.

### Notes: Definitions

- Ratio: a comparison of two quantities of the same kind by division; a : b with the order mattering and no unit.
- Equivalent ratios: ratios with the same value, got by multiplying or dividing both terms by the same non-zero number.
- Proportion: an equality of two ratios, a : b = c : d, equivalent to a x d = b x c.
- Common term: a quantity that appears in two ratios and must be given the same value before the ratios are joined or compared.
- Direct variation: y = k x, so y/x is fixed. Inverse variation: y = k / x, so x y is fixed. Joint variation: y = k x / z.
- Constant of variation k: the fixed quotient or product found from a given case.
- Continued proportion: X : Y = Y : Z, so Y squared = X Z.
- Retention factor: the fraction (1 - r/V) of the original liquid that stays after r litres are drawn from V and replaced.
- Alligation: finding the ratio in which two things of different strengths are mixed to get a given strength.
- Partnership unit: capital x time, in which profit is shared.

### Notes: Formulae and rules

- Share of a part = (part / sum of parts) x amount.
- Fractional ratio: multiply all terms by the LCM of the denominators.
- Chain a : b and b : c: make b equal using the LCM of its two values; then a : b : c.
- y = k x (direct); y = k / x (inverse); y = k x / z (joint).
- Scaling by length factor s: area times s squared, volume times s cubed.
- Total = rate x time; for a fixed total, rate and time vary inversely.
- Clock that gains g minutes per 24 hours: clock : real = (1,440 + g) : 1,440; real = clock x 1,440 / (1,440 + g).
- n strikes have n - 1 gaps (usual reading; flagged for 2017 Q33).
- Replacement: original left = V (1 - r/V)^n.
- Alligation: weaker : stronger = (s2 - m) : (m - s1).
- Partnership: profit ratio = ratio of capital x time.
- Clock that loses g minutes per 24 real hours: clock : real = (1,440 - g) : 1,440; real = clock x 1,440 / (1,440 - g). Original: g = 16 gives 89 : 90.

**A.** 7 parts of 600: 1,800 and 2,400.

**Q.** Simplify 45 : 60 : 75.

**A.** Divide by 15: 3 : 4 : 5.

**Q.** If y = 3 x and x = 4, what is y? If y x = 12 and x = 4, what is y?

**A.** 12; and 3.

**Q.** Why is the ratio of total workers not obtained by comparing 13 : 10 with 7 : 5 directly?

**Q.** Why does repeated replacement multiply by (1 - r/V) each round rather than subtract r?

**A.** The mixture is uniform, so each draw removes the same fraction r/V of every ingredient, not r litres of the original.

**Q.** Why can 2025 Q9 not be solved for N?

**A.** The condition that a 50% higher rate gives 12 overs is true for every N (1.5 N / 18 x 12 = N), so it adds no equation.

**Q.** Why does a clock that gains 10 minutes in 24 hours need a ratio, not a subtraction of 10 minutes a day?

**A.** The gain accrues per real time, but the elapsed time you see is clock time; converting by 1,440 / 1,450 gives real time exactly. Counting clock days (58/24) instead of real days would also slip.

**Q.** Prices 40 and 55 mixed to 48: why are the amounts in the ratio 7 : 8 and not 8 : 7?

**A.** The mean is closer to 55 by 7 and to 40 by 8, so more of the cheaper is needed: cheaper : dearer = 7 : 8. Check: (7 x 40 + 8 x 55) / 15 = 48.

**Q.** A and B invest 20,000 for 12 months and 25,000 for 6 months. Share a 13,000 profit.

**A.** Units 240,000 and 150,000, ratio 8 : 5: 8,000 and 5,000.

**Q.** A wall takes 12 men 15 days. How long would 9 men take at the same pace?

**A.** 180 man-days / 9 = 20 days.

### Memory skeleton

- Five item families: split and compare ratios, variation and exchange, clock rates, mixtures, partnership (the lesson's own sorting)
- A ratio fixes proportions, not amounts: find one real quantity; share by parts; clear a fractional ratio with the LCM of the denominators
- Make the shared term equal before joining or comparing; ratio plus a condition becomes an equation; continued proportion Y² = XZ
- Variation: y = kx, y = k/x, y = kx/z; find k from the given case; scaling s² and s³; exchange chains; fixed total makes rate and time inverse
- Clocks: n strikes have n − 1 gaps (usual reading, state it); a gaining or losing clock is a ratio (1,440 ± g) : 1,440
- Mixtures: track one ingredient; retention factor (1 − r/V)^n; alligation (s2 − m) : (m − s1)
- Partnership in capital × time; routine: family, real quantity, form, calculate, test, match; state the reading of a loose stem
