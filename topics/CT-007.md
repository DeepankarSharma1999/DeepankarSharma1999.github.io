## CT-007: Timed mixed passage practice

Prelims CSAT (qualifying) · CSAT · Reading comprehension

### 1. Why practise in timed mixed sets

*Objective:* State what a timed mixed set trains that single-skill practice does not, and what this lesson adds to CT-001 to CT-006.

## The gap this lesson fills

CT-001 to CT-006 each taught one skill on its own: main idea (CT-001), inference (CT-002), tone, assumption and context (CT-003), logical conclusion (CT-004), fact versus opinion (CT-005) and elimination among close options (CT-006). In those lessons you knew the skill before you opened the passage, because the lesson title told you. In the examination nobody tells you. A main-idea passage is followed by a vocabulary item, then an assumption, then a table of numbers. This lesson trains the thing that is left over once the skills exist: **switching between them, against a clock, with a decision about what to skip.**

## Three things a mixed timed set tests

1. **Recognition.** Naming the question type from the stem in a few seconds, so that you pick the right tool (module 4 gives the table).
2. **Pace.** Finishing a passage item in about two minutes instead of five, without losing the qualifications that decide the answer (CT-001 module 6).
3. **Judgement.** Deciding to attempt, guess or skip using the marking arithmetic of CT-006 module 5, and recording that decision.

None of these can be practised one passage at a time with no timer.

## Why this matters for a qualifying paper

Per the official notification as recorded in the project registry (verify against the current notification): the CSAT is a qualifying paper with a 33 per cent bar, two hours and one-third negative marking. The 80-question count and 2.5 marks per question are not in the registry and need an official source (as flagged in CT-001-B2, CT-006 module 5). Taken as working assumptions, the bar is 66 marks of 200 and a wrong answer costs 0.833 marks, so the aim is a safe margin with controlled risk.

## What 'mixed' means here

A mixed set holds items of different families in an order you did not choose. It is not a mock paper: full-length papers belong to the examination laboratory of Days 91 to 120. Here a set is **six to eight passage items, about twenty minutes**, repeated on several days (module 7).

## What this lesson does not claim

No verified past-paper question is mapped to this topic, so there is no PYQ analysis. Every time target is a **Mission 90 practice target, not an official allotment**. The eight sample passages are original and copied from no paper. Finishing a set is practice, not mastery.


### 2. The time budget: arithmetic you can run in your head

*Objective:* Compute the per-question average, a per-passage target and a start-to-finish plan for an eight-item set.

## Start with the whole paper

Two hours is 120 minutes, which is 7,200 seconds. With 80 questions the average is 7,200 / 80 = **90 seconds per question** (80: verify against the notification), the figure of CT-001-B2 and module 6.

## Passage items run above the average

A short numerical item may take 45 seconds; a passage item takes longer because you must read first. CT-006 module 5 set about two minutes as a practice target, and CT-002 and CT-003 capped an item at two and a half to three minutes. Here each item has a **target** (what you aim for) and a **cap** (when you stop and mark it).

Targets and caps (target / cap): vocabulary 1:30 / 2:00; tone 1:40 / 2:15; main idea and inference 2:00 / 2:30; assumption and strengthening 2:15 / 2:45; data conclusion and EXCEPT 2:30 / 3:00.

## A start-to-finish plan for the eight-item sample set

The eight items of module 5 have targets of 120, 120, 135, 100, 90, 135, 150 and 150 seconds. Adding them: 120 + 120 = 240; + 135 = 375; + 100 = 475; + 90 = 565; + 135 = 700; + 150 = 850; + 150 = 1,000 seconds, which is **16 minutes 40 seconds**. For comparison, eight items at the paper average would be 8 x 90 = 720 seconds, or 12 minutes. That is 280 seconds (4 minutes 40 seconds) over the average, as expected for passage items.

Give yourself a set clock of **20 minutes**:

- 16 min 40 s: first sweep, every item once (about 1,000 s).
- 2 min: return sweep, only for items marked with a question mark (module 3).
- 1 min 20 s: copy answers and re-check the stems of negative items.

That plan adds up: 1,000 + 120 + 80 = 1,200 seconds = 20 minutes.

## Scaling to the whole paper (illustration only)

Illustration only (the real count of passage items in a year is not verified): 28 passage items at 2 minutes take 56 minutes. To end the first sweep at 95 minutes, the other 52 items get 95 - 56 = 39 minutes, or 45 seconds each. Add 20 minutes for the return sweep and 5 for the final check: 95 + 20 + 5 = 120. This is a practice skeleton, not an official allotment.

## Checks while you work

- At item 4 the clock should show about 8 minutes (the first four targets sum to 475 seconds).
- If you are more than 90 seconds behind then, skip and return on the next two items; do not read faster, which loses the contrast word (CT-001 module 6).


### 3. Triage, pass-skip-return and marking uncertain items

*Objective:* Apply a fixed triage order, the pass-skip-return rule and a three-symbol marking code to a set.

## The triage order

Go **in printed order, sorting each item in five to ten seconds** from the stem and the first line:

1. **Quick:** vocabulary in context, tone, clear structure. Do now.
2. **Standard:** main idea, inference, strengthening, assumption. Do now, by the lesson's method.
3. **Slow:** long numerical or table items, fine EXCEPT or NOT stems, anything needing a third reading. Mark and return.

Attempt in print order; the sort decides only whether you **commit now or mark and move on**. Do not reorder the paper unless you have practised it: jumping costs time and risks shifted answers.

## The pass-skip-return rule

1. **Pass (answer now)** when you can name a claim line (CT-006 module 2) and one option survives by a named defect code.
2. **Skip (leave and return)** when, by the cap, you cannot name a surviving option, or when three or more options still stand. Mark it and go on.
3. **Return** only after the first sweep, to marked items, with the remaining time. Re-read once with fresh eyes; the missed contrast word is often obvious on the second look.

Caps are in the module 2 table. Exceeding one once is an accident; three times in a set is a habit to log.

## Three marks beside the question number

Use three symbols on the question paper only (never on the answer sheet):

| Mark | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| tick | Answered; one option survived by a named defect code | Do not revisit |
| ? | Answered or half-answered, with doubt (exactly two options left) | Return sweep, apply the 25 per cent rule |
| dash | Skipped | Return sweep if time remains; otherwise decide by the 25 per cent rule |

These are not defect codes: S, W, O, R, T and U (CT-006 module 2) stay reserved for the **reasons** options fail.

## The decision at the end of the clock

The arithmetic is in CT-006 module 5: a right answer earns 2.5, a wrong one costs 0.833, and a guess pays above an honest 25 per cent chance (+0.278 with one option removed, +0.833 with two). A skipped item scores zero (working figures; verify, CT-006 module 5). So answer a dash only if you can name a defect for at least one option and rate your chance above 25 per cent; otherwise leave it.

## A worked mini-example

In a fresh set planned like the sample set (475 seconds for the first four items), you are about to start item 5 and the clock reads 9 minutes 55 seconds (595 seconds). The plan says 475 seconds after four items, so you are 595 - 475 = 120 seconds behind, past the 90-second limit of module 2. In this fresh set, item 5 is a long data item. Skip it with a dash, do items 6 and 7, and return.


### 4. Recognising the question type in ten seconds

*Objective:* Read a stem, name its family and call up the right tool and lesson without rereading the passage first.

## The stem-first habit

CT-006 module 2 said to read the stem first. In a mixed set the stem **chooses the tool**. Spend about ten seconds on it and name the *family* and the *strength it needs* (certain, probable, supported, best).

## The routing table

Each row names the lesson that teaches the tool. The table does not repeat those lessons; it only routes you.

| Stem wording (typical) | Family | Tool (lesson) |
|---|---|---|
| 'central idea', 'main message', 'best summarises' | Main idea | Claim line, then reject S, W, O (CT-001 modules 3 to 5) |
| 'can be inferred', 'most logical inference' | Inference | Strength ladder, contradiction test (CT-002 modules 3 to 6) |
| 'assumption', 'underlying', 'presupposes' | Assumption | Denial test (CT-003 module 4) |
| 'tone', 'attitude', 'mood' | Tone | Direction, intensity, target (CT-003 modules 2 and 3) |
| 'most nearly means', 'as used in the passage' | Vocabulary in context | Substitute each option into the sentence; use the passage's own clues (CT-003 module 6) |
| 'conclusion', 'strengthen', 'weaken', numbered statements | Conclusion | Group, strength, link check (CT-004 modules 2 to 6); strengthen and weaken versus necessary assumption (CT-003 module 5) |
| 'fact', 'opinion', 'author's view' | Fact versus opinion | Verifiability test; voice split (CT-005 modules 2 to 6) |
| 'NOT', 'EXCEPT', 'least' | Flipped stem | Mark supported options, find the odd one (CT-006 module 4) |

## Two-way recognition: stem and passage together

When the stem is generic ('Which one of the following is correct?'), the passage's shape names the family: a plan ending in 'therefore' invites assumption or strengthening; figures invite a data conclusion; two contrasted views invite main idea or author's view. If the shape does not decide, treat it as inference.

## The five-step routine (about two minutes)

1. **Stem (10 s):** family and strength.
2. **Passage (about 45 s):** one reading, mark the contrast word and the final claim (CT-001 module 3).
3. **Claim line (10 s):** your own ten words.
4. **Options (remaining):** reject by codes S, W, O, R, T, U; settle between survivors by rank order if needed.
5. **Mark** with a tick, a question mark or a dash (module 3).

For a vocabulary item, drop step 3 and look for the context clue; for a data item, replace it with a calculation line.

## Three errors of recognition

- **Tone treated as main idea:** choosing an option about the topic when the stem asked for the attitude.
- **Assumption treated as inference:** an inference follows from the passage; an assumption sits behind it (CT-003 module 4).
- **Missing the negative:** re-read the stem after choosing.


### 5. The worked sample set and two think-alouds

*Objective:* Run the eight-item sample set against the clock and study a model think-aloud for an assumption item and a strengthening item.

## The set

The eight original passages are in the practice section (CT-007-P1 to P8), each with one question and an explanation of all four options. Attempt them in one sitting on a 20-minute clock (module 2), using the marks of module 3:

| Item | Type | Target |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Main idea (seed library) | 2 min |
| P2 | Inference (hospital appointments) | 2 min |
| P3 | Assumption (sports day) | 2 min 15 s |
| P4 | Tone (library form) | 1 min 40 s |
| P5 | Vocabulary in context ('sober') | 1 min 30 s |
| P6 | Strengthening (white roofs) | 2 min 15 s |
| P7 | Conclusion from data (survey) | 2 min 30 s |
| P8 | Inference, EXCEPT (milk cooperative) | 2 min 30 s |

Do not open the explanations until the clock is over.

## Think-aloud 1: P3, an assumption item

Passage in brief: the principal proposes moving sports day from two in the afternoon in April to seven in the morning, because students fell ill on the last two sports days, and she concludes that the change will reduce illness. She has not yet asked a doctor about the cause.

*Stem (10 s):* 'necessary assumption'. Family: assumption; tool: denial test (CT-003 module 4). I need what must be true for the conclusion to hold, not what would be nice.

*Passage (40 s):* the evidence is illness on two afternoons; the conclusion is that earlier timing will reduce illness. The gap is the **reason** timing matters, and the doctor line says the cause is not established.

*Claim line (10 s):* 'Moving earlier will cut illness.' Morning must be gentler than afternoon in a way that matters to illness.

*Options (50 s).* A, preference does not touch illness: T. B, number of sports days: not in the argument, O. C, performance is another outcome: T. D, some afternoon condition absent in the early morning contributed to the illness: deny it. If nothing that differs between the two times played a part, the move cannot help and the conclusion collapses, so D is necessary.

*Mark:* tick; about 110 seconds, under the 135-second target.

## Think-aloud 2: P6, a strengthening item

Passage in brief: the municipality says white roofs will lower indoor temperature and cut electricity spent on fans and coolers, and will subsidise white paint, but has commissioned no study.

*Stem (10 s):* 'if true, would most strengthen'. Family: conclusion, strengthening; tool: the link test (CT-004 module 4) and the strengthen test (CT-003 module 5). The right option makes the link from white roofs to lower electricity use **more likely**. It need not prove it.

*Passage (35 s):* the chain is white roof, cooler rooms, less electricity; no evidence is given for either step.

*Claim line (10 s):* 'White roofs mean lower cooling bills.' The best option will be evidence about cooling bills, from a comparable place.

*Options (55 s).* A, a trial where white roofs used less electricity than comparable dark roofs: direct evidence with a comparison group; it survives. B, higher cost: weakens the plan. C, liking the look: irrelevant to electricity. D, water tanks: a new element, no stated link. A alone survives.

*Mark:* tick; about 110 seconds.

## What the think-alouds model

Both start with the stem, write a claim line before the options, reject by code and reason, and stop at one survivor.


### 6. Scoring a set and keeping the error log

*Objective:* Score a set with net marks, log each error with an existing code and a process fault, and read the log for a pattern.

## Score first, then analyse

Net marks for a set = 2.5 x (right) minus (2.5 / 3) x (wrong), with skipped items at zero (module 3; CT-006 module 5). A set of eight items is worth 20 marks (working figures; verify, CT-006 module 5).

An **illustration, not a real learner's attempt:** suppose someone gets five right, one wrong and two skipped. Net = 5 x 2.5 - 1 x 0.833 = 12.5 - 0.833 = **11.67** marks out of 20. Three wrong answers would cancel one right one (3 x 0.833 = 2.5), so one wrong answer here is a small loss; the larger cost is the two skipped items, each a possible 2.5.

Do not compare a set score with the qualifying line as if it predicted it; eight items are too few. The useful evidence is the log and the trend over several sets.

## The codes you must reuse

Do not invent new codes. The vocabulary of earlier lessons is:

- **Option defects (CT-006 module 2):** S too strong, W too weak, O out of scope, R reversed, T true but irrelevant, U unsupported inference. These regroup the seven wrong-option families of CT-001 module 4 and the traps of CT-002 module 5.
- **Trap names where useful:** intensity, extreme, true-but-not-needed, outside knowledge, purpose (CT-003 module 10); over-generalisation, causal slip (CT-004).

For process faults in a mixed set, use plain words, not codes: *misread stem*, *no claim line*, *over cap*, *marked but never returned*, *guessed with no named defect*, *changed a right answer*.

## The log: one row per item that was wrong, skipped or over the cap

| Date | Set and item | Type | Outcome | Time vs target | Defect code of the option chosen (or 'none, skipped') | Process fault | Cure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Nov | A, P3 | Assumption | wrong (chose C) | 3:20 vs 2:15 | T | no denial test | Deny each option in one line |
| 1 Nov | A, P6 | Strengthening | skipped | cap reached, no survivor | none | no claim line | Write ten-word claim line first |

These rows are examples only. Do not log items you got right on target.

## Reading the log

After three sets count down each column:

- **Type column:** the skill to repair first (module 7).
- **Code column**: a pile of T and O suggests you answer from the topic, not from the claim; a pile of S suggests you do not check absolutes; a pile of R suggests you do not check direction.
- **Process faults** such as 'no claim line' and 'over cap' are habits to fix before any reading skill.

## What one set does not show

One log may reflect a bad day. Look for a **pattern across at least three sets** on different days.


### 7. The multi-day practice procedure

*Objective:* Follow the attempt, analyse, classify, repair and retest cycle over several days for a mixed set.

## The five-step cycle

1. **Attempt** a set under the clock (module 2), with marks beside each question (module 3). No explanations yet.
2. **Analyse** the same day: score it, then read the explanation of every option, including options you rejected correctly.
3. **Classify** each error in the log (module 6) with a defect code and a process fault.
4. **Repair** by going back to the lesson for the type with the most errors (module 4 routing table) and doing two to three untimed items of that type from CT-001 to CT-006 practice, with codes written beside every option.
5. **Retest** with a fresh set, then retest the old set after seven days, answering before reading the explanation.

## A six-day pattern

- Day 1: Set 1 under the clock, score, log (20 + 25 min).
- Day 2: repair the most frequent type, untimed (40 min).
- Day 3: Set 2, a fresh set of six to eight items, timed, logged (20 + 20 min).
- Day 4: repair a second type; re-read the routing table (30 min).
- Day 5: Set 3, timed; compare the three logs (20 + 20 min).
- Day 6: rest from CSAT; ten-minute review of marks and process faults.

Retest Set 1 on Day 7 or later, which fits the topic's study target (retest after 3 and 7 study days).

### Notes: Definitions

- Timed mixed set: six to eight passage items of different families, attempted in one sitting on a fixed clock.
- Target: the time you aim to spend on an item; cap: the time at which you mark the item and move on. Both are Mission 90 practice figures, not official allotments.
- Triage: sorting each item in five to ten seconds into answer-now or mark-and-return.
- Claim line: your own ten-word paraphrase of the passage's claim, written before the options (CT-006).
- Process fault: a habit error such as misreading the stem, skipping the claim line or going over the cap; recorded in plain words, not codes.

### Notes: Pass-skip-return rule and marks

- Pass: claim line written and one option survives by a named defect code; mark with a tick.
- Skip: no survivor by the cap, or three or more options standing; mark with a dash and go on.
- Return: after the first sweep, only to question-mark and dash items; re-read once.

### Notes: Net-score arithmetic

- Net marks = 2.5 x (right) - (2.5 / 3) x (wrong); wrong answer costs 0.833; skipped items score 0.
- Eight-item set: maximum 20. Example (illustrative): 5 right, 1 wrong, 2 skipped = 12.5 - 0.833 = 11.67.
- Three wrong answers cancel one right answer: 3 x 0.833 = 2.5.
- EV of a guess = 2.5 p - 0.833 (1 - p); zero at p = 0.25 (CT-006 module 5).

### Notes: Common process faults and cures

- Reading faster to catch up: loses the contrast word; skip and return instead.
- Doing the hard item to prove a point: breaks the cap; mark it and return.
- Marking with defect codes: codes name why an option fails; the three marks name the state of the item.
- Skipping the stem re-read on NOT items: re-read the stem after choosing.
- Logging only wrong answers: also log skipped and over-cap items, which show pace problems.

**Q.** Two hours for 80 questions: how many seconds per question on average?

**A.** 7,200 / 80 = 90 seconds (80 questions: verify against the notification).

**Q.** Why is a passage item's target above the paper average?

**A.** You must read the passage before you can answer, so a passage item takes longer than a short item; the paper balances this with quicker items.

**Q.** What do the three marks mean?

**A.** Tick: answered with a named survivor. Question mark: answered with doubt. Dash: skipped.

**Q.** A defect code and a mark are both written beside an item. How do they differ?

**A.** A code (S, W, O, R, T, U) names why an option fails; a mark states whether the item is done, doubtful or skipped.

**Q.** Why not reorder the printed paper freely?

**A.** It costs time on the answer sheet and risks answers in the wrong place; use mark-and-return instead unless you have practised reordering.

**Q.** Why does one set not predict the qualifying result?

**A.** Eight items are too few and may reflect a good or bad day; patterns across at least three sets are more informative.
