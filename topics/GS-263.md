## GS-263: Ecological succession

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain Ecological succession and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Communities change towards balance

The cited pages approach community change only indirectly, through NCERT's definition of natural vegetation: a plant community left undisturbed for a long period, allowing each species to adjust to climate and soil as completely as possible. India's tropical evergreen forests are described as well stratified, with shrubs and creepers near the ground, short trees above them and tall trees on top. Decomposition in the soil produces humus, a colloid that breaks down very slowly and acts as a nutrient reservoir.

### Disturbance redirects the path

Human action can push communities away from their natural trajectory. Under British rule, oak forests of Garhwal and Kumaon were replaced by chir pine needed for railways, and forests were cleared for tea, rubber and coffee plantations, shifting from protective to commercial use. Grazing, lopping for fodder and fuel, and fire also degrade forests. Gradients show how communities sort along moisture: dry deciduous forest passes to moist deciduous where rain increases and to thorn forest where it decreases, and scanty vegetation in western Rajasthan reflects both low rain and overgrazing. These are the conditions from which recovery must start.

### Restoration and an exam caution

Recovery of degraded land is aided by protection. Joint Forest Management, formally begun in Odisha in 1988, lets village institutions protect degraded forest land in exchange for non-timber produce and a timber share. Sacred groves have kept patches of forest untouched by local people. Social and farm forestry raise trees on barren and community lands. A key exam caution: NCERT describes the Himalaya as showing a succession of vegetation from tropical to tundra types with altitude; that is spatial zonation, not temporal ecological succession. The cited NCERT pages do not detail primary versus secondary seres, so supplement carefully.

### Worked example

Statement test: 'Himalayan vegetation from foothill deciduous forest to alpine meadows is an example of ecological succession.' Incorrect — it is altitudinal zonation in space. A correct example of community change over time is degraded forest regenerating under Joint Forest Management protection.

### Recall

- - **q:** How does NCERT define natural vegetation?
  - **a:** A plant community left undisturbed long enough for species to adjust fully to climate and soil.
- - **q:** Give a historical example of human-driven change in forest composition.
  - **a:** Oak forests in Garhwal and Kumaon replaced by chir pine for railways.
- - **q:** Why is Himalayan altitudinal zonation not ecological succession?
  - **a:** It is a spatial sequence with height, not a change over time at one site.

### Common trap

Confusing altitudinal 'succession of vegetation' in the Himalaya (a spatial sequence from tropical to tundra types with height) with ecological succession, which is a change over time at one place.
