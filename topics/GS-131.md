## GS-131: Location-based map practice: protected areas

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS1 · Geography · Indian geography

### 1. Orientation: the nine labels and the six landscapes

*Objective:* Name the nine kinds of protected place, the six landscapes used to organise WHERE, and the two official questions this topic has produced.

## What a location question really asks

A location question gives the name of a place (a sanctuary, a lake, a park) and asks *where it is* (state or landscape), or gives a species and asks *where to see it*. It is a map question disguised as an environment question. Day 18 asks you to build a map in your head, not to memorise lists.

## The nine labels

Protected places carry labels, and the label tells you who declared them and what they restrict:

- **National park** and **wildlife sanctuary**: declared by a State Government under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (the Centre can also declare them, section 38; verify).
- **Tiger reserve**: a core-and-buffer area run under Project Tiger and the National Tiger Conservation Authority.
- **Elephant reserve**: a landscape set aside under Project Elephant (standard knowledge; verify).
- **Conservation reserve** and **community reserve**: State-declared, on government land and on private or community land respectively.
- **Biosphere reserve**: designated under UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere Programme.
- **Ramsar site**: a wetland listed under the Ramsar Convention, an international treaty.
- **World Heritage site**: a place inscribed by UNESCO for outstanding universal value.

One place can carry several labels at once. Module 4 shows the overlay.

## The six landscapes

To answer WHERE, sort India into six landscapes (this lesson's scheme, built on NCERT physiography and PMF IAS's own grouping of tiger landscapes):

1. **Himalaya and the Shiwalik-Terai foothills** (north).
2. **North-eastern hills and the Brahmaputra plains**.
3. **Central India** (the plateau of Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra).
4. **Eastern Ghats and the east**.
5. **Western Ghats** (the southwest).
6. **West and coast**: Gujarat, Rajasthan's arid west, the sea coast and the islands.

## What UPSC has asked

Two official questions are verified. The 2017 one asks where to see gharials in their natural habitat. The 2018 one asks in which State Pakhui Wildlife Sanctuary lies. Both are answered by a landscape reason, not by rote, and module 9 works them. Counts and lists change (the number of national parks, tiger reserves and Ramsar sites grows); every count in this lesson is as of the book's edition and carries a verify flag.

### 2. Foundation: map vocabulary and the physical landscapes behind the places

*Objective:* Define the map words used in location questions and describe, from NCERT, the physical landscapes in which protected areas sit.

## Words first

A **state** is one of India's administrative divisions shown on the NCERT administrative map. A **landscape** is a large area of similar relief, climate and vegetation that crosses state lines. **Habitat** is the natural home of a species. A **corridor** is a strip of habitat that lets animals move between protected areas (PMF IAS defines tiger corridors as natural pathways joining fragmented habitats). A **buffer** is a protective ring around a core area. **Ecosystem** and species ecology are taught on other days; here you only need them to locate things.

## The landscapes in NCERT's words

- **Himalaya and the northern plain edge.** The Himalayas are a wall of young mountain ranges. At their southern foot the plains begin with the **Bhabar**, a narrow belt of 8 to 10 km where streams dump boulders and disappear, then the **Tarai**, 10 to 20 km wide, where streams re-emerge to make marshy, swampy ground with luxuriant natural vegetation that "houses a varied wildlife" (NCERT *India: Physical Environment*, chapter 2). The Tarai is the classic home of big mammals in the north.
- **The Brahmaputra plains and north-eastern hills.** NCERT notes riverine islands, sand bars and periodic floods in the Brahmaputra plains, and the Meghalaya and Karbi Anglong plateau, detached from the main peninsula, with the Garo, Khasi and Jaintia Hills.
- **The Peninsular plateau.** An old, stable land. The Deccan Plateau lies between the Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats and Satpura-Maikal ranges; the Central Highlands lie north of it, bordered west by the Aravali.
- **The Western Ghats**, locally the Sahyadri in Maharashtra, the Nilgiri hills in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and the Anaimalai and Cardamom hills in Kerala; continuous, with an average height of about 1,500 m, rising from north to south. **Anaimudi (2,695 m)** is the highest peak of the Peninsular plateau (NCERT).
- **The Eastern Ghats**, low, discontinuous hills cut by the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri. The two Ghats meet in the **Nilgiri hills**.
- **The desert and the west.** The Marusthali lies northwest of the Aravali, with the southern part sloping towards the **Rann of Kachchh**. Day 17 lesson GS-116 teaches the Ghats in detail.
- **The coast and islands.** A narrow western coastal plain and a broad eastern one; the Andaman and Nicobar group in the Bay of Bengal and Lakshadweep in the Arabian Sea. Day 17 lesson GS-130 teaches the coasts.

## The WHERE formula

For any place give three coordinates: **state, landscape, nearest big river or sea**. For Kaziranga: Assam, Brahmaputra plains, the Brahmaputra. For Corbett: Uttarakhand, Shiwalik-Terai edge, the Ramganga (the river is standard knowledge; verify). If you can state all three, the place is on your map. If you can state only the state, you will fail the neighbouring-state trap.

> Neighbours (NCERT map exercise): learn the states that touch each landscape, so that a wrong option from a different landscape is eliminated fast.

### 3. Core: the legal ladder, from reserved forest to national park

*Objective:* Distinguish reserved and protected forests, sanctuaries, national parks, tiger reserves, conservation reserves and community reserves, and state what an eco-sensitive zone is.

## Forests first

**Reserved forests** and **protected forests** are notified by State Governments under the **Indian Forest Act, 1927**. In a reserved forest all activities such as hunting and grazing are banned unless permitted; in a protected forest such rights are sometimes granted to fringe communities. PMF IAS ranks protection as: national parks > wildlife sanctuaries > reserved forests > protected forests.

## Sanctuaries and national parks

Both are declared by a State Government **by notification** (no new Act needed) and are protected under the **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972**. A sanctuary is a refuge where endangered species are safeguarded from hunting, predation and competition; it may be promoted to a national park. The differences PMF IAS draws:

| Question | Wildlife sanctuary | National park |
|---|---|---|
| Rights of people inside | Certain rights may be permitted (regulated grazing, firewood) | No rights permitted |
| Livestock grazing | The Chief Wildlife Warden may regulate or prohibit | Not allowed |
| Boundaries | Described by PMF IAS as not specified | Clearly demarcated |
| Declared by | State notification | State notification |

Caution: PMF IAS's boundary row is compressed. In standard law a sanctuary's boundary is also fixed in its notification, and national park boundaries can be changed only with legislative and National Board for Wildlife steps (verify the exact procedure). Treat the *rights* row as the examinable distinction. The Supreme Court in June 2022 directed that mining within national parks and sanctuaries not be permitted (PMF IAS).

## Tiger reserves

A tiger reserve is created on the **core-buffer** strategy. Its protection status equals that of a sanctuary but it is monitored by the **National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA)**, a statutory body created by the 2006 amendment of the Wildlife (Protection) Act. The core has the legal status of a national park or sanctuary and is freed of human activity; the buffer allows sustainable use. Project Tiger began in 1973 at Jim Corbett National Park, Uttarakhand.

## Conservation and community reserves

A **conservation reserve** is declared by the State Government on land it owns, especially land adjoining parks and sanctuaries or linking one protected area with another, after consulting local communities. A **community reserve** is declared on **private or community land** where a person or community has volunteered to conserve wildlife; it cannot lie within a national park, sanctuary or conservation reserve. A committee with five representatives of the village panchayat or gram sabha and one forest or wildlife officer manages it; restrictions of sanctuaries apply. In both, people's rights are not affected.

**Protected Area (PA)** in the Wildlife (Protection) Act means national parks, sanctuaries, conservation reserves and community reserves. Biosphere reserves, Ramsar sites and World Heritage sites are not separate PA categories under that Act.

## Eco-sensitive zones (ESZs)

An ESZ is a belt notified by the Environment Ministry around a national park or sanctuary to act as a shock absorber. The Wildlife Action Plan (2002-2016) suggested 10 km; the Supreme Court in June 2022 directed a minimum of 1 km from the boundary (PMF IAS; later modifications are possible, verify). ESZs are **not** declared under the Wildlife Act; they rest on the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.

### 4. Core: the overlay labels, biosphere reserve, Ramsar site and World Heritage site

*Objective:* State what UNESCO and the Ramsar Convention designate, what changes (and what does not) in law, and why one place can carry several labels.

## Biosphere reserve

A biosphere reserve is a large area of protected land, marine and coastal ecosystems **designated under UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere (MAB) Programme** after the national government nominates it (PMF IAS). Key legal point: **designation does not change the site's legal status**. Land and forest laws, ownership and sovereignty stay as they were. In India the Indian National MAB Committee under the Environment Ministry recommends sites, and the State prepares a management plan. The zoning is **core** (strictly protected, no human activity, usually a national park or sanctuary under the Wildlife Act), **buffer** (limited activity: research, monitoring, training, limited recreation) and **transition** (settlements and sustainable economic activity). A biosphere reserve may contain one or more national parks or sanctuaries. Central financial help is on a 90:10 basis to the North-Eastern and three Himalayan states and 60:40 for others.

## Ramsar site

The Ramsar Convention (adopted at Ramsar, Iran, on 2 February 1971; India joined in 1982 per PMF IAS) lists **wetlands of international importance**. A wetland is area of marsh, fen, peatland or water, natural or artificial, fresh, brackish or salt, including marine water up to six metres deep at low tide (Wetlands Rules, 2017). Listing is a *commitment* to maintain the site's ecological character; it does not by itself make the site a national park. PMF IAS names **Chilika Lake and the Sundarbans** as Indian Ramsar examples. The listing criteria include a rare wetland type, threatened species, biodiversity, 20,000 or more waterbirds (or 1 per cent of a species), and fish spawning or migration. The **Montreux Record** flags a listed site whose ecological character has changed or is likely to change. The Wetlands Rules, 2017 apply to Ramsar sites and notified wetlands, and exclude protected areas already covered by the Wildlife Act and similar laws.

## World Heritage site

The World Heritage Convention (UNESCO, 1972) inscribes places of **outstanding universal value**, cultural or natural. A nominated site must meet at least one of ten criteria; four of them are natural: outstanding examples of earth's record of life or geology; ongoing ecological and evolutionary processes; rare or superlative natural phenomena or beauty; habitats of rare threatened species or exceptional biodiversity. PMF IAS names the **Western Ghats** as a World Heritage site, across Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Gujarat and Goa, and records that **Manas** regained its World Heritage status once its rhino population recovered. Other Indian natural sites (Kaziranga, Sundarbans and more) are standard knowledge, listed in the master notes with a verify flag.

## Important Bird and Biodiversity Areas

BirdLife International, through the Bombay Natural History Society, has identified **554 IBAs** in India (PMF IAS, as of the book's edition, verify). IBAs are not legal protected areas.

## The overlay principle

Labels stack. Manas, for example, is a national park in Assam (500 sq km, adjoining Bhutan's 1,057 sq km Royal Manas), a transboundary conservation area, a rhino translocation site and a World Heritage site. The Sundarbans appears in PMF IAS both as a Ramsar example and among the tiger landscapes. A question that asks "which is a Ramsar site" is therefore not answered by "which is a national park": check the **label named in the stem** each time.

> Hand-drawn figure: draw three overlapping circles labelled State law (park or sanctuary), UNESCO (biosphere, heritage) and Ramsar, and place Manas and the Sundarbans in the overlaps.

### 5. Core: Himalaya, the Terai and the north-east, with places and species

*Objective:* Place the protected areas and species named in the extracts in the Shiwalik-Terai landscape and the north-east, by state.

## Landscape 1: Himalaya and the Shiwalik-Terai foothills

PMF IAS groups tiger corridors into landscapes. For the **Shivalik Hills and Gangetic Plains** it names **Rajaji-Corbett (Uttarakhand), Corbett-Dudhwa (Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Nepal) and Dudhwa-Kishanpur-Katerniaghat (Uttar Pradesh, Nepal)**. The same landscape's states for tiger counts are Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh and Bihar. Anchors:

- **Jim Corbett National Park (Uttarakhand)**: where Project Tiger began in 1973; highest tiger count of any reserve in the 2022 table (260).
- **Dudhwa and Kishanpur, Katerniaghat (Uttar Pradesh)**, near the Nepal border; these sit in the Tarai of module 2.
- **Pilibhit Tiger Reserve** won the first TX2 award in 2020 (PMF IAS does not name the state; standard knowledge says Uttar Pradesh; verify).
- **Valmiki Tiger Reserve (Bihar)**: genetically unique tiger population; **Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary (Bihar)** is the only sanctuary for dolphins.
- **Rajaji (Uttarakhand)**, linked to Corbett by the Rajaji-Corbett corridor.
- **Vulture Safe Zones** include Corbett in Uttarakhand and Dudhwa and Katerniaghat in Uttar Pradesh near the Indo-Nepal border (PMF IAS).

**High-altitude Himalaya.** India's snow leopard landscapes are **Hemis-Spiti (Ladakh and Himachal Pradesh), Nanda Devi-Gangotri (Uttarakhand) and Khangchendzonga-Tawang (Sikkim and Arunachal Pradesh)**. Species of the high Himalaya and Trans-Himalaya listed by PMF IAS include snow leopard, red panda, wild yak, chiru, Tibetan gazelle, argali, musk deer, Himalayan goral, serow and takin. Eco-sensitive zone notifications in 2024-26 include Lippa-Asrang and Rupi Bhaba (Himachal Pradesh) and Tral (Jammu and Kashmir) (the list is garbled in the extract in places).

## Landscape 2: north-eastern hills and the Brahmaputra plains

Corridors named: **Kaziranga with Itanagar Wildlife Sanctuary (Assam, Arunachal Pradesh), Karbi Anglong, Nameri, Orang and Papum Pane**, **Manas-Buxa (Assam, West Bengal, Bhutan)**, **Pakke-Nameri-Sonai Rupai-Manas (Arunachal Pradesh, Assam)**, **Dibru Saikhowa-D'Ering-Mehaong (Assam, Arunachal Pradesh)**, **Kamlang-Kane-Tale Valley (Arunachal Pradesh)** and **Buxa-Jaldapara (West Bengal)**. The pattern: Assam holds the river-plain parks, Arunachal Pradesh the hill sanctuaries on the border, North Bengal (Buxa, Jaldapara) the western tail.

**Rhino geography.** The great one-horned rhinoceros survives only in small pockets of the Indo-Nepal Terai, northern West Bengal and the Brahmaputra floodplains of Assam (PMF IAS). Assam's rhino sites: **Kaziranga NP, Orang NP, Pobitora Wildlife Sanctuary, Manas NP (first translocation site), Dibru Saikhowa WLS and Laokhowa-Bura Chapori WLS**. India Rhino Vision 2020 (from 2005) targeted 3,000 wild rhinos spread across seven Assam protected areas to avoid concentrating them at Kaziranga.

**Elephants.** More than 60 per cent of Asian elephants are in India; the study "Right of Passage" identified 101 corridors, most of them in the north-east (PMF IAS).

## Landscape 3: central India

PMF IAS lists these corridors under **Central India and Eastern Ghats**:

- **Ranthambhore-Kuno-Madhav**: Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan.
- **Bandhavgarh-Achanakmar**: Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh.
- **Bandhavgarh-Sanjay Dubri-Guru Ghasidas**: Madhya Pradesh.
- **Guru Ghasidas-Palamau-Lawalong**: Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand.
- **Kanha-Achanakmar**: Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh.
- **Kanha-Pench**: Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra.
- **Pench-Satpura-Melghat**: Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra.
- **Kanha-Navegaon Nagzira-Tadoba-Indravati**: Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh (the extract also prints Andhra Pradesh, as printed: a pre-2014 state listing, since that segment now falls partly in Telangana; verify).
- **Indravati-Udanti Sitanadi-Sunabeda**: Chhattisgarh and Odisha.

Pench Tiger Reserve straddles Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra, and the two parts won the TX2 award jointly in 2023. **Satpura Tiger Reserve (Madhya Pradesh)** won the Conservation Excellence Award for 2023. Madhya Pradesh has the largest tiger population in the 2022 table (785), followed by Karnataka (563), Uttarakhand (560) and Maharashtra (444).

**Kuno National Park (748 sq km, Madhya Pradesh)** received eight cheetahs from Namibia in 2022, the first intercontinental translocation of a carnivore (PMF IAS). It was chosen for space, prey density and its historical range; about 24 villages had been relocated earlier. Proposed second-home sites for Asiatic lions include **Madhav NP and Gandhi Sagar WLS (Madhya Pradesh)**, **Sitamata WLS, Mukundra Hills Tiger Reserve and Kumbhalgarh WLS (Rajasthan)**, and **Jessore-Balaram-Ambaji and Barda (Gujarat)**.

## Landscape 4: the Eastern Ghats and the east

Named: **Similipal-Satkosia (Odisha)** and **Nagarjunasagar-Sri Venkateshwara NP (Andhra Pradesh)**. Tigers have been lost from the core areas of Kawal and Chennur (Telangana), Sri Venkateswara NP (Andhra Pradesh), Satkosia (Odisha) and Sahyadri (Maharashtra). Simlipal (Odisha) and Valmiki (Bihar) hold genetically unique tiger populations. PMF IAS warns of red sanders poaching in the Seshachalam Hills (Eastern Ghats). Chilika Lake, a Ramsar example, is on the Odisha coast (state standard knowledge; verify).

## Landscape 6: the west and the desert

- **Gir (Gujarat)**: the last home of the Asiatic lion (Project Lion, 2020); 891 lions in the 2025 estimate, a single isolated population (PMF IAS; counts change, verify). Canine distemper killed over 20 lions in 2018.
- **Sariska (Rajasthan)**: all tigers disappeared by 2006, according to PMF IAS.
- **Rann of Kutch (Gujarat)**: the Asiatic wild ass or khulan.
- **Great Indian Bustard Wildlife Sanctuary (Maharashtra)** appears among the 2024-26 ESZ notifications.
- **Sacred groves**: Aravalli Hills of Rajasthan (the orans), as well as Khasi and Jaintia Hills (Meghalaya), Western Ghats of Karnataka and Maharashtra, and Chanda and Bastar of Chhattisgarh.

## A quick check

Madhya Pradesh is the state that appears most often: Kanha, Bandhavgarh, Satpura, Pench, Panna, Kuno and Madhav. If an option puts one of these in another State, suspect it.

### 7. Core: the Western Ghats, the coast and the islands

*Objective:* Place the Western Ghats reserves by state, and the coastal and island protected places and species named in the extracts.

## Landscape 5: the Western Ghats

The Western Ghats (about 164,280 sq km, six states: Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Gujarat and Goa, per PMF IAS) are a World Heritage site and a biodiversity hotspot. Their ground, height and rain are taught in Day 17 lesson GS-116. PMF IAS lists these corridors, which double as a north-to-south map:

- **Sahyadri-Radhanagari-Goa**: Maharashtra and Goa.
- **Dandeli Anshi-Shravathi Valley**: Karnataka.
- **Kudremukh-Bhadra**: Karnataka.
- **Nagarahole-Pushpagiri-Talakaveri**: Karnataka.
- **Nagarahole-Bandipur-Mudumalai-Wayanad** and **Nagarahole-Mudumalai-Wayanad**: Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu. This is where the Nilgiri hills sit, the point where the Western and Eastern Ghats meet (NCERT).
- **Parambikulam-Eranikulam-Indira Gandhi**: Kerala and Tamil Nadu.
- **Kalakad Mundanthurai-Periyar**: Kerala and Tamil Nadu.

Other Ghats places named: **Biligiri Rangana (BR) Hills and Male Mahadeshwara Hills** in Karnataka's Chamarajnagar district, home of the Soliga people whose forest rights inside a tiger reserve were recognised; **Sathyamangalam Tiger Reserve** (TX2 award 2022; state Tamil Nadu is standard knowledge, verify); **Sahyadri Tiger Reserve** (Maharashtra), which has lost its tigers. The 2022 table gives Karnataka 563, Tamil Nadu 306, Kerala 213 and Goa 5 tigers, 1,087 in the Western Ghats as a whole, and says numbers have declined there.

**Kasturirangan and Gadgil.** Two committees dealt with how much of the Ghats to protect as ecologically sensitive: the Gadgil panel recommended 64 per cent; a high-level working group under Kasturirangan reduced it to 37 per cent; the 2018 draft notification covered about 57,000 sq km across six states (PMF IAS; the draft's final status is to be verified).

## The coast

**Sundarbans** (West Bengal): a Ramsar example, a large delta, and a tiger landscape with 100 tigers in the reserve-wise list and 101 in the landscape table (state is standard knowledge; verify). **Chilika Lake** (Odisha): Ramsar example. **Olive ridley turtles**: the Sea Turtle Conservation Project works in 10 coastal states with special emphasis on **Odisha**. The Madras Crocodile Bank Trust near Chennai is a reptile zoo and research station, an *ex-situ* place, not a protected area.

## The islands and the sea

The Andaman and Nicobar group (about 572 islands, between 6 and 14 degrees N and 92 and 94 degrees E) and Lakshadweep (about 36 islands, 11 inhabited) are described in Day 17 lesson GS-130. NCERT mentions their coral deposits, beaches and equatorial vegetation; it does not list their protected areas.

## Sacred groves as a Ghats check

PMF IAS lists sacred groves in the Western Ghats of Karnataka and Maharashtra and cites the Devara Kadu (Karnataka) and Sarpa Kavu (Kerala) traditions. The Supreme Court has directed that sacred groves be classified as forests and notified as community reserves under the Wildlife Act (PMF IAS; verify the current status).

*Objective:* State the species-to-place pairs named in the extracts and use habitat logic to reason about the rest.

## The pairs

The pairs below come from PMF IAS unless marked. Counts change; read them as of the book's edition.

- **Tiger**: Project Tiger began at Corbett, Uttarakhand (1973); India held 3,167 tigers in the 2022 census (75 per cent of the world's tigers) according to PMF IAS, with a further Wildlife Institute of India analysis giving an upper limit of 3,925 and an average of 3,682 (PMF IAS p. 183). The state table totals 3,682 for 2022 and 2,967 for 2018, and its landscape rows (819, 1,439, 1,087, 236 and 101) add to 3,682, so the table is the like-for-like comparison: 2,967 to 3,682 is +715, about 24 per cent. The headline 3,167 is the minimum (camera-trap-based) count, so the book's 200, or 6.7 per cent, sets a 2022 minimum against a 2018 mean (verify against the NTCA 2022 report).
- **Asiatic lion**: Gir, Gujarat, the only wild population.
- **Cheetah**: Kuno National Park, Madhya Pradesh (reintroduced in 2022 after being declared extinct in India in 1952).
- **Great one-horned rhinoceros**: Kaziranga, Orang, Pobitora and Manas in Assam; also pockets of the Indo-Nepal Terai and northern West Bengal.
- **Ganges river dolphin**: Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary, Bihar (the only sanctuary for this species); National Aquatic Animal; Project Dolphin launched 2020.
- **Asiatic wild ass (khulan)**: Rann of Kutch.
- **Snow leopard**: Hemis-Spiti, Nanda Devi-Gangotri, Khangchendzonga-Tawang.
- **Great Indian Bustard**: its sanctuary appears in the Maharashtra ESZ list; critically endangered.
- **Olive ridley turtle**: nesting coasts with special emphasis on Odisha.
- **Crocodilians**: marsh (mugger) crocodile, saltwater crocodile and gharial (critically endangered), bred at the Madras Crocodile Bank. The gharial is a river species (habitat logic below).
- **Vultures**: Vulture Safe Zones at Corbett, Dudhwa and Katerniaghat. Diclofenac, banned for veterinary use in 2006, caused the crash.
- **Elephants**: corridors across the north-east first, then south India, central India, northern West Bengal and the north-west; Project Elephant began in 1992 and merged into the Project Tiger and Elephant Division in 2023.
- **Soligas**: BR Hills, Karnataka.

## Habitat logic for the pairs you do not know

Ask what the species eats and where that food lives.

- **River specialists** (gharial, Ganges dolphin) need long flowing rivers with sandbanks and fish: rivers of the northern plains, not coasts.
- **Grassland and desert species** (bustard, wild ass, cheetah) need open ground: Rajasthan, Gujarat, the savanna of Kuno.
- **Wet forest and floodplain giants** (rhino, wild buffalo, elephant) need tall grass, swamps and rivers: the Brahmaputra plains and the Tarai.
- **High-altitude species** (snow leopard, chiru, takin) stay above about 3,000 m in the Himalaya and Trans-Himalaya.
- **Coastal and mangrove species** (saltwater crocodile, olive ridley) need tidal flats and beaches.

This is a reasoning device to narrow the choice, not proof of the answer.

## Common swap errors

Kaziranga and Orang (both rhino parks in Assam); Bandipur (Karnataka) and Bandhavgarh (Madhya Pradesh); Kanha and Kaziranga; Gir (lion) and Girnar; and any two Assam parks with a sanctuary and a national park label. When two names look alike, tie each to a unique anchor: Bandipur with the Nilgiris, Bandhavgarh with Madhya Pradesh.

### 9. Exam: how UPSC asks location questions, elimination and a map drill

*Objective:* Apply a five-step elimination method to location questions, work the two official questions, and drill with map-style questions.

## Five steps

1. **Classify the stem.** Is it asking state, landscape, species, label or law?
2. **Habitat step.** Turn the species or feature into a habitat (river, mangrove, grassland, high altitude).
3. **Landscape step.** Map the habitat to one of the six landscapes.
4. **State step.** Map the landscape to its states; cross out options from other landscapes.
5. **Label step.** If the stem says sanctuary, park, reserve or Ramsar, check that the option is of that label.

## The 2017 question: gharials

The options are Bhitarkanika Mangroves, Chambal River, Pulicat Lake and Deepor Beel. Habitat: the gharial is a fish-eating crocodilian of long flowing rivers with sandbanks; PMF IAS lists it with the mugger and saltwater crocodile at the Madras Crocodile Bank, and NCERT notes the ravines of the Chambal in the Peninsular plateau's north-west. Bhitarkanika is a coastal mangrove (saltwater crocodile habitat), Pulicat is a coastal lagoon, and Deepor Beel is a freshwater wetland in Assam. Only the Chambal River is a long flowing river. Analysis points to the Chambal; that the Chambal holds a National Chambal Sanctuary across Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh is standard knowledge to verify. This is analysis only, not the official key.

## The 2018 question: Pakhui

The options are Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya and Nagaland. PMF IAS names the **Pakke-Nameri-Sonai Rupai-Manas** corridor as lying in Arunachal Pradesh and Assam; Pakhui is another spelling of Pakke (verify). The corridor listing gives no Pakke link with Manipur, Meghalaya or Nagaland. Analysis points to Arunachal Pradesh; no official key is asserted.

## Typical traps

- **Neighbouring states**: Assam against Arunachal Pradesh, Karnataka against Kerala, Madhya Pradesh against Maharashtra.
- **Label swap**: calling a national park a sanctuary, or a Ramsar wetland a protected area under the Wildlife Act.
- **Stale counts**: the number of parks and reserves grows; never learn it as a fixed number.
- **Overlay confusion**: Manas is a national park, a World Heritage site and a corridor.

### Core concepts

- Nine labels: national park, sanctuary, tiger reserve, elephant reserve, conservation reserve, community reserve, biosphere reserve, Ramsar site, World Heritage site.
- Parks and sanctuaries are State-notified under the Wildlife Act; parks allow no rights, sanctuaries may allow some.
- Biosphere reserve and World Heritage are UNESCO designations that do not change domestic legal status; Ramsar is a treaty listing.
- Organise WHERE by landscape, then state, then water body.
- Habitat logic narrows species-place questions.
