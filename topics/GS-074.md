## GS-074: Cold War blocs and proxy conflicts

Mains GS1 · World History · 18th–20th century

*Goal:* Explain Cold War blocs and proxy conflicts and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Formation of the blocs

Post-war dependence on aid and credit pushed many developing countries to back the foreign policies of powerful patrons, dividing the world into a US-led western camp and a Soviet-led camp. After 1945 the east European countries the Soviet army had freed from fascist forces were remodelled on the Soviet pattern and bound together by the Warsaw Pact, forming the 'second world'. The US-led NATO stood opposite. The rivalry was ideological — whether socialism would outdo capitalism — and military, generating an arms race, nuclear stockpiles and rival blocs. The Berlin Wall, raised in 1961 between East and West Berlin, became the Cold War's most powerful symbol of the capitalist–communist divide.

### Proxy conflict and the non-aligned response

Korea shows how the rivalry was fought indirectly. Freed from Japanese colonial rule in 1945, the peninsula was split at the 38th parallel, with Soviets in the North and the UN in the South; separate governments in 1948 made the division permanent. War erupted in June 1950, with US-led UN forces supporting the South and communist China supporting the North, and ended in a July 1953 armistice that left Korea divided and devastated. India, meanwhile, refused to join either military alliance. Nehru's non-alignment sought to protect sovereignty and pursue development, while Pakistan joined US-led alliances, causing friction in Indo-US relations during the 1950s.

### End of the Cold War and exam angles

The Soviet Union kept pace in the arms race only at great cost, lagged in technology and consumer goods, and was further weakened by its 1979 invasion of Afghanistan. Gorbachev's reforms from 1985 and his decision not to intervene let east European communist regimes fall after mass protests; the Berlin Wall came down in November 1989 and Germany reunified. Following a failed hardliner coup in 1991, Russia, Ukraine and Belarus declared the USSR disbanded in December 1991. Consequences: end of bloc confrontation, US as sole superpower, and many new states. Prelims: NATO versus Warsaw Pact; Wall 1961–89; Korean armistice 1953. Mains: phases, proxy wars, India's non-alignment.

### Worked example

Question: 'Discuss the Cold War as a contest of ideology, alliances and proxy wars, and evaluate India's response.' Frame: (1) Ideology: socialism versus capitalism. (2) Alliances: NATO versus Warsaw Pact; Berlin Wall as symbol. (3) Proxy war: Korea 1950–53. (4) India: non-alignment, Suez protest versus silence on Hungary, aid from both blocs. (5) End: Gorbachev's reforms, 1989 revolutions, USSR dissolved 1991, unipolarity. Conclude on the Cold War's legacy for divided states and the global order.

### Recall

- - **q:** When was the Berlin Wall built and when did it fall?
  - **a:** Built in 1961; broken by the people on 9 November 1989.
- - **q:** Who backed each side in the Korean War?
  - **a:** US-led UN forces supported South Korea; communist China supported North Korea.
- - **q:** Which policies did Gorbachev introduce?
  - **a:** Perestroika (restructuring) and glasnost (openness).

### Common trap

Equating non-alignment with neutrality or isolation. India took positions on issues (Suez 1956), drew aid from both blocs and contributed to UN peacekeeping, though its stance was not always balanced (Hungary 1956).
