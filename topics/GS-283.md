## GS-283: NDCs and net-zero commitments

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Climate and treaties

*Goal:* Explain NDCs and net-zero commitments and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### From the first NDC to the updated NDC

India's first NDC (2015) had three quantitative goals for 2030: lower the emission intensity of GDP by 33-35% from 2005, reach 40% cumulative non-fossil installed capacity, and create an additional carbon sink of 2.5 to 3 billion tonnes. By 2019 intensity had fallen 33%, and non-fossil capacity reached 43.82% by October 2023, so the first two targets were met early. The Union Cabinet approved an updated NDC on 3 August 2022, converting the Panchamrit announced at COP26 into a 45% intensity reduction and about 50% non-fossil capacity by 2030, with the capacity goal to be met with the help of technology transfer and low-cost international finance including from the Green Climate Fund, plus a mass movement for LiFE.

### Progress on the updated targets

MoEFCC reports that emission intensity of GDP fell 36% between 2005 and 2020. The share of non-fossil sources in installed capacity rose from about 30% in 2014 to 51.93% in December 2025, and the 50% milestone was reached in June 2025, five years early. On sinks, ISFR 2023 implies an additional 2.29 billion tonnes CO2e since 2005 against the 2.5-3.0 billion tonne goal. India's 2020 inventory, reported in BUR-4, gives total emissions of 2,959 million tonnes CO2e without land use and 2,437 million tonnes net, with energy contributing about 75.66%. India is preparing its successive NDC.

### Net zero 2070 and how India frames it

The updated NDC is described as a step towards India's long-term goal of net-zero by 2070, and the Long-Term Low Emission Development Strategy was submitted in November 2022, grounded in India's claim to an equitable share of the global carbon budget. At COP30, India stated that developed countries should reach net zero significantly earlier than their present timelines, and that net zero should be read as a global objective around 2050 rather than a single deadline for every country. It cited initiatives such as the International Solar Alliance, Global Biofuel Alliance, Nuclear Mission and Green Hydrogen Mission as part of its pathway.

### Worked example

Prelims-style: India's updated NDC (2022) commits to (1) 45% reduction in emission intensity by 2030 from 2005 - yes; (2) 50% of electricity generation from renewables - no, it is about 50% of installed capacity from non-fossil sources; (3) net zero by 2050 - no, India's goal is 2070.

### Recall

- - **q:** When did the Cabinet approve India's updated NDC?
  - **a:** 3 August 2022.
- - **q:** What is the updated emission-intensity target?
  - **a:** 45% reduction by 2030 from 2005 levels.
- - **q:** When was the 50% non-fossil capacity milestone reached?
  - **a:** June 2025.

### Common trap

Confusing installed capacity with generation: the 50% target is about installed electricity capacity from non-fossil sources, not their share in electricity generated.
