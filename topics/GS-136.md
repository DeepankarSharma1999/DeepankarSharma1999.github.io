## GS-136: World distribution of minerals and energy

Mains GS1 + Prelims GS1 · Geography · Human/economic/world

*Goal:* Explain World distribution of minerals and energy and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Geology sets the pattern

Where a mineral occurs depends on how it was concentrated. NCERT's modes: veins and lodes in igneous and metamorphic rock (tin, copper, zinc, lead), sedimentary beds (coal, some iron ore, gypsum and potash), residual weathering crusts (bauxite), placers in river sands (gold, tin, platinum) and sea water or seabed nodules. This explains the world map. Metallic ores cluster in the ancient crystalline plateaus - iron ore in north Sweden, copper and nickel in Ontario, platinum and chromite in South Africa - while sedimentary plains and young fold belts hold limestone, phosphate (Algeria), manganese (Georgia, Ukraine), coal and petroleum. Oil gathers in anticlines and fault traps beneath sealing rock.

### Minerals continent by continent

NCERT's 2021-22 Class 8 chapter maps metals by continent. Asia holds large iron ore in China and India and produces over half the world's tin, chiefly China, Malaysia and Indonesia; China also leads in lead, antimony and tungsten. Europe is the leading iron-ore producer, with large deposits in Russia, Ukraine, Sweden and France. North America's minerals lie in three belts: the Canadian Shield (iron, nickel, gold, uranium, copper), the Appalachians (coal) and the western Cordilleras (copper, lead, zinc, gold, silver). Brazil produces the most high-grade iron ore, Chile and Peru lead in copper, and Bolivia and Brazil in tin. Africa leads in diamonds, gold and platinum, and Australia in bauxite, with gold at Kalgoorlie and Coolgardie.

### Energy producers and the economics of mining

Fuels follow sedimentary basins. The same text lists China, USA, Germany, Russia, South Africa and France as leading coal producers; Iran, Iraq, Saudi Arabia and Qatar as chief oil producers, followed by USA, Russia, Venezuela and Algeria; and Russia, Norway, the UK and the Netherlands for natural gas. Paraguay, Norway, Brazil and China lead hydel power, which supplies about a quarter of world electricity. Geology only creates possibility: mining pays when size, grade, mode of occurrence, demand, technology, capital and transport costs align. High labour costs are pushing developed economies out of mining, while several developing economies draw over half their earnings from minerals. India, whose import bill is topped by petroleum, shows the gap between demand and domestic output.

### Worked example

Question: 'Explain the uneven world distribution of minerals and energy resources.' Frame: (1) geological control - metallic ores in crystalline shields (Canadian Shield, north Sweden, South Africa), non-metallics and fuels in sedimentary basins; (2) continental examples - Brazil iron ore, Chile/Peru copper, Asian tin, Australian bauxite, African gold/diamonds/platinum; (3) fuels - West Asian oil, coal in China/USA/Russia, gas in Russia/Norway; hydel in Paraguay/Norway/Brazil/China; (4) economic filters and the shift of mining to developing countries; (5) consequence - import dependence (India's oil bill).

### Recall

- - **q:** Which country does NCERT name as the largest producer of bauxite, and which of high-grade iron ore?
  - **a:** Australia (bauxite); Brazil (high-grade iron ore).
- - **q:** Name the three mineral zones of North America.
  - **a:** The Canadian Shield north of the Great Lakes, the Appalachian region (coal) and the western Cordilleras.
- - **q:** Which countries lead in hydel power, per NCERT?
  - **a:** Paraguay, Norway, Brazil and China.

### Common trap

Treating a reserve and a mine as the same thing: NCERT notes that ore concentration, ease of extraction and closeness to market decide when a deposit or reserve actually becomes a mine.
