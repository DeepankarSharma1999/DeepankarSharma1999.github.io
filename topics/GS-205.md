## GS-205: Anti-defection law

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS2 · Polity · Institutions, law and politics

*Goal:* Explain Anti-defection law and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Purpose and grounds

NCERT explains that many legislators used to change parties after election, often to become ministers or for cash rewards, and that parties eventually agreed to restrict this through the 52nd Amendment in 1985, later modified by the 91st Amendment. Under paragraph 2 a member elected on a party ticket is disqualified if he voluntarily gives up party membership or votes or abstains contrary to the party's direction without prior permission, unless the party condones it within fifteen days. Independents lose their seat if they join a party after election, while nominated members have six months from taking their seat to join a party without penalty.

### Exceptions and the decision-maker

Paragraph 4 protects members when their original party merges with another, provided at least two-thirds of the legislature party agree; members may then join the merged or new party or function as a separate group. Paragraph 3 was omitted by the 91st Amendment, and paragraph 2 was amended to make it subject only to paragraphs 4 and 5. Paragraph 5 protects presiding officers, such as a Speaker, Deputy Speaker or Deputy Chairman, who give up party membership on election to that office. Paragraph 6 makes the Speaker or Chairman the deciding authority, deeming such proceedings to be legislative proceedings. Paragraph 7 sought to bar courts, but the official footnote records that it was declared invalid.

### Evaluating the law

NCERT's Class X text says the law has helped bring defections down but made dissent harder, since legislators must accept what party leaders decide. The Class XI text is more critical, observing that the amendment has not been able to curb defections and has given additional power to party leadership and presiding officers over members; it also notes that a defector is barred from holding political office such as ministership. A balanced Mains answer should weigh stability of governments and respect for the voters' mandate against intra-party democracy, freedom of conscience in voting, and concerns over the presiding officer's role.
