## GS-320: Women and child welfare schemes

Mains GS2 · Social Justice · Rights, welfare and services

*Goal:* Organize women’s and child welfare around needs across the life course.

### Link services to a chain of needs

A girl’s wellbeing depends on nutrition, safe schooling, healthcare and freedom from violence. For women, maternity support, childcare, safety and economic opportunities interact. Child-focused delivery through anganwadis and school meals connects nutrition with care and education. An isolated benefit may help while leaving the next bottleneck untouched, such as unsafe travel or unpaid care work.

### Evaluate agency as well as coverage

A good scheme is accessible, timely and respectful, with clear eligibility and complaints arrangements. Ask whether women can control the benefit and make decisions, not only whether a household received funds. For children, combine prevention, early identification and referral. Disaggregate outcomes by age, disability and location; aggregate enrollment can hide those facing the greatest barriers.

### Worked example

A training programme enrolls mothers but schedules classes during childcare hours. A nearby crèche and suitable timings raise completion. The change removes a practical barrier rather than treating absence as lack of motivation.

### Recall

- - **q:** What is a life-course approach?
  - **a:** Linking support across stages such as childhood, adolescence and maternity.
- - **q:** Why measure control over benefits?
  - **a:** Receipt alone does not establish women’s agency.

### Common trap

A scheme’s name or announced allocation does not show that services reached the intended woman or child.
