## GS-215: Comparison with other constitutions

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS2 · Polity · Institutions, law and politics

*Goal:* Explain Comparison with other constitutions and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Borrowing without imitation

NCERT emphasises that the framers drew on many constitutional traditions but did not copy slavishly; every provision had to be defended as suited to Indian problems and aspirations. Ambedkar argued that a constitution framed so late could only innovate by correcting faults and adapting ideas to national needs. NCERT's table credits Britain with FPTP, parliamentary government, rule of law, the Speaker's institution and law-making procedure; the USA with the charter of rights, judicial review and judicial independence; Ireland with Directive Principles; France with liberty, equality and fraternity; and Canada with a quasi-federal system and residual powers. The table is a selection, not an exhaustive list of influences.

### Executive models compared

In a presidential system, such as the USA or Brazil, the president is both head of state and head of government and is very powerful in theory and practice. In a parliamentary system, such as the UK, Germany, Italy, Japan or India, the prime minister and cabinet hold real power while the president or monarch is largely ceremonial. Semi-presidential systems like France, Russia and Sri Lanka combine a president with significant powers and a prime minister, sometimes from different parties; Sri Lanka's directly elected executive presidency dates from 1978. India chose the parliamentary form because the 1919 and 1935 Acts had shown that it lets elected representatives control the executive and reduces the risk of personality cult.

### Federal models compared

Federations form along two routes. In 'coming together' federations, such as the USA, Switzerland and Australia, independent states pool sovereignty and usually retain equal, strong powers. In 'holding together' federations, such as India, Spain and Belgium, a large country divides power, the centre tends to be stronger and units may have unequal powers. India's design fits the second pattern: the Union holds residuary legislative power, some States have special provisions under Article 371, and Union Territories have limited powers, which matches the Canadian-inspired strong-centre model in NCERT's table. For Mains, contrast how the executive is held to account: in India through the legislature, while a presidential executive concentrates power in the president.
