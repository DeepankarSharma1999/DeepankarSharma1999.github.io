## GS-175: Vice President and Rajya Sabha chair

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS2 · Polity · Union government

### Bridge: Ex officio: holding one post because you hold another

**Ex officio** is a Latin phrase meaning 'by virtue of one's office'. A person is ex officio something when the law attaches one post to another, so that whoever holds the first post automatically holds the second. Nobody votes for the second post separately, and the person does not apply for it.

The Vice-President (VP) of India is the ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (RS). Parliament does not elect a Chairman; the electoral college elects a Vice-President, and the Constitution (Article 64) makes that person the Chairman. If the person stops being Vice-President, he stops being Chairman at the same moment. That is why Laxmikanth says the Chairman can be removed only by removing him from the office of Vice-President.

Contrast this with the Speaker of the Lok Sabha, who is *elected by the Lok Sabha itself from among its members*. The Speaker is a member of the House; the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is not a member of it. You will meet 'ex officio' again in committees: the Speaker is ex officio chairman of the Business Advisory Committee of the Lok Sabha, meaning he sits there automatically because of his post.

A useful test sentence: 'X is ex officio Y' means 'whoever is X is automatically Y'.

### Bridge: Casting vote and the 'first instance'

When a House of Parliament decides a question, members vote 'Aye' or 'No'. The person presiding (the Speaker in the Lok Sabha, the Chairman in the Rajya Sabha) has a special rule. He does **not vote in the first instance**, meaning in the first round of voting, when he is acting only as the referee. If the Ayes and the Noes are exactly equal, the question would be stuck in a **tie**. Only then does the presiding officer vote, and that vote is called a **casting vote**, because it 'casts' the decision one way and ends the deadlock.

Laxmikanth says that this arrangement makes the position of the Speaker impartial, since he cannot take part in ordinary contests between the government and the Opposition; the same logic applies to the Chairman. A casting vote is therefore rare.

A worked example. Suppose 99 members vote Aye and 99 vote No. The Chairman, who does not vote in the first instance, now votes: if he says Aye, the Ayes win 100 to 99. If instead the Houses divide 100 to 98, the question is decided without him and he never votes.

A second idea you need: the **Deputy Chairman**, when presiding, has the same rule. When the Chairman presides, the Deputy Chairman is just an ordinary member who may speak and vote like everyone else.

### Bridge: Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha: the basics you need first

Parliament of India has three parts: the **President**, the **Lok Sabha** (House of the People) and the **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States). The Lok Sabha is the lower, directly elected House. The Rajya Sabha is the upper House that, as NCERT puts it, represents the States of India; its members are elected indirectly, by the elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies, and the President nominates 12 members with special knowledge or practical experience (literature, science, art and social service). The Rajya Sabha is never dissolved as a whole: one-third of its members retire every second year, so it is a permanent House.

Why this matters for the VP: the VP presides over the Rajya Sabha, while the Lok Sabha has its own presiding officer, the Speaker. The two Houses are equal in many matters but not in money matters and in control of the council of ministers, which is responsible only to the Lok Sabha. You will study those inequalities in GS-179 (legislative procedure) and GS-180 (Money Bills); for now remember only: **the Rajya Sabha is a House of Parliament where the VP presides, but the government survives or falls on the Lok Sabha's confidence.**

The word **member** matters too. The Speaker is a member of the Lok Sabha; the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is not a member of the Rajya Sabha. That one difference explains several rows in the Speaker-versus-Chairman table you will meet in module 7.

### 1. Orientation: the second highest office and its two jobs

*Objective:* State what the Vice-President is, name the two jobs of the office and locate Articles 63 to 71 on a map.

## The office in one sentence

The **Vice-President** (VP) of India holds the **second highest office in the country**. In the official **warrant of precedence** (the government's ranked list used to seat and order dignitaries at State functions) he ranks next to the President. Laxmikanth adds that the office is modelled on the American Vice-President, though the Indian version differs in a crucial way that you will study in module 8.

## The two jobs

The Constitution gives the VP two functions, and almost every UPSC question is about one of them:

1. **Ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.** In this role his powers and functions are similar to those of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha. This is his everyday work.
2. **Acting President.** When the office of President falls vacant (resignation, impeachment, death or otherwise) he acts as President, but only for a limited time until a new President is elected. He also discharges the President's functions when the President is absent or ill.

Dr B.R. Ambedkar summed up the logic in the Constituent Assembly: the VP's normal function is to preside over the Council of States, and only on a rare occasion, for a temporary period, may he have to take over the President's duties. Hold on to this sentence; it explains why State legislators do not elect him (module 3).

## The Articles map

Laxmikanth Table 18.2 lists the Articles. Learn them as a mini-story from existence to disputes:

- **Article 63**: there shall be a Vice-President.
- **Article 64**: the VP is ex officio Chairman of the Council of States.
- **Article 65**: the VP acts as President during casual vacancies, or during the President's absence.
- **Article 66**: election of the VP.
- **Article 67**: term of office (resignation and removal are part of the same Article).
- **Article 68**: time of holding election to fill a vacancy, and term of the person elected to fill a casual vacancy.
- **Article 69**: oath or affirmation.
- **Article 70**: discharge of the President's functions in other contingencies.
- **Article 71**: matters relating to or connected with the election of the VP (disputes).

### 2. Who can be Vice-President: qualifications, nomination, oath and conditions

*Objective:* List the four qualifications, the nomination rule, the oath and the two conditions of office, and compare them with the President's.

## The four qualifications

To be eligible for election as Vice-President a person must:

1. be a **citizen of India**;
2. have completed **35 years** of age;
3. be **qualified for election as a member of the Rajya Sabha**; and
4. **not hold any office of profit** under the Union government, a State government, a local authority or any other public authority.

An **office of profit** is a government post that brings financial gain; holding one would create a conflict of interest. Laxmikanth adds a safe-list: a sitting President or Vice-President, a Governor of any State and a Union or State minister are *not deemed* to hold an office of profit, so they can stand.

Notice the contrast with the President (GS-174): the President must be qualified for election to the *Lok Sabha*, the VP to the *Rajya Sabha*. Being 'qualified for election to the Rajya Sabha' brings in the general conditions of the Representation of the People Act (such as being a registered elector); the extract does not list these, so check them in a later lesson on membership of Parliament.

## Nomination and deposit

A nomination paper must be subscribed by at least **20 electors as proposers and 20 electors as seconders**. Every candidate deposits **₹15,000** in the Reserve Bank of India. Laxmikanth cites the Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952 as amended in 1997; for the President the numbers of proposers and seconders are 50 each (GS-174). These figures are from the 6th-edition text; verify them against the Election Commission's current rules before relying on them.

## Oath or affirmation

Before entering office the VP makes and subscribes an oath or affirmation, swearing (1) to bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India and (2) to faithfully discharge the duties of his office. The oath is administered by the **President or some person appointed by him** (Article 69).

## The two conditions of office

1. He should **not be a member of either House of Parliament or of a State legislature**. If a sitting member is elected VP, he is **deemed to have vacated his seat** in that House on the date he enters upon the office of VP.
2. He should **not hold any other office of profit**.

## Why these rules?

The first condition follows from the VP's role as Chairman: he presides over the Rajya Sabha but is not its member, so he cannot be an MP or an MLA at the same time. The 'no office of profit' rule keeps the second-highest office free of dependence on the executive. Notice how the VP, as a presiding officer, is kept outside the House's own membership, unlike the Speaker (module 7).

### 3. The electoral college: who elects the Vice-President and why

*Objective:* Describe the electoral college of the VP, the two differences from the President's, the voting system and the Supreme Court's role under Article 71.

## Indirect election

The VP is not elected directly by the people. He is elected by an **electoral college** (a body of electors chosen to elect one office-holder) consisting of the **members of both Houses of Parliament**. Under the original Constitution the two Houses sat in a joint meeting to elect him; Laxmikanth notes that this cumbersome method was dropped by the **11th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1961**. Today the rule is in Article 66.

## Two differences from the Presidential college

Laxmikanth lists two ways in which the VP's college differs from the President's (compare GS-174):

1. **It includes nominated members as well as elected members of Parliament.** (The President's college has only *elected* members.) So the 12 persons nominated to the Rajya Sabha can vote for the VP. The 6th-edition text also treats nominated Lok Sabha members as electors; a 2019-20 amendment ended the nomination of Anglo-Indian members to the Lok Sabha, so check the current position before using that detail (verify).
2. **It does not include members of State legislative assemblies.** (The President's college does include the elected MLAs of the States, and of Delhi and Puducherry; after the 2019 reorganisation it also appears to include the Jammu and Kashmir UT assembly: verify, see GS-174.)

## Why State legislators are left out

Ambedkar gave the reason. The President is head of the State and his power extends to the administration of the Centre and the States, so State legislatures should have a voice in choosing him. The VP's normal function is to preside over the Council of States; only rarely, and for a temporary period, may he be called to act as President. So it did not seem necessary to ask the State legislatures to take part.

## What is the same

The *manner* of election is the same in both cases. The VP's election, like the President's, is held in accordance with the system of **proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote (STV)**, and the voting is by **secret ballot**. STV means each elector marks candidates in order of preference (1, 2, 3 and so on), and if no candidate wins on first preferences, the lowest candidate is eliminated and his ballots pass to the next preference. You will see this done by hand in module 4. Because the VP's college is *entirely Parliament*, every elector's vote can carry the same weight, whereas in the Presidential election votes have different values to give uniformity and parity (GS-174); the extract does not state the equal-weight point, so treat it as standard knowledge to verify.

## Disputes: Article 71

All doubts and disputes connected with the VP's election are inquired into and decided by the **Supreme Court**, whose decision is final. Two protections matter for exams:

- The election **cannot be challenged** on the ground that the electoral college was incomplete (that is, that some seats of members were vacant).
- If the Supreme Court declares the election void, **acts done by him before that declaration are not invalidated**.

### 4. A hand-checked count: how a single-seat STV election works

*Objective:* Compute the quota and complete a three-candidate STV count by hand, and explain how it differs from the Presidential election count.

## The quota

The STV mechanism you saw in GS-174 for the President applies here, with one simplification: every member of Parliament carries **one vote of equal weight**, so you can count ballots rather than weighted vote-values. For one seat, the **quota** (the number of votes that guarantees victory) is:

$$ Quota = (Total valid votes ÷ 2) + 1, using the whole number of the quotient $$

If the number of valid ballots is 20, the quota is 20 ÷ 2 + 1 = **11**. If it is 21, then 21 ÷ 2 = 10.5, take the whole number 10 and add 1: the quota is **11**. If it is 31, the quota is 15 + 1 = **16**. (The extract does not print the quota formula; this is the standard rule also used in GS-174, flagged for verification.)

## A toy Parliament of 20 voters

Imagine a tiny Parliament of 20 members, of whom 2 are nominated; all 20 vote and all ballots are valid. Three candidates stand: **A**, **B** and **C**. First preferences are:

| Candidate | First preferences |
|---|---|
| A | 8 |
| B | 7 |
| C | 5 |
| **Total** | **20** |

The quota is 11. Nobody has it. The candidate with the fewest votes, C, is eliminated, and C's 5 ballots are transferred to the next preference marked on each. Suppose 3 ballots show A next, 1 shows B next and 1 shows no further preference (an **exhausted** ballot, which simply stops counting).

| Candidate | After transfer |
|---|---|
| A | 8 + 3 = **11** |
| B | 7 + 1 = **8** |
| Exhausted | 1 |
| **Total** | **20** |

A has reached the quota of 11, so **A is declared elected**. Check the arithmetic: 11 + 8 + 1 = 20, so no ballot has been lost. [Verify] The quota is calculated once, at the start, from the valid ballots; it is not recalculated as ballots become exhausted. If exhausted ballots mean nobody can reach the quota (for example 20 ballots, A 10, B 9, 1 exhausted: the quota of 11 is never reached), exclusion continues until one candidate reaches the quota or only one continuing candidate remains, who is then declared elected [verify against the Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Rules, 1974].

## Two-candidate contests

With two candidates there is no transfer at all: one of them gets more than half on first preferences. Laxmikanth's Table 18.1 shows, for example, 2017: Venkaiah Naidu 516 against Gopal Krishna Gandhi 244. Four elections in the table (1952, 1957, 1979 and 1987) were won **unopposed**, so no count took place.

## How this differs from the President's count

In GS-174 each MLA's and MP's vote has a different *value*, computed by formulas using the 1971 population, so the quota is calculated on total vote-value. Here every elector's vote is equal, so the arithmetic is a head-count of ballots. That is the practical consequence of leaving the State legislators out.

### 5. Term, resignation, removal and vacancy (Articles 67 and 68)

*Objective:* State the term, how the VP resigns and how he can be removed, with the exact majorities; explain vacancy rules.

## Term of five years

The VP holds office for **five years** from the date he enters upon his office. He can hold office beyond five years until his successor assumes charge (Article 67), and he is eligible for **re-election**, any number of times. Table 18.1 shows that Dr S. Radhakrishnan was elected for a second term (1952 and 1957) and that Hamid Ansari won in 2007 and 2012.

## Resignation

He may resign at any time by addressing his resignation letter to the **President**. (Compare the Deputy Chairman, who resigns by writing to the Chairman.)

## Removal under Article 67(b): no impeachment

A formal impeachment is not required. The VP can be removed by a **resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members of the House and agreed to by the Lok Sabha**. Four rules are tested again and again:

1. The resolution can be **introduced only in the Rajya Sabha**, not in the Lok Sabha.
2. In the Rajya Sabha it needs an **effective majority**: a majority of *all the then members* (members at that time, not counting vacant seats). Laxmikanth says an effective majority is a type of special majority, not a separate one.
3. In the Lok Sabha it needs only a **simple majority**, a majority of members present and voting, to *agree*.
4. **At least 14 days' advance notice** must be given before such a resolution is moved.

Laxmikanth notes that **no ground** for removal is mentioned in the Constitution. Contrast the President: impeachment under Article 61 is for violation of the Constitution (GS-174).

**A hand-checked majority.** Suppose the House has 245 seats of which 5 are vacant, so 240 'then members'. A majority of all the then members is more than half of 240, that is **121** (240 ÷ 2 = 120; 120 + 1 = 121). Even if only 200 members are present, 121 Aye votes are still needed; 101 votes out of 200 present would be a simple majority but would fail. The vacant seats are subtracted, but absent members are not.

NCERT's one-box summary says the resolution is 'passed by a majority'. That is shorthand; use Laxmikanth's precise 'majority of all the then members' in the exam.

## Vacancy (Article 68)

A vacancy can arise from: expiry of the five-year term; resignation; removal; death; or otherwise (for example, disqualification or an election declared void). The timing rule is simple:

- If the vacancy comes from **expiry of term**, the election must be held **before** the term expires.
- If it comes from resignation, removal, death or otherwise, the election is to be held **as soon as possible**.
- The person elected to fill a casual vacancy serves a **full term of five years** from the date he assumes charge, not merely the unexpired term of his predecessor.

Krishna Kant was the first VP to die in office (a note in the extract); dates of such events are outside the extract, so verify them if needed.

### 6. The two functions: Chairman of the Rajya Sabha and acting President

*Objective:* Explain the VP's role as Chairman, the circumstances and limits of acting as President, and the pay rules.

## Function 1: ex officio Chairman (Article 64)

The VP acts as the ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. His powers and functions in that role are similar to the Speaker's in the Lok Sabha; module 7 sets out the detailed powers.

## Function 2: acting President (Article 65)

The VP acts as President when a vacancy occurs in the office of the President because of **resignation, impeachment, death or otherwise**. Laxmikanth says he can do so for **a maximum of six months**, within which a new President must be elected. A separate situation: when the sitting President is unable to discharge his functions due to **absence, illness or any other cause**, the VP discharges those functions until the President resumes office.

### Examples

- When two Presidents, Dr Zakir Hussain and Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed, died in office, the Vice-Presidents of the time, V.V. Giri and B.D. Jatti, acted as President.
- Dr Radhakrishnan discharged the President's functions in June 1960, when President Rajendra Prasad was on a 15-day tour of the USSR, and in July 1961, when he was very ill.
- NCERT cites B.D. Jatti acting as President on the death of Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed until a new President was elected.

### The contrast that matters

An **acting President** is a stand-in; he does not become President for the remainder of the term. A new President is elected, and the acting period ends (see module 8 for the American comparison).

## What the VP gives up while acting

While acting as, or discharging the functions of, the President the VP **does not perform the duties of the Chairman** of the Rajya Sabha. During this period the duties of the Chairman are performed by the **Deputy Chairman**. He is also **not entitled to the Chairman's salary or allowance**; he receives the salary and allowances of the **President**. Article 65(3), from standard knowledge (verify), also gives the VP, while acting as President, the powers and immunities of the President.

Article 70 lets Parliament provide for the discharge of the President's functions in 'other contingencies'. Standard knowledge (verify): Parliament has done this through the President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969, under which the Chief Justice of India and then the senior-most Supreme Court judge may discharge the functions if neither President nor Vice-President is available. The extract confirms only the title of Article 70, so this detail is flagged.

## Salary and pension

The Constitution fixes **no emoluments for the VP as VP**. He draws his regular salary as ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. In 2018 Parliament increased the Chairman's salary from ₹1.25 lakh to ₹4 lakh per month (the Finance Act, 2018, with effect from 1 January 2016). In 2008 the pension of a retired VP was set at **50% of his salary** per month, and he receives other facilities (daily allowance, residence, medical and travel). These money figures are from the 2020-edition text and can change; verify the current figures. The Chairman's salary is charged on the Consolidated Fund of India (module 7).

### 7. The Chairman's powers, the Deputy Chairman and the Speaker compared

*Objective:* List the Chairman's powers, describe the Deputy Chairman and panel, and compare the Chairman with the Speaker on six points.

## The Chairman: head of the House

The presiding officer of the Rajya Sabha is the **Chairman**. Laxmikanth says the powers and functions of the Chairman in the Rajya Sabha are **similar to those of the Speaker in the Lok Sabha**. The Speaker's main powers are: maintaining order and decorum (his primary responsibility, with final power); acting as the final interpreter, within the House, of the Constitution, the Rules and precedents; adjourning the House or suspending the sitting when there is no quorum (**one-tenth** of the total strength); and exercising a casting vote only in a tie. The Chairman has the equivalent powers in the Rajya Sabha. Further points:

- **Conduct of business and order.** He regulates proceedings. Detailed provisions, such as directing a member to withdraw or suspending a member for the rest of the session, are in the Rajya Sabha Rules of Procedure (Rules 255 and 256 are commonly cited; standard knowledge, verify numbering before citing).
- **Point of order.** The presiding officer decides points of order raised by members. The Speaker's decision in parliamentary matters is final (extract); the Chairman's position is similar under the Rules (verify).
- **Casting vote.** He does not vote in the first instance; he votes only in an equality of votes (bridge B2).
- **Disqualification on defection.** Under the Tenth Schedule the Speaker decides for Lok Sabha members (extract). For the Chairman this is only a cross-link: the Chairman decides for Rajya Sabha members, a topic for the anti-defection lesson (verify there).
- **Committees.** The Chairman nominates Rajya Sabha members to the departmental standing committees and is ex officio chairman of the Rajya Sabha's Business Advisory Committee and Rules Committee (GS-182).
- **Secretariat.** The secretary-general of each House is a permanent officer appointed by the presiding officer.
- **Removal.** The Chairman can be removed only by removing him from the office of VP.
- **During his own removal.** He cannot preside while a resolution for his removal is pending, but can be present, speak and take part in proceedings **without voting** (unlike the Speaker, who may vote in the first instance in such a case).

## Two Speaker powers the Chairman lacks

1. The Speaker alone **decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill** and endorses the certificate.
2. The Speaker **presides over a joint sitting** of both Houses (GS-179).

## Deputy Chairman and panel of Vice-Chairpersons

The **Deputy Chairman** is elected by the Rajya Sabha from among its members. He vacates office if he ceases to be a member, if he resigns by writing to the Chairman, or if removed by a resolution of a majority of all the then members with 14 days' notice. He performs the Chairman's duties when the office is vacant or when the VP is acting as President, and acts as Chairman when the Chairman is absent from a sitting. He is **not subordinate** to the Chairman; he is directly responsible to the House. When the Chairman presides he is an ordinary member and can vote; when he himself presides he has only a casting vote.

The Chairman nominates a **panel of vice-chairpersons** under the Rules. Any of them can preside in the absence of the Chairman and the Deputy Chairman, with the same powers. They cannot preside if the offices of both are vacant; then the President appoints a member to perform the Chairman's duties.

### 8. Indian and American Vice-Presidents, a short history and the Rajya Sabha's position

*Objective:* Contrast the Indian VP with the American VP, outline the list of Vice-Presidents and explain the Rajya Sabha's position in the Constitution.

## Indian and American Vice-Presidents

Laxmikanth's own comparison is short and exact. Both act as the **chairman of the Upper House** (the American VP chairs the Senate). But the American VP **succeeds to the presidency** when it falls vacant and remains President for the unexpired term of his predecessor. The Indian VP does not: he merely serves as an **acting** President until the new President assumes charge. Laxmikanth adds that some scholars call the VP 'His Superfluous Highness', because the Constitution has assigned him no significant function in that capacity; the office was created to maintain the **political continuity** of the Indian State. A compact comparison table is in the master notes (the rows on the USA beyond succession and chairing the Senate are standard knowledge, flagged).

## The list in outline (Table 18.1)

Laxmikanth lists fifteen elections from 1952 to 2017. In outline: Dr S. Radhakrishnan (1952, 1957, both unopposed); Dr Zakir Hussain (1962); V.V. Giri (1967); G.S. Pathak (1969); B.D. Jatti (1974); M. Hidayatullah (1979, unopposed); R. Venkataraman (1984); Dr Shankar Dayal Sharma (1987, unopposed); K.R. Narayanan (1992); Krishna Kant (1997); B.S. Shekhawat (2002); Mohd. Hamid Ansari (2007, 2012); Venkaiah Naidu (2017). The extract stops at 2017. Elections after 2017, including any mid-term vacancy reported in 2025, must be taken from the Election Commission or Rajya Sabha websites (verify); no names are given here beyond the extract.

## Position of the Rajya Sabha

Because the VP presides over the Rajya Sabha, you must know its constitutional position. Laxmikanth studies it from three angles. It is **equal** to the Lok Sabha in: ordinary Bills; Constitution amendment Bills; financial Bills involving expenditure from the Consolidated Fund; election and impeachment of the President; **election and removal of the VP** (though the Rajya Sabha alone initiates removal); recommendation for the removal of judges, the Chief Election Commissioner and the CAG; approval of ordinances and of emergency proclamations; and consideration of the reports of constitutional bodies.

It is **unequal** in: Money Bills (introduced only in the Lok Sabha; the Rajya Sabha cannot amend or reject and must return the Bill within 14 days); the Speaker's final say on Money Bill status; the Speaker presiding over joint sittings; the budget (the Rajya Sabha can discuss but not vote on demands for grants); the no-confidence motion and the discontinuance of a national emergency (Lok Sabha only).

It has **special powers** that the Lok Sabha lacks: authorising Parliament to legislate on a State List subject (Article 249); creating new All-India Services (Article 312); alone initiating the removal of the Vice-President (Article 67); and the rule under Articles 352, 356 and 360 that a proclamation can remain effective if approved by the Rajya Sabha alone when the Lok Sabha is dissolved. Laxmikanth's verdict: its position is neither as weak as the House of Lords' nor as strong as the American Senate's. For the full Money Bill rules see GS-179 and GS-180.

### 9. How UPSC tests this topic: patterns, the 2022 Mains question and traps

*Objective:* Recognise the recurring Prelims patterns and plan an answer to the verified Mains question on the Chairman's role.

## What the verified question asks

The registry holds one verified question for this topic: **Mains GS II 2022, Q4 (10 marks): 'Discuss the role of the Vice-President of India as the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.'** The directive **'Discuss'** asks for a balanced look at roles, powers and limits; it is not a request for an essay on the whole office. A plan for about 150 words:

- **Introduction (2 lines):** VP is the second highest office; under Article 64 he is ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, though not a member.
- **Role 1, presiding and order:** conducts business, maintains decorum, interprets rules and decides points of order; the Rules give him powers to direct a member to withdraw or to suspend (verify).
- **Role 2, impartial referee:** no vote in the first instance; casting vote only in a tie; removable only as VP, by an effective majority of the Rajya Sabha with the Lok Sabha's consent.
- **Role 3, institutional:** nominates members to committees; ex officio chairman of the Business Advisory and Rules committees; link between the House, the government and the Opposition.
- **Limits:** no Money Bill certificate, no presiding over joint sittings; not a member of the House; debates about the neutrality of the Chair (use dated news only with 'verify').
- **Conclusion:** a holder who is neutral and uses rules fairly strengthens the House as a forum of the States.

This is an answer plan, not a model answer, and no official marks scheme is implied.

## Prelims patterns

1. **Election comparisons.** Statements ask who votes in the VP election (nominated members, State legislators). The trap is to apply the President's rules; the VP's college is Parliament only, with nominated members included.
2. **Removal.** The classic trap swaps 'majority of all the then members' with 'majority of members present and voting', or says the Lok Sabha initiates, or says 'impeachment'.
3. **Article-number matching.** Learn Articles 64, 65, 67 and 71 firmly.
4. **Acting President.** Six months; no Chairman duties; President's salary; not President for the remainder of the term.
5. **Chairman versus Speaker.** Money Bill certificate, joint sitting, first vote during removal, membership of the House.

## Thought-aloud on a statement

Statement: 'The Vice-President can be removed by a resolution passed by the Lok Sabha and agreed to by the Rajya Sabha.' Reasoning: Article 67(b) reverses the order. The resolution is introduced and passed in the **Rajya Sabha** (effective majority) and then only **agreed to** by the Lok Sabha. The statement conflicts with the rule in module 5; eliminate it.

## NCERT versus Laxmikanth

NCERT's Vice-President box says removal by 'a majority'; Laxmikanth gives 'a majority of all the then members'. When two sources differ in precision, answer with the more precise version.

### 10. Guided practice: one MCQ thought aloud and one Mains skeleton

*Objective:* Apply the lesson to one thought-aloud MCQ and one Mains skeleton, then log your errors.

## Worked MCQ (thought aloud)

**Q.** Consider the following statements about the Vice-President of India:
1. He is elected by an electoral college that includes nominated members of Parliament.
2. The resolution for his removal can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.

### Notes: Acting President and who performs the Chairman's duties

- Step 1: A vacancy occurs in the President's office (resignation, impeachment, death or otherwise).
- Step 2: The VP acts as President for at most six months, within which a new President must be elected.
- Step 3: While acting, the VP does not perform the Chairman's duties; the Deputy Chairman performs them.
- Step 4: The VP receives the President's salary and allowances, not the Chairman's.
- Step 5: When the new President assumes charge, the VP resumes his own office and the Chairman's chair.

### Notes: Confused concepts

- The VP's removal is not 'impeachment'; there is no stated ground and the proceeding starts in the Rajya Sabha.
- An effective majority is not 'two-thirds'; it is a majority of all the then members.
- The Chairman is not a member of the Rajya Sabha; the Speaker is a member of the Lok Sabha.
- An acting President is not the President; he does not succeed for the unexpired term.
- The VP's college includes nominated members; the President's does not.

### Core concepts

- The VP is the second highest office, with two functions: Chairman of the Rajya Sabha and acting President.
- The VP is elected indirectly by Parliament only (elected and nominated members), by STV and secret ballot.
- Removal is by an effective-majority Rajya Sabha resolution agreed to by the Lok Sabha, with 14 days' notice; there is no stated ground.
- An acting President stands in only until a new President takes charge.
- The Chairman is ex officio and not a House member; the Speaker is elected from the members.

**Q.** What does 'ex officio' mean in the phrase 'ex officio Chairman'?

**A.** By virtue of holding another office; the person who is Vice-President automatically is Chairman.

**Q.** Which Article makes the Vice-President ex officio Chairman of the Council of States?

**A.** Article 64.

**Q.** What is the minimum notice for a resolution to remove the Vice-President?

**A.** 14 days.

**Q.** Why is the Vice-President's removal procedure simpler than the President's impeachment?

**A.** The VP's functions are less significant; there is no ground to prove and the Rajya Sabha initiates it with the Lok Sabha's consent, whereas the President's impeachment needs a formal charge and two-thirds of total membership in each House.

**Q.** Why can a Vice-President not preside while his own removal is under consideration, yet still speak?

**A.** To avoid being judge in his own cause; he is still allowed to be present, speak and take part in proceedings without voting.

**Q.** How does the electoral college for the VP lead to equal-weighted votes?

**A.** Because only members of Parliament vote, no weighting between States is needed (standard knowledge, verify).
