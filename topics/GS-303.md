## GS-303: Intellectual property rights and patents

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Science · General science / technology

*Goal:* Distinguish a patent from other intellectual property and its limits.

### The patent bargain

A patent is a territorial, time-limited right to exclude others from specified commercial uses of an invention in exchange for public disclosure. Indian patentability requires novelty, an inventive step and industrial applicability, subject to statutory exclusions. The normal term is twenty years from filing. A patent is not a certificate that a product is safe or commercially successful.

### Choose the correct protection

Copyright protects original expression, a trademark distinguishes commercial origin, and a geographical indication connects goods to a place and qualifying reputation or characteristics. An idea, a brand name and a written manual therefore raise different questions. Patent rights coexist with public-interest safeguards such as compulsory licensing under legal conditions; protection does not remove every regulatory obligation.

### Worked example

A startup creates a new water-filter mechanism, a brand and an instruction booklet. The mechanism may qualify for patent examination, the brand for trademark protection and the booklet for copyright. Each protects a different object.

### Recall

- - **q:** What three tests normally support patentability?
  - **a:** Novelty, inventive step and industrial applicability.
- - **q:** Does one Indian patent automatically apply worldwide?
  - **a:** No. Patent protection is territorial.

### Common trap

A discovery or minor modification does not automatically satisfy patentability requirements.
