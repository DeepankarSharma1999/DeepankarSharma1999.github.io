## GS-206: Constitutional bodies versus statutory bodies

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS2 · Polity · Institutions, law and politics

*Goal:* Explain Constitutional bodies versus statutory bodies and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Defining the two categories

Status depends on the source of authority. Constitutional bodies are named and structured in the text: Article 315 requires a Public Service Commission for the Union and each State, and Articles 338, 338A and 338B create national commissions for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and backward classes with a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and three members appointed by the President. Statutory bodies owe their existence to legislation: the PHRA 1993 directs the Central Government to constitute the National Human Rights Commission, and the same Act identifies the National Commission for Women, the National Commission for Minorities and the child-rights commission as bodies set up under separate Acts.

### Why status matters

Constitutional status gives entrenchment. Members of a Public Service Commission get fixed terms, conditions that cannot be varied to their disadvantage, expenses charged on the Consolidated Fund, and removal only by the President after a Supreme Court inquiry for misbehaviour. The SC and ST commissions have civil-court powers written into the Constitution and must be consulted by the Union and every State on major policy matters. A statutory body's composition, powers and tenure can be changed by ordinary amendment: the PHRA's own history shows changes in 2006 and 2019 to membership, chair eligibility and the list of ex-officio members.

### Status can change

Bodies may move between categories. The PHRA still describes the National Commission for Backward Classes as a body set up under the NCBC Act, 1993, reflecting its statutory origin; the 102nd Amendment of 2018 inserted Article 338B, which now provides for the Commission in the Constitution itself, and the same amendment removed the older reference to backward classes from Article 338. Examiners exploit such transitions, so verify the current source rather than relying on older lists. A good comparison table covers source of authority, appointment, tenure, removal, powers, reporting to the President or Parliament and how the body can be altered. Remember that constitutional status does not automatically mean binding recommendations: PSC advice can be departed from, provided reasons for non-acceptance are laid before the legislature.
