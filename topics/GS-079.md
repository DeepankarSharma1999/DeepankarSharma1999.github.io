## GS-079: Nagara, Dravida and Vesara temple styles

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS1 · Culture · Indian art, literature and architecture

*Goal:* Explain Nagara, Dravida and Vesara temple styles and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Shared plan and the Nagara order

Early shrines were classified as sandhara (with a circumambulatory path), nirandhara (without one) and sarvatobhadra (open on all sides). Gupta-period shrines at Udaigiri near Vidisha and at Sanchi were flat-roofed porch-and-cell structures. By Deogarh the curving latina tower had arrived, with Ganga and Yamuna flanking the doorway and Vishnu reliefs as Sheshashayana, Nara-Narayana and Gajendramoksha. Later Nagara temples clustered many towers around the tallest one above the sanctum, as at Khajuraho, crowned by amalaka and kalasha. Odisha forms a sub-style whose deul stays almost vertical before curving sharply near the top; Konark's Sun temple (c. 1240) is designed as a giant chariot with twelve pairs of wheels.

### The Dravida order

Southern temples sit within compound walls with a gopuram at the entrance and often a temple tank inside. Fierce dvarapalas, rather than river goddesses, guard the sanctum. As temple towns grew, new and taller enclosures were added, so at Srirangam the outermost of seven concentric walls is the newest and the central sanctum the oldest; the main shrine can therefore have one of the smallest towers. Pallava activity under Narasimhavarman I (Mamalla) created Mahabalipuram, and the Shore temple, probably of Rajasimha's reign, unusually houses three shrines. The Chola temple at Thanjavur has a vimana about 70 metres high and, for the first time, two large gopurams planned together with the temple.

### Deccan experiments and Vesara

Karnataka shows deliberate mixing. Under the early western Chalukyas, Aihole, Badami and Pattadakal combine forms: Pattadakal's Virupaksha temple, commissioned by Loka Mahadevi in the reign of Vikramaditya II, is the most elaborate, while Papanatha is an early Dravida example; Aihole's Durga temple keeps an apsidal plan recalling chaitya halls but adds a Nagara-like shikhara, and Lad Khan imitates wooden hill roofs in stone. The Rashtrakutas carved the wholly Dravida Kailashnath temple at Ellora from living rock. Hoysala temples of the twelfth century, such as Halebid's Hoysaleshvara (1150), developed star-shaped plans and dense carving. NCERT notes that some ancient texts call such hybrids vesara, while some scholars prefer to class these buildings as either nagara or dravida.

### Worked example

Q: Distinguish Nagara and Dravida temple architecture with examples, and explain why Vesara is debated. Frame: tabulate tower (curving shikhara vs stepped vimana), crown (amalaka-kalasha vs stupika), enclosure (platform vs walls with gopurams), door guardians (Ganga-Yamuna vs dvarapalas); cite Deogarh/Khajuraho/Konark vs Mahabalipuram/Thanjavur/Srirangam; then show Chalukya and Hoysala hybrids and note that Vesara is a classification for mixed forms.

### Recall

- - **q:** What is a panchayatana temple?
  - **a:** A main shrine on a plinth with four subsidiary shrines at the corners — five in all, as at Deogarh.
- - **q:** What are the deul and jagamohana in Odisha temples?
  - **a:** The deul is the tower over the sanctum; the jagamohana is the mandapa in front of it.
- - **q:** Which Hoysala feature makes their temples instantly recognisable?
  - **a:** The star-shaped (stellate) ground plan with profuse carving in soft stone.

### Common trap

Using 'shikhara' for the whole southern tower. In the Dravida vocabulary the tower is the vimana and 'shikhara' names only the small crowning dome, equivalent to the north's amalaka and kalasha.
