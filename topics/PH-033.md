## PH-033: Four-momentum vector

Physics Paper I · Physics · Special relativity

### Bridge: Events, coordinates and why a 'vector' helps

An **event** is something that happens at one place and one time: a flash, a collision, a decay. In frame S it has four coordinates (ct, x, y, z). We write time as **ct** (time multiplied by the speed of light) so that all four components have the dimension of length.

Think of ordinary rotation. A point (x, y) on a page has different coordinates if you tilt the axes, but the distance √(x² + y²) does not change. Distances are *invariants* of rotation, and quantities like velocity or force are *vectors* because their components change together, in a fixed way. Relativity has the same structure: the Lorentz transformation mixes ct and x (PH-028 module 4) the way a rotation mixes x and y, and a quantity that is unchanged by it (the *interval*, module 2) is an **invariant**.

A **four-vector** is any set of four numbers (A⁰, A¹, A², A³) that transform between S and S′ exactly as (ct, x, y, z) do. The point of the idea: once momentum and energy are written as one four-vector, a single equation holds in every frame, and one invariant replaces long algebra.

Index notation is kept minimal: the time component is called the 0 component (for example A⁰), and the superscripts 1, 2, 3 are x, y, z, not powers.

### Bridge: The dot product with a minus sign

For two four-vectors A = (A⁰, a) and B = (B⁰, b), with a and b the ordinary three-component parts, define the **dot product**

$$ A·B = A⁰B⁰ − a·b $$

(the signature (+, −, −, −)). The norm squared is A·A = (A⁰)² − |a|². It can be positive, zero or negative. For the position four-vector it is c²t² − x² − y² − z², the interval of PH-028 module 8, which this lesson takes as positive for timelike separations (Mathur's convention; Upadhyaya writes the opposite sign and uses an imaginary fourth component ict, see module 2).

Why a minus sign? A boost mixes ct and x, and the combination that survives is the difference of squares, not the sum (a sum of squares is the invariant of an ordinary rotation). Example: A = (5, 3, 0, 0) gives A·A = 25 − 9 = 16; B = (2, 1, 0, 0) gives A·B = 10 − 3 = 7.

### Bridge: Units: MeV, MeV/c and the habit of keeping c

Particle problems use the electron-volt: 1 MeV = 10⁶ eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹³ J. Instead of converting to kg and m/s, give every quantity an energy unit: energy E in MeV, momentum as **pc** in MeV, and mass as **mc²** in MeV (the rest energy). The relation E² = (pc)² + (mc²)² then involves only MeV, and the velocity is β = pc/E. Typical rest energies: electron 0.511 MeV, proton 938.27 MeV, charged pion 139.57 MeV, muon 105.66 MeV (standard values, verify).

This lesson keeps c written out so that the dimensions stay visible. When you shorten with c = 1, say so in the answer, and restore c before the final numerical answer: a final speed must come out as a fraction of c, or in m/s.

### 1. Orientation: what the four-vector adds to PH-028 to PH-032, and the conventions

*Objective:* Say what a four-vector is, what the invariant of four-momentum is, and which results from Day 13 become one-line consequences.

## The idea in one paragraph

Position in spacetime is the four-vector (ct, x, y, z). Dividing its change by the proper time of a moving particle gives the **four-velocity**; multiplying that by the rest mass m₀ gives the **four-momentum**

$$ P = (E/c, p_x, p_y, p_z) $$

Its components change between frames exactly as (ct, x, y, z) do, and its invariant length squared is the single number E²/c² − p² = m₀²c². Everything about energy and momentum in relativity is then geometry.

### 2. Foundation: the interval and the Lorentz boost as a rotation (derivation 1)

*Objective:* Write the boost as a 2 × 2 matrix with cosh and sinh, show that it keeps c²t² − x² unchanged, and use rapidity to add velocities.

## Recap, linked

PH-028 module 8 proves that Δs² = c²Δt² − Δx² − Δy² − Δz² is unchanged by the Lorentz transformation and classifies timelike, lightlike and spacelike intervals. Mathur §3.7.4 (PDF pp. 144 to 146) does the same. The Lorentz transformation of PH-028 module 4, for S′ moving at +v along +x, in terms of (ct, x):

$$ ct′ = γ(ct − βx),   x′ = γ(x − β ct),   y′ = y,   z′ = z $$

## Matrix form (new)

In matrix form, with the column (ct, x):

$$ ( ct′ ; x′ ) = [[ γ, −γβ ], [ −γβ, γ ]] ( ct ; x ) $$

The determinant is γ² − γ²β² = γ²(1 − β²) = 1, so the map preserves areas in the (ct, x) plane, as a rotation does.

## Invariance of the interval (linked)

PH-028 module 8 proves c²t′² − x′² = c²t² − x² by two lines of algebra (the cross terms ∓2βctx cancel and γ²(1 − β²) = 1); it is not repeated here. The new material is the matrix and rapidity form below.

## Rapidity: the rotation parameter

Because γ² − (γβ)² = 1, there is a number φ with γ = cosh φ and γβ = sinh φ, and then β = tanh φ. The boost reads

$$ ct′ = ct cosh φ − x sinh φ,   x′ = −ct sinh φ + x cosh φ $$

compare an ordinary rotation by angle θ, x′ = x cos θ + y sin θ, y′ = −x sin θ + y cos θ. The structure is the same, with cosh and sinh in place of cos and sin and a minus sign in the invariant. For β = 0.6: γ = 1.25, γβ = 0.75, and φ = artanh 0.6 = ½ ln(1.6/0.4) = ln 2 = 0.693. Check: cosh(ln 2) = (2 + ½)/2 = 1.25 ✓ and sinh(ln 2) = (2 − ½)/2 = 0.75 ✓.

**Rapidities add.** Two successive boosts along x with rapidities φ₁ and φ₂ combine to φ₁ + φ₂ (hyperbolic-angle addition), so the combined speed is β = tanh(φ₁ + φ₂) = (β₁ + β₂)/(1 + β₁β₂): the velocity-addition law of PH-030 module 2. For two boosts of 0.6c: (0.6 + 0.6)/(1 + 0.36) = 0.8824 = tanh(2 ln 2) ✓ (not 1.2).

## Upadhyaya's version

Upadhyaya §6.19 (PDF pp. 363 to 366) takes x₄ = ict, so that s² looks like an ordinary squared length, and writes the boost as a rotation matrix with entries ±iγβ; Theorem 2 shows that the length of any four-vector is invariant. A rotation through the imaginary angle iφ is the hyperbolic rotation above.

## Worked check

Event (ct, x) = (5, 3), β = 0.6: ct′ = 1.25(5 − 1.8) = 4.0 and x′ = 1.25(3 − 3.0) = 0. Interval 25 − 9 = 16 = 4² ✓; ct′ = 4 (same length unit), so the proper time between the two events is 4/c, read on a clock at the S′ origin, which is present at both events.

### 3. Core: proper time and the four-velocity, whose norm is c² (derivation 2)

*Objective:* Define the four-velocity from proper time, show U·U = c² in every frame, and explain why this is the root of E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴.

## Proper time as the invariant parameter

A particle moves along a **worldline**. In S, in a short time dt, it moves a distance dx = u dt with u its speed. The interval along its path is ds² = c²dt² − u²dt² = c²dt²(1 − u²/c²) = c²dt²/γ². Define the **proper time** dτ = ds/c = dt/γ, the time on a clock carried by the particle (PH-029 module 3). Since dτ comes from an invariant interval, it has the same value in every frame, which makes it the right thing to divide by.

## Four-velocity (derivation)

Take the position four-vector (ct, x, y, z) and differentiate each component with respect to **τ**, not t:

1. U⁰ = d(ct)/dτ = c dt/dτ = γc.
2. U¹ = dx/dτ = (dt/dτ)(dx/dt) = γ u_x, and similarly U² = γu_y, U³ = γu_z.

$$ U = γ(c, u) $$

Because dτ is invariant and (ct, x) is a four-vector, U is a four-vector: it transforms between frames exactly as (ct, x, y, z).

## The norm (derivation)

$$ U·U = γ²c² − γ²u² = γ²c²(1 − u²/c²) = c² $$

using γ²(1 − u²/c²) = 1. **The norm of the four-velocity is c² for every particle in every frame**: it is a constant of nature, not a property of the motion. Check at u = 0.6c: U = (1.25c, 0.75c), U·U = (1.5625 − 0.5625)c² = c² ✓; at u = 0.8c: U = (5/3, 4/3)c, (25 − 16)/9 = 1 ✓.

## Meaning

U has the fixed length c: at rest all of it is in the time part, U = (c, 0, 0, 0); for u ≪ c, U ≈ (c, u). A photon has dτ = 0, so U does not exist for it (module 4 uses P instead).

## Boost of the four-velocity

Using the matrix of module 2 on (U⁰, U¹) = (γ_u c, γ_u u): U′¹ = γ(γ_u u − βγ_u c) = γγ_u(u − v). Since U′¹ = γ_{u′}u′ and U′⁰ = γγ_u c(1 − uv/c²) = γ_{u′}c, dividing the two gives u′ = (u − v)/(1 − uv/c²): the velocity transformation of PH-030 module 2, obtained in two lines.

## Why this matters

The ordinary velocity dx/dt is not a good vector (dx and dt both change under a boost), while dx/dτ is. Replacing u by U in p = mu gives P = m₀U (module 4).

### 4. Core: the four-momentum P = (E/c, p) and its invariant norm (derivation 3)

*Objective:* Define P = m₀U, read off E and p, and obtain E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴, v = pc²/E and dE/dp = v from its norm.

## Definition

Multiply the four-velocity (module 3) by the invariant rest mass m₀:

$$ P = m₀ U = (γ m₀ c, γ m₀ u_x, γ m₀ u_y, γ m₀ u_z) $$

The space part γm₀u is the relativistic momentum p and γm₀c² is the total energy E, both derived in PH-032 modules 2 and 3. So

$$ P = (E/c, p_x, p_y, p_z) $$

Since m₀ is invariant and U is a four-vector, **P is a four-vector**: energy is the time part and momentum the space part of one object. Upadhyaya §6.17 reaches the same statement from p = m₀ dx/dτ and E/c² = m₀ dt/dτ (PDF p. 356 to 357).

## The norm (derivation)

Compute P·P two ways. First, P·P = m₀²U·U = m₀²c² (module 3). Second, from components, P·P = E²/c² − p². Equate:

$$ E²/c² − p² = m₀²c²  ⇒  E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴ $$

This is PH-032 module 4's identity, now seen as the length of a four-vector. Because P·P is a norm, E² − p²c² has the same value in every frame (Mathur §3.12, PDF pp. 169 and 170, Upadhyaya §6.16, PDF p. 356). Dimension: E²/c² and p² are both (kg m s⁻¹)² ✓.

## Three immediate consequences

1. **Velocity.** The ratio of space part to time part of U is u/c, so the same holds for P: p/(E/c) = u/c, giving u = pc²/E ✓ (agrees with PH-032 module 4).
2. **dE/dp = u** (the second part of the 2020 verified question). Differentiate E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴ with m₀ fixed: 2E dE = 2pc² dp, so dE/dp = pc²/E = u ✓.
3. **Massless particles.** If m₀ = 0, P·P = 0: P is a null vector, E = pc, and u = pc²/E = c. A photon has P = (E/c)(1, n̂), with n̂ its direction. Its norm is zero in every frame, so no frame can bring a photon to rest.

## Numbers

A proton (m₀c² = 938.27 MeV, standard) with pc = 1000 MeV: E = √(1000² + 938.27²) = 1371.3 MeV, T = E − m₀c² = 433.0 MeV, β = pc/E = 0.7293.

## Invariant against conserved

Rest mass m₀ is invariant but not conserved (coalescence changes it); total E and p are conserved but not invariant; E² − p²c² of the total is both (master notes table). Confusing the two ideas is the commonest conceptual slip here.

### 5. Core: the Lorentz transformation of energy and momentum, derived and checked

*Objective:* Obtain E′ and p′ₓ from the boost matrix, prove E′² − p′²c² = E² − p²c², and verify with numbers.

## Derivation

Apply the boost of module 2 to (P⁰, P¹) = (E/c, pₓ) in place of (ct, x). Frames as in module 1 (S′ moves at +v along +x):

1. E′/c = γ(E/c − β pₓ), so multiplying by c: **E′ = γ(E − v pₓ)**.
2. p′ₓ = γ(pₓ − β E/c) = **γ(pₓ − vE/c²)**.
3. p′_y = p_y and p′_z = z-component unchanged: **p′_y = p_y, p′_z = p_z**.

These are Upadhyaya §6.17 eq. (5) (PDF p. 357) and Mathur §3.10 (PDF pp. 168 to 169); both argue that p and E/c² change like x and t because m₀ and dτ are invariant. The inverse transformation (S′ to S, v → −v):

$$ E = γ(E′ + v p′ₓ),   pₓ = γ(p′ₓ + vE′/c²) $$

**Proof that the norm is preserved.** Write pₓ = p. Then

E′² − p′²c² = γ²[(E − vp)² − (pc − βE)²]
 = γ²[E² − 2vEp + v²p² − p²c² + 2βEpc − β²E²].

With 2vEp = 2βcEp, the cross terms −2vEp and +2βEpc cancel. Collect:

 = γ²[E²(1 − β²) − p²c²(1 − v²/c²)] = E² − p²c² ✓ (the p_y and p_z terms are unchanged).

## Numerical verification

Particle in S: E = 5.0 GeV, pₓc = 4.0 GeV (so m₀c² = √(25 − 16) = 3.0 GeV). Frame S′ moves at β = 0.6 along +x: γ = 1.25.

- E′ = 1.25(5.0 − 0.6 × 4.0) = 1.25 × 2.6 = **3.25 GeV**.
- p′ₓc = 1.25(4.0 − 0.6 × 5.0) = 1.25 × 1.0 = **1.25 GeV**.
- Norm: 3.25² − 1.25² = 10.5625 − 1.5625 = 9.00 = 3.0² ✓.
- Inverse: E = 1.25(3.25 + 0.6 × 1.25) = 5.00 ✓ and pₓc = 1.25(1.25 + 0.6 × 3.25) = 4.00 ✓.

**The rest frame.** Choose v = pc²/E = 0.8c (the particle's own velocity), γ = 5/3: E′ = (5/3)(5 − 0.8 × 4) = 3.00 GeV = m₀c² and p′c = (5/3)(4 − 0.8 × 5) = 0 ✓. So in the particle's own frame P = (m₀c, 0, 0, 0), and E = γm₀c² is just this rest energy seen from a moving frame, the reason the rest energy is the smallest energy a particle can have in any frame.

## Interpretation

A particle at rest in S′ has p′ₓ = 0 and E′ = m₀c²; from S it has E = γm₀c² and pₓ = γm₀v (the inverse equations), PH-032's results without a collision argument.

### 6. Core: conservation of four-momentum, invariant mass and two-body decay

*Objective:* Use the total four-momentum, its invariant mass and the squaring trick to solve decay and invariant-mass problems in one line.

## Conservation as one equation

In any isolated process, **ΣP_in = ΣP_out**: four component equations (energy and three momentum components) in a single statement. Because the transformation of module 5 is linear, if the four components balance in S they balance in every frame. In fact momentum balance alone in all frames forces energy balance: for a change ΔP, Δp′ₓ = γ(Δpₓ − vΔE/c²) = 0 for all v together with Δpₓ = 0 gives ΔE = 0. This is the argument of Mathur §3.11 (PDF p. 169) in four-vector form.

## Invariant mass

For particles i with four-momenta Pᵢ, the total is P = ΣPᵢ and its norm defines the **invariant mass** M of the system:

$$ M²c⁴ = (ΣEᵢ)² − (Σpᵢ)²c² $$

It has the same value in every frame, and in the frame where Σp = 0 (the centre-of-momentum frame) it is Mc² = ΣE*ᵢ ≥ Σmᵢc², since each E*ᵢ ≥ mᵢc². So **M is not the sum of the masses**: equality holds only when the particles are all at rest relative to each other. Two photons of 1 GeV each, head-on, have M = 2 GeV/c², though each has zero mass.

## Two photons: M from the opening angle

For two photons, P₁·P₂ = (E₁E₂/c²)(1 − cos θ) with θ the angle between them, and M²c² = (P₁ + P₂)² = 2P₁·P₂, so

$$ M²c⁴ = 2 E₁E₂ (1 − cos θ) $$

Example: a π⁰ (Mc² = 134.98 MeV, standard) decays to photons of 80 and 100 MeV: cos θ = 1 − 134.98²/(2 × 80 × 100) = −0.1387, θ = 97.97°. For equal energies E each, sin(θ/2) = Mc²/(2E), 84.9° at E = 100 MeV; this is the smallest opening angle possible for a π⁰ of total energy 2E.

## Two-body decay in the parent's rest frame (derivation)

Parent M at rest: P = (Mc, 0). Decay products 1 and 2: P = P₁ + P₂.

1. Isolate the particle you do *not* want: P₂ = P − P₁.
2. Square: P₂·P₂ = P·P − 2P·P₁ + P₁·P₁, so m₂²c² = M²c² − 2P·P₁ + m₁²c².
3. P·P₁ = (Mc)(E₁/c) − 0 = ME₁.
4. Solve:

$$ E₁ = (M² + m₁² − m₂²)c²/(2M),   E₂ = (M² + m₂² − m₁²)c²/(2M) $$

This is PH-032 module 6's result, reached without eliminating the momenta; p²c² = E₁² − m₁²c⁴ follows.

**Numbers (π⁺ → μ⁺ + ν, standard masses, verify):** Mc² = 139.57, mc² = 105.66 MeV, m₂ = 0: E_μ = (139.57² + 105.66²)/(2 × 139.57) = 109.78 MeV, T_μ = 4.12 MeV, E_ν = pc = 29.79 MeV, β_μ = pc/E_μ = 0.2714 (the 2026 Q3(b) task; PH-032 module 6 has the same). **Limits:** m₁ = m₂ = 0 gives Mc²/2 each (π⁰ → γγ: 67.49 MeV); M < m₁ + m₂ gives no decay.

**Trap: adding invariant masses.** The decay pion's mass M is not the sum of the product masses, and the invariant mass of the two photons above is not zero. Always form M from the *total* energy and *total* momentum.

### 7. Core: threshold energy for particle production by the invariant

*Objective:* Derive the threshold condition M = Σm_final for a fixed target and apply it to pion production and pair production, with numbers.

## Why the invariant

To create new particles, the total energy in the centre-of-momentum frame must be at least the rest energy of the products. That quantity is the invariant mass M of the incoming pair. It can be computed in the lab frame, where the target is at rest, and the threshold condition M = Σm_f (all products at rest in the centre-of-momentum frame, therefore moving together in the lab) is a single equation.

## Derivation (fixed target)

Beam particle: rest mass m₁, energy E₁ and momentum p₁ (lab). Target: rest mass m₂ at rest. Total energy E₁ + m₂c², total momentum p₁.

1. M²c⁴ = (E₁ + m₂c²)² − p₁²c².
2. Expand and use p₁²c² = E₁² − m₁²c⁴:

$$ M²c⁴ = m₁²c⁴ + m₂²c⁴ + 2E₁m₂c² $$

3. Threshold: M = Σm_f (the final particles have all the energy of motion removed in the centre-of-momentum frame):

$$ E₁,th = [(Σm_f)² − m₁² − m₂²]c²/(2m₂) $$

*Dimension:* mass² × c²/mass = energy ✓. *Check:* if Σm_f = m₁ + m₂, E₁ = m₁c² (the beam at rest: zero kinetic energy, no reaction needed) ✓.

## Examples (standard masses, verify: mₚ = 938.27, m_π⁰ = 134.98, mₑ = 0.511 MeV/c²)

| Reaction | Threshold | Result |
|---|---|---|
| p + p → p + p + π⁰ | T = [(2mₚ + m_π)² − 4mₚ²]c²/(2mₚ) = 2m_π c²(1 + m_π/(4mₚ)) | **T = 279.7 MeV** (E = 1217.9 MeV) |
| γ + p → p + π⁰ | E_γ = m_π c²(1 + m_π/(2mₚ)) | **144.7 MeV** |

The photon row has m₁ = 0, so the general formula reduces to [(Σm_f)² − m₂²]c²/(2m₂).

**Recap, not re-derived (PH-032 module 8):** p p → p p p p̄ has E = 7mₚc², T = 5.63 GeV; pair production on a nucleus M has E_γ = 2mₑc²(1 + mₑ/M), 1.0226 MeV for a proton and 1.0220 MeV for a 12 u nucleus. For pair production on a nucleus, Σm_f = M + 2mₑ and m₂ = M give [(M + 2mₑ)² − M²]/(2M) = 2mₑ(1 + mₑ/M) ✓ (the same invariant step PH-032 module 8 used in c = 1 units; here it is justified as the norm of the total four-momentum).

## Why not just 'kinetic energy = rest energy created'?

For p p → p p π⁰ this guess gives T = 135 MeV, but 279.7 MeV is needed (ratio 2.07), because the products also carry the beam's momentum; only the energy left in the centre-of-momentum frame can create mass.

### 8. Core: Compton scattering by four-momentum, and the photon under a boost

*Objective:* Derive the Compton formulas by squaring a four-vector, find the wavelength shift, and recover Doppler shift and aberration from the photon's four-momentum.

## Compton scattering (derivation, the 2026 Q3(a) task)

A photon of energy E hits a free electron (rest energy mc²) at rest and scatters through θ; the photon leaves with E′. Photon four-momenta: P_γ = (E/c)(1, n̂), P′_γ = (E′/c)(1, n̂′) with n̂·n̂′ = cos θ. Electron: P_e = (mc, 0), P′_e after. All momenta in a plane; signature (+, −, −, −).

1. Conservation: P_e + P_γ = P′_e + P′_γ.
2. Isolate the unobserved electron: P′_e = P_e + P_γ − P′_γ.
3. Square both sides, using P′_e·P′_e = m²c², P_e·P_e = m²c² and P_γ·P_γ = P′_γ·P′_γ = 0:

m²c² = m²c² + 2P_e·P_γ − 2P_e·P′_γ − 2P_γ·P′_γ.

4. The dot products: P_e·P_γ = (mc)(E/c) = mE; P_e·P′_γ = mE′; P_γ·P′_γ = (EE′/c²)(1 − cos θ).
5. Substitute: 0 = 2m(E − E′) − 2(EE′/c²)(1 − cos θ), so

$$ E − E′ = (EE′/mc²)(1 − cos θ) $$

6. Divide by EE′: 1/E′ − 1/E = (1 − cos θ)/(mc²), hence

$$ E′ = E/[1 + (E/mc²)(1 − cos θ)],   T = E − E′ = (E²/mc²)(1 − cos θ)/[1 + (E/mc²)(1 − cos θ)] $$

These are the two results of the verified question, found without ever writing the electron's momentum, which is the advantage of the squaring trick (PH-032 module 8 derives them by components). With E = hc/λ, step 6 reads **λ′ − λ = (h/mc)(1 − cos θ)**, the Compton shift, with h/(mc) = 2.426 pm for the electron.

**Numbers (original):** λ = 70.9 pm, E = hc/λ = 17.487 keV, θ = 90°: λ′ = 70.9 + 2.426 = 73.33 pm; E′ = 17.487/(1 + 17.487/510.999) = 16.909 keV; T = 0.579 keV; the electron leaves at angle φ with tan φ = E′ sin θ/(E − E′ cos θ) = E′/E = 0.967 (φ = 44.0°, on the other side of the axis). **Limits:** θ = 0 gives E′ = E and T = 0; E ≪ mc² gives E′ ≈ E: the fractional shift Δλ/λ = (E/mc²)(1 − cos θ) tends to zero (Thomson limit), although the absolute shift (h/mc)(1 − cos θ) is the same at every energy.

## The photon under a boost (link to PH-030 and PH-031)

A photon in S: P = (E/c)(1, cos θ, sin θ, 0), θ measured from +x. Boost to S′ (module 5):

- E′ = γ(E − v pₓ) = γE(1 − β cos θ), and with E = hν: **ν′ = γν(1 − β cos θ)**, the relativistic Doppler shift of PH-031 module 5.
- p′ₓ c = γ(E cos θ − βE) and p′_y c = E sin θ. Dividing by E′:

$$ cos θ′ = (cos θ − β)/(1 − β cos θ),   sin θ′ = sin θ/[γ(1 − β cos θ)] $$

which is the aberration law of PH-030 module 6. Check at β = 0.6, θ = 60°: E′/E = 1.25(1 − 0.3) = 0.875; cos θ′ = (0.5 − 0.6)/(1 − 0.3) = −0.143, θ′ = 98.2°. Head-on (θ = 180°): E′/E = γ(1 + β) = 2.0, a blueshift. Both PH-030 and PH-031 results are therefore the two halves of one four-vector transformation.

### 9. Master: the four-force, convention clashes and the trap list

*Objective:* Name the four-force, know where textbooks differ in sign convention, and avoid the standard mistakes.

## The four-force (beyond the syllabus minimum; standard, verify)

Differentiating P with respect to proper time gives the **four-force** F = dP/dτ. With ordinary force f = dp/dt and speed u, standard steps give

$$ F = γ ( f·u/c , f ) $$

since d(E/c)/dτ = γ(1/c)dE/dt = γ f·u/c (work-energy: dE/dt = f·u) and dp/dτ = γ f. For constant rest mass, P·P is constant, so differentiating gives P·F = 0: check by components, γ(E/c)(f·u)/c − γ f·p = γ(f·u)E/c² − γ f·(Eu/c²) = 0 ✓ (using p = Eu/c²). Upadhyaya names velocity, momentum and acceleration four-vectors without developing them (PDF p. 365); the verified questions do not need it. Covariance of physics equations is to come on Day 15 (PH-034, not yet written).

## Trap list

1. **Adding invariant masses.** M of a system is not Σmᵢ; form it from the total E and total p (module 6).
2. **Lab energies in the invariant, with the wrong frame.** M²c⁴ = (ΣE)² − (Σp)²c² needs E and p in the *same* frame; do not take the beam energy from the lab and the target energy from the beam frame.
3. **Sign errors:** the minus sign in the dot product, p′ₓ = γ(pₓ − vE/c²) (not +), and v → −v in the inverse.
4. **Boosting rest energy.** E = m₀c² only in the rest frame.
5. **Photon angle frames:** the Doppler formula ν′ = γν(1 − β cos θ) uses θ in S; the aberration formula gives θ′ in S′ (PH-031 module 5 flags the same mix-up).
6. **Threshold by kinetic energy alone** (module 7): momentum conservation makes the threshold higher than the rest energy created.
7. **Invariant against conserved** (module 4): P·P of a single free particle is invariant and constant, but m₀ changes in coalescence.

### Notes: Definitions and conventions

- Event: a point in spacetime with coordinates (ct, x, y, z) in a frame.
- Four-vector: four components that transform between frames exactly as (ct, x, y, z).
- Dot product (signature +, −, −, −): A·B = A⁰B⁰ − a·b; norm A·A = (A⁰)² − |a|².
- Interval: s² = c²t² − x² − y² − z² (Mathur); Upadhyaya writes the negative, with x₄ = ict.
- Proper time: dτ = dt/γ, the time on the particle's own clock, an invariant.
- Invariant mass of a system: M²c⁴ = (ΣE)² − (Σp)²c², the same in every frame.

### Notes: Core equations

- Boost: ct′ = γ(ct − βx), x′ = γ(x − βct); γ = cosh φ, γβ = sinh φ, β = tanh φ.
- Four-velocity: U = γ(c, u); U·U = c².
- Four-momentum: P = m₀U = (E/c, p); P·P = E²/c² − p² = m₀²c².
- E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴; u = pc²/E; dE/dp = u; photon: E = pc.
- E′ = γ(E − v pₓ); p′ₓ = γ(pₓ − vE/c²); p′_y = p_y; p′_z = p_z (Upadhyaya eq. 5; Mathur §3.10).
- Two-body decay at rest: E₁ = (M² + m₁² − m₂²)c²/(2M).
- Threshold, fixed target: E₁ = [(Σm_f)² − m₁² − m₂²]c²/(2m₂).
- Compton: 1/E′ − 1/E = (1 − cos θ)/(mc²); λ′ − λ = (h/mc)(1 − cos θ).

### Notes: Derivation skeleton: Compton by four-momentum

- Assumptions: free electron at rest, plane scattering, signature (+, −, −, −).
- P_e + P_γ = P′_e + P′_γ; isolate P′_e = P_e + P_γ − P′_γ; square.
- Use P_e² = P′_e² = m²c² and P_γ² = P′_γ² = 0 to leave 0 = P_e·P_γ − P_e·P′_γ − P_γ·P′_γ.
- P_e·P_γ = mE, P_e·P′_γ = mE′, P_γ·P′_γ = (EE′/c²)(1 − cos θ).
- So m(E − E′) = (EE′/c²)(1 − cos θ), i.e. 1/E′ − 1/E = (1 − cos θ)/(mc²).
- Checks: θ = 0 gives E′ = E; Δλ = (h/mc)(1 − cos θ) = 2.426 pm at 90°.

### Notes: Invariant against conserved

### Notes: Confused concepts

- Invariant (frame-independent value) is not the same as conserved (unchanged by a process).
- Rest mass m₀ is invariant; relativistic mass γm₀ and the energy are frame-dependent.
- The invariant mass of a system is not the sum of the masses of its parts.
- p′_y = p_y for a boost along x, but the velocity component u_y does change.

### Notes: Exceptions and limits

- Photons: P·P = 0 and no four-velocity (no proper time).
- β → 0, to first order in β: E′ ≈ E − v pₓ and p′ₓ ≈ pₓ − mv (the Galilean momentum rule).
- Decay is impossible if M < Σm_f; threshold formulas assume the target at rest.
- The four-force F = γ(f·u/c, f) is standard, beyond the syllabus minimum; verify.

### Core concepts

- Energy and momentum are the time and space parts of one four-vector, P = (E/c, p).
- The norm P·P = m₀²c² is the same in every frame and gives E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴.
- Conservation of the total four-momentum plus the invariant-mass trick solves decays, thresholds and Compton in a few lines.
