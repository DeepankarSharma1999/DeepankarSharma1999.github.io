## GS-252: PDS and food security architecture

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Agriculture and food

*Goal:* Explain PDS and food security architecture and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### From production surplus to public stocks

During the Green Revolution, rice and wheat output rose and a good share of it was sold in the market, forming the marketed surplus. That surplus lowered relative food prices — helping low-income households who spend much of their income on food — and allowed the state to procure grain and hold stocks for lean years. This is the supply foundation of food security: without procurement there is nothing to distribute, and without production growth there is nothing to procure.

### NFSA 2013: food as a legal right

The National Food Security Act, 2013 shifted food policy from welfare to a legal entitlement. It reaches up to three-quarters of rural and half of urban residents, about 81.35 crore people on Census 2011 figures. Each member of a priority household may draw 5 kg of grain a month, while an Antyodaya Anna Yojana family, the poorest tier, gets 35 kg per household. Schedule I first capped prices at ₹3, ₹2 and ₹1 per kg for rice, wheat and coarse grains. The Act goes beyond rations: free meals via anganwadis and schools, maternity benefit of at least ₹6,000, a food security allowance if grain is not supplied, the eldest adult woman as ration-card head, and grievance officers plus State Food Commissions. The Centre must procure for the central pool and allocate grain to States.

### Delivery: TPDS, PMGKAY and ONORC

Targeting began in 1997, when TPDS replaced the universal PDS with BPL and APL categories; AAY followed in 2000 and now reaches about 2.5 crore households (Economic Survey 2025-26). PMGKAY, born as COVID-19 relief, now gives NFSA grain free at Central cost and was extended for five years from 1 January 2024. One Nation One Ration Card lets migrants collect rations at any fair price shop after Aadhaar authentication; it runs in all 36 States/UTs. About 99.6 per cent of 5.43 lakh shops use ePoS, and cross-checks with tax and other databases led States to drop over 2.12 crore ineligible beneficiaries. Remember that the Economic Survey 2024-25 defines food security by availability, access, utilisation and stability, so full stocks alone are not enough; NCERT adds that IT tools can help flag food-insecure areas before an emergency.
