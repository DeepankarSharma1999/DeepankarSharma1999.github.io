## GS-228: Unemployment types and measurement

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Basic macro and monetary

*Goal:* Explain Unemployment types and measurement and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Who is a worker

NCERT links work to national product: activities that contribute to GNP are economic activities, and everyone engaged in them is a worker, whatever their status. This includes the self-employed and helpers in family enterprises, and people temporarily away because of illness, weather or festivals. It excludes unpaid household chores, which is why many women doing cooking, fuel collection and unpaid farm help are not counted — a classification economists dispute. The worker-population ratio, workers divided by population times 100, shows how much of the population is directly engaged in production; NCERT's table for 2023-24 puts it at 43.7 (45.6 rural, 38.9 urban), higher in rural areas where people join work earlier and stay in education less.

### Types of unemployment in India

The National Statistical Office treats someone as unemployed if, lacking work, they are not working but are seeking it through exchanges, intermediaries or applications, or say they are available. Open unemployment is the visible kind: people queuing for daily work or registering at exchanges. Disguised unemployment is typical of family farms: if a plot needs three people but five or more work on it, the extra hands add little or nothing to output, though they appear employed. Seasonal unemployment arises because farm work is concentrated in certain months, pushing people to migrate to towns in the lean season. NCERT also notes that poverty forces many to accept any job rather than stay openly unemployed.

### How PLFS measures it now

India's main labour data now come from the Periodic Labour Force Survey. The Economic Survey 2025-26 notes that MoSPI launched a monthly PLFS bulletin in April 2025, giving rural and urban indicators in current weekly status, and revamped the quarterly survey. Three ratios matter. The labour force participation rate is the share of the population working or seeking or available for work. The unemployment rate is the unemployed share of that labour force. Current weekly status classifies a person by activity over the seven days before the survey, and the Survey reports that the unemployment rate on this basis declined while participation stabilised in April–September 2025. The Survey reports 56.2 crore employed persons aged 15+ in July–September 2025.

### Worked example

Population aged 15+ = 1,000; working = 530; not working but seeking/available = 20. Labour force = 550. LFPR = 550 ÷ 1,000 = 55%; worker-population ratio = 53%; unemployment rate = 20 ÷ 550 ≈ 3.6% (not 20 ÷ 1,000 = 2%).

### Recall

- - **q:** What is the denominator of the unemployment rate?
  - **a:** The labour force (employed plus unemployed).
- - **q:** What reference period does current weekly status use?
  - **a:** The seven days preceding the survey date.
- - **q:** Give NCERT's illustration of disguised unemployment.
  - **a:** A four-acre farm that needs only the farmer and two workers but engages five workers plus family members.

### Common trap

Computing the unemployment rate as unemployed ÷ total population: the denominator is the labour force.
