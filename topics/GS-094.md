## GS-094: Cultural institutions and preservation

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS1 · Culture · Indian art, literature and architecture

*Goal:* Explain Cultural institutions and preservation and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Constitution and the AMASR Act

Article 29 lets any section of citizens conserve a distinct language, script or culture; Article 49, a Directive Principle, makes the State responsible for monuments Parliament's law declares nationally important; Article 51A(f) asks citizens to value the composite culture. The AMASR Act of 1958 is that law. It treats a structure, cave, rock sculpture, inscription or monolith as an ancient monument once it is at least a century old and of historical, archaeological or artistic interest. The Centre declares national importance by notification after a two-month window for objections, may acquire or take guardianship of monuments through the Director-General of Archaeology, and must allow public access.

### Buffer zones and the National Monuments Authority

The 2010 amendment wrote protective rings into the Act. Measured from the limit of a protected monument or area, the first 100 metres form the prohibited area, where only archaeological officers may build; the next 200 metres form the regulated area, where construction, reconstruction or repair needs permission. The Centre may notify larger zones depending on how a monument is classified. Owners apply to a competent authority, which consults the National Monuments Authority; the Authority's view on impact is final, and every permission granted or refused must be published online. Heritage bye-laws for each monument are prepared with INTACH or other expert bodies. Mining or building inside a protected area itself needs central permission.

### Institutions and conservation ethics

Intangible heritage needs living institutions. Sangeet Natak Akademi, the national academy for music, dance and drama, runs Jawaharlal Nehru Manipur Dance Academy, Kathak Kendra and Rabindra Rangashala, national projects for Kutiyattam, Chhau and Sattriya, fellowships, awards and a large performing-arts archive; it founded the National School of Drama in 1959. Physical heritage raises the question of where objects belong. Amaravati's stupa, found in 1796, was stripped as sculptures went to Madras, Calcutta and London. H.H. Cole argued that museums should hold plaster casts while originals stay in place. His view failed for Amaravati but was adopted for Sanchi, which survives in situ.
