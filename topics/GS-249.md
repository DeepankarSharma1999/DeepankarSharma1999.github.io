## GS-249: Agricultural productivity and farm inputs

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Agriculture and food

*Goal:* Explain Agricultural productivity and farm inputs and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Land-saving technology and cropping intensity

Total cultivable land in India has declined marginally as a share of reporting area, so expanding the net sown area offers little scope. NCERT therefore highlights two land-saving routes: technologies that raise yield of a crop per hectare, and those that raise output from all crops in a year by using the same plot more intensively. The second route also creates more demand for labour, which suits a land-scarce, labour-abundant country. Cropping intensity is measured as gross cropped area divided by net sown area, times 100. A value of 150 means half the net sown area is cropped twice in the year.

### The HYV package and its preconditions

High-yielding varieties transformed output only when fertiliser, pesticide and reliable water arrived together in the right proportions. Farmers therefore needed both irrigation and money to buy inputs, which is why early adoption concentrated in prosperous, irrigated regions and in wheat. The state reduced the risk of a rich-farmer bias by giving small farmers cheap loans and subsidised fertiliser, and research institutes helped against pest attacks, to which HYV crops were more prone. Over time small-farm output caught up with large farms. The marketed surplus from rice and wheat lowered relative food prices and let the government build stocks.

### Why yields still lag

Indian yields of rice, wheat, cotton and oilseeds remain much lower than those of the USA, Russia and Japan. Causes include heavy dependence on erratic monsoons, low-yielding dryland coarse cereals, pulses and oilseeds, costly modern inputs that push small and marginal farmers into debt, and small, fragmented holdings that are uneconomic to operate. The average holding keeps shrinking under population pressure. Input intensity also has a cost: because HYVs are prone to pests and diseases, pesticide use has climbed steeply since the 1960s. Productivity gains must therefore be paired with care for soil and water.

### Worked example

Applied example: a district has NSA 100,000 ha and GCA 140,000 ha. Cropping intensity = 140,000/100,000 × 100 = 140. Raising it through assured irrigation and short-duration varieties increases output and farm employment without new land — the reason NCERT calls high cropping intensity desirable for India.

### Recall

- - **q:** Formula for cropping intensity?
  - **a:** Gross cropped area ÷ net sown area × 100.
- - **q:** Where did HYV wheat and rice seeds originate?
  - **a:** Wheat from Mexico, rice from the Philippines.
- - **q:** Name two state measures that let small farmers share Green Revolution gains.
  - **a:** Low-interest loans and subsidised fertiliser (plus research support against pests).

### Common trap

Equating productivity with land productivity only: NCERT stresses that labour productivity in Indian farming is also very low because of heavy pressure on land, and rain-fed drylands depress average yields.
