## GS-234: Public debt and debt sustainability

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Growth, public finance and infrastructure

*Goal:* Explain Public debt and debt sustainability and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### From deficit flow to debt stock

A deficit is a flow and debt is a stock. When the government spends more than its revenue and non-debt receipts, the gap, the fiscal deficit, is met by borrowing at home, from the RBI or from abroad. Year after year this borrowing accumulates, and the interest on it becomes part of the next year's spending, which is why NCERT stresses the primary deficit as a cleaner signal of current policy. A revenue deficit means the state is dissaving and borrowing even for consumption. If a large share of the fiscal deficit is revenue deficit, borrowed money is not building assets, and committed items such as interest eventually force cuts in capital or welfare spending.

### Is public debt a burden?

NCERT sets out competing views. One holds that borrowing shifts the cost to future taxpayers and, by absorbing household savings, crowds private borrowers out of financial markets. Ricardian equivalence argues that forward-looking families save more today because they expect higher future taxes, so borrowing and taxing have the same effect. Domestic debt keeps purchasing power within the nation, but debt owed abroad requires real resources to be sent out. Against the pessimistic view, deficits that raise output also raise income and saving, and debt-financed infrastructure leaves future generations better off if its return exceeds the interest rate. Debt growth should therefore be judged against the growth of the whole economy.

### India's current debt position

According to the 2026 FRBM statements, the Centre's debt is 56.1 per cent of GDP on revised 2025-26 figures and is budgeted to ease to 55.6 per cent in 2026-27; excluding NSSF investments in state securities, which are effectively state liabilities, it is about 55.1 per cent at end-2026-27 (BE). Almost all central debt is domestic, with only about 3.3 per cent of liabilities sourced externally and mostly from multilateral lenders. Interest payments are budgeted at ₹14.04 lakh crore in 2026-27. Debt management uses long maturities, switches and buybacks of securities to lower rollover risk. The FRBM debt endeavour of 40 per cent for the Centre remains unmet, and Budget 2026-27 names a declining debt glide path as its fiscal anchor.
