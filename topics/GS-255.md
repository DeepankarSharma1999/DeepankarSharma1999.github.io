## GS-255: Food processing and value chains

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Agriculture and food

*Goal:* Explain Food processing and value chains and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Processing as rural diversification

Rural development in NCERT includes giving people opportunities to diversify into non-farm activities such as food processing. Agriculture is overcrowded, so a growing labour force must find work elsewhere. NCERT separates the non-farm economy into dynamic segments — agro-processing, food processing, leather, tourism — and segments with potential but weak support, such as pottery, crafts and handlooms. NCERT places food processing among the segments with dynamic linkages that permit healthy growth. Jaggery making is shown as an allied activity of farming.

### Size and shape of the sector

India is the largest producer of milk and second in fruits, vegetables and sugar, so processors have a wide raw-material base; NCERT adds that horticulture supplies nearly one-third of farm output value. Processing reduces waste of perishables, lengthens shelf life and pulls farming towards diversification. Official data show its weight. Sector GVA reached ₹1.92 lakh crore in 2022-23, against ₹1.30 lakh crore in 2013-14, about 7.66 per cent of manufacturing GVA (Economic Survey 2023-24). The Economic Survey 2025-26 puts its share of organised manufacturing employment at 12.91 per cent. Agri-food exports, processed goods included, were USD 49.43 billion in FY25, around 11.2 per cent of all exports, and processed items climbed from 14.9 per cent of that basket in FY18 to 20.4 per cent in FY25.

### Schemes and value-chain bottlenecks

PM Kisan SAMPADA Yojana builds modern infrastructure and farm-to-retail supply chains to cut post-harvest losses; 1,185 projects were complete by 30 November 2025. The PLI scheme for food processing (2021) backs globally competitive firms and their overseas branding, with 169 applications approved by December 2025. PMFME (2020) gives credit-linked subsidy, training and seed capital to micro units, including women SHG members. Operation Greens, launched in 2018-19 for tomato, onion and potato, now covers 22 perishables through price stabilisation and value-chain projects. By November 2025 the Agriculture Infrastructure Fund had supported over 25,000 processing units and 2,700 cold storage projects. For allied value chains such as fisheries, the Survey still flags gaps in post-harvest handling, cold chains and logistics, echoing NCERT's call for power, cold storage and market links.

### Worked example

Mains frame — 'Food processing as a rural employment engine': Scale (12.91 per cent of organised manufacturing jobs; processed share of agri-food exports 20.4 per cent in FY25) → raw-material base (milk, fruits, vegetables) → schemes (PMKSY, PLISFPI, PMFME, Operation Greens, AIF) → constraints (cold chain, logistics, post-harvest handling) → policy (small units, FPO and SHG linkages, infrastructure).

### Recall

- - **q:** What share of organised manufacturing employment is in food processing?
  - **a:** 12.91 per cent (Economic Survey 2025-26).
- - **q:** Name three central schemes for food processing.
  - **a:** Any three: PMKSY (Kisan SAMPADA), PLISFPI, PMFME, Operation Greens.
- - **q:** How far has Operation Greens expanded?
  - **a:** From tomato, onion and potato to 22 perishables.
- - **q:** Name two dynamic sub-sectors of the rural non-farm economy.
  - **a:** Any two: agro-processing, food processing, leather, tourism.

### Common trap

Seeing processing purely as an industrial topic: NCERT frames it as a rural-diversification strategy whose bottleneck is infrastructure (power, cold storage, linkages), not just factory investment.
