## GS-072: United Nations formation

Mains GS1 · World History · 18th–20th century

*Goal:* Explain United Nations formation and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### From the League to the Charter

The First World War convinced many that an international organisation could help avoid war, giving rise to the League of Nations; despite early successes it failed to stop the Second World War. Planning for a successor began during that war. The Atlantic Charter of August 1941 was followed by the January 1942 Declaration by United Nations, signed in Washington by 26 Allied states fighting the Axis. The Tehran Conference of December 1943 brought together the US, Britain and the Soviet Union, and at Yalta in February 1945 the Big Three agreed to convene a conference on the new organisation. That two-month conference at San Francisco produced the Charter.

### Structure and mandate at birth

The UN aimed to stop disputes between states from escalating into war, to limit hostilities if war broke out, and — because conflict often grew out of poverty — to promote social and economic development. The General Assembly gives each member one vote. The Security Council's five permanent members were chosen because they were the strongest powers and the victors in 1945; they enjoy permanence and the veto, meaning a single negative vote from any of them blocks a decision. The Secretary-General is the organisation's public head, and specialised bodies such as WHO, UNDP, UNHCR, UNICEF and UNESCO handle social and economic work. NCERT notes the UN's belligerent, wartime-alliance origins.

### Legacy, reform debates and exam angles

Because the UN's design reflected the world of 1945, the end of the Cold War sharpened calls for change. A 1992 General Assembly resolution complained that the Security Council no longer reflected political realities, reflected mainly Western values and interests under the domination of a few powers, and lacked equitable representation. Proposed criteria for new members include economic and military weight, budget contribution, population, democratic record and regional diversity. Many see the veto as conflicting with sovereign equality, yet abolishing it risks great powers abandoning the body. The 2005 summit agreed on a Peacebuilding Commission, a Human Rights Council and winding up the Trusteeship Council. Prelims: founding sequence and dates. Mains: origins explain present representational deficits.

### Worked example

Prelims-style sequencing: arrange (a) Yalta Conference, (b) Atlantic Charter, (c) UN Day, (d) Declaration by United Nations. Correct order: b (Aug 1941) → d (Jan 1942) → a (Feb 1945) → c (24 Oct 1945). Mains frame for 'How did the circumstances of the UN's formation shape its institutions?': League's failure → wartime Allied planning → Charter → P5 drawn from 1945 victors with veto → post-Cold War reform demands.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why does the UN have 51 original members if 50 signed on 26 June 1945?
  - **a:** Poland signed the Charter later, on 15 October 1945.
- - **q:** When did India join the UN?
  - **a:** 30 October 1945.
- - **q:** What is the veto?
  - **a:** A negative vote by any permanent Security Council member, which blocks a decision even if all others support it.

### Common trap

Confusing the date the Charter was signed (26 June 1945) with the date the UN came into existence (24 October 1945, UN Day) — and assuming all 51 founders signed in June, when Poland signed only in October.
