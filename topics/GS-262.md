## GS-262: Productivity and biogeochemical cycles

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain Productivity and biogeochemical cycles and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Productivity and decomposition

Primary productivity is the rate at which producers build organic matter per unit area over time, measured in g m⁻² yr⁻¹ or kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹. Gross primary productivity (GPP) is total photosynthetic output; after plants spend part of it on respiration, the remainder is net primary productivity (NPP), the food available to herbivores and decomposers. Secondary productivity is the rate at which consumers form new organic matter. Of roughly 170 billion tons of annual biosphere NPP, oceans contribute only about 55 billion. Decomposition recycles dead matter through fragmentation by detritivores, leaching of soluble nutrients, microbial catabolism, humification into slow-decaying humus, and mineralisation that frees inorganic nutrients. It needs oxygen, runs faster in warm, moist conditions and on nitrogen- and sugar-rich detritus, and slows with lignin, chitin, cold or waterlogging.

### The carbon cycle: fast and slow loops

NCERT's Class 9 science book calls the cyclic movement of matter between the living and non-living parts of the Earth a biogeochemical cycle. Carbon is held as CO2 in the air, in plants and animals, in carbonate rocks and fossil fuels, and as dissolved CO2 and shells in the sea. In the fast loop, lasting days to years, photosynthesis turns CO2 into glucose, while respiration and decay return it to the air. In the slow loop, spanning millions of years, buried organisms turn into coal, oil and gas; burning them releases that ancient carbon almost at once. Seawater absorbs CO2 as carbonate and bicarbonate ions that phytoplankton and shell-builders use. Oceans store about 71 per cent of global carbon, the air about 1 per cent, yet atmospheric CO2 climbed from about 315 ppm in 1960 to 420 ppm.

### Nitrogen cycle and human disruption

Nitrogen, needed for proteins and nucleic acids, is most plentiful in the atmosphere, but N2 is too unreactive for organisms to use directly. Rhizobium in legume root nodules and free-living Azotobacter fix it as ammonia, and lightning fixes small amounts as oxides. During nitrification, Nitrosomonas changes ammonia to nitrite and Nitrobacter changes nitrite to nitrate, which plants take up and animals obtain by eating. Decomposers release ammonia from wastes and remains (ammonification), and denitrifiers such as Pseudomonas return nitrate to N2. Industrial Haber-Bosch fixation now supplies most fertiliser nitrogen; surplus nitrate reaching lakes feeds algal blooms that strip oxygen, called eutrophication. The Class 12 text classes carbon as a gaseous cycle, with its reservoir in air or water, and phosphorus as sedimentary, with its reservoir in the Earth's crust.

### Worked example

Calculation frame: a grassland has GPP of 2,000 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹ and plant respiration of 800 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹; NPP = 1,200 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹, the energy available to herbivores and decomposers. Link to why cold, waterlogged soils accumulate organic matter: decomposition slows under low temperature and anaerobiosis.

### Recall

- - **q:** Write the relation between GPP and NPP.
  - **a:** NPP = GPP − respiration losses.
- - **q:** Name the five steps of decomposition.
  - **a:** Fragmentation, leaching, catabolism, humification, mineralisation.
- - **q:** Give one gaseous and one sedimentary cycle.
  - **a:** Carbon (gaseous); phosphorus (sedimentary).
- - **q:** Which bacteria convert ammonia to nitrite and nitrite to nitrate?
  - **a:** Nitrosomonas (ammonia to nitrite); Nitrobacter (nitrite to nitrate).
- - **q:** Distinguish the fast and slow carbon loops.
  - **a:** Fast: photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition over days to years; slow: burial into fossil fuels over millions of years.

### Common trap

Assuming the largest area means the highest productivity: oceans cover about 70 per cent of the surface yet contribute only about a third of global NPP.
