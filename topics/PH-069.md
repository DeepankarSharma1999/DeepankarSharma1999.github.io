### 1. Orientation: what the three-level scheme is, what the earlier laser lessons already give, and what the examiner asks

*Objective:* Say what the three-level scheme does, name the earlier lessons it relies on, and map the five verified questions to the modules.

## The meaning in one paragraph

A laser medium must have more atoms in an upper level than in a lower one (a **population inversion**). In the **three-level scheme** the pump lifts atoms from the ground level E1 to a high level E3; from there they fall at once, without emitting light, to a long-lived **metastable** level E2; and the laser transition runs from E2 back to the ground level E1 (Brijlal Section 22.10.1, PDF p. 633). The ruby laser is the standard example. Because the lower laser level is the ground level, which starts full, the pump has a hard job: it must empty E1 until E2 holds more atoms.

## Words to know

- **Population** N: the number of atoms in a level (per unit volume). N1, N2, N3 for E1, E2, E3; N = N1 + N2 + N3 for all active atoms.
- **Pumping:** supplying energy so that atoms go to higher levels; the **pump rate** W_p is the probability per second that a ground-level atom is lifted.
- **Non-radiative transition:** a downward jump in which the energy goes to the lattice as heat, not as a photon.
- **Metastable level:** a level with a long lifetime because the direct jump down is forbidden; lifetime τ = 1/A21.
- **Threshold:** the smallest inversion at which gain equals loss and oscillation starts.

### 2. Foundation: the three levels, the three rates and the sizes that make the scheme work

*Objective:* Draw the three-level scheme with its pump, fast drop and laser transition, give the size of each rate, and explain why E3 stays almost empty.

## The scheme (Brijlal Section 22.10.1, PDF p. 633)

```
 energy
   ^
   |   E3 =====================  pump band (broad, short-lived)
   |    ^            :
   |    | pump W_p   : fast, non-radiative S32 (heat to the lattice)
   |    |            v
   |   E2 ---------------------  metastable (lifetime τ = 1/A21)
   |    |            :
   |    |            : laser photon hν = E2 − E1 (stimulated, rate W)
   |    |            v
   |   E1 =====================  ground level = lower laser level
```

The chain of events: (1) the pump raises ground-level atoms to E3; (2) they drop to E2 in a very short time without radiation; (3) at E2 a direct jump to E1 is forbidden, so they wait; (4) when N2 exceeds N1, a chance photon of energy hν = E2 − E1 starts a chain of stimulated emissions along the resonator axis.

## The three rates and their sizes

| Process | Symbol | Meaning | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pumping E1 → E3 | W_p | probability per second for a ground atom | set by the lamp (module 4) |
| Fast drop E3 → E2 | S32 | non-radiative; atoms stay in E3 only about 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁹ s | S32 about 10⁸ to 10⁹ s⁻¹ (the book gives about 10⁻⁸ s for the four-level pump level; PH-067 gives about 10⁻⁹ s for ruby's bands) |
| Decay E2 → E1 | A21 = 1/τ | spontaneous; metastable lifetime 10⁻⁶ to 10⁻³ s (PDF p. 628) | A21 about 10³ to 10⁶ s⁻¹; ruby about 333 s⁻¹ for τ = 3 ms (typical, verify) |
| Stimulated E2 ↔ E1 | W | B21 ρ(ν) per atom, same for emission and absorption | zero before the laser field builds up |

**The key ratio.** For ruby-like values S32/A21 is about 10⁵ to 10⁶ (it can be as small as 10² for short-lived metastable levels). Atoms therefore pass through E3 far faster than they leave E2. In steady state the fraction of atoms in E3 is N3/N1 = W_p/S32 (module 3). For a pump rate of 10³ s⁻¹ and S32 = 10⁹ s⁻¹ this is 10⁻⁶: **E3 is practically empty**, and the three-level scheme behaves like a two-level system E1 and E2 with the pump acting as an upward rate W_p.

## Why a third level is needed (a recap of PH-066)

For two levels pumped by light on the transition itself, absorption and stimulated emission have equal probabilities per atom (g1 B12 = g2 B21), and spontaneous emission only pushes downward, so N2 can approach N1 but never exceed it (N2/N1 = s/(1 + s) < 1). The third level breaks the symmetry: the pump acts on **E1 → E3** while the laser acts on **E2 → E1**, and the fast drop E3 → E2 is one-way. The pump is then effectively a one-way upward rate E1 → E2.

## Terms the examiner expects

- **Pump level or pump band** (E3). The book says a broad band of closely spaced levels is better, because a flash lamp emits over a wide range of frequencies and a broad band absorbs more of it (Section 22.10.4, PDF p. 634).
- **Metastable level** (E2): a state from which single-photon emission is forbidden by angular momentum conservation (PDF p. 628).
- **Active centres:** the atoms that take part (Cr³⁺ in ruby); the rest is host (PDF p. 629).

## Looking ahead

Module 3 turns the picture into three equations. The next question is **how fast must the pump be?** The answer, W_p > A21, will come out of the algebra.

### 3. Core derivation 1: rate equations of the three-level scheme and the condition for inversion

*Objective:* Write the three rate equations, solve them in steady state, and show that inversion needs the pump rate to exceed A21.

## Assumptions (the lesson's own model; verify)

Identical atoms; equal degeneracies (g1 = g2); N = N1 + N2 + N3 constant; W_p, S32, A21 as in module 2; a laser field of stimulated rate W per atom (first W = 0). The pump photons themselves stimulate negligible E3 → E1 emission because N3 is tiny.

## Step 1: the rate equations

$$ dN3/dt = W_p N1 − S32 N3 $$

$$ dN2/dt = S32 N3 − A21 N2 − W (N2 − N1) $$

$$ dN1/dt = −W_p N1 + A21 N2 + W (N2 − N1) $$

Each term is atoms gained or lost per second. The three right-hand sides add to zero, so N is conserved ✓.

## Step 2: steady state with no laser field (W = 0)

Set dN3/dt = 0: N3 = W_p N1/S32. Set dN2/dt = 0: A21 N2 = S32 N3 = W_p N1. Hence

$$ N2 = (W_p/A21) N1 = x N1,    x = W_p/A21 = W_p τ $$

**Meaning:** in steady state the pump moves atoms from E1 to E2 at the rate W_p N1, and spontaneous decay removes them at the rate A21 N2. N2/N1 is simply the ratio of the two rates.

## Step 3: the inversion

Use N = N1 + N2 + N3 = N1 (1 + x + W_p/S32), so N1 = N/(1 + x + W_p/S32). With W_p/S32 negligible (module 2),

$$ N1 = N/(1 + x),    N2 = N x/(1 + x),    ΔN = N2 − N1 = N (x − 1)/(x + 1) $$

**Result:** ΔN > 0 if and only if **x > 1, that is W_p > A21 = 1/τ**. Each ground atom must be pumped faster than the metastable level empties. Then N2 > N/2, which is the book's 'more than half' (PDF p. 633).

| x = W_p τ | N2/N | ΔN/N |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.333 | −0.333 |
| 1 | 0.500 | 0 |
| 2 | 0.667 | +0.333 |
| 5 | 0.833 | +0.667 |
| 10 | 0.909 | +0.818 |

## Step 4: checks

- **Limit W_p → 0:** ΔN → −N: all atoms in E1, the medium absorbs.
- **Limit W_p → ∞:** N2 → N and ΔN → N, but the assumption W_p ≪ S32 fails first, so the limit is theoretical.
- **Finite S32:** the exact steady state is ΔN = N (x − 1)/(1 + x + W_p/S32); for x = 2, τ = 3 ms and a small S32 = 10⁶ s⁻¹ this gives ΔN/N = 0.33326 instead of 0.33333, so S32 hardly matters.
- **Symbolic check (sympy):** solving the three equations exactly and letting S32 → ∞ gives N (W_p − A21)/(W_p + A21), matching Step 3.
- **Dimensions:** x is a ratio of rates, so ΔN has the dimension of N.

## Two things to remember

1. The condition is on a **rate**, not on a count: 'more than half the atoms in E2' is the same statement as 'W_p > 1/τ' once the steady state is reached.
2. **The lifetime sets the price.** A longer τ (a more metastable level) lowers the required pump rate: τ = 3 ms needs W_p above about 333 s⁻¹; τ = 1 μs would need above 10⁶ s⁻¹.

## A source note

The book writes the condition as 'N2 > N1/2' (PDF p. 634). If N1 means the instantaneous ground population the condition is N2 > N1; the 'half' refers to half of the **total** (initial) ground-state atoms, N2 > N/2. This lesson uses N2 > N/2 = ΔN > 0.

### 4. Core: threshold pumping, the time to reach inversion and the energy bill (illustrative ruby numbers)

*Objective:* Turn the threshold inversion into a threshold pump rate, find how long the pump needs to build the inversion, and estimate the energy and power for a ruby-sized rod.

## From threshold inversion to threshold pump rate

Day 19 lesson PH-066 (section 'Core: conditions for laser action, the gain coefficient and the threshold population inversion') shows that oscillation starts when the inversion reaches ΔN_th, set by the mirror and scattering losses (the book's threshold gain, Eq. 22.39, PDF p. 632). Write ε = ΔN_th/N, usually tiny. From module 3, ΔN/N = (x − 1)/(x + 1) = ε gives

$$ x_th = (1 + ε)/(1 − ε),    W_p,th = A21 (1 + ε)/(1 − ε) ≈ A21 (1 + 2ε) $$

**Reading:** the threshold pump rate is only a little above A21 = 1/τ, however small the threshold inversion. For ε = 10⁻³, x_th = 1.002; for ruby with τ = 3 ms (typical, verify), W_p,th = 334 s⁻¹. Making the mirrors better (smaller ε) cannot lower it below 1/τ.

## How long does the pump need? (derivation, N3 ≈ 0)

Start with every atom in E1 and switch on a constant pump. Then dN2/dt = W_p (N − N2) − A21 N2, with solution

$$ N2(t) = N [x/(1 + x)] [1 − e^(−(1 + x) t/τ)] $$

(t = 0 gives 0; t → ∞ gives the steady state of module 3). Inversion for ε → 0 means N2 = N/2:

$$ 1 − e^(−(1 + x) t/τ) = (1 + x)/(2x)   so   t_inv = [τ/(1 + x)] ln[2x/(x − 1)] $$

| x = W_p τ | t_inv/τ | t_inv for τ = 3 ms |
|---|---|---|
| 1.2 | 1.130 | 3.39 ms |
| 1.5 | 0.717 | 2.15 ms |
| 2 | 0.462 | 1.39 ms |
| 5 | 0.153 | 0.46 ms |
| 10 | 0.0726 | 0.22 ms |

**Limits.** As x → 1⁺ the time diverges (the steady state is just the threshold). For large x, t_inv → τ ln 2/x = ln 2/W_p, the time for the pump to lift half the atoms. Because the xenon flash lasts a few milliseconds (book, PDF p. 640), the pump must reach x of at least about 1.5 to 2 for the inversion to build inside the flash. If x < 1 the inversion never forms, however long the flash lasts.

## The energy bill (all numbers illustrative; verify)

Take N = 3 × 10¹⁹ Cr³⁺ ions (book problem 1, PDF p. 652), laser photons of 694.3 nm (1.786 eV), pump photons of 550 nm (2.254 eV; the extract names only 'green and blue') and τ = 3 ms.

- **Energy to reach N2 = N/2:** (N/2) hν_p = 1.5 × 10¹⁹ × 3.612 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = **5.4 J** absorbed at least (more in practice, because some atoms decay while the rest are being pumped: about 7 J at x = 2 for τ = 3 ms). The same number of laser photons would carry 4.29 J (PH-067), a share hν_L/hν_p = 0.79.
- **Power to hold the inversion at threshold:** atoms must reach E2 at the rate A21 N2 ≈ N/(2τ) = 5 × 10²¹ per second, an absorbed power of 5 × 10²¹ × 3.612 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = about **1.8 kW** (PH-067 quotes 1.4 kW counted at the 1.786 eV laser photon; at 550 nm pump photons it is 1.8 kW). A flash gives the energy in about a millisecond, which is why the ruby laser is pulsed (module 8).
- **Four-level contrast:** the inversion needed is ΔN_th = εN, so the pump power is about (εN/τ) hν_p = 3.6 W for ε = 10⁻³: smaller by 1/(2ε) = 500 (module 5).

## Checks

- **Dimensions:** x and ε are pure numbers; t_inv carries the unit of τ; power is atoms per second × joule per atom.
- **Scale:** the book's 8.6 J is the energy of all 3 × 10¹⁹ laser photons (PDF p. 652); 5.4 J absorbed is the same order.

### 5. Core derivation 2: the four-level scheme beside it - rate equations, threshold and the 1/ε advantage

*Objective:* Write the four-level rate equations, solve them in steady state, and show why the threshold pump rate is smaller by about 1/ε.

## The four-level scheme (Brijlal Section 22.10.2, PDF pp. 633 to 634)

Levels: E1 ground, E4 pump level, E3 metastable upper laser level, E2 lower laser level. The pump lifts E1 → E4; atoms stay in E4 about 10⁻⁸ s and fall to E3; the laser transition is E3 → E2; atoms leave E2 for E1 by non-radiative decay. E2 lies well above E1, (E2 − E1) > kT, so at ordinary temperature it is virtually empty.

```
   E4 =============  pump band
        ^     : fast drop (S43)
  W_p   |      v
   E3 ------------- metastable (decay rate A32)
        |      :  laser photon, stimulated rate W
        |      v
   E2 ------------- lower laser level, emptied fast (S21)
        |      : fast drop (S21)
   E1 ============= ground
```

## Derivation 2: assumptions and equations

Identical atoms, g3 = g2, N = N1 + N2 + N3 + N4, no laser field (W = 0) first. Rates: pump W_p (E1 → E4), S43, A32 (= 1/τ3, decay of E3), S21 (decay of E2).

$$ dN4/dt = W_p N1 − S43 N4 $$

$$ dN3/dt = S43 N4 − A32 N3 − W (N3 − N2) $$

$$ dN2/dt = A32 N3 + W (N3 − N2) − S21 N2 $$

$$ dN1/dt = S21 N2 − W_p N1 $$

**Steady state (W = 0).** N4 = W_p N1/S43; then N3 = W_p N1/A32; then N2 = A32 N3/S21 = (A32/S21) N3. If S21 ≫ A32 (E2 empties fast), N2 ≈ 0 and N4 ≈ 0, so N ≈ N1 + N3:

$$ N3 = N y/(1 + y),    ΔN = N3 − N2 ≈ N y/(1 + y),    y = W_p/A32 = W_p τ3 $$

**ΔN > 0 for every y > 0.** There is no minimum pump rate: the lower level is empty before any atoms arrive.

## The 1/ε advantage

For the same threshold inversion fraction ε = ΔN_th/N:

| | Three-level | Four-level |
|---|---|---|
| ΔN/N | (x − 1)/(x + 1) | y/(1 + y) |
| Threshold pump (units of the decay rate) | x_th = (1 + ε)/(1 − ε) | y_th = ε/(1 − ε) |
| For ε = 10⁻³ | 1.002 | 0.001001 |
| Ratio | | 1001 = (1 + ε)/ε |

With the same lifetime (3 ms), the threshold pump rates are 334.0 s⁻¹ and 0.334 s⁻¹: about a thousand to one for ε = 10⁻³ (501 : 1 for ε = 2 × 10⁻³, 334.7 against 0.668 s⁻¹). In power terms module 4 found 1/(2ε): the rate per ground atom differs by about 1/ε, but the three-level scheme has only about N/2 ground atoms left to pump while the four-level scheme still has about N, which brings the factor 2.

## What the stimulated term does (preview of module 7)

Solving the same equations with W ≠ 0 gives, in the limit of fast S43 and S21, ΔN = N W_p/(A32 + W_p + W) for the four-level scheme, against ΔN = N (W_p − A21)/(A21 + W_p + 2W) for the three-level scheme. In the three-level case each stimulated emission **removes one atom from E2 and adds one to E1**, changing ΔN by 2; in the four-level case it adds an atom to E2, which empties at once, changing ΔN by 1. That difference is the root of pulsed against continuous output (module 8).

## Checks

- Limit y → ∞: ΔN → N, but the assumption W_p ≪ S43 fails first.
- Thermal population of E2 at 300 K for (E2 − E1) = 0.5 eV (illustrative): exp(−0.5/0.02585) = 4.0 × 10⁻⁹, negligible, as the book's (E2 − E1) > kT requires (PDF p. 634).
- Symbolic solution (sympy) of the four equations reproduces ΔN = N W_p/(A32 + W_p + W) at S43, S21 → ∞.

### 6. Core derivation 3: negative temperature of an inverted pair, and the 2024 numerical

*Objective:* Define the temperature of a pair of levels from the population ratio, explain why inversion gives a negative value, and solve the 2024 question with a stated reading of the stem.

## The definition (Brijlal PDF p. 627)

At thermal equilibrium the Boltzmann law fixes the ratio of two populations at temperature T (equal degeneracies): N2/N1 = exp(−hν/kT), with hν = E2 − E1. At any positive T this is less than 1, so N2 < N1. Run the formula backwards for **any** pair of populations and define the temperature of that pair:

$$ T = − hν / (k ln(N2/N1)) $$

For an inverted pair N2/N1 > 1, ln(N2/N1) > 0 and **T < 0**. The book calls the inverted state a negative-temperature state and stresses that it does not mean a temperature below absolute zero: it is a name for a **non-equilibrium** condition reached at normal laboratory temperature (PDF p. 627). The crystal is at 300 K; only the two-level population ratio behaves as if it were at T < 0.

## The ordering of temperatures

Use β = 1/(kT). Positive temperatures run from T = 0⁺ (β = +∞, populations all in the lower level) up to T = +∞ (β = 0, equal populations N2 = N1). Beyond β = 0 the ratio exceeds 1 and β is negative: T = −∞ (barely inverted) up to T = 0⁻ (β = −∞, all atoms in the upper level). Such a system is hotter than any positive temperature: joined to a normal system, it gives energy to it (standard thermodynamics; verify).

## The 2024 numerical (Q7(b)(ii), 10 marks; analysis only)

*A three-level laser emits at 550 nm. The population of the upper level exceeds that of the lower by 25 per cent. Find the negative temperature that characterises the system.*

**Reading of the stem (stated).** 'Exceeds by 25 per cent' is read as N2 = 1.25 N1. The pair is E2 and E1 (the lasing pair of a three-level laser); the scheme itself does not enter the arithmetic.

1. **Photon energy.** hν = hc/λ = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 2.998 × 10⁸/5.5 × 10⁻⁷ = 3.612 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 2.254 eV.
2. **hν/k** = 3.612 × 10⁻¹⁹/1.381 × 10⁻²³ = 2.616 × 10⁴ K.
3. **ln 1.25** = 0.2231.
4. **T** = −2.616 × 10⁴/0.2231 = **−1.17 × 10⁵ K**.

**Other readings (shown for completeness; the stem is solved as printed above).** If 'exceeds by 25 per cent' meant (N2 − N1)/N2 = 0.25, then N2/N1 = 4/3 and T = −9.09 × 10⁴ K. If it meant N2 − N1 = 0.25 N, then N2/N1 = 5/3 and T = −5.12 × 10⁴ K. The reading used above (N2 = 1.25 N1) is the usual meaning of 'exceeds by 25 per cent'.

## Checks

- **Sign:** inversion must give T < 0 ✓.
- **Limit 1:** N2/N1 → 1⁺ gives T → −∞ (e.g. ratio 1.0001 gives −2.6 × 10⁸ K).
- **Limit 2:** N2/N1 → ∞ gives T → 0⁻ (ratio 10⁶ gives −1.9 × 10³ K).
- **Scale:** at T = 300 K the same pair would have N2/N1 = exp(−87.2) = 1.3 × 10⁻³⁸, so even 1.25 is an enormous departure from equilibrium.
- **Link to module 3:** in steady state N2/N1 = x, so the pump parameter fixes the temperature: x = 2 gives −3.77 × 10⁴ K and x = 3 gives −2.38 × 10⁴ K at 550 nm. A stronger pump means a smaller |T|, a 'hotter' pair.

Timed target (practice, not an official allotment): 10 marks, about 12 minutes.

### 7. Master derivation 4: above threshold - saturation of the inversion, clamping and one photon per extra pumped atom

*Objective:* Solve the three-level rate equations with a laser field present, show that oscillation clamps the inversion at its threshold value, and find the output as a function of the pump rate.

## Why go beyond module 3

Modules 3 to 6 treat the medium with no laser field. Once the laser oscillates, **stimulated emission becomes the main way atoms leave E2**, and it changes the inversion. This module shows how, in the steady state of a continuously pumped three-level laser (the lesson's own model; verify).

## Assumptions

Constant pump W_p, S32 so fast that N3 ≈ 0, N1 + N2 = N, equal degeneracies, and a steady field that gives each atom the stimulated rate W (same for emission and absorption). Net stimulated rate per volume is W (N2 − N1).

## Derivation

1. Steady state of E2, using N1 = N − N2 and N2 − N1 = 2 N2 − N:

$$ 0 = W_p (N − N2) − A21 N2 − W (2 N2 − N) $$

2. Collect the terms in N2:

$$ N2 (W_p + A21 + 2W) = N (W_p + W) $$

3. The inversion ΔN = 2 N2 − N is therefore

$$ ΔN = N (W_p − A21) / (W_p + A21 + 2W) = ΔN₀ / (1 + 2W/(W_p + A21)) $$

where ΔN₀ is the inversion without a field (module 3). **Saturation:** a stronger field (larger W) lowers ΔN, but the denominator is always positive, so ΔN tends to zero from above and never turns negative.

4. **Clamping.** In steady oscillation gain equals loss, so ΔN = ΔN_th (PH-066, threshold). Put ΔN = ΔN_th in step 3 and solve for W:

$$ 2 W ΔN_th = N (W_p − A21) − ΔN_th (W_p + A21) = (N − ΔN_th) W_p − (N + ΔN_th) A21 $$

With W_p,th = A21 (N + ΔN_th)/(N − ΔN_th) (module 4):

$$ W = (N − ΔN_th)(W_p − W_p,th) / (2 ΔN_th) $$

**Reading:** W is zero at threshold and rises **linearly** with the pump above it. Raising the pump does not raise the inversion (it is clamped at ΔN_th); it raises the stimulated rate.

5. **Output.** Net stimulated emissions per volume per second: W ΔN_th = (N − ΔN_th)(W_p − W_p,th)/2.

6. **Pump bookkeeping.** Pump excitations per volume per second beyond threshold: (W_p − W_p,th) N1, with N1 = (N − ΔN_th)/2 because N1 + N2 = N and N2 − N1 = ΔN_th. This equals the result of step 5.

$$ extra stimulated photons per second = extra pumped atoms per second $$

**Above threshold each additional pumped atom ends as one additional stimulated photon** (ideal slope; real lasers lose some to other processes). In power terms the slope also includes the factor hν_L/hν_p, 0.79 for the illustrative ruby numbers of module 4.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| W τ | 0 | 249 | 499 | 1997 |

The stimulated rate per atom is hundreds of times the spontaneous rate 1/τ, which is why the radiation field dominates once the laser is running.

## Checks and limits

- At W_p = W_p,th, W = 0 ✓. At W = 0 step 3 reduces to module 3 ✓.
- Dimensions: W has the unit of a rate; ΔN the unit of N.
- The same procedure for the four-level scheme gives ΔN = N W_p/(A32 + W_p + W): there W counts once, not twice (module 5).

## What the steady state does not show

A flash is not a steady pump. The sudden rise of W empties E2 in a burst, ΔN falls below ΔN_th, the burst stops, and the pump rebuilds it: **spiking**, treated in Day 20 lesson PH-067, section 'Core: how the ruby laser works in time - pulses and laser spiking (derivation 1)'.

## Pulsed or continuous?

The book states (PDF p. 634): in the three-level scheme, once stimulated emission begins the inversion reverts to the normal population, lasing stops, and the output is pulsed; the four-level scheme can hold the inversion and runs continuously. The rate equations make this precise.

- Module 7 shows a three-level laser **can** be steady if the pump stays above W_p,th, because the inversion is clamped.
- Module 4 shows the price: about 1.8 kW of absorbed power for a ruby-sized rod (illustrative), against about 3.6 W for a four-level medium with the same τ and ε.
- A flash supplies its energy in about τ, so the inversion builds, is used up in a burst of spikes (PH-067), and must be rebuilt. **Practical three-level lasers are therefore pulsed.** Continuous ruby operation with very intense pumping is reported in the literature (standard knowledge; verify).

The statement 'three-level lasers cannot be continuous' is too strong; the correct statement is that continuous three-level operation needs a very high, steady pump.

## True or false elimination

'In a three-level scheme the pump level is the upper laser level' is false: atoms drop from E3 to E2. 'A four-level scheme needs a larger pump power than a three-level scheme' contradicts the book (PDF p. 634) and the 1/(2ε) result.

### Notes: Definitions

- Population inversion: a non-equilibrium condition in which the upper level of a transition holds more atoms than the lower one (N2 > N1 for equal degeneracies; more exactly N2/g2 > N1/g1).
- Three-level scheme: ground level E1 (also the lower laser level), pump level E3 (broad band, short-lived), metastable upper laser level E2; the laser transition is E2 → E1 (Brijlal Section 22.10.1).
- Four-level scheme: ground E1, pump E4, metastable E3, lower laser level E2 well above E1 and nearly empty; laser transition E3 → E2 (Section 22.10.2).
- Pump rate W_p: probability per second that a ground-level atom is lifted to the pump level. A21 = 1/τ: decay rate of the metastable level. S32: fast non-radiative rate E3 → E2. W: stimulated rate per atom.
- x = W_p τ (three-level) and y = W_p τ3 (four-level): the pump rate in units of the decay rate of the metastable level.
- Threshold fraction ε = ΔN_th/N: the inversion needed for oscillation as a fraction of all active atoms.
- Negative temperature: the temperature defined by T = −hν/(k ln(N2/N1)) for an inverted pair; a name for a non-equilibrium state, not a temperature below absolute zero (PDF p. 627).

### Notes: Equations to know

- Three-level rate equations: dN3/dt = W_p N1 − S32 N3; dN2/dt = S32 N3 − A21 N2 − W (N2 − N1); dN1/dt = −W_p N1 + A21 N2 + W (N2 − N1).
- Steady state, W = 0, S32 ≫ W_p: N2/N = x/(1 + x), ΔN/N = (x − 1)/(x + 1), x = W_p/A21. Inverted iff W_p > A21.
- Threshold pump: x_th = (1 + ε)/(1 − ε); W_p,th = A21 (1 + ε)/(1 − ε) ≈ A21 (1 + 2ε).
- Time to inversion from the ground state: t_inv = [τ/(1 + x)] ln[2x/(x − 1)].
- Four-level steady state: ΔN/N = y/(1 + y); y_th = ε/(1 − ε); ratio of thresholds (1 + ε)/ε.
- With a field: three-level ΔN = N (W_p − A21)/(W_p + A21 + 2W); four-level ΔN = N W_p/(A32 + W_p + W).
- Clamping: ΔN = ΔN_th gives W = (N − ΔN_th)(W_p − W_p,th)/(2 ΔN_th); stimulated photons per volume per second = (N − ΔN_th)(W_p − W_p,th)/2.
- Temperature of a pair: T = −hν/(k ln(N2/N1)); hν/k = 1.4388 × 10⁻² m K/λ.
- Threshold gain (book Eq. 22.39): γ_th = α_s + (1/2L) ln(1/r1 r2); ΔN_th = γ_th/σ (PH-066).

### Notes: Three-level against four-level scheme

- Source for the first and last rows: Brijlal Section 22.10.3 (PDF p. 634); the other rows are derived in modules 3, 5 and 7.

### Notes: Derivation summary 1: steady-state inversion in the three-level scheme (10 marks, practice target about 12 minutes)

- Assumptions: identical atoms, equal degeneracies, N = N1 + N2 + N3, S32 ≫ W_p, no laser field.
- Rate equations for N3, N2, N1 (sum zero).
- dN3/dt = 0: N3 = W_p N1/S32 ≈ 0. dN2/dt = 0: A21 N2 = S32 N3 = W_p N1, so N2 = x N1.
- N = N1 (1 + x) gives N1 = N/(1 + x), N2 = N x/(1 + x), ΔN = N (x − 1)/(x + 1).
- Inversion iff x > 1, i.e. W_p > A21; then N2 > N/2.
- Checks: W_p → 0 gives ΔN → −N; the sympy solution of the three equations agrees; the finite-S32 correction is 1/(1 + x + W_p/S32).

### Notes: Derivation summary 2: the four-level scheme and the 1/ε ratio (10 marks, about 12 minutes)

- Four rate equations with W_p, S43, A32, S21.
- Steady state: N4 = W_p N1/S43, N3 = W_p N1/A32, N2 = (A32/S21) N3 ≈ 0 for S21 ≫ A32.
- N ≈ N1 + N3 gives ΔN ≈ N3 = N y/(1 + y), y = W_p/A32.
- Threshold: y_th = ε/(1 − ε), against x_th = (1 + ε)/(1 − ε): ratio (1 + ε)/ε.
- Power ratio 1/(2ε) because the three-level scheme has only N/2 ground atoms left to pump.

### Notes: Derivation summary 3: above threshold (15 marks, about 18 minutes)

- Steady E2 population with a field: 0 = W_p (N − N2) − A21 N2 − W (2 N2 − N).
- N2 (W_p + A21 + 2W) = N (W_p + W) and ΔN = N (W_p − A21)/(W_p + A21 + 2W).
- Oscillation: ΔN = ΔN_th, so 2 W ΔN_th = (N − ΔN_th) W_p − (N + ΔN_th) A21 and W = (N − ΔN_th)(W_p − W_p,th)/(2 ΔN_th).
- Net stimulated photons W ΔN_th equal extra pump excitations (W_p − W_p,th) N1 with N1 = (N − ΔN_th)/2.

### Notes: Confused concepts

- Pump level (broad, short-lived, E3) against metastable level (narrow, long-lived, E2).
- Pump rate per atom W_p (a rate in s⁻¹) against pump power (watts).
- N2 > N/2 (against the initial total) against N2 > N1 (instantaneous populations): the same condition.
- Negative temperature against a temperature below absolute zero: the first is a non-equilibrium label, the second does not exist.
- Laser threshold (gain equals loss, ΔN = ΔN_th) against the inversion threshold (ΔN = 0).
- Three-level scheme (lower level is the ground level) against four-level scheme (lower level is an empty excited level).

### Notes: Limits of the simple model

- Equal degeneracies are assumed; with degeneracies the inversion condition is N2/g2 > N1/g1 and every x-threshold shifts by the ratio g2/g1.
- The stimulated term uses one rate W for emission and absorption; in a real rod W varies along the length and across the beam (standing-wave hole burning), so the steady formulae are averages.
- S32 → ∞ is an idealisation: for very strong pumping N3 is no longer negligible and the three-level scheme becomes bottlenecked.
- Real ruby has an E2 doublet and broad bands E3 (book Fig. 22.25, PH-067); the two-level effective model hides this and is a reasonable simplification, flagged verify.
- A flash is a transient pump: the steady-state results give the long-time limit; the transient is spiking (PH-067).

  - - **q:** What happens to atoms lifted to E3?
    - **a:** They drop to E2 almost at once by a non-radiative transition, giving the energy to the lattice as heat.
  - - **q:** Why must E2 be metastable?
    - **a:** A long lifetime lets atoms accumulate there until N2 exceeds N1; with a short lifetime they fall back before any inversion forms.
  - - **q:** Why should the pump level be a broad band?
    - **a:** A flash lamp emits over a wide range of frequencies, so a broad band absorbs more of the light and raises the pumping efficiency.
  - - **q:** In the four-level scheme, why is the lower laser level almost empty?
    - **a:** It lies above the ground level by more than kT, so thermal excitation does not populate it, and its atoms decay quickly to the ground level.
- **conceptual:**
  - - **q:** Why does the three-level scheme need a pump rate above 1/τ?
    - **a:** In steady state N2/N1 = W_p/A21, so N2 > N1 only if W_p > A21 = 1/τ.

    - **task:** Write the rate equations of a three-level laser and show that a steady-state population inversion needs the pump rate per ground atom to exceed the decay rate of the metastable level (10 marks; practice target about 12 minutes, not an official allotment).
    - **steps:**
      - Assumptions: identical atoms, equal degeneracies, N = N1 + N2 + N3, pump rate W_p (E1 → E3), fast non-radiative rate S32 (E3 → E2), metastable decay rate A21 = 1/τ (E2 → E1), no laser field first.

      - Equation for E2: dN2/dt = S32 N3 − A21 N2 (stimulated term W (N2 − N1) added later).
      - Equation for E1: dN1/dt = −W_p N1 + A21 N2; the three right-hand sides add to zero.
      - Steady state of E3: N3 = W_p N1/S32, which is tiny when S32 ≫ W_p.
      - Steady state of E2: A21 N2 = S32 N3 = W_p N1, so N2 = x N1 with x = W_p/A21.

### Memory skeleton

- Three levels: E1 ground (also the lower laser level), E3 pump band (broad, short-lived), E2 metastable upper laser level
- Chain: pump E1 → E3; fast non-radiative drop E3 → E2 (heat to the lattice); atoms wait in E2; a chance photon E2 → E1 starts stimulated emission along the axis
- Why a third level: the pump acts on E1 → E3 and the laser on E2 → E1, and the fast drop is one-way; pumping two levels on their own transition never passes N2 = N1
- Inversion: N2 > N1, i.e. more than half of all active atoms in E2; the book's 'N2 > N1/2' holds only with N1 the initial total
- Metastable E2: single-photon decay forbidden, 10⁻⁶ to 10⁻³ s; broad pump band to absorb more of the flash lamp's light
- Beside it: four-level lower laser level more than kT above ground, nearly empty; ruby three-level; Nd:YAG, He-Ne, CO₂ four-level
- Negative temperature: T = −hν/(k ln(N2/N1)) < 0 for an inverted pair, a non-equilibrium label (2024 numerical)
