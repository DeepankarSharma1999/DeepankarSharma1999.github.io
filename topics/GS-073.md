## GS-073: Decolonisation in Asia and Africa

Mains GS1 · World History · 18th–20th century

*Goal:* Explain Decolonisation in Asia and Africa and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Why empires unravelled

Powerful nationalist movements had challenged colonial exploitation almost everywhere before 1945. The Second World War devastated Europe, while the US and the Soviet Union emerged as dominant powers. India's national movement, NCERT notes, belonged to a worldwide struggle against colonialism and influenced liberation movements across Asia and Africa. Independence came quickly: within roughly twenty years of 1945 most Asian and African colonies were free. NCERT's timeline marks Ghana in 1957 as sub-Saharan Africa's first independent state, followed by the Organisation of African Unity in 1963, while in South Africa the National Party imposed apartheid from 1948.

### Solidarity among new states

Nehru made Asian and African unity a foreign-policy priority. India hosted the Asian Relations Conference in March 1947, five months before its own independence, convened a conference in 1949 backing Indonesia against Dutch rule, and opposed racism and apartheid. The 1955 Bandung Conference was the high point of India's engagement with newly free Afro-Asian nations and later led to the Non-Aligned Movement, whose first summit met at Belgrade in 1961 with Nehru as a co-founder. India itself stayed out of both the US-led NATO and the Soviet-led Warsaw Pact. In 1956 India led world protest when Britain attacked Egypt over the Suez Canal.

### Economic legacy and exam angles

New nations were burdened by poverty and lack of resources after long colonial rule. The IMF and World Bank had been designed for industrial countries and only from the late 1950s turned towards developing ones, so ex-colonies ended up advised by agencies dominated by former colonisers, while foreign corporations secured cheap rights to their resources. Most did not share the rapid Western growth of the 1950s–60s, which led them to form the G-77 and demand an NIEO. From the mid-1970s, borrowing from private banks brought debt crises, especially in Africa and Latin America. Prelims: Bandung 1955 versus Belgrade 1961; Ghana 1957; OAU 1963. Mains: political versus economic decolonisation.

### Worked example

Question: 'Decolonisation brought political freedom but not economic sovereignty.' Examine with reference to Asia and Africa. Frame: (1) Rapid independence after 1945; Ghana 1957. (2) Afro-Asian solidarity: Asian Relations Conference 1947, Bandung 1955, NAM 1961. (3) Continuing dependence: ex-colonial control of minerals and land, Bretton Woods agencies dominated by former powers, cheap resource rights for foreign firms. (4) Response: G-77 and NIEO; later debt crises. Conclude that formal sovereignty coexisted with structural dependence.

### Recall

- - **q:** Which was the first sub-Saharan African country to become independent, and when?
  - **a:** Ghana, in 1957.
- - **q:** What did the NIEO demand?
  - **a:** Real control over natural resources, more development assistance, fairer raw-material prices and better market access for manufactures.
- - **q:** Where and when was the first NAM summit?
  - **a:** Belgrade, September 1961.

### Common trap

Equating political independence with economic freedom. NCERT notes that former colonial powers still controlled vital minerals and land in many ex-colonies, and new states came under the guidance of agencies dominated by those same powers.
