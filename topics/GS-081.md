## GS-081: Mughal and Rajput architecture

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS1 · Culture · Indian art, literature and architecture

*Goal:* Explain Mughal and Rajput architecture and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Tombs and the charbagh idea

Monumental tombs over royal graves include those of Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq, Humayun and Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan in Delhi and of Akbar and Itmaduddaula in Agra. NCERT, citing Anthony Welch, links the tomb to the promise of paradise for the believer, which explains Quranic verses on walls and settings in gardens or beside water. Humayun's tomb and the Taj Mahal follow the charbagh, a garden criss-crossed by paths and water courses, yet such ordered landscapes also displayed the might of the person buried there. The Taj marks the peak: a chamfered, eight-sided tomb, a double dome, white Makrana marble against red sandstone, and decoration by relief carving, jalis, pietra dura and jasper-inlaid calligraphy.

### Forts, Rajput strongholds and shared courts

Forts symbolised the king's seat: losing one could mean losing sovereignty. Builders used heights for surveillance and awe; Gwalior's steep rock impressed even Babur, and Chittorgarh, with its victory towers and many water bodies, is described by NCERT as Asia's largest fort and the longest-occupied seat of power. Amer Fort is illustrated for its jali screens. Inside fort palaces, NCERT stresses, styles and ornaments were borrowed liberally. Political ties fed the exchange: Jahangir's mother was a Kachhwaha princess of Amber and Shah Jahan's a Rathor princess of Marwar, while many Rajputs held high Mughal office and others resisted.

### Heritage status of Mughal and Rajput forts

UNESCO recognition is a quick way to fix the key monuments. The PIB list of India's World Heritage Sites (August 2024) groups six Rajasthan strongholds into one serial property, Hill Forts of Rajasthan, inscribed in 2013: Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh, Jaisalmer, Ranthambhore, Amber and Gagron. Mughal sites on the same list are Agra Fort and the Taj Mahal (both 1983), Fatehpur Sikri (1986), Humayun's Tomb (1993) and the Red Fort Complex in Delhi (2007). Rajasthan's later entries on the list include the Jantar Mantar, Jaipur (2010) and Jaipur City (2019). The Red Fort, once the Mughal residence, is still where the Independence Day address is given.
