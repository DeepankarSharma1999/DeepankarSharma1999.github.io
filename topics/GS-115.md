## GS-115: Northern plains and peninsular plateau

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS1 · Geography · Indian geography

### Bridge: Reading a north-south cross-section

Several diagrams in this lesson are cross-sections, so learn to read one first.

Imagine cutting the country along a north-south line from the Himalaya to the south of the Ganga plain, then looking at the cut face from the side. The **horizontal line** shows distance along the cut (north on the left, south on the right in this lesson). The **vertical height** shows how high the land stands above mean sea level (the average sea surface, taken as zero). A steep line means a steep slope; a long flat line means level land.

Two cautions. First, cross-sections usually exaggerate height, because the true heights are tiny compared with the distances. A 150 m rise over 300 km would be an invisible line at true scale, so the drawer stretches the vertical scale. Second, the widths in a sketch of zones are for order, not for measurement; always use the numbers in the text.

How to read one: start at the left end, name what you stand on (a hill, a belt, a river), move right one step at a time and say what changes (slope, material, wetness). That is exactly how module 3 walks you across the Northern Plain.

### 1. Orientation: the plain and the plateau on one page

*Objective:* Say in one line each what the Northern Plain and the Peninsular Plateau are and what separates them in age, rock and relief.

## Why this lesson exists

Day 21 lesson GS-113 gave you the map of all six physiographic divisions of India. This lesson opens up the two biggest land divisions: the **Northern Plain** and the **Peninsular Plateau**. The Himalaya (Day 22 lesson GS-114), the Ghats (Day 17 lesson GS-116) and the rivers (Day 18 lesson GS-117) have their own lessons; here we use them only as borders and as causes.

## The one-page contrast

| Feature | Northern Plain | Peninsular Plateau |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A vast plain built by rivers | An irregular triangle of table-land |
| Material | River-laid alluvium | Ancient gneiss and granite basement |
| Age and stability | Young deposit, still being added to | One of the oldest and most stable landmasses of India |
| Surface | Featureless, 50-150 m above sea level | Rises about 150 m above the plains to 600-900 m (general band) |
| Rivers | Meandering, shifting, flood-prone, large deltas | Shallow, graded valleys, fixed courses, deltas only on the east |
| Soil and use | Fertile alluvium: wheat, rice, sugarcane, jute | Black soil in the west and north-west; mineral-rich in the east and north-east |

All of this comes from NCERT Class XI (kegy102 pages 2-6). The word to hold is **structure**: the plain is a trough that was filled; the plateau is a block that stood firm.

*Location note.* NCERT's location chapter says the southern part of India lies within the tropics and the northern part in the sub-tropical zone (kegy101 p. 2). Climate therefore changes along the same north-south line as relief; the climate side is Day 20 lesson GS-119.

## A figure worth drawing

Sketch a north-south line: Himalaya, then the plain, then the plateau. Label the plain "young, low, soft" and the plateau "old, high, hard". That one picture answers many Prelims statements.

North  ........................................................  South
Himalaya  | Bhabar | Tarai | Bhangar and Khadar plain | Peninsular Plateau
(young)   | boulders| marsh | alluvium, 50-150 m       | old block, 600-900 m

## What you will be able to do

By module 10 you will walk the plain zone by zone, split the plateau into three groups, explain the Narmada and Tapi, and frame the Deccan Trap question. The **core first pass** is modules 1 to 6 and 10; modules 7 to 9 are deferred deep dives.

### 2. Foundation: the words you need before the details

*Objective:* Define alluvium, geo-syncline, relict mountain, plateau, scarp, rift valley, block mountain, tor, dyke, water divide and gradient in plain words.

## Words about the plain

- **Sediment**: loose pieces of rock (gravel, sand, silt, clay) carried by water and dropped when the flow slows.
- **Alluvium**: sediment laid by rivers. It is the material of the Northern Plain.
- **Geo-syncline** (NCERT's word): a long, sinking trough in the crust that keeps filling with sediment. NCERT says the plain "was originally a geo-synclinal depression" that was filled by Himalayan and Peninsular rivers.
- **Deposition** and **erosion** (Day 21 lesson GS-099 and Day 22 lesson GS-100): rivers wear land away upstream and drop sediment downstream.
- **Delta**: a fan of land built where a river drops its load at the sea.

## Words about the plateau

- **Plateau**: a large area of high, fairly level land that stands above its surroundings, cut by valleys.
- **Gneiss and granite**: very old, hard crystalline rocks. NCERT says the Peninsula is made of a great complex of them.
- **Rigid block**: land that does not fold easily; NCERT says the Peninsula has stood like a rigid block since the Cambrian period (a very ancient time period, about 540 million years ago; verify the date in an earth-history lesson).
- **Relict** or **residual mountain**: a mountain that is a worn-down remnant of something much larger. NCERT names the Aravali, Nallamala, Javadi, Veliconda, Palkonda and Mahendragiri hills as examples.
- **Scarp**: a steep face or cliff-like slope at the edge of higher land. NCERT says the south has "developed series of scarps".
- **Rift valley**: a long valley that has dropped between parallel cracks (faults) in the rock. The Narmada, Tapi and Mahanadi flow in rift valleys.
- **Block mountain**: a block of land lifted or left standing between faults, for example the Satpura block.
- **Tor**: a rocky knob left standing as the surrounding rock weathers away.
- **Dyke**: a wall-like sheet of hard rock that cuts across older rock, usually formed when magma cooled in a crack (Day 18 lesson GS-096). NCERT mentions wall-like **quartzite dykes**, hard quartz-rich walls that stand up as softer rock wears away and offer natural sites for water storage.

## Words about movement

- **Gradient**: how steeply a river bed falls. High gradient means a fast, cutting river (V-shaped valley, gorge, rapids). Low gradient means a slow, wandering one.
- **Water divide** or **watershed**: the line separating two drainage basins (kegy103: the boundary between one drainage basin and another).
- **Endogenic and exogenic forces**: forces from inside the earth (folding, faulting) and from outside (rain, rivers, wind). NCERT says structure and relief come from their interplay.

## Two quick habits

NCERT states the contrast itself: the Himalayas are "young, weak and flexible", dissected by fast-flowing rivers in their youthful stage, while Peninsular river valleys "are shallow with low gradients" (kegy102 p. 3). So in general "young" or "V-shaped valley" points to the Himalaya, and "relict", "shallow valley" or "rigid" points to the Peninsula.

### 3. Core: how the Northern Plain was built and its zones

*Objective:* Explain how the plain was formed and list its zones from the Shiwalik foothills southwards with width and behaviour.

## The story in four steps

1. When the Indian plate pushed north and the Himalaya rose (Day 19 lesson GS-097), the land in front of the mountains sagged into a long trough, the **geo-syncline** of NCERT.
2. NCERT says the trough "attained its maximum development during the third phase of the Himalayan mountain formation approximately about 64 million years ago". Treat the figure as the book's; compare it with the dating in Day 19 lesson GS-097 and verify.
3. Rivers from the Himalaya and from the Peninsula kept dropping sediment into it.
4. The result is the **Indo-Ganga-Brahmaputra Plain**, a river-made plain of the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra.

## Size

- About **3,200 km** east to west.
- Average **width 150-300 km**, north to south.
- Alluvium **1,000-2,000 m** deep. *Source slip:* NCERT calls this the *average* depth on page 3 and the *maximum* depth on page 5. Quote it as a rough range, and check the wording in your answer.
- General elevation **50-150 m** above mean sea level.

## The zones, north to south (kegy102 p. 5)

NCERT divides the plain into three major zones: the Bhabar, the Tarai and the alluvial plains; the alluvial plains split into Khadar and Bhangar.

| Zone | Width | What the rivers do |
|---|---|---|
| **Bhabar** | 8-10 km, parallel to the Shiwalik foothills | Rivers leave the mountain, the slope breaks, and they drop heavy rocks and boulders; at times they vanish into this belt |
| **Tarai** | about 10-20 km | The streams re-emerge without a clear channel; marshy, swampy; thick natural vegetation and varied wildlife |
| **Bhangar** | no width given | **Old** alluvial deposits |
| **Khadar** | no width given | **New** alluvial deposits |

Check: Bhabar plus Tarai is only about 18-30 km of a plain 150-300 km wide, so most of the width is Bhangar and Khadar.

## Why the order matters

The pattern follows the slope. Rivers lose speed as they leave the mountain, so the **heaviest** material (boulders) drops first at the Bhabar. Water that sank there rises again at the Tarai. Further south the river carries fine silt and clay across a flat plain. Khadar is the newer deposit (NCERT p. 5), the part that flood water still reaches (standard reasoning); Bhangar is older and sits at the slightly higher level (standard; verify).

```
Himalaya -> Shiwalik -> BHABAR -> TARAI -> BHANGAR + KHADAR (alluvial plain)
              hills     boulders   marsh    old and new alluvium
```

## Reading note

Do not confuse the Bhabar (rivers go **down** and out of sight) with the Tarai (rivers come **up** again and spread). That one contrast appears in many Prelims statements.

### 4. Core: landforms, rivers and regions of the plain

*Objective:* Describe the river landforms of the plain, the Brahmaputra plains and the Haryana-Delhi water divide, and say why the plain feeds so many people.

## Mature river landforms

NCERT says the Bhangar and Khadar plains show the **mature stage** of fluvial erosional and depositional landforms: sand bars, meanders, ox-bow lakes and braided channels (kegy102 p. 5). These words are defined in Day 22 lesson GS-100; here is the quick picture.

- A **meander** is a bend of a slow river.
- An **ox-bow lake** is a bend that the river has cut off and left as a curved lake.
- A **braided channel** is a river split into many threads around bars of sand.
- A **sand bar** is a ridge of sand dropped in or beside the channel.

## The Brahmaputra plains

The Brahmaputra plains are known for **riverine islands and sand bars**. NCERT says most of these areas are subject to periodic floods and shifting river courses forming braided streams. The Brahmaputra flows from the north-east to the south-west, then takes an almost **90 degree** turn southward at **Dhubri** before it enters Bangladesh (kegy102 p. 6). The reason for the heavy load is given in the drainage chapter: most of its tributaries are large and carry much sediment because of heavy rain in the catchment (kegy103 p. 7).

## The Kosi as the case study

The Kosi, called the 'sorrow of Bihar', is the NCERT example of a river that shifts. It brings a huge quantity of sediment from its upper reaches and drops it on the plain. The course gets blocked, so the river changes course (kegy103 p. 4). This is a small, clean cause-and-effect chain: steep upper course, heavy load, flat plain, deposit, block, shift.

## Deltas and the water divide

- The mouths of the great rivers build some of the largest deltas of the world; NCERT gives the **Sunderbans** as the example.
- **Haryana and Delhi** form a **water divide** between the Indus and Ganga river systems. Flat as the land looks, a slight rise there sends water one way or the other.

## Regions of the plain (standard names; verify)

NCERT does not divide the plain by region. Many textbooks speak of the **Punjab-Haryana plain** (Indus side), the **Ganga plain** and the **Brahmaputra plain**. Use these names as standard knowledge and verify them in an atlas before using them in an answer. A *doab* (Persian, 'two waters') is the land between two confluent rivers, such as the Ganga-Yamuna doab (standard; verify; Punjab doabs in the master notes).

## Why the plain feeds India

The fertile alluvial soil supports crops such as **wheat, rice, sugarcane and jute**, and hence supports a large population (kegy102 p. 6). Soil plus water plus flat land is the chain behind density. Population patterns belong to Day 21 lesson GS-127; do not quote density figures from this lesson.

## Hazards in one line

Flat land and heavy sediment mean periodic flooding and shifting courses (Day 21 lesson GS-120 for floods). Fertility and flood come from the same cause: the river drops silt.

### 5. Core: the Peninsular Plateau as a whole

*Objective:* Give the outer limits, height, rocks, tilt and typical landforms of the Peninsular Plateau and its geological story.

## Shape and outer limits (kegy102 p. 6)

The plateau is an **irregular triangle**. Its outer extent is marked by the **Delhi ridge** in the north-west (an extension of the Aravali), the **Rajmahal hills** in the east, the **Gir range** in the west and the **Cardamom hills** in the south. An extension of it appears in the north-east as the **Shillong** and **Karbi-Anglong** plateau.

NCERT adds that the Peninsula is a series of *patland* plateaus (flat-topped surfaces): Hazaribagh, Palamu, Ranchi (Jharkhand), Malwa (western Madhya Pradesh), Coimbatore (Tamil Nadu) and Karnataka (kegy102 p. 6; states standard, verify).

NCERT says it rises from about **150 m above the river plains** up to **600-900 m**. This is the general band; parts exceed it. The Central Highlands run 700-1,000 m, the Satpura scarps 600-900 m, the Western Ghats average about 1,500 m and Anaimudi reaches 2,695 m. So quote 600-900 m only as the general level.

## The oldest part of India

NCERT calls it "one of the oldest and the most stable landmass of India". The geological chapter (p. 2) adds that the Peninsula is a great complex of very ancient gneisses and granites that has stood as a rigid block since the Cambrian period. Two exceptions: part of the west coast is submerged beneath the sea, and some parts changed through tectonic activity without disturbing the original basement.

The plateau's northern boundary, as a line on the map, runs from Kachchh along the western side of the Aravali near Delhi, then roughly parallel to the Yamuna and the Ganga to the Rajmahal Hills and the Ganga delta. In Rajasthan the desert overlies this block.

## Slope

The general elevation falls from **west to east**, and NCERT says the flow of rivers proves it. This explains why most big Peninsular rivers flow east to the Bay of Bengal. The Narmada and Tapi flow west because they follow rift valleys (module 8).

## Typical features (the list to learn)

**Tors, block mountains, rift valleys, spurs, bare rocky structures, series of hummocky hills and wall-like quartzite dykes.** The western and north-western part has an emphatic presence of black soil. The valleys are generally shallow with low gradients, so the plateau lacks the deep V-shaped valleys of the Himalayan kind; local gorges occur where rivers cross fault scarps or plateau edges (module 8).

## Faults and tremors

The plateau has had recurrent uplift and submergence with crustal faulting. NCERT singles out the **Bhima fault** for its recurrent seismic activity. The north-western part has complex relief of ravines and gorges, with the **Chambal, Bhind and Morena** ravines as examples.

## Block faulting

As a part of the Indo-Australian Plate the Peninsula has had vertical movements and block faulting. The rift valleys of the **Narmada, Tapi and Mahanadi** and the **Satpura block mountains** are examples.

## Two rules for answers

1. Peninsular mountains are mostly **relict and residual** (Aravali, Nallamala, Javadi, Veliconda, Palkonda, Mahendragiri), not young fold mountains.
2. Peninsular rivers have **shallow valleys with low gradients**, and the east-flowing ones build deltas (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri).

*Reading note:* NCERT's heading 'The Himalayas and other Peninsular mountains' (kegy102 pp. 2-3) is usually read as the northern and north-eastern mountains (verify), while p. 3 calls the Peninsular hills relict. The fold mountains are young; the hills named above are old remnants.

### 6. Core: the Deccan Plateau and the Central Highlands

*Objective:* Place the borders of the Deccan Plateau and the Central Highlands and give the key ranges, heights and rivers of each.

NCERT splits the Peninsular Plateau into three broad groups: the **Deccan Plateau**, the **Central Highlands** and the **Northeastern Plateau**. This module takes the first two; module 7 takes the third.

## Group 1: the Deccan Plateau (kegy102 pp. 6-7)

**Borders.** Western Ghats in the west, Eastern Ghats in the east, and in the north the **Satpura, Maikal range and Mahadeo hills**.

**Western Ghats.** Locally known as **Sahyadri** in Maharashtra, **Nilgiri hills** in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and **Anaimalai hills** and **Cardamom hills** in Kerala. They are higher and more continuous than the Eastern Ghats, with an average elevation of about **1,500 m** that rises from north to south. The highest peak of the Peninsular plateau is **Anaimudi (2,695 m)** on the Anaimalai hills, then **Dodabetta (2,637 m)** on the Nilgiri hills. Most Peninsular rivers rise here. The detailed Ghats story is Day 17 lesson GS-116.

**Eastern Ghats.** Discontinuous, low hills, highly eroded by the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri. Ranges named: Javadi hills, Palconda range, Nallamala hills, Mahendragiri hills.

**The meeting point.** The Eastern and Western Ghats meet each other at the **Nilgiri hills**.

## Group 2: the Central Highlands (kegy102 p. 7)

**Borders.** Bounded in the west by the **Aravali range**. The **Satpura range**, a series of scarped plateaus on the south at about 600-900 m, forms the northernmost boundary of the Deccan plateau and is a classic **relict mountain**: highly denuded, in discontinuous ranges.

**Height and slope.** General elevation about **700-1,000 m**, sloping to the north and north-east.

**Rivers.** Most tributaries of the **Yamuna** rise in the **Vindhyan and Kaimur ranges**. The **Banas** is the only significant tributary of the **Chambal** that rises in the Aravali in the west.

**Rocks.** The region has been through metamorphic processes: marble, slate and gneiss occur there.

**Edges.** The plateau extends west as far as **Jaisalmer**, where it is covered by longitudinal sand ridges and crescent-shaped **barchans** (sand dunes). The **Rajmahal hills** are an eastern extension, and south of them the **Chotanagpur plateau** holds a large reserve of mineral resources.

## A reading note

The Satpura appears twice, as the northern edge of the Deccan plateau and as part of the Central Highlands: one range described from two sides. Keep both statements.

## Two checks

1. The Deccan Plateau is **south** of the Satpura; the Central Highlands are **north** of it.
2. Central Highlands (700-1,000 m) and Satpura (600-900 m) overlap in height, so height cannot separate them; use position.

## Map habit

Draw the Satpura first as a line across the middle, the Aravali to its north-west, then the two Ghats as the sides of the Deccan triangle; Chotanagpur sits in the east, south of the Rajmahal hills.

### 7. Core: the Northeastern Plateau, Chotanagpur and the great faults

*Objective:* Explain how the Meghalaya and Karbi Anglong plateaus came to stand apart and compare the three groups of the plateau in a table.

## Group 3: the Northeastern Plateau (kegy102 p. 7)

NCERT calls it an extension of the main Peninsular plateau. Its story is a good case of cause and effect:

1. During Himalayan building the Indian plate pushed north-east.
2. That force made a huge **fault** (a crack along which rock blocks move) between the **Rajmahal hills** and the **Meghalaya plateau**. NCERT's geology chapter calls it the **Malda fault**, and the drainage chapter the **Malda gap**, whose down-thrusting in the mid-Pleistocene turned the Ganga and Brahmaputra towards the Bay of Bengal.
3. Rivers filled this depression with deposits.
4. So today the **Meghalaya** and **Karbi Anglong** plateaus stand **detached** from the main Peninsular block.

NCERT says the Malda fault separates the north-eastern parts from the Chotanagpur plateau (kegy102 p. 2).

**Meghalaya** is divided into three: the **Garo Hills**, the **Khasi Hills** and the **Jaintia Hills**, named after tribal groups of the region. An extension is the **Karbi Anglong hills** of Assam.

**Resources.** Like the Chotanagpur plateau, Meghalaya is rich in minerals: coal, iron ore, sillimanite, limestone and uranium.

**Rain and erosion.** The area receives its maximum rainfall from the south-west monsoon, so the surface is highly eroded. NCERT says Cherrapunji displays a bare rocky surface devoid of any permanent vegetation cover. Rainfall detail is in Day 21 lesson GS-120.

## Three groups compared

| Group | Position | Typical height | Key features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deccan Plateau | Between the two Ghats, south of the Satpura | Ghats average about 1,500 m; peaks to 2,695 m | Western Ghats higher and continuous, Eastern Ghats low and broken |
| Central Highlands | North of the Satpura, east of the Aravali | 700-1,000 m, slopes to the north and north-east | Relict Satpura, Vindhyan and Kaimur, metamorphic rocks, Chotanagpur minerals |
| Northeastern Plateau | North-east, detached by the Malda fault | (no figure given in the extract) | Garo, Khasi, Jaintia; coal, uranium; heavy rain |

## Two source flags

1. Both names point to the zone between the Rajmahal hills and the Meghalaya plateau. The physiography chapter ties the fault to the Himalayan origin; the drainage chapter dates the down-thrusting to the mid-Pleistocene. Quote the chapter whose dating you use.
2. The drainage chapter writes 'Potwar Plateau (Delhi Ridge)' for the uplift that divided the Indus and Ganga drainage. The Delhi Ridge belongs to the Aravali extension in the plateau, as the physiography chapter says; the Potwar Plateau is in the north-west of the subcontinent in Pakistan (standard knowledge; verify). Treat the bracket as a source slip (see Day 18 lesson GS-117).

## Cause and effect to keep

Crack in the crust, then a depression, then deposition, then a detached plateau. If an examiner asks why the Meghalaya plateau is not joined to the main block, answer in exactly that chain.

### 8. Master: how the plateau shapes its rivers, and the waterfalls

*Objective:* Explain why Peninsular rivers are older and graded, why the Narmada and Tapi flow west, and place the waterfalls the extracts support.

## Peninsular drainage is older (kegy103 p. 7)

NCERT says the Peninsular drainage is **older** than the Himalayan one. The evidence is broad, largely graded, shallow valleys and the **maturity** of the rivers. The Western Ghats act as the water divide: major rivers drain east to the Bay of Bengal, and small rivulets drain west to the Arabian Sea. Most major Peninsular rivers except the Narmada and Tapi flow from west to east. Peninsular rivers have fixed courses, no meanders and non-perennial flow; the Narmada and Tapi in their rift valleys are exceptions.

## Three events that shaped it

1. **Subsidence of the western flank** of the Peninsula below the sea in the early Tertiary period, which disturbed the symmetrical river plan on either side of the original watershed.
2. **Upheaval of the Himalayas**, when the northern flank of the Peninsular block sank and trough faulting followed. The **Narmada and Tapi** flow in trough faults and fill the cracks with their load, so they lack alluvial and deltaic deposits.
3. **Slight tilt** of the Peninsular block from north-west towards the south-east, which gave the whole drainage its direction towards the Bay of Bengal.

## Narmada and Tapi: the exceptions

- **Narmada**: rises on the western flank of the Amarkantak plateau at about 1,057 m, flows through a rift valley between the Satpura (south) and Vindhyan range (north), forms a gorge in marble rocks and the **Dhuandhar waterfall** near **Jabalpur**, runs about 1,312 km and meets the Arabian Sea south of Bharuch in a broad 27 km estuary.
- **Tapi**: rises at Multai in Betul district of Madhya Pradesh, 724 km long.

*Estuary versus delta.* NCERT says the Narmada meets the sea in a broad 27 km estuary and ties the missing delta to the lack of alluvium in these rivers. The short west-flowing rivers of the western coastal plain also form no delta (kegy102 p. 8).

## Plateau rivers that join the Ganga

The Chambal, Sind, Betwa, Ken and Son rise in the northern Peninsula and belong to the Ganga system (kegy103 p. 7). The Chambal is famous for its ravines, the Son forms a series of waterfalls at the edge of the Amarkantak plateau, and the Damodar flows in a rift valley on the Chotanagpur plateau's eastern margin. These details are in Day 18 lesson GS-117.

## Waterfalls: what is in the extracts

The extracts name only one waterfall: **Dhuandhar** on the Narmada near Jabalpur. Other waterfalls that UPSC uses are **standard knowledge to verify** in an atlas:

| Waterfall | Region (standard; verify) | River (standard; verify) |
|---|---|---|
| Dhuandhar | near Jabalpur, on the Narmada rift (extract); Jabalpur is in the Mahakoshal region, not the Malwa plateau (standard; verify) | Narmada (extract) |
| Hundru | Chotanagpur plateau, near Ranchi | Subarnarekha |
| Gersoppa (Jog) | Western Ghats, Karnataka | Sharavati |

A trap in this table is that the Netravati is a different river of the Western Ghats; do not swap rivers because the region matches.

## Why this matters

Waterfalls cluster where a river falls over the **edge of a resistant plateau** or a fault step. That is why the plateau has so many, and why local gorges (Narmada marble gorge, Chambal) occur though Peninsular valleys are generally shallow.

### 9. Master: resources, soils and the Deccan Trap question

*Objective:* Link plain and plateau to soil, minerals and water, and build a four-dimension frame for the resource potential of the Deccan Trap.

## Same land, different gifts

The structure sets the resources. On the plain, the gift is **soil and water**: fertile alluvium and perennial Himalayan rivers. On the plateau, the gift is **rock and minerals**: ancient rocks and lava plateaus.

| Division | NCERT gift | Linked lessons |
|---|---|---|
| Northern Plain | Fertile alluvium for wheat, rice, sugarcane, jute; large population | Day 22 lesson GS-121 for soils; Day 21 lesson GS-127 for people |
| Peninsular Plateau | Black soil in the west and north-west; mineral reserves in Chotanagpur; coal, iron ore, sillimanite, limestone and uranium in Meghalaya; marble and slate in the Central Highlands | Day 18 lesson GS-124 for minerals |

## What the Deccan Trap is

The **Deccan Trap** is the great basalt lava plateau of the western and central Peninsula, formed when huge flows of lava piled up (Day 18 lesson GS-096 and Day 19 lesson GS-097; NCERT puts the outpouring about 60 million years ago; verify). The extracts for this lesson do not use the term 'Deccan Trap'; they say the western and north-western plateau has an emphatic presence of black soil. Everything below beyond that sentence is **standard knowledge to verify**.

## Resource potentials (frame for the 2022 question)

1. **Soil (physical and economic):** black soil from weathered basalt, rich in clay that holds moisture, the basis of cotton and other dry crops (Day 22 lesson GS-121).
2. **Rock (economic):** basalt as road metal, building stone and aggregate; cut-rock monuments such as the cave temples at Ajanta and Ellora (verify).
3. **Minerals (economic):** laterite caps on higher flows can carry aluminium-rich ore such as bauxite in parts of Maharashtra (Day 18 lesson GS-124; verify).
4. **Water and energy (resource):** scarp-edge rivers, dam sites and hydropower (Day 19 lesson GS-125), wind sites along the Ghats (Day 18 lesson GS-124; verify). Basalt aquifers are thin and patchy, so groundwater is a limit.
5. **Limits and costs (environmental):** quarrying, mining scars, black soil prone to waterlogging and cracking, and a dry rain-shadow tract in the plateau interior behind the Western Ghats (Day 17 lesson GS-116; verify).

Write it in 150 words: one line of definition, three linked potentials, one limit, one way forward.

## Other plateau resources

The **Chotanagpur plateau** has a large reserve of mineral resources (kegy102 p. 7). The **quartzite dykes** offer natural sites for water storage (kegy102 p. 6). The **Meghalaya plateau** carries coal, iron ore, sillimanite, limestone and uranium.

## Plain and plateau together

Several Ganga-system rivers (Chambal, Betwa, Ken, Son) rise on the plateau, and Peninsular rivers carry sediment to the plain, which NCERT gives as one reason for its thick alluvium.

### 10. Exam: the three official questions and the structure-to-feature method

*Objective:* Apply the structure-to-feature method to the three verified questions without treating any reasoning as an official key.

## The method

For any plain or plateau question run three steps.

1. **Structure.** Is it the young Himalayan or plain world, or the old rigid Peninsular world?
2. **Feature.** What does that structure produce: generally deep V-shaped valleys and gorges (young), or generally shallow valleys, relict hills and rift valleys (old)?
3. **Place and source.** Which NCERT page or standard fact fixes the name? Flag what is outside the extracts.

## 2026 Prelims, Peninsular Block features (three statements)

The statements ask whether three things go with the Peninsular Block: submergence of parts of the western coast due to tectonic activity; relict ranges such as the Veliconda and Mahendragiri hills; and deep, V-shaped river valleys formed by fast-flowing rivers. The reasoning from the book: NCERT says some of the western coast of the Peninsula is submerged, and the drainage chapter attributes this to subsidence of the western flank (kegy102 p. 2; kegy103 p. 7). The Veliconda and Mahendragiri hills are named relict and residual mountains (kegy102 p. 3). V-shaped valleys and fast-flowing youthful rivers are NCERT's mark of the Himalayas, while Peninsular valleys are shallow with low gradients. So the analysis supports statements 1 and 2 and not 3. This is analysis, not an official key.

## 2022 Mains, resource potentials of the Deccan Trap (10 marks)

Use the module 9 frame. A good answer uses the dimension words physical, economic and environmental, gives one Indian place per point where you are sure, and ends with a limit and a way forward.

## Exam traps

- 'Average' versus 'maximum' depth of alluvium: choose your wording carefully.
- Bhabar versus Tarai.
- Deep V-shaped valleys put on the wrong division.
- Rivers swapped when the region matches (Netravati for Sharavati).
- Calling the Satpura only a Deccan or only a Central Highlands feature.

## Elimination habits

If a statement says Peninsular river valleys are generally deep, V-shaped and youthful, doubt it: NCERT calls them shallow and graded. But local gorges and falls occur at fault scarps and plateau edges (Narmada marble gorge and Dhuandhar, Son falls, Chambal ravines), so a statement naming one specific Peninsular gorge or fall is not false for that reason. If it gives a relict or residual hill for the Himalaya, doubt it. If a map question asks for a region or river outside the extracts, say plainly what must be verified rather than guessing.

### Notes: Definitions to know cold

- Alluvium: sediment laid by rivers; the material of the Northern Plain.
- Geo-syncline (NCERT): a long sinking trough that fills with sediment; NCERT's account of how the plain began.
- Bhabar: narrow belt (8-10 km) parallel to the Shiwalik foothills where rivers drop boulders and sometimes disappear.
- Tarai: belt about 10-20 km wide south of the Bhabar where streams re-emerge without clear channels, giving marshy conditions.
- Bhangar: old alluvium; Khadar: new alluvium (kegy102 p. 5).
- Relict or residual mountain: worn remnant of an older, larger mountain system (Aravali, Nallamala, Javadi, Veliconda, Palkonda, Mahendragiri).
- Rift valley: a valley dropped between faults; the Narmada, Tapi and Mahanadi rift valleys are named in kegy102 p. 2.
- Tor: rocky knob left by weathering; quartzite dyke: wall-like hard-rock sheet used as a natural water-storage site (kegy102 p. 6).
- Patland plateau (NCERT): one of the flat-topped plateau surfaces that make up the Peninsula, for example Hazaribagh, Palamu, Ranchi, Malwa, Coimbatore and Karnataka (kegy102 p. 6; meaning of 'pat' and state names standard, verify).

### Notes: Numbers to hold (as printed in NCERT)

- Plain: about 3,200 km east to west; width 150-300 km; alluvium 1,000-2,000 m; general elevation 50-150 m.
- Bhabar 8-10 km; Tarai about 10-20 km.
- Plateau: about 150 m above the plains to 600-900 m; Central Highlands 700-1,000 m; Satpura 600-900 m.
- Western Ghats average elevation about 1,500 m; Anaimudi 2,695 m; Dodabetta 2,637 m (difference 58 m).
- Narmada: about 1,312 km; source about 1,057 m; estuary 27 km; Tapi: 724 km.
- Trough development about 64 million years ago (the book's date; verify).

### Notes: Relief to consequence (cause and effect)

- Himalayan rise, then a sagging trough, then sediment from rivers, then the alluvial plain.
- Slope breaks at the foothills, then boulders drop in the Bhabar, then water re-emerges in the Tarai.
- Steep upper Kosi, heavy load, flat plain, blocked course, then a shifted course and floods.
- Plate pushed north-east, then a fault between Rajmahal and Meghalaya, then deposition, then the detached Meghalaya plateau.
- Trough faulting along the Narmada and Tapi, then the cracks fill with detritus, then no deltas and a west-flowing course.
- Tilt of the Peninsular block towards the south-east, then east-flowing river system and eastern deltas.

### Notes: Source notes and slips (checked)

- Alluvial depth: kegy102 p. 3 says 'average depth ... 1,000-2,000 m', p. 5 says 'maximum depth ... 1,000-2,000 m'. Both cannot be right for the same range; quote it as 'about 1,000-2,000 m' and avoid the words average or maximum unless the question uses them.
- Potwar Plateau (Delhi Ridge): kegy103 p. 4 puts the two names together. The Delhi Ridge is the north-western tip of the Aravali, which kegy102 p. 6 counts in the plateau outline; the Potwar Plateau lies in the north-west of the subcontinent in Pakistan (standard; verify). The bracket is a source slip.
- Plateau height: 600-900 m is the general band (kegy102 p. 6), but Central Highlands (700-1,000 m), the Western Ghats (average about 1,500 m) and peaks to 2,695 m exceed it. Never write 'the plateau is nowhere above 900 m'.
- Rift valley and delta: Mahanadi is listed with the rift-valley rivers (kegy102 p. 2) yet has a delta (p. 3). So 'rift valley means no delta' fails for the Mahanadi; the no-delta rule applies to the Narmada and Tapi, which meet the sea in an estuary and on the west coast.
- Malda fault (kegy102 p. 2, tied to the Himalayan origin) and Malda gap (kegy103 p. 4, down-thrusting in the mid-Pleistocene) name the same zone between the Rajmahal hills and the Meghalaya plateau but are dated differently; quote the chapter whose dating you use.
- Dodabetta (text) and Dodabeta (exercise) are the same peak.
- kegy102 p. 3 has a heading 'The Himalayas and other Peninsular mountains' that describes young, weak mountains, while the same page calls the Peninsular hills relict: the heading is usually read as the northern and north-eastern mountains (verify); the hills listed are old remnants.
- The Satpura is described under both the Deccan Plateau and the Central Highlands.
- Youthful features on the Peninsula: NCERT calls Peninsular valleys generally shallow, yet the Narmada forms a marble gorge and the Dhuandhar fall, the Son falls over the Amarkantak edge and the north-west has ravines and gorges. Treat 'no gorges on the plateau' as false; treat 'typically deep V-shaped valleys' as the Himalayan mark.

### Notes: Standard knowledge added (verify)

- Regional names Punjab-Haryana plain, Ganga plain, Brahmaputra plain.
- Older alluvium (Bhangar) sits at the slightly higher level and newer alluvium (Khadar) at the flood level.
- Waterfalls: Hundru on the Subarnarekha in Chotanagpur; Gersoppa (Jog) on the Sharavati in the Western Ghats; Jabalpur (Dhuandhar) lies in the Mahakoshal region, not the Malwa plateau.
- Deccan Trap resources: basalt as building stone, laterite caps with bauxite, wind sites on the Ghats, groundwater limits, cave monuments at Ajanta and Ellora.
- The Cambrian period is about 540 million years ago; the Potwar Plateau's location.
- Patland plateau state names: Hazaribagh, Palamu and Ranchi in Jharkhand; Malwa in western Madhya Pradesh; Coimbatore in Tamil Nadu; the Karnataka plateau.
- Doabs (land between two confluent rivers): Bist-Jalandhar (Beas-Sutlej), Bari (Beas-Ravi), Rechna (Ravi-Chenab), Chaj (Chenab-Jhelum), Sind Sagar (between the Indus and the Jhelum-Chenab); Ganga-Yamuna doab in Uttar Pradesh. Kankar (calcareous nodules) occurs in the Bhangar.
- Wind sites along the Ghats belong to the energy resources of Day 18 lesson GS-124; dam sites and hydropower to Day 19 lesson GS-125.

### Notes: Confused pairs

- Bhabar (rivers vanish, boulders) and Tarai (rivers re-emerge, marsh).
- Bhangar (old) and Khadar (new).
- Deccan Plateau (between the Ghats, south of the Satpura) and Central Highlands (north of the Satpura).
- Fault (a crack with movement) and rift valley (a dropped valley between faults).
- Anaimudi (Anaimalai hills, 2,695 m) and Dodabetta (Nilgiri hills, 2,637 m).
- Estuary (Narmada) and delta (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi).

### Core concepts

- The plain is a sediment-filled trough; the plateau is a rigid ancient block.
- Bhabar, Tarai, Bhangar and Khadar run in that order from the Shiwalik foothills southwards.
- The plateau slopes west to east, and its three groups are the Deccan Plateau, the Central Highlands and the Northeastern Plateau.
- Peninsular rivers are older with shallow valleys; the Narmada and Tapi flow west through rift valleys.
- Black soil marks the western and north-western plateau.

### Practice

**Q.** Name the four zones of the Northern Plain from the Shiwalik foothills southwards.

**A.** Bhabar, Tarai, then the alluvial plains, which split into Bhangar (old) and Khadar (new).

**Q.** What does 'relict mountain' mean? Give two examples from NCERT.

**A.** A worn-down remnant of an older, larger range; for example the Aravali and Nallamala hills.

**Q.** Give the heights of Anaimudi and Dodabetta and name the range of each.

**A.** Anaimudi 2,695 m on the Anaimalai hills; Dodabetta 2,637 m on the Nilgiri hills.

**Q.** What is a geo-syncline in NCERT's account of the plain?

**A.** A sinking trough in front of the rising Himalaya that was gradually filled with sediment by Himalayan and Peninsular rivers.

**Q.** Why are Peninsular valleys shallow and low in gradient while Himalayan valleys are V-shaped?

**A.** The Peninsula is an old, rigid, stable block whose rivers are mature and graded; the Himalayas are young, still rising, and cut by fast youthful rivers.

**Q.** Why does the Bhabar belt lie right beside the foothills?

**A.** The slope breaks where rivers leave the mountain, they lose speed and drop heavy rocks and boulders first.

**Q.** Why do the Narmada and Tapi flow west when most Peninsular rivers flow east?

**A.** They follow trough faults (rift valleys) formed when the northern flank of the Peninsular block sank, while the general tilt of the block guides the other rivers to the Bay of Bengal.

**Q.** How does the Malda fault explain why Meghalaya is detached from the main plateau?

**A.** The fault between the Rajmahal hills and the Meghalaya plateau was later filled by river deposits, leaving the plateau standing apart.

**Q.** A student writes that the Peninsular Plateau is nowhere above 900 m. Correct the statement using NCERT.

**A.** 600-900 m is the general band, but the Central Highlands run to 1,000 m, the Western Ghats average about 1,500 m and Anaimudi reaches 2,695 m.

**Q.** Sketch a north-south cross-section from the Shiwalik hills to the plateau edge and label the zones with the widths in the lesson.

**A.** Shiwalik hills, Bhabar (8-10 km), Tarai (about 10-20 km), Bhangar and Khadar alluvial plain (50-150 m high), then the rising plateau edge.

**Q.** Mark on an outline map the Satpura, Aravali, Rajmahal hills, Nilgiri hills and the Meghalaya plateau, and say which is the Deccan plateau's northern boundary.

**A.** The Satpura is the northern boundary of the Deccan plateau; the Aravali bounds the Central Highlands on the west; Rajmahal is the eastern extension; Nilgiri is where the two Ghats meet; Meghalaya is in the north-east.

### Memory skeleton

- Zones north to south: Bhabar, Tarai, Bhangar, Khadar; Brahmaputra plains and the Dhubri turn
