## GS-274: Pollution: air and AQI

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain Pollution: air and AQI and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Standards first, index second

India notified ambient standards for twelve parameters. Eight of them, including CO, NO2, SO2, PM2.5, PM10, ozone, lead and ammonia, have short-term standards of one to 24 hours, while benzo(a)pyrene, benzene, arsenic and nickel have only annual limits. Because an index is meant to convey air quality almost in real time, the AQI uses the short-term pollutants. Each concentration is mapped to a sub-index through a segmented linear scale. The first two breakpoints are tied to the standard: up to half of the standard counts as good, and up to the standard counts as satisfactory. Higher breakpoints were fixed after reviewing dose-response studies and practice in other countries.

### Why the maximum operator and six bands

CPCB notes that many ways of combining sub-indices suffer from ambiguity and eclipsing, where one high pollutant can be masked by the rest. The Indian design therefore reports the largest sub-index as the AQI. Results fall into six categories, each with a health message: minimal impact when good; minor breathing discomfort to sensitive people when satisfactory; discomfort for people with asthma or heart disease, children and older adults when moderate; and, at severe levels, respiratory effects even in healthy people during light activity. For very poor and severe days the report recommends that regulators curb the sources contributing most, for instance open burning, construction and visibly polluting vehicles.

### NCAP as the policy response

The National Clean Air Programme, part of the Control of Pollution scheme, works through city Clean Air Action Plans with yearly PM10 reduction targets across road dust, waste burning, vehicles, construction and industry. It covers 130 cities across 24 States/UTs and aims for up to a 40% cut in PM10 against the 2019-20 baseline or attainment of the 60 µg/m³ standard by 2025-26. Monitoring runs at 855 stations in these cities, and performance is judged on annual PM10 and AQI. The Swachh Vayu Survekshan ranks cities, with awards on National Swachh Vayu Diwas, 7 September. By 2024-25, 64 cities had reduced PM10 by over 20% from 2017-18 and 25 by over 40%.
