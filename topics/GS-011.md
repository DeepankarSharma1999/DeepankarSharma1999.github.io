## GS-011: Regional kingdoms: Pallava, Chalukya, Chola

Prelims GS I (Mains culture cross-links) · History · Ancient India

*Goal:* Explain Regional kingdoms: Pallava, Chalukya, Chola and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Chalukyas of Badami and hybrid temple styles

Pulakesin I established the western Chalukya kingdom around Badami in 543. Early Chalukyan work was rock-cut, such as the Ravana Phadi cave at Aihole with its Nataraja flanked by the saptamatrikas, and later work was structural. Mixing styles was their signature. At Pattadakal, the Virupaksha temple was built by chief queen Loka Mahadevi in the reign of Vikramaditya II (733–44), and the Papnath temple is an early Dravida example; the Durga temple at Aihole has an apsidal shrine recalling Buddhist chaitya halls. Karnataka is where the hybrid style that some texts call vesara is best seen. By about 750 CE the Rashtrakutas had taken over early western Chalukya control of the Deccan.

### Pallavas of Kanchipuram

Starting out in the Andhra region around the second century CE, the Pallavas later shifted south into Tamil Nadu; their history is clearest for the sixth to eighth centuries, when they left many stone inscriptions and monuments. Mostly Shaivite, they also left Vaishnava shrines and had strong links with South-East Asia. Their early buildings are associated with Mahendravarman I, a contemporary and rival of Pulakesin II. His son Narasimhavarman I (Mamalla) avenged that defeat and launched the Mahabalipuram works. The Shore Temple there probably belongs to Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha, 700–728) and unusually contains three shrines, two to Shiva and one to Vishnu.

### Cholas and exam angles

The Muttaraiyar held the Kaveri delta as Pallava subordinates until Vijayalaya took it in the mid-ninth century. His successors absorbed Pandyan territory to the south and Pallava territory to the north. Rajaraja I, seen as the most powerful Chola ruler, reorganised administration; his son Rajendra I built Gangaikondacholapuram. Delta agriculture rested on embankments, canals, wells and tanks, and Brahmana assemblies such as Uttaramerur's ran committees for irrigation and temples. Prelims pairs to fix: Badami/Aihole/Pattadakal–Chalukya; Mahabalipuram/Kanchipuram–Pallava; Thanjavur/Gangaikondacholapuram–Chola; Ellora Kailashnath–Rashtrakuta. Mains: regional states drew power from irrigated agriculture, temple patronage and rivalry.
