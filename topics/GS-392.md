## GS-392: Conflict of interest and whistleblowing

Mains GS4 · Ethics · Thinkers, administration and probity

*Goal:* Explain Conflict of interest and whistleblowing and use it to reason through the worked example.

### Concept and distinctions

A conflict of interest arises when private interests could improperly influence official duties. An actual biased decision need not be proved before disclosure or recusal is appropriate. Whistleblowing raises concerns about wrongdoing through channels that require careful evidence handling and protection from retaliation.

### How to apply it

Distinguish personal grievance, unverified allegation and documented public-interest concern. Confidential reporting, independent examination and fair procedure protect both the reporter and those accused. Disclosure alone may not remove a conflict if the person still controls the decision. A proportionate response can include recusal, reassignment or restrictions, with recorded reasons. Institutions should make honest reporting possible without treating every accusation as established guilt.

### Worked example

A procurement officer whose relative owns a bidder should disclose the link and avoid evaluating the bid.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why is disclosure sometimes insufficient to manage a conflict?
  - **a:** The person may still influence the decision, so recusal or reassignment may be needed.

### Common trap

Assuming a potential conflict is harmless until corruption is proved.
