## GS-069: Great Depression and interwar politics

Mains GS1 · World History · 18th–20th century

*Goal:* Explain Great Depression and interwar politics and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### From post-war fragility to collapse

The First World War left Britain indebted and facing unemployment, while wheat output expanded overseas and then met revived eastern European supply, creating a glut. The US grew fast in the 1920s through mass production, hire-purchase consumption and a housing boom, and from 1923 became the world's largest overseas lender. Two weaknesses then combined. Farmers answered falling prices by producing more, deepening the glut. And countries financing investment with American loans were exposed when US lenders panicked and cut lending sharply after 1928. In Europe, major banks failed and currencies such as sterling collapsed; in Latin America, commodity prices slumped further; and doubled US import duties dealt world trade another blow.

### Impact in the US and in India

The US was the industrial country worst affected. Banks cut domestic lending and recalled loans, households lost homes and cars, and thousands of banks failed. Output fell by roughly a third and unemployment jumped to a quarter of the workforce. In colonial India, an exporter of farm goods, trade nearly halved and prices plunged, but the government kept revenue demands unchanged. Bengal's jute growers, producing for gunny-bag exports, fell deep into debt. Peasants used up savings, mortgaged land and sold jewellery, making India an exporter of gold, which helped Britain's recovery more than Indian farmers. Rural unrest was high when Gandhi launched civil disobedience at the depression's peak.

### Political and intellectual consequences; exam angles

NCERT stresses that the Depression's effects on society, politics, international relations and people's minds outlasted the modest recovery visible by 1935. Intellectually, the classical assumption that labour and factories would always be fully employed was overturned; Keynes's 1936 book founded macroeconomics. After the war, policymakers drew two lessons: mass production needs mass consumption backed by stable incomes and full employment, which requires government intervention; and governments must be able to manage flows of goods, capital and labour. These ideas shaped the Bretton Woods system. Prelims: 1929 start, 4,000 bank closures, Indian price data. Mains: transmission channels, uneven sectoral impact, rise of the interventionist state.

### Worked example

Question: 'The Great Depression revealed how integrated the world economy had become.' Discuss with reference to India. Frame: (1) Causes: farm glut and dependence on US lending. (2) Transmission: loan withdrawal, bank failures in Europe, US tariff doubling, commodity price collapse. (3) India: trade halved, wheat −50%, jute −60%+, unchanged revenue, gold exports, rural unrest and civil disobedience; urban fixed-income gains and tariff-protected industry. (4) Legacy: Keynes and the state's role in ensuring employment. Conclude on uneven but global impact.

### Recall

- - **q:** What were the two main causes of the Depression according to NCERT?
  - **a:** Agricultural overproduction with falling prices, and dependence on US loans that were suddenly withdrawn.
- - **q:** How did the Depression make India a gold exporter?
  - **a:** Indebted peasants sold jewellery and precious metals to meet expenses.
- - **q:** Which book is linked to the emergence of macroeconomics?
  - **a:** Keynes's The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money (1936).

### Common trap

Assuming the Depression hurt everyone in India equally. Peasants suffered most because revenue demands were not reduced, but urban groups on fixed incomes gained from falling prices, and industrial investment rose under tariff protection.
