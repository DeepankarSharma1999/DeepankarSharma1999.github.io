## GS-267: IUCN categories and species protection

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain IUCN categories and species protection and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### The nine IUCN categories

A 2006 Zoological Survey of India volume explains that IUCN's Red List Categories and Criteria, version 3.1 adopted in 2001, sort taxa into nine categories. Extinct (EX) applies once it is beyond reasonable doubt that the final individual has died; Extinct in the Wild (EW) means a taxon survives only in captivity, cultivation or naturalised populations far from its former range. The threatened tiers are Critically Endangered (CR), Endangered (EN) and Vulnerable (VU), facing extremely high, very high and high risk of dying out in the wild. Near Threatened (NT) taxa are close to qualifying for a threatened tier; Least Concern (LC) covers widespread, abundant taxa; Data Deficient (DD) lacks enough information to judge risk; Not Evaluated (NE) has never been assessed. NCERT's Class 11 text uses an older, simpler trio: endangered, vulnerable and rare.

### Criteria and the scale of threat

Placement in CR, EN or VU rests on five quantitative criteria tied to extinction biology: how fast a population is shrinking, how small it is, how narrow its geographic range is, and how fragmented its population and distribution are. For example, under criterion A a fall of 90 per cent across ten years or three generations (whichever is longer), where the causes are understood, reversible and have ceased, can qualify a taxon as CR. Global data show the scale of loss: the Red List (2004) recorded 784 extinctions over 500 years (338 vertebrates, 359 invertebrates, 87 plants), and more than 15,500 species face extinction, with 32 per cent of amphibians and 31 per cent of gymnosperms threatened against 12 per cent of birds. India launched a National Red List Roadmap and Vision 2025–2030 at the IUCN World Conservation Congress in 2025.

### From risk status to legal protection

A Red List category measures risk; it is not law. India's legal tool is the Wildlife Act of 1972, which protects species listed in its schedules and gives legal backing to protected areas; a 1991 amendment stiffened penalties and covered specified plants. The Asian elephant shows the two layers side by side: MoEFCC's 2025-26 report notes that it is Endangered on the IUCN Red List and, separately, protected in India under Schedule I of the Act. Indian agencies also apply IUCN criteria themselves; the 2023-24 report records 21 new plant taxa assessed as CR, 5 as EN and 3 as VU. For species already near extinction in the wild, ex situ measures such as zoos, seed banks and cryopreservation serve as insurance.

### Worked example

Statement analysis: 'Inclusion in the IUCN Red List automatically bans hunting of a species in India.' Incorrect — hunting bans flow from scheduling under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972; the Red List is a global risk assessment. The Asian elephant is both IUCN Endangered and WPA Schedule I, two separate decisions. Second drill: arrange by decreasing risk — EX, EW, CR, EN, VU, NT, LC; DD and NE sit outside this scale.

### Recall

- - **q:** Define a vulnerable species per NCERT.
  - **a:** One likely to become endangered in the near future if threatening factors continue.
- - **q:** How many extinctions did the 2004 Red List record over 500 years?
  - **a:** 784.
- - **q:** What gives a species legal protection in India?
  - **a:** Listing in the schedules of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972.
- - **q:** Name the three IUCN threatened categories.
  - **a:** Critically Endangered (CR), Endangered (EN), Vulnerable (VU).
- - **q:** What does Data Deficient mean?
  - **a:** Information is inadequate to assess extinction risk.

### Common trap

Assuming an IUCN Red List status is itself a law, or that 'Near Threatened' and 'Data Deficient' are threatened categories: only CR, EN and VU are threatened, and protection in India depends on WPA schedules and habitat protection.
