## GS-276: Waste management and circular economy

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Ecology fundamentals

*Goal:* Explain Waste management and circular economy and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Why some waste persists

Enzymes act on particular substances only, which is why decomposers cannot break down every material. Substances that microbes can degrade are biodegradable; those they cannot, such as many plastics, persist under ordinary conditions and may be inert or may harm organisms. Lifestyle changes, disposable products and new packaging have increased the share of non-biodegradable waste. Local bodies therefore need separate systems for the two streams, and households can reduce the load by measuring what they generate and keeping biodegradable material apart.

### Rule-based management of waste streams

The Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 widened coverage to urban agglomerations, census towns and industrial townships and made segregation at source and doorstep collection compulsory. The E-Waste Rules, 2022 replaced the 2016 version, apply from April 2023 to 106 kinds of electrical and electronic equipment including solar panels, and create an EPR regime. Producers, recyclers, manufacturers and refurbishers register on a CPCB portal. Battery and waste tyre EPR portals work similarly. The End-of-Life Vehicles Rules, 2025 assign producers scrapping targets, and registered scrapping facilities issue EPR certificates based on the steel they recover.

### EPR as the engine of circularity

Government treats the circular economy as a focus area and describes EPR as the tool that operationalises 'Waste to Wealth'. Under EPR, the producer, importer or brand owner who places a product on the market becomes legally responsible for its environmentally sound management after use. Since 2022, market-based EPR has been notified for eight streams, from plastic packaging to construction and demolition waste. Officials credit it with rapid growth of the recycling sector and lower use of virgin materials. Certificates are verified through periodic audits, and a common EPR portal and an electronic trading platform for certificates are being built.
