## GS-327: Poverty reduction programmes

Mains GS2 · Social Justice · Rights, welfare and services

*Goal:* Compare pathways out of poverty and explain why programmes must connect.

### Income support and productive capacity

Poverty includes inadequate consumption and deprivation in services and opportunities. Transfers can stabilize consumption; public works can provide earnings and create assets; livelihoods programmes can strengthen skills, finance and collective organization. Health and education services reduce risks that keep people poor. These instruments address different constraints and work best when their timing and design complement one another.

### Measure lasting change

India’s historical MGNREGA design illustrates a demand-based employment guarantee rather than discretionary relief. SHG-based livelihoods illustrate collective capability building. For any current programme, check the operative law and guidelines for eligibility and entitlements. Evaluate timely payment, access for vulnerable households, useful assets and resilience after support ends. A beneficiary count alone cannot show sustained poverty reduction.

### Worked example

A household receives work income during a lean season and invests in repairing irrigation. Reliable employment support protects consumption; a useful community asset can also reduce vulnerability in later agricultural seasons.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why distinguish relief from poverty reduction?
  - **a:** Relief addresses an immediate shock; lasting reduction requires lower vulnerability and stronger capabilities.
- - **q:** What makes targeting incomplete?
  - **a:** Eligible households may be missing or newly vulnerable.

### Common trap

Do not assume every renamed or revised programme retains earlier legal entitlements unchanged.
