## GS-246: Social-sector expenditures and outcomes

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Growth, public finance and infrastructure

*Goal:* Explain Social-sector expenditures and outcomes and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Why the state spends on social sectors

NCERT gives three reasons for public involvement in education and health. These services create benefits for society, not just for the user. Choices made in them are hard to undo, since a child left in a poor school suffers lasting harm before parents can react. Users also lack full information on quality and cost, which gives providers power to exploit them. So government must both provide services and regulate private providers. Poverty adds a fourth reason: many households cannot afford basic schooling or care, and treating them as rights obliges free provision for the poor and the socially disadvantaged. NCERT also shows large gaps between states in per capita public spending on elementary education, which translate into unequal opportunity.

### How much is spent and on what

The Economic Survey 2025-26 reports that general government (Centre and states combined) social services expenditure has risen since FY22, growing at a compound annual rate of 12 per cent to FY26 BE, with education at 11 per cent and health at 8 per cent a year. Social services are broad: besides education and health, they include water and sanitation, housing, urban development, welfare of disadvantaged groups, labour welfare, social security, nutrition and disaster relief. The Survey links this spending to coverage gains recorded in MoSPI's SDG progress report: social protection now covers 64.3 per cent of people (2025) versus 22 per cent in 2016, rural use of improved drinking water at 99.6 per cent (2024-25) and universal household electrification (2021-22).

### From inputs to outcomes

Enrolment gains do not guarantee learning, so the Survey stresses learning outcomes as the next priority. National Achievement Surveys conducted by NCERT since 2001 have been succeeded by PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan; its 2024 round found Grade III proficiency rebounding to 65 per cent in mathematics and 57 per cent in language, with state government schools doing well in foundational learning. The Survey recommends moving beyond aggregate scores to diagnose conceptual gaps, strengthening teacher training institutions, real-time monitoring through Vidya Samiksha Kendras and community participation to raise accountability. The same input-to-outcome test can be applied to health spending. Good answers measure both money spent and change achieved.
