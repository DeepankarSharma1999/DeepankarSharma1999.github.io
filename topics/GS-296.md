## GS-296: Basic chemistry in everyday life

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Science · General science / technology

*Goal:* Explain Basic chemistry in everyday life and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### pH in body and environment

Living organisms tolerate only a narrow pH range; the human body functions between pH 7.0 and 7.8. Rain becomes acid rain when its pH falls below 5.6, and such rain flowing into rivers lowers their pH and endangers aquatic life. The stomach produces hydrochloric acid for digestion; excess acid causes indigestion, relieved by mild bases called antacids such as magnesium hydroxide (milk of magnesia). In the mouth, bacteria break down sugars into acids, and once pH falls below 5.5 the enamel begins to corrode, which is why basic toothpastes help. A bee sting is acidic and is soothed by baking soda; nettle hairs inject methanoic acid.

### Chemicals made from common salt

Passing electricity through brine produces sodium hydroxide near the cathode, chlorine at the anode and hydrogen at the cathode, hence the name chlor-alkali process. Chlorine reacting with dry slaked lime gives bleaching powder, used to bleach textiles and paper pulp, as an oxidising agent, and to disinfect drinking water. Baking soda, sodium hydrogencarbonate, is a mild non-corrosive base; heated with an acid it releases carbon dioxide, which makes cakes rise, and it is used in antacids and soda-acid fire extinguishers. Heating baking soda gives sodium carbonate, which on recrystallisation forms washing soda.

### Combustion and pollution

Carbon and most carbon compounds burn in oxygen to give carbon dioxide, water, heat and light. Saturated hydrocarbons usually burn with a clean flame, while unsaturated compounds produce a yellow, smoky flame; even saturated fuels give soot when air is limited, which is why stoves have air inlets for a clean blue flame and why blackened vessels signal wasted fuel. Coal and petroleum contain some nitrogen and sulphur, so their combustion forms oxides of sulphur and nitrogen, which NCERT lists as major environmental pollutants. A flame forms only when gaseous substances burn, which is why charcoal can glow without a flame.

### Worked example

Why do toothpastes tend to be basic? Because mouth bacteria produce acids from food residue and enamel corrodes below pH 5.5; a basic paste neutralises excess acid. Similar logic explains antacids (milk of magnesia) and baking soda on bee stings.

### Recall

- - **q:** Below what pH is rain called acid rain?
  - **a:** 5.6.
- - **q:** What are the three products of the chlor-alkali process?
  - **a:** Sodium hydroxide, chlorine and hydrogen.
- - **q:** Why do blackened vessel bottoms indicate fuel waste?
  - **a:** Blocked air holes cause incomplete combustion, producing soot.

### Common trap

Thinking a lower pH number means a weaker acid; lower pH indicates higher acidity.
