## GS-241: MSMEs, industrial policy and PLI

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Economy · Growth, public finance and infrastructure

*Goal:* Explain MSMEs, industrial policy and PLI and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### The planned-era design

At independence, Indian industrialists lacked capital and markets were small, so the state took the lead, aiming to control the economy's commanding heights. The 1956 Industrial Policy Resolution reserved some industries for the state, let the private sector supplement the state in a second group, and left the rest to private firms, all under licensing. Licences were easier to obtain in backward areas, which promoted regional balance, and even expansion needed permission. Small-scale units, seen as labour-intensive, were protected by reserving products for them and by cheaper credit and lower excise. NCERT records the cost: big houses used licences to block rivals, protected firms neglected quality, and loss-making public units survived on public funds.

### PLI: paying for performance

The Production Linked Incentive scheme, launched in 2020 under the Aatmanirbhar Bharat vision, now covers 14 sectors with an outlay of ₹1.97 lakh crore. Its stated aims are to attract investment and advanced technology, reach economies of scale and make Indian manufacturers globally competitive. Up to September 2025 the Survey reports actual investment above ₹2.0 lakh crore, incremental production or sales above ₹18.70 lakh crore, over 12.60 lakh direct and indirect jobs, and exports above ₹8.20 lakh crore, led by electronics, pharmaceuticals and telecom. Incentives disbursed total ₹23,946 crore across 12 sectors. Budget 2025-26 announced a National Manufacturing Mission to complement PLI and integrate MSMEs into value chains.

### MSMEs: from protection to finance

The Survey calls MSMEs the backbone of industry and the second-largest employer after agriculture, with over 7.47 crore enterprises reporting more than 32 crore workers on the Udyam portal (January 2026). Their binding constraint is formal credit: limited collateral and documentation mean 27 per cent of MSMEs name finance as their biggest obstacle, according to the World Bank's 2025 Financial Sector Assessment. Policy responses include revised classification thresholds from April 2025 that widened priority-sector lending eligibility, a revamped credit guarantee scheme whose ceiling was raised to ₹10 crore from April 2025, equity support through the Self-Reliant India Fund, and PMEGP margin-money subsidies. For Mains, contrast this finance-led approach with the old reservation model, which shielded small units from large firms.
