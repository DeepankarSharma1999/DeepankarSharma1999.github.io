## GS-365: Human values and value formation

Mains GS4 · Ethics · Ethics and human interface

*Goal:* Explain Human values and value formation and use it to reason through the worked example.

### Concept and distinctions

Values are relatively enduring beliefs about what matters; virtues concern cultivated qualities of character. Values can conflict: efficiency, equality, loyalty and liberty may support different choices. Ethical reasoning explains priorities in context rather than merely listing desirable words.

### How to apply it

Value formation occurs through experience, reflection, socialization and institutional practice. Rewarding only speed can weaken fairness if difficult cases are neglected; encouraging reasoned feedback can reinforce responsibility. Values also require behavioural evidence: declaring integrity has little meaning without consistent decisions under pressure. Recognize the difference between a personal preference and a value justified for public administration through constitutional commitments and citizens' legitimate interests.

### Worked example

A service office rewarding quick disposal alone may encourage rejection of complex but deserving applications.

### Recall

- - **q:** Why must declared values be tested against conduct?
  - **a:** A value matters administratively through consistent choices and incentives, especially when following it is costly.

### Common trap

Treating a list of virtues as a complete ethical argument.
