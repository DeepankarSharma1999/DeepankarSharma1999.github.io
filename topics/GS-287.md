## GS-287: Desertification and UNCCD

Prelims GS1 + Mains GS3 · Environment · Climate and treaties

*Goal:* Explain Desertification and UNCCD and apply the distinction in the worked example.

### Defining desertification and LDN

Article 1 of the UNCCD describes desertification as degradation of land in dry sub-humid, semi-arid and arid areas, caused by several factors including climatic variations and human activities. These drylands are areas outside polar and sub-polar regions where annual precipitation divided by potential evapotranspiration lies between 0.05 and 0.65. Land degradation here means reduced or lost biological or economic productivity of rainfed or irrigated cropland, range, pasture, forest or woodland, through processes such as wind and water erosion, deterioration of soil properties and long-term loss of natural vegetation. Combating it means preventing or reducing degradation, rehabilitating land that is partly degraded and reclaiming land already desertified. The IPCC, citing the UNCCD (2020), describes Land Degradation Neutrality as a state in which the quantity and quality of land resources that sustain ecosystem services and food security hold steady or grow.

### India's UNCCD machinery and natural variability

India ratified the UNCCD on 17 December 1996. MoEFCC's desertification work coordinates restoration of degraded land, LDN and SDG target 15.3, and supplies land degradation data for the national State of Environment report. The Desertification Cell has been merged into the National Afforestation and Eco-Development Board, which now handles LDN, drought and UNCCD matters and is piloting Bonn Challenge reporting in Haryana, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Nagaland and Karnataka. On scale, NITI Aayog's SDG India Index 2023-24 reports that roughly 27.77 per cent of the country's land was degraded in 2015-16, ranging from 3.15 per cent in Punjab to 52.69 per cent in Rajasthan, and that the desertified area grew by about 1.5 per cent by 2018-19. NCERT adds a caution: dry and wet phases alternate naturally. The Rajasthan desert was wet and cool around 8,000 BC, and the Sahel suffered a devastating drought from 1967 to 1977, so a drought by itself is not proof of degradation.

### Recent action: Aravallis, Jodhpur and CRIC-23

The Aravalli Green Wall initiative plans to green a five-kilometre buffer around the Aravalli hills across Rajasthan, Gujarat, Haryana and parts of Delhi-NCR; its Detailed Action Plan was released in mid-2025 (MoEFCC reports both a May release at Udaipur and a June launch) for delivery through line departments anchored in state forest departments. On the 2025 World Day to Combat Desertification and Drought, a national workshop at ICFRE-AFRI, Jodhpur released the revised Green India Mission document and launched the National Afforestation Monitoring System. At CRIC-23 in Panama City in December 2025, the mid-term review of the UNCCD's 2018-2030 Strategic Framework discussed LDN monitoring under PRAIS, drought resilience, early warning and sand and dust storms.
