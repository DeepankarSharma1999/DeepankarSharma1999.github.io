### Bridge: Bridge: goods and services, and the five classes of work

A **good** is a thing you can hold, such as wheat or a shirt. A **service** is work done for you, such as teaching, medical care, transport or a haircut. Economists sort work into classes. **Primary** activities take from nature (farming, mining, fishing). **Secondary** activities make goods from raw material (a mill, a factory). **Tertiary** activities provide services and move or sell goods (trade, transport, teaching, banking). NCERT adds two knowledge-based classes inside the service world: **quaternary** (collecting, producing and spreading information, research and development) and **quinary** (the top level of decision making and policy).

NCERT says the main difference between secondary and tertiary activity is that services depend more on the specialised skill, experience and knowledge of the worker than on machines and factory processes.

Try it: a farmer grows rice (primary), a mill polishes it (secondary), a shopkeeper sells it (tertiary), a researcher studies yield data (quaternary) and a ministry fixes a policy on rice (quinary).

### 1. Orientation: services on one page

*Objective:* Say what tertiary activities are, why they grow with development, and what decides where they are located.

## What this topic is about

When you visit a doctor, learn from a teacher, buy books from a shop or travel by bus, you use a **service**. NCERT Class XI (chapter 6) says tertiary activities belong to the service sector and are special skills offered in exchange for payment: health, education, law, governance and recreation need professional skills, theory and training. Most are done by skilled labour, trained experts and consultants.

NCERT adds that tertiary activities include both **production** (the provision of services that are consumed, measured indirectly by wages and salaries) and **exchange** (trade, transport and communication that overcome distance). They are not directly involved in processing physical raw material. Examples named: plumber, electrician, technician, launderer, barber, shopkeeper, driver, cashier, teacher, doctor, lawyer and publisher.

## Why the sector grows

In the early stages of development most people work in the primary sector. In a developed economy most workers are in tertiary activity, with a moderate share in the secondary sector. In more developed countries a higher share of workers is in services than in less developed ones, and employment in services is rising while it is unchanged or falling in primary and secondary activity (NCERT).

## The location question in one line

A factory goes to its raw material or its market. A service goes to **people who can pay, routes that bring customers and skilled workers, and a place where the cost of running it is acceptable**. That one sentence is the lesson's spine. Each module adds an NCERT statement for one kind of service.

### 2. Foundation: the words and the sector ladder

*Objective:* Define the words of the chapter and place any job on the primary-to-quinary ladder.

## Words to own

- **Tertiary activity:** a service, such as trade, transport, communication, teaching, health care or tourism.
- **Quaternary activity:** collection, production and spread of information, research and development; an advanced form of service involving specialised knowledge and technical skill (NCERT).
- **Quinary activity:** the highest level of decision makers and policy makers; creation, re-arrangement and interpretation of new and existing ideas, data interpretation and evaluation of new technology (NCERT).
- **Retail trade:** sale of goods directly to the consumer. **Wholesale trade:** bulk business through intermediaries and supply houses.
- **Node:** meeting point of two or more routes, or a point of origin or destination. **Link:** a route joining two nodes.
- **Low-order service:** common and widespread, such as grocery shops and laundries. **High-order service:** specialised, such as accountants, consultants and physicians (NCERT).
- **CBD:** Central Business District, the commercial heart of a city (NCERT uses the abbreviation).
- **Outsourcing:** giving work to an outside agency to improve efficiency and reduce cost. **Off-shoring:** outsourcing to overseas locations.
- **BPO:** business process outsourcing. **KPO:** knowledge processing outsourcing; it involves highly skilled workers and is information-driven.
- **Digital divide:** the unequal spread of benefits from information and communication technology (ICT) between and within countries.

## The sector ladder

| Class | What it does | Example in NCERT or its context |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Takes from nature | Farming, hunting (the chapter's exercise) |
| Secondary | Makes goods | Weaving, iron smelting, making garments |
| Tertiary | Services, trade, transport, communication | Trading, shopkeeping, driving, barbering (teaching and medical care overlap; see below) |
| Quaternary | Information, research | Software developers, statisticians, tax consultants, mutual fund managers |
| Quinary | Top decisions and policy | Senior executives, government officials, research scientists |

NCERT places quaternary and quinary activities as a knowledge-oriented segment of the service sector, and calls quinary activities 'another subdivision of the tertiary sector'. NCERT's exercise treats university teaching as quaternary.

## The ladder is not a wall

Many jobs sit between rungs. NCERT itself overlaps: it names teachers, doctors and lawyers as tertiary workers, yet lists staff of schools, universities and hospitals under quaternary. Working rule (analysis): routine provision of a service is tertiary; knowledge- or information-based professional work is also counted as quaternary; top-level decision making is quinary; in Prelims follow the stem's own framing. Use the NCERT reason: ask whether the work is mainly **provision of a service**, **handling information**, or **top-level decision making**.

## Collar colours

The chapter prints a prompt asking the nature of work for red, gold, white, grey, blue and pink collars. The text explains only 'gold collar' (quinary, highly paid senior professions). The other colours are standard classroom terms and are given in the Master notes with a verify flag.

## Why the classes matter for location

Primary work is tied to land and resources; secondary work to raw material, power and markets; tertiary work to customers and routes; quaternary activities, in NCERT's words, are not tied to resources, not affected by the environment, and not necessarily localised by market (NCERT says this of quaternary activities; quinary work is similar by extension). This shift from place-bound to footloose work is the logic behind the later modules.

### 3. Core: trade and commerce, markets and retail

*Objective:* Describe rural and urban marketing centres, periodic markets, retail and wholesale trade and the location logic of each.

## What trade is

Trade is the buying and selling of items produced elsewhere. NCERT says all services in retail and wholesale trading are intended for profit, and towns and cities where this happens are **trading centres**. Trade rose from local barter to money exchange on an international scale, producing trading centres and institutions that are collection and distribution points.

## Rural and urban marketing centres

- **Rural marketing centres** serve nearby settlements. They are quasi-urban, the most rudimentary trading centres, where personal and professional services are not well developed. They are local collecting and distributing centres with *mandis* (wholesale markets) and retail areas, meeting the goods most often demanded by rural people.
- **Periodic markets** appear where there are no regular markets. They are held weekly or bi-weekly on fixed dates and move from place to place, so shopkeepers stay busy on all days while a large area is served. This is the location solution for a thin, scattered demand: the market comes to the people in turn.
- **Urban marketing centres** offer ordinary goods and also specialised goods and services: markets for labour, housing and semi-finished or finished products, and professionals such as teachers, lawyers, consultants, physicians, dentists and veterinary doctors.

## Retail trade

Retail is sale directly to consumers. Most takes place in fixed stores. **Non-store retail** includes street peddling, handcarts, trucks, door-to-door, mail-order, telephone, vending machines and the internet. NCERT notes: consumer cooperatives were the first large-scale retailing innovation; **departmental stores** delegate purchase and sale to department heads; **chain stores** buy most economically, even direct manufacture to specification, employ skilled specialists and can experiment in one store and apply the result to many.

## Wholesale trade

Wholesale is bulk business through numerous intermediary merchants and supply houses. Some large stores, including chains, buy directly from manufacturers, but most retail stores buy from an intermediary. Wholesalers often give credit, so retailers may operate largely on the wholesaler's capital.

## Location logic (a synthesis)

| Kind of trade | Where it locates (from the NCERT statements) |
|---|---|
| Rural marketing centre | Among nearby settlements; serves frequent, simple demand |
| Periodic market | Where regular markets are absent; moves among places on fixed days |
| Urban retail | In towns and cities where a large, varied and paying population lives |
| Wholesale and mandi | At collection and distribution points; many intermediaries |
| Non-store retail | Anywhere with a delivery link or internet; not tied to a shop site |

Standard concepts, not in the extracts (verify): a service needs a minimum number of customers (a *threshold*) and customers will travel only a limited distance (a *range*). High-order services need larger thresholds, so they appear in larger centres. This matches NCERT's remark that urban centres offer many specialised services.

### 4. Core: transport and communication as services

*Objective:* State how transport networks and communication are located and how communication became independent of transport.

## Transport as a service

NCERT (chapter 6) defines transport as a service by which people, materials and goods are physically carried from one location to another, an organised industry created to satisfy the need for mobility. At every stage the value of the material is significantly enhanced by transport. Distance can be measured in km, time or cost, and the choice of mode depends on time or cost distance (see Day 20 lesson GS-126, bridge module on nodes and links).

**Networks and accessibility.** As transport develops, places link up in a network of nodes and links. A developed network has many links, so places are well connected: accessibility is high.

## What decides the demand and routes

- **Demand** for transport rises with the size of the population.
- **Routes** depend on the location of cities, towns, villages, industrial centres and raw materials, the pattern of trade between them, the nature of the landscape, the climate, and the funds available to overcome obstacles.

This is the transport location rule: lines join places that trade with each other, and are built where terrain and funds allow. The Class XII chapter gives the Indian outcome: railway density is lower in hill states, the north-east, central India and Rajasthan, and rural-road density is low in hilly, plateau and forested areas. Class XII also asks why Bengaluru and Hyderabad in the south and Delhi, Kanpur and Patna in the north have emerged as important nodes, but does not answer it; a reasoned answer is the junction of many routes plus large markets (analysis).

## Communication

Communication is the transmission of words, messages, facts and ideas. Writing made it depend on transport: messages were carried by hand, animals, boat, road, rail and air, so all forms of transport are called lines of communication. Where the transport network is efficient, communications spread easily. **Mobile telephony and satellites have made communication independent of transport**, but not fully, because older systems are cheap: very large volumes of mail still go through post offices.

- **Telecommunications:** the time reduced from weeks to minutes; mobile telephony made communication direct and instantaneous (Class XI, chapter 6).
- **Mass media:** radio, television and newspapers reach vast audiences; in India radio broadcasting began in 1923 and was changed to All India Radio in 1936; television began in Delhi in 1959 (Class XII).
- **World cases (Class XI, chapter 7):** the telephone was a critical factor in the urbanisation of America, because firms centralised in city headquarters and put branch offices in smaller towns; optic fibre cables carry large quantities of data rapidly and securely; via satellite it costs the same to communicate over 500 km as over 5,000 km.
- **Satellites in India:** the Indian National Satellite System (INSAT, 1983) and the Indian Remote Sensing Satellite System; the National Remote Sensing Centre is at Hyderabad (Class XII).

## The location lesson

When cost does not rise with distance, a service that uses only communication can sit anywhere. That is why later modules show quaternary work and outsourcing leaving the resource centres.

### 5. Core: services at different levels

*Objective:* Classify services as low-order or high-order, personal, professional or public, and explain where each locates.

## Levels and customers

NCERT says services occur at many levels: some are geared to industry, some to people, and some to both, for example the transport systems. **Low-order services** such as grocery shops and laundries are more common and widespread than **high-order services**, the more specialised ones such as those of accountants, consultants and physicians. Services are provided to individual consumers who can afford to pay. NCERT also distinguishes physical labour (gardener, launderer, barber) from mental labour (teacher, lawyer, physician, musician).

## Public services

Many services have been regulated. Making and maintaining highways and bridges, fire-fighting departments, supplying or supervising education and customer care are often supervised or performed by governments or companies. State and Union legislation has set up corporations to supervise the marketing of transport, telecommunication, energy and water supply. Location of public services follows the settlement they serve and the administrative hierarchy (analysis, standard; verify).

## Professional services

These are primarily health care, engineering, law and management. They need skilled people and a market that can pay, so they gather in larger centres where urban markets exist (module 3).

## Recreational and entertainment services

NCERT says the location of recreational and entertainment services depends on the market, and gives a clear contrast. Multiplexes and restaurants might find a location within or near the CBD, whereas a golf course would choose a site where land costs are lower than in the CBD. The factor is **land cost against customer access**: a service that needs many customers pays for a central site; a service that needs a lot of land goes where land is cheap.

## Personal services

Personal services help people in their daily work. Workers migrate from rural areas in search of employment, are unskilled and are employed in domestic service as housekeepers, cooks and gardeners; this segment is generally unorganised. The Indian example is Mumbai's dabbawala (tiffin) service, quoted as serving about 1,75,000 customers across the city (as printed; no year given). A dabbawala service works because dense housing, offices and rail routes bring many customers close together (analysis).

## Table: level, example, typical place

| Level | Example (NCERT) | Typical location (synthesis) |
|---|---|---|
| Low order | Grocery shop, laundry | Everywhere, close to customers |
| High order | Accountant, consultant, physician | Larger towns and cities |
| Recreational | Multiplex, restaurant | In or near the CBD |
| Land-hungry | Golf course | Where land is cheaper than in the CBD |
| Personal | Housekeeper, cook, gardener | Where affluent households and migrant workers meet |
| Public | Highways, fire services, education | Follows population and administration |

## A caution

The table is a synthesis. NCERT states the individual facts; the third column joins them. Mark any additional example as standard knowledge (verify).

### 6. Core: tourism and medical tourism

*Objective:* State what tourism is, the four attractions NCERT names, the tourist regions it lists, and how medical tourism and home-stays locate.

## What tourism is

NCERT: tourism is travel undertaken for recreation rather than business. It is described as the world's single largest tertiary activity in total registered jobs (250 million). The same sentence prints total revenue as '40 per cent of the total GDP', which cannot be a share of the whole world's output; treat the revenue figure as a probable slip (check) and do not quote it. Standard estimates put travel and tourism at roughly a tenth of world GDP (verify; do not quote without a source). Many local people provide accommodation, meals, transport, entertainment and special shops; tourism supports infrastructure industries, retail trading and craft industries (souvenirs). In some regions tourism is seasonal because vacations depend on weather, but many regions attract visitors all year.

## Tourist regions named by NCERT

The warmer places around the Mediterranean Coast and the West Coast of India; winter-sports regions, mainly in mountainous areas; scenic landscapes and national parks; and historic towns with monuments, heritage sites and cultural activities.

## The four attractions (the location factors of tourism)

| Factor | NCERT statement | Example in the text |
|---|---|---|
| Climate | People from colder regions expect warm, sunny weather for beach holidays; the Mediterranean offers higher temperatures, long sunshine and low rain in the peak season; winter holidays need warmth or snow for skiing | Southern Europe and the Mediterranean lands; ski slopes in Switzerland (figure) |
| Landscape | Attractive environment: mountains, lakes, spectacular coasts and landscapes not altered by people | Scenic regions and national parks |
| History and art | Ancient or picturesque towns, archaeological sites, castles, palaces and churches | Historic towns |
| Culture and economy | Ethnic and local customs; a region that serves tourists cheaply becomes popular | Home-stays: heritage homes in Goa, Madikere and Coorg in Karnataka |

Note on the spelling: the extract prints 'Madikere'; the usual spelling is Madikeri, the main town of Kodagu (Coorg), so the two names point to one region (verify).

## Medical tourism

When medical treatment is combined with international tourism it is called medical tourism. NCERT (chapter 6) says about 55,000 patients from the U.S.A. visited India in 2005 (as in the book; a dated figure; verify), that India has emerged as the leading country of medical tourism in the world (as printed; verify), and that world-class hospitals in metropolitan cities cater to patients from many countries. It names India, Thailand, Singapore and Malaysia as developing countries that benefit. Beyond medical tourism is the outsourcing of medical tests and data interpretation: hospitals in India, Switzerland and Australia perform services ranging from reading radiology images to interpreting Magnetic Resonance Images (MRIs) and ultrasound tests. Outsourcing holds advantages for patients if it improves quality or provides specialised care.

## Why this is a location lesson

Tourism locates where the attraction is, so the supply is tied to place (unlike footloose work). Medical tourism locates where specialist hospitals and low cost meet in a city with air links (analysis). The location of the visitor's accommodation, food and transport services follows the attraction.

### 7. Core: quaternary and quinary work and outsourcing

*Objective:* Explain quaternary and quinary activities, outsourcing, BPO, KPO and off-shoring, and the factors that put them in a place.

## The knowledge segment

NCERT: a CEO of a multinational in Copenhagen or New York and a medical transcriptionist in Bangalore (the book's spelling) both work in a knowledge-oriented segment of the service sector, divided into quaternary and quinary activities.

**Quaternary** activities involve collecting, producing and spreading information, and centre on research and development. NCERT says the quaternary sector, along with the tertiary sector, has replaced most primary and secondary employment as the basis for economic growth, and that over half of all workers in developed economies are in the knowledge sector (as printed). Examples include mutual fund managers, tax consultants, software developers and statisticians, and staff of offices, schools, universities, hospitals, theatres, accounting and brokerage firms. NCERT says quaternary activities can be outsourced and are not tied to resources, not affected by the environment, and not necessarily localised by market.

**Quinary** activities are performed by the highest-level decision makers and policy makers: creation, re-arrangement and interpretation of ideas, data interpretation and evaluation of new technology. Often called 'gold collar' professions, they cover senior business executives, government officials, research scientists, and financial and legal consultants. Their importance in advanced economies far outweighs their numbers.

## Financial services

NCERT names mutual fund managers, brokerage firms, the banking sector (under KPO) and financial consultants (quinary) but gives no location rule. Standard logic (verify): financial services cluster in a CBD where regulators, headquarters, skilled staff and fast connectivity meet, and gain from agglomeration; Mumbai is India's financial centre, and London and New York are world centres.

## Outsourcing and off-shoring

Outsourcing, or contracting out, is giving work to an outside agency to improve efficiency and reduce costs. When the work moves overseas it is **off-shoring**. Outsourced activities include information technology, human resources, customer support, call-centre services and at times manufacturing and engineering.

## Why outsourcing goes where it goes

NCERT gives the factors:
- **Cheap and skilled workers.** Data processing is an IT-related service easily carried out in Asian, East European and African countries where IT-skilled staff with good English are available at lower wages than in developed countries.
- **Low overhead costs.** Overheads are much lower, so job-work abroad is profitable, even in a less populous country such as Botswana.
- **Comparative advantage.** This is the main reason for continuing outsourcing.

Example from the text: a company in Hyderabad or Manila does a project based on GIS techniques for a country such as the U.S.A. or Japan. Call centres have opened in India, China, Eastern Europe, Israel, the Philippines and Costa Rica.

## BPO and KPO

KPO differs from BPO because it involves highly skilled workers and is information-driven knowledge outsourcing. Examples of KPO: research and development, e-learning, business research, intellectual property research, the legal profession and the banking sector. 'Home shoring' is a newer alternative to outsourcing. NCERT also says KPO enables companies to create additional business opportunities, which is why a statement that BPOs have better opportunities than KPOs does not fit the text (analysis, not an official key).

## Effects the book notes

Outsourcing creates new jobs in receiving countries, and as work becomes available, out-migration from these countries may fall. Outsourcing countries face resistance from job-seeking youth in their own countries.

## Location lesson

These activities are footloose: they go where skilled English-speaking labour is cheap, communication lines are good and (analysis, not NCERT) the firm can supervise. The factor list is gathered in the table in module 9.

### 8. Master: the digital divide and the IT cities of India

*Objective:* Define the digital divide and organise the socio-economic implications of IT industries in Indian cities into a balanced answer, with extract facts separated from analysis.

## The digital divide

NCERT: opportunities from information and communication technology (ICT) are unevenly distributed. How quickly a country can provide ICT access and benefits to its citizens is the deciding factor. Developed countries have surged forward and developing countries have lagged: this is the **digital divide**. It also exists inside countries: in a large country like India or Russia, metropolitan centres have better connectivity and access than peripheral rural areas.

## What the extracts say about Internet spread (Class XI, chapter 7)

Internet users: fewer than 50 million in 1995, about 400 million in 2000, over two billion in 2010 and about 5.4 billion in 2023 (as printed; verify). The U.S.A.'s share dropped from 66 per cent in 1995 to 25 in 2005, and a shift has occurred to developing countries. Check: 5.4 billion divided by 400 million is 13.5 times the 2000 value (a rough growth, not a forecast). Cyberspace expands the economic and social space through e-mail, e-commerce, e-learning and e-governance.

## The Indian cities in the extracts

The extracts name Bangalore (medical transcriptionist), Hyderabad (GIS work for the U.S.A. or Japan), Mumbai (the dabbawala service) and the world-class hospitals in metropolitan cities; Class XII asks why Bengaluru and Hyderabad in the South and Delhi, Kanpur and Patna in the North have emerged as important nodes. Beyond this, the sector's size, jobs and exports are outside the extracts. Standard list of major IT and IT-enabled-services cities (verify): Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, the National Capital Region (Gurugram, Noida), Mumbai and Kolkata. Standard location factors (verify): skilled graduates and technical institutions; telecom bandwidth, power and air links; state policy and SEZ or STPI incentives; agglomeration of firms and suppliers. Give no statistics.

## Socio-economic implications of IT growth in a city (frame for the 2021 PYQ)

| Dimension | Implication (analysis; extract support where it exists) |
|---|---|
| Economic | Skilled jobs, higher incomes, a multiplier on local services (retail, transport, food), export of services; NCERT: outsourcing creates new jobs and comparative advantage |
| Spatial | Growth of office clusters and suburbs, pressure on land, housing, roads and metro rail (module 4 on nodes) |
| Social | In-migration of young workers; rise of personal services such as domestic work, often unorganised (NCERT module 5); changes in lifestyle and consumption |
| Inequality | Gap between high-paid IT workers and informal service workers; digital divide between city and rural hinterland |
| Environmental | Higher demand for water and power, waste, traffic and air pollution (analysis; verify with a city source) |
| Policy | Skill training, affordable housing, public transport, digital inclusion |

## A balanced answer in four steps

1. **Define** the sector (IT and IT-enabled services such as BPO and KPO, module 7).
2. **Benefits:** jobs, income, services multiplier, global links.
3. **Costs:** housing and land stress, congestion, inequality, dependence on foreign demand (NCERT notes resistance in outsourcing countries; verify the Indian side).
4. **Way forward:** planned cities, transport, skills and rural digital access.

## Caution

Do not quote statistics such as employment counts or export values unless you have a verified current source. Strong answers use mechanisms, one extract fact and one named city.

## The verified PYQ (2021 Mains GS I, 15 marks)

'What are the main socio-economic implications arising out of the development of IT industries in major cities of India?'

Directive: 'What are the main ... implications' asks for a list of effects with reasons, not a history of IT.

**Frame (about 250 words):** two lines of definition (IT and IT-enabled services; BPO and KPO); economic effects; spatial and urban effects; social effects including migration and unorganised services; inequality and the digital divide; environmental stress; a way forward. Use the table in module 8 as the skeleton. Add one extract fact (NCERT outsourcing creates new jobs; digital divide between metropolitan and rural areas) and mark Indian statistics as verify.

## Prelims traps

1. **Classification traps.** NCERT counts university teaching and hospital staff as quaternary in one place and teachers and doctors as tertiary in another, so apply the overlap rule in module 2 and follow the stem; a barber is tertiary; a call centre is tertiary but the BPO and KPO split follows skill.
2. **Superlatives.** 'Tourism is the largest ...' and 'India is the leading country in medical tourism' are as printed and dated; do not treat as a verified fact.
3. **Outsourcing words.** Off-shoring is overseas outsourcing, not all outsourcing.
4. **Only/all statements.** Statements saying services are 'tied to resources' contradict NCERT for quaternary work.

## An elimination method

Ask: (a) is the activity a service or a good; (b) does it process raw material; (c) does it handle information; (d) does it make top-level decisions? Then apply the location factor.

### Notes: Definitions to know cold

- Tertiary activities: services such as trade, transport, communication, health, education, law and recreation, offered in exchange for payment (NCERT Class XI).
- Quaternary activities: collection, production and spread of information; research and development; an advanced form of service needing specialised knowledge and technical skill.
- Quinary activities: the highest-level decision making and policy making; 'gold collar' professions.
- Retail trade: sale of goods directly to consumers. Wholesale trade: bulk business through intermediaries.
- Node: a meeting point, origin or destination of routes; link: a route joining two nodes.
- Outsourcing: giving work to an outside agency to improve efficiency and reduce cost; off-shoring: outsourcing overseas.
- BPO: business process outsourcing; KPO: knowledge processing outsourcing involving highly skilled workers.
- Digital divide: uneven access to the benefits of information and communication technology, between and within countries.
- Medical tourism: medical treatment combined with international tourism.

### Notes: The collar colours (standard; verify)

- NCERT explains only the gold collar (quinary, senior professionals). The other colours are standard classroom terms; check them in a standard source before using them in an answer.

### Notes: Source notes and slips (checked)

- Tourism revenue printed as 40 per cent of total GDP: implausible as a world share; treated as a probable slip (check).
- Class XI exercise (vi) asks which statement is not true and offers 'BPOs have better business opportunities as compared to KPOs'; NCERT says KPO enables companies to create additional business opportunities, so the exercise is answerable from the text (analysis, not an official key).
- Class XI exercise (iii) asks which sector provides most employment in Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata; the text supports services as the majority sector in developed economies, and does not give city shares; no figure is stated here.
- 'Madikere' is the book's spelling for the town usually spelt Madikeri, the main town of Kodagu (Coorg), so 'Madikere and Coorg' names one region twice (verify).

### Notes: Confused pairs

- Tertiary versus quaternary: provision of a service versus handling information and research.
- Quaternary versus quinary: information work versus top-level decision making.
- Outsourcing versus off-shoring: any contracting-out versus overseas contracting-out.
- BPO versus KPO: business process work versus highly skilled, information-driven knowledge work.
- Low-order versus high-order services: common and widespread versus specialised and larger-centre.
- Rural marketing centre versus periodic market: fixed rudimentary centres versus moving markets on fixed days.
- Medical tourism versus outsourcing of medical tests: patients travel versus data and images travel.
- Where NCERT overlaps: teachers, doctors and lawyers are tertiary workers (p1), yet staff of schools, universities and hospitals are listed under quaternary (p7). Rule (analysis): routine service is tertiary; knowledge-based professional work is also counted as quaternary; top decisions are quinary; follow the stem.

### Answer Lab facts

- **F-NCERT-TR-PERIODIC (verified):** NCERT: periodic markets in rural areas are found where there are no regular markets, and are organised at different temporal intervals, such as weekly or bi-weekly, drawing people from the surrounding areas.

  Source: [NCERT Class XI, Fundamentals of Human Geography, ch. 6 Tertiary and Quaternary Activities (legy106), 2026-27 reprint](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/legy106.pdf)
- **F-NCERT-TR-TOURISM (verified):** NCERT: the Mediterranean climate offers almost consistently higher temperatures than other parts of Europe, long hours of sunshine and low rainfall throughout the peak holiday season, which is a main reason for tourism in Southern Europe; historic towns attract tourists for monuments, heritage sites and cultural activities.
  Source: [NCERT Class XI, Fundamentals of Human Geography, ch. 6 Tertiary and Quaternary Activities (legy106), 2026-27 reprint](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/legy106.pdf)
- **F-NCERT-TR-HOMESTAY (verified):** NCERT: if a region provides for the needs of tourists at a cheap cost it is likely to become very popular, and home-stay has emerged as a profitable business, such as heritage homes in Goa, Madikere and Coorg in Karnataka.
  Source: [NCERT Class XI, Fundamentals of Human Geography, ch. 6 Tertiary and Quaternary Activities (legy106), 2026-27 reprint](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/legy106.pdf)
- **F-NCERT-TR-QUATFOOTLOOSE (verified):** NCERT: quaternary activities can be outsourced like some tertiary functions, and they are not tied to resources, affected by the environment, or necessarily localised by market.
  Source: [NCERT Class XI, Fundamentals of Human Geography, ch. 6 Tertiary and Quaternary Activities (legy106), 2026-27 reprint](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/legy106.pdf)
- **F-NCERT-TR-GOLDCOLLAR (verified):** NCERT: quinary activities focus on the creation, re-arrangement and interpretation of ideas, data interpretation and evaluation of new technologies; often called 'gold collar' professions, they represent special, highly paid skills of senior executives, government officials, research scientists, and financial and legal consultants.

- **F-NCERT-TR-OUTSOURCE (verified):** NCERT: data processing is an IT-related service that can be carried out in countries where IT-skilled staff with good English are available at lower wages than in developed countries, and overhead costs are also much lower; comparative advantage is the main reason for continuing outsourcing.
