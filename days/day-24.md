#### Checks

**Q.** Day 17, GS-116 (Western and Eastern Ghats): Give two differences between the Ghats.

**A.** Western: higher, continuous, source of most Peninsular rivers. Eastern: low, discontinuous, cut by large rivers.

**Q.** Day 17, GS-130 (Coastal processes and island groups): What is accretion?

**A.** Expansion of a coastal area through an increase in the width or length of the coastline.

**Q.** Day 17, PH-207 (Production and detection of linearly/circularly polarised light): State the two ingredients for producing circular light from natural light.

**A.** A polariser and a quarter-wave plate whose axis is at 45 degrees to the polariser's direction.

**Q.** Day 17, GS-102 (Coastal landforms and ocean processes), Memory Card GS-102-C8: Who proposed the subsidence and the glacial-control theories of coral reefs, and when?

**A.** Darwin (1842): subsidence turns fringing to barrier to atoll. Daly (1910): glacial-control sea-level rise. Leong: a combination fits best.

**Q.** Day 17, PH-057 (Circular aperture and Airy pattern), Memory Card PH-057-C3: Rayleigh limit for a telescope: assumptions, and the condition imposed?

**A.** Two equal, mutually incoherent point sources, so intensities add; small angles. Star 2's centre is placed on star 1's first dark ring.

**Q.** Day 17, CT-008 (Number system and divisibility): What is the expected value of a blind guess among four options at 2.5 marks with a 0.833 penalty?

**A.** 0.625 - 0.625 = 0.

**Q.** Day 18, GS-096 (Earth interior, rock cycle and minerals): Name the direct sources of information about the interior.

**A.** Mining, deep drilling and volcanic eruptions.

**Q.** Day 18, GS-110 (World climatic regions and biomes): Why are hot deserts found at 15 to 30 degrees on western sides of continents?

**A.** The sub-tropical high gives sinking air and no rain; cold currents on west coasts add fog but not rain.

**Q.** Day 18, PH-051 (Michelson interferometer): Why do the fringes fade periodically for sodium light?

**A.** The two D lines make fringe patterns that drift in and out of step; the visibility is V = |cos(pi x delta lambda / lambda_mean^2)|, where x = 2d is the path difference, so it repeats every Delta d = lambda_mean^2/(2 delta lambda) of mirror travel.

**Q.** Day 18, GS-124 (Minerals and energy resources), Memory Card GS-124-C1: How does NCERT classify minerals? Give examples of each class.

**A.** Metallic: ferrous (iron ore, manganese) and non-ferrous (copper, bauxite). Non-metallic: organic fuels (coal, petroleum) and inorganic (mica, limestone).

**Q.** Day 18, PH-208 (Step-index versus parabolic-index fibre pulse dispersion), Memory Card PH-208-C4: Parabolic delay: final spread, its small-Δ form, and the exact ratio to step index?

**A.** Marginal ray y = Δ′: δt = L(n1 − n2)²/(2c n2) ≈ n1LΔ²/(2c), Δ = (n1 − n2)/n1. Step : parabolic = 2/Δ exactly.

**Q.** Day 18, WR-029 (Précis: essential ideas and suitable title): What does the English paper's precis task say about length and wording?

**A.** Write about one-third of the printed length, in your own words (or own language).

**Q.** Day 19, GS-118 (Lakes, wetlands and water bodies): What makes a rift valley lake?

**A.** Water collects in a trough formed by the sinking of land between two parallel faults.

**Q.** Day 19, GS-132 (Location-based map practice: neighbouring countries): Why does 30 degrees of latitude exceed 30 degrees of longitude in distance for India?

**A.** Meridians converge towards the poles, so a degree of longitude covers less distance away from the equator, whereas a degree of latitude is the same everywhere.

**Q.** Day 19, PH-059 (Zone plates and Fresnel circular aperture): Why does a screen with a finite source start its bright-dark sequence at n_inf = r^2/(a lambda), the zone count for a screen at infinity, and not at n = 0?

**A.** A real screen distance needs 1/b > 0, so n = (r^2/lambda)(1/a + 1/b) is at least r^2/(a lambda).

**Q.** Day 19, GS-104 (Pressure belts and planetary winds), Memory Card GS-104-C10: How does the ITCZ shift through the year?

**A.** North in the northern summer, south in the northern winter, following the sun.

**Q.** Day 19, PH-052 (Multiple-beam interference), Memory Card PH-052-C5: Half-width and finesse: the half-maximum condition, the full width, and the finesse?

**A.** F sin²(ε/2) = 1, so sin(ε/2) = 1/√F (offset only); FWHM = 4 arcsin(1/√F) ≈ 2(1−R)/√R; ℱ = 2π/FWHM ≈ π√R/(1−R).

**Q.** Day 19, CT-009 (Fractions, decimals, LCM and HCF): Three signals take 25, 39 and 60 s from green to red with equal red and green times. What cycle lengths go into the LCM?

**A.** Full cycles of 50, 78 and 120 s.

**Q.** Day 20, GS-098 (Earthquakes and volcanoes): Name the five kinds of earthquake.

**A.** Tectonic, volcanic, collapse, explosion and reservoir-induced.

**Q.** Day 20, GS-112 (World physiography and major straits/seas): Why are continental shelves rich fishing grounds?

**A.** Shallow water lets sunlight reach plankton on which fish feed.

**Q.** Day 20, PH-046 (Two-lens systems): Why does a positive F not guarantee a real image focus?

**A.** F is measured from H2; the focus is at F beyond H2, which can still be to the left of lens 2 (virtual), as in the 2024 PYQ numerical worked in PH-046 module 4.

**Q.** Day 20, GS-126 (Major ports, transport routes and corridors), Memory Card GS-126-C5: Give the distance between the rails for each Indian gauge and the total route length (2019-20).

**A.** Broad 1.676 m, metre 1 m, narrow 0.762 m or 0.610 m; together 67,956 km, broad gauge about 94 per cent.

**Q.** Day 20, PH-067 (Ruby and He-Ne lasers), Memory Card PH-067-C2: Ruby laser: what are the active centres and the host, and what three things do the chromium ions do?

**A.** Cr³⁺ (about 0.05% by weight) in inert Al₂O₃. They absorb green and blue pump light, provide the metastable pair E₂, and emit 6943 Å.

**Q.** Day 20, CT-010 (Percentages and successive percentage): Give the two steps to find a whole from 75% being 9000.

**A.** Divide by 0.75 to get 12000; test: 75% of 12000 is 9000.

**Q.** Day 21, GS-120 (Rainfall regions and climatic hazards): What are the very high landslide zones?

**A.** Himalaya, Andaman and Nicobar, Western Ghats and Nilgiris, north-east and areas with earthquakes and heavy construction.

**Q.** Day 21, GS-099 (Weathering, erosion and mass wasting): Why do Western Ghats slopes see landslides despite hard rock?

**A.** Steep cliffs and escarpments, strong mechanical weathering and heavy short rains.

**Q.** Day 21, PH-061 (Double refraction and quarter-wave plate): Why does the e-ray walk off the wave normal?

**A.** The wave surface is an ellipsoid, so the line from the origin to the tangency point is not perpendicular to the tangent wave front; equivalently S is not parallel to s because D and E are not parallel. (Along or perpendicular to the optic axis there is no walk-off.)

**Q.** Day 21, GS-113 (India relief and physiographic divisions), Memory Card GS-113-C2: Name India's six physiographic divisions.

**A.** Northern and North-eastern Mountains; Northern Plain; Peninsular Plateau; Indian Desert; Coastal Plains; Islands.

**Q.** Day 21, PH-054 (Fraunhofer single slit), Memory Card PH-054-C2: Integral method: which path term is dropped (the Fraunhofer step), when is that allowed, and what does the integral give?

**A.** Drop z² cos²θ/(2r); allowed when πa²/(4λr) ≪ 1 or a lens is used. The integral is a sin α/α, so I₀ = K²a².

**Q.** Day 22, GS-107 (Tropical cyclones and temperate storms): How does a temperate cyclone end?

**A.** Occlusion: the cold front overtakes the warm front and lifts the warm air completely.

**Q.** Day 22, GS-121 (Soil types and soil distribution): Name the three dangers to soil once vegetation is cleared.

**A.** Deterioration, desiccation and accelerated erosion.

**Q.** Day 22, PH-048 (Young double slit and coherence): What limits the number of fringes seen with white light?

**A.** Its coherence length is about 1 µm, so only orders up to about 2 have good contrast; coloured fringes persist a little further.

**Q.** Day 22, GS-100 (Fluvial landforms and river cycles), Memory Card GS-100-C3: Match each drainage pattern to its setting: dendritic, trellis, radial, centripetal.

**A.** Dendritic: tree-like, uniform rock. Trellis: right-angle tributaries, alternating hard and soft belts. Radial: outwards from a dome or cone. Centripetal: converging on a depression.

**Q.** Day 22, PH-069 (Three-level laser scheme), Memory Card PH-069-C7: In the book, which of ruby, Nd:YAG, He-Ne and CO₂ work on a three-level scheme and which on a four-level scheme?): Memory Card PH-069-C7: Which of ruby, Nd:YAG, He-Ne and CO2 lasers work on a three-level scheme and which on a four-level scheme?

**A.** Ruby (Cr³⁺ in Al₂O₃, 6943 Å, flash pumped) is three-level. Nd:YAG, He-Ne and CO₂ are four-level.

**Q.** Day 22, CT-011 (Ratio, proportion and variation): When is a guess worth it?

**A.** When at least one option is eliminated with confidence: if the removed option is surely wrong, three are left and the expected value is (1/3)(2.5) - (2/3)(0.833) = +0.278 marks; if the elimination may itself be wrong, the gain shrinks (Day 12, CT-006).

**Q.** Day 23, GS-129 (Industrial belts and location factors): Name the three refinery sites in Leong.

**A.** In or near the oilfield, at the port of export, at the port of import.

**Q.** Day 23, GS-108 (Ocean currents and upwelling): Name NCERT's four primary forces on ocean currents.

**A.** Heating by solar energy, wind, gravity and the Coriolis force.

**Q.** Day 23, PH-063 (Fibre optics, numerical aperture and attenuation): Convert 0.5 dB/km to km⁻¹.

**A.** 0.5/4.343 = 0.1151 km⁻¹.

**Q.** Day 23, GS-122 (Natural vegetation and forest belts), Memory Card GS-122-C5: At what height does NCERT put the Himalayan pine forests, and where does deodar mainly grow?

**A.** Pine, with commercial chir pine, at 1,500-1,750 m. Deodar, a valued endemic with durable wood, grows mainly in the western Himalaya.

**Q.** Day 23, PH-056 (Diffraction grating and resolving power), Memory Card PH-056-C4: Angular dispersion of a grating: the step, the result, and which grating quantity sets it?

**A.** Differentiate d sin θ = nλ: dθ/dλ = n/(d cos θ). Set by the order and line spacing d, not the total lines.

#### Checks

**Q.** How is each planetary wind named, and which way does the north-east trade wind travel?

**A.** A wind is named by the direction it comes from; the trade winds are north-easterly in the north and south-easterly in the south, so the north-east trade travels towards the south-west.

**Q.** What happens to crust at the three kinds of plate boundary?

**A.** Diverging: crust is created. Converging: crust is destroyed (continent-continent collisions crumple instead). Transform: crust is neither produced nor destroyed.

**Q.** Why do the first two grating orders stay apart while the third overlaps the second?

**A.** Order n runs from 400n to 700n nm: 700 is below 800, so orders 1 and 2 are apart, but 3rd-order violet-blue (400-467 nm) falls on 2nd-order red (600-700 nm).

**Q.** What approximation gives the gap under the lens in the Newton's rings geometry?

**A.** R^2 = r^2 + (R - t)^2, so for a thin gap t is approximately r^2/2R.
