### Module 0: Week 2 checkpoint: what is different this time

*20 minutes · lesson*

See why Day 16 is a checkpoint again, how it differs from Day 8, and how to use the recall tools you now have.

#### Why a second checkpoint

Day 16 is the second weekly checkpoint. The roadmap leaves it free of new topics so that Days 9 to 15 can be tested, repaired and filed. It also gives a first chance to test week 1 again after a gap of about a week. That delayed check is the important new feature: a fact you can recall on Day 16 after learning it on Day 2 has been retrieved over a longer interval than anything on Day 8, and that is the kind of memory the examination needs. Nothing you do today marks any topic mastered. Mastery stages move only on recorded evidence, and a delayed-retention stage needs a successful check at least seven study days after earlier evidence.

#### Use the recall tools, not rereading

Three tools you may not yet have used properly. First, every lesson has a 'Lock it in' tab with a blank-page recall prompt, a short skeleton to reproduce, and look-alike pairs to keep apart. Second, the Memory Cards screen schedules short cards on a 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 day pattern; a miss repeats in the same session. Third, many lessons show a hand-drawable diagram inside the module that teaches the idea. Today, use them in this order: attempt from memory, check, then repair. Rereading a lesson module is the last resort, not the first move.

#### What the roadmap asks for today

The roadmap lists the Day 16 drill as map drills with geographical questions, two Mains answers and one Physics derivation. Modules 3 and 4 below give you those. Module 1 is closed-book retrieval of the week's lessons, module 2 re-tests week 1 after the gap, and module 5 turns the misses into a short, scheduled repair plan. The roadmap also lists geographical PYQs; no verified geographical PYQ set is attached to this checkpoint yet, so the map drill stands in for it. Today's modules add up to about 330 minutes, so use the cut rule in module 5 if time runs short. The aim is a record of what you can and cannot bring back and a plan, never a score. Treat any score as information about this week only, never as a prediction of the examination.

#### Checks

**Q.** What is new about the Day 16 checkpoint compared with Day 8?

**A.** It re-tests week 1 after a gap of about a week (delayed retrieval) as well as week 2, and it asks you to use the Lock it in tab, Memory Cards and the lesson diagrams.

**Q.** In what order do you use the tools when a recall attempt fails?

**A.** Attempt from memory, check against the lesson, repair the cause, and reread the module only as a last resort.

### Module 1: Week 2 closed-book retrieval

*90 minutes · lesson*

Retrieve the main facts, distinctions and methods from Days 9 to 15 before looking at any answer.

#### How to run the retrieval set

Below the text you will find questions taken from the active-recall notes of the lessons for Days 9 to 15: modern Indian history, Physics, and one CSAT or writing item per day where the day has one. For each question, cover the answer, write or say your answer in full, and only then reveal it. If you cannot start, write the first thing you do remember; a partial answer is far more useful than a blank. Mark each as correct, partly correct or missed. Work in blocks of twenty-five minutes with short breaks, and stop when the block ends rather than when the list ends.

#### What to do with the misses

For every missed or partial question write one line naming the cause: never learned, forgot, confused two similar things, misread, or slow. Then act on the cause. If you never learned it, return to the lesson module that teaches it. If you forgot it, add it to the Memory Cards or revision queue. If you confused two ideas, rebuild the comparison from memory and check it against the lesson. If you misread, rewrite the question in your own words. If you were slow, repeat that type of question later under a timer. Keep the repair list short enough that you will actually do it.

#### Checks

**Q.** Day 9, GS-052 (Women, peasants, Dalits and regional contributions): Give two differences between 19th-century and post-1918 peasant movements.

**A.** Earlier: local, economic, limited aims, no long-term organisation; later: linked with Congress and nationalism through Kisan Sabhas, no-rent campaigns and the AIKS.

**Q.** Day 9, GS-031 (Tribal and peasant movements): How did peasant movements change after 1857?

**A.** Peasants became the main force with economic, limited, local aims against immediate enemies; colonialism was not the target; legal awareness grew.

**Q.** Day 9, PH-028 (Lorentz transformations): When can the order of two events be reversed by a change of frame?

**A.** Only when they are spacelike separated (|Δx| > c|Δt|).

**Q.** Day 9, WR-014 (अपठित गद्यांश: तर्क और निष्कर्ष): दो-भाग वाले प्रश्न में क्या सावधानी रखें?

**A.** दोनों भागों का अलग-अलग उत्तर दें।

**Q.** Day 10, GS-025 (Arrival of European trading companies): Why did the French company lose?

**A.** State control, shortage of funds, European wars, one base, inferior sea power.

**Q.** Day 10, GS-039 (Revolutionary movements in India and abroad): Name the first-phase revolutionary centres abroad.

**A.** London (India House), Paris and Geneva, Berlin, and the US-Canada west coast (Ghadr).

**Q.** Day 10, PH-029 (Time dilation and length contraction): Give the Δt₀ and L₀ frames for a 10 km atmosphere and a muon's 2.2 μs mean life.

**A.** The 10 km is a proper length in the Earth frame; the 2.2 μs is a proper time in the muon frame.

**Q.** Day 10, CT-005 (Fact versus opinion): What are the three questions of the verifiability test, in order?

**A.** Time (is it about the future?), check (can records or observation of the past or present settle it?), disagreement (could informed people differ on the same evidence without a factual error?).

**Q.** Day 11, GS-047 (Government of India Act 1935): What replaced dyarchy in the provinces?

**A.** Provincial autonomy with responsible ministers, subject to the Governor's safeguards.

**Q.** Day 11, GS-033 (Administrative changes after 1858): What was the Aitchison three-fold classification?

**A.** Imperial, Provincial and Subordinate civil services.

**Q.** Day 11, PH-037 (Forced oscillations and resonance): What is the phase of the current relative to the emf at LCR resonance?

**A.** Zero: in phase, analogous to velocity resonance.

**Q.** Day 11, WR-028 (Comprehension: grammar, argument and tone): Why is 'proves' risky when the passage says 'concluded'?

**A.** It moves the claim up the hedge ladder and misreports the author.

**Q.** Day 12, GS-027 (Plassey, Buxar and East India Company rise): What did Shah Alam II grant at Allahabad?

**A.** The diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa, for Rs 26 lakh a year.

**Q.** Day 12, GS-041 (Gandhi in Champaran, Kheda and Ahmedabad): Who organised the Kheda villages?

**A.** Vallabhbhai Patel with Narahari Parikh, Mohanlal Pandya and Ravi Shankar Vyas.

**Q.** Day 12, PH-024 (Poiseuille equation): Why is the viscous dissipation zero on the tube's axis?

**A.** Dissipation per volume is η(dv/dr)² and dv/dr = 0 on the axis by symmetry.

**Q.** Day 12, CT-006 (Elimination in close options): Why must elimination be reliable for a guess to pay?

**A.** If r is the chance the removed option was not the key, p = r / (options left); with one of four removed p must exceed 0.25, so r must exceed 0.75.

**Q.** Day 13, GS-035 (Press, education and nationalist consciousness): Who was the first Indian journalist jailed?

**A.** Surendranath Banerjea in 1883.

**Q.** Day 13, GS-049 (INA and Subhas Chandra Bose): What did Gandhi say of the RIN mutiny?

**A.** That it was badly advised and they should have awaited their leaders.

**Q.** Day 13, PH-032 (Mass energy relation and decay applications): What is conserved in Compton scattering?

**A.** Total energy and total momentum of photon plus electron.

**Q.** Day 14, GS-043 (Non-Cooperation and Khilafat movements): What was the Awadh Kisan Sabha?

**A.** A peasant body formed in October 1920 against eviction, illegal levies and begar in Awadh (GS-031).

**Q.** Day 14, GS-029 (Land revenue systems: Permanent, Ryotwari, Mahalwari): Why did the Company move from Hastings' farming to a permanent settlement?

**A.** Farming led to extortion, over-promised sums and corruption and left the country impoverished; Francis recommended permanence in 1776 and Cornwallis fixed it in 1793 to secure regular revenue and encourage improvement.

**Q.** Day 14, PH-040 (Pulses and wave packets): Why must a localised pulse contain many wave numbers?

**A.** Waves of a single k fill all space; localisation needs waves that cancel outside the pulse, which requires different k.

**Q.** Day 14, CT-007 (Timed mixed passage practice): When do you answer a dash item (an item marked '-' on the first pass) at the end?

**A.** Only if at least one option has a named defect and your honest chance of being right exceeds 25 per cent.

**Q.** Day 15, GS-051 (Partition, Cabinet Mission and Independence Act): What did the Independence Act provide?

**A.** Two dominions from 15 August 1947, a Governor-General each, constituent assemblies as legislatures, and the 1935 Act as the interim framework.

**Q.** Day 15, GS-037 (Extremist politics and Swadeshi movement): What are the four tenets of the Extremist school?

**A.** Hatred for foreign rule, swaraj as the goal, direct political action, and belief in the masses with readiness for sacrifice.

**Q.** Day 15, PH-027 (Michelson–Morley experiment): State two reasons the apparatus was floated on mercury and rotated.

**A.** To rotate it smoothly without strain, and because only the change of fringe position on rotation is meaningful.

### Module 2: Delayed check: week 1 after a gap

*30 minutes · lesson*

Re-test items from Days 1 to 7, which you first studied at least nine days ago, and see what survived the gap.

#### Why the gap matters

These questions come from the lessons of Days 1 to 7 and are different from the ones you answered on Day 8. They test memory after a longer interval. If you answer them easily, the earlier recall work is holding. If you miss many, that is useful information and not a failure: it tells you which week 1 ideas need a spaced card, a diagram redraw or a second pass. Answer closed-book first and mark each as correct, partly correct or missed. Do not reread week 1 before this module.

#### Recording the result

Write the count of correct, partly correct and missed items in your error log as one line for today, then list the misses with their cause. Where Day 8 or your Memory Cards tested the same idea, a delayed-check miss of something you answered correctly then is a forgetting problem, so the repair is a spaced card, not a new explanation; a miss of something you also missed then is a learning problem, so the repair is to return to the module. If neither tested it, treat the miss as a learning problem.

#### Checks

**Q.** Day 1, GS-179 (Legislative procedure and types of Bills): What happens if the President returns a Bill and both Houses pass it again with changes?

**A.** The President must give assent; the veto is only suspensive.

**Q.** Day 2, PH-009 (Two-body problem and reduced mass): Why can a collision not change ½ M V²?

**A.** V is the CM velocity, and with no external force it is unchanged by internal forces during the collision.

**Q.** Day 3, GS-195 (Panchayati Raj: 73rd Amendment): Who can remove the State Election Commissioner?

**A.** Only in the manner and on the grounds for removal of a High Court judge, so not at the Governor's will.

**Q.** Day 4, PH-004 (Conservation of angular momentum): State and prove that a central force conserves angular momentum.

**A.** F = f(r) r-hat; tau = r x F = (f/r)(r x r) = 0; dL/dt = tau = 0.

**Q.** Day 5, GS-169 (Union and state territories): State the two conditions in Article 3.

**A.** Prior recommendation of the President; reference to the State legislature for its views.

**Q.** Day 6, PH-020 (Precession, spinning top and gyroscope): Which angular momenta are conserved for the heavy top?

**A.** p_φ = L·ẑ and p_ψ = I₃ω₃ (and the energy E); not p_θ or the vector L.

**Q.** Day 7, GS-185 (Constitutional offices and appointment process): How did the 89th Amendment change the SC commission?

**A.** It split the combined commission into the National Commission for SCs (Article 338) and the National Commission for STs (Article 338-A).

### Module 3: Map, timeline and diagram drill

*60 minutes · lesson*

Place the week's key events on a rough map, rebuild the national-movement timeline from memory and redraw four lesson diagrams.

#### Rough map drill (about 20 minutes)

On a blank sheet draw a rough outline of India; accuracy of the outline does not matter, relative position does. Mark each place with its number and event, then check yourself against the lessons. (1) Plassey (West Bengal, 1757) and Buxar (Bihar, 1764). (2) Arcot (Tamil Nadu, 1751) in the Carnatic Wars and Seringapatam (Karnataka, 1799) in the Mysore Wars. (3) The main centres of 1857: Meerut, Kanpur, Lucknow and Jhansi (Uttar Pradesh) and Delhi (the National Capital Territory). (4) The three early satyagrahas: Champaran (Bihar, 1917), Ahmedabad and Kheda (Gujarat, 1918). (5) The Salt March from Sabarmati to Dandi (Gujarat, 1930), and Chauri Chaura (Uttar Pradesh, February 1922), whose violence led the Congress Working Committee at Bardoli to suspend the Non-Cooperation Movement. (6) Amritsar (Punjab), where Jallianwala Bagh took place in 1919. (7) Surat (Gujarat), where the Congress split in 1907. Mark the state that contains each place, not only the place.

Circle every year you got wrong and every event you left out; those become Memory Cards. Remember that Spectrum has date slips in some places and the lessons flag the corrected dates, so check the lesson, not your first instinct.

#### Diagram redraw (about 20 minutes)

Open each lesson module below, study the figure for one minute, close it, and redraw it in under a minute. Then check against the lesson. (1) 'Causes of the Revolt of 1857', GS-032 module 4. ### Module 4: Two Mains answers and one derivation

*90 minutes · lesson*

Write two timed Mains answers by hand and one timed derivation, then check them against the lesson frameworks.

#### The two Mains questions

Write both answers by hand on paper against the clock, without notes. First: 'Neither first, nor national, nor a war of independence.' Critically examine the nature of the Revolt of 1857. (15 marks, about 250 words, suggested 11 minutes; lesson GS-032). Second: Why did Gandhi choose salt as the theme of the Civil Disobedience Movement? Analyse the Salt March as a political strategy. (10 marks, about 150 words, suggested 7 minutes; lesson GS-045). After writing, open each lesson's Practice tab and compare your structure with its framework: which parts did you include, which did you miss, and where did the answer lose its argument? Do not rewrite the answer; list the missing parts in your error log.

#### The derivation

On paper, without notes: Derive Δt = γΔt₀ using a light clock. Timed-solution target (practice target, not an official allotment): 10 marks about 12 minutes, 15 marks about 18 minutes. Then check each step against the lesson's derivation in PH-029. A derivation is repaired only when you can reproduce it a few days later, so add the step you lost to a Memory Card and plan a retry.

#### Checks

**Q.** What do you do with the gaps you find after writing a Mains answer?

**A.** List the missing framework parts in the error log and plan a retry; do not just reread or rewrite the answer.

### Module 5: Error log, repair plan and week 3 plan

*40 minutes · lesson*

Turn this checkpoint's misses into a short, scheduled repair list, retest Day 8 repairs and decide what carries into the next week.

#### Close the Day 8 loop first

Open the error log from Day 8. For each repair you planned then, find out whether a retest has succeeded. An error is repaired only when a later retest succeeds, not when you have reread the answer. Mark each old entry repaired, still open, or cut on purpose. Entries that are still open and appeared again today move to the top of this week's list.

#### Plan the coming week

List the misses from modules 1 to 4, rank them by how often the idea is likely to matter and how badly you missed it, and pick the top few. For each, write the smallest repair that fixes the cause: a five-minute recall card, a comparison from memory, a redrawn diagram, or one timed question of the same type. Assign each repair to a specific day this coming week and put it in the revision queue instead of in your head. Cut the rest on purpose and note that you cut them. If your total time today ran over, that is information: record which module took longest and shorten the next checkpoint's drill, not your sleep.

#### Checks
