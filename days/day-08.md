### Module 0: How a weekly checkpoint works

*25 minutes · lesson*

Understand why Day 8 contains no new topics and how to use it to turn week 1 reading into something you can retrieve.

#### Why there is no new topic today

Day 8 is the first weekly checkpoint. The canonical roadmap deliberately leaves it free of new topics so that the reading of Days 1 to 7 can be tested, repaired and filed. Seven days of reading feels like progress, but reading alone earns no mastery stage in this system. Today is where you find out what you can actually bring back from memory, and where you decide what to repair. Nothing you do today marks any topic mastered. Stages move only on evidence from the practice and retrieval tools, and the delayed-retention stage needs a check at least seven study days after earlier evidence.

#### The four jobs of a checkpoint day

1. **Retrieve:** answer closed-book questions drawn from the week's lessons, then compare with the answer.
2. **Diagnose:** for every miss, name the cause: never learned, learned but forgot, confused two similar things, misread the question, or slow.
3. **Repair:** schedule a targeted fix for each cause; do not simply reread the whole lesson.
4. **Plan:** decide what carries into next week, what is cut, and what the revision queue should hold. Do not move every missed item to tomorrow; choose.

A good checkpoint feels uncomfortable. A checkpoint where everything is answered easily has probably tested recognition, not recall.

#### Using the standard Day 8 drill

The roadmap lists the Day 8 drill as a timeline and short Mains answers, ten to twenty multiple-choice questions and one Physics numerical. Use that alongside the retrieval set in the next module. The aim of the drill is not a score but a record: which questions you got wrong, why, and what you will do about it. Treat any score as information about this week only, never as a prediction of the examination.

#### Checks

**Q.** Why does Day 8 have no new topic, and does finishing it mark anything mastered?

**A.** The roadmap leaves it free so the week can be tested and repaired. It marks nothing mastered; mastery stages move only on recorded evidence, and reading earns no stage.

**Q.** Name the five causes of a miss used in a checkpoint.

**A.** Never learned, learned but forgot, confused two similar things, misread the question, and too slow.

### Module 1: Week 1 closed-book retrieval

*120 minutes · lesson*

Retrieve the main facts, distinctions and methods from Days 1 to 7 before looking at any answer.

#### How to run the retrieval set

Below the text you will find a set of questions taken from the active-recall notes of the lessons for Days 1 to 7. For each one, cover the answer, write or say your answer in full, and only then reveal it. If you cannot start, write the first thing you do remember; a partial answer is far more useful than a blank. Mark each question as correct, partly correct or missed in your own error log. Work in blocks of twenty-five minutes with short breaks, and stop when the block ends rather than when the list ends; the unfinished questions can carry to a later session.

#### What to do with the misses

For every missed or partial question, write one line that names the cause (never learned, forgot, confused, misread, slow). Then act on the cause. If you never learned it, return to the lesson module that teaches it. If you forgot it, add it to the revision queue for a recall check in two or three days. If you confused two ideas, build a two-row comparison from memory and check it against the lesson's master notes. If you misread, rewrite the question in your own words. If you were slow, repeat that type of question later under a timer. Keep the repair list short enough that you will actually do it.

#### Checks

**Q.** Day 1, GS-165 (Directive Principles and welfare state): What did the first provision of Article 31C protect, and against which Articles?

**A.** Laws implementing Articles 39(b) and (c), against challenge under Articles 14, 19 and (then) 31.

**Q.** Day 1, GS-186 (Supreme Court: jurisdiction and review): What two things did the Third Judges case add to the collegium?

**A.** The CJI must consult the four senior-most judges (a collegium of five including the CJI), and two adverse opinions block the recommendation.

**Q.** Day 1, PH-008 (Gravitational self energy): State the physical reason the field inside a uniform shell is zero.

**A.** For any interior point, the nearer smaller patch and the farther larger patch of the shell pull in opposite directions with exactly equal magnitude because of the inverse-square law.

**Q.** Day 2, GS-173 (Landmark constitutional judgments): Which case introduced the basic structure doctrine?

**A.** Kesavananda Bharati v State of Kerala (1973).

**Q.** Day 2, GS-194 (Interstate Council and dispute resolution): Who is the common chairman of the Zonal Councils?

**A.** The Union Home Minister.

**Q.** Day 2, PH-016 (Angular velocity and rotation matrices basics): State why infinitesimal rotations commute but finite ones do not.

**A.** The product of two infinitesimal rotations differs in order only by a second-order commutator, negligible at first order; finite rotations carry that commutator in full.

**Q.** Day 3, GS-181 (Parliamentary privileges and motions): Name two individual privileges of members.

**A.** Freedom of speech in Parliament and freedom from civil arrest around sessions; also exemption from jury service.

**Q.** Day 3, GS-202 (Separation of powers and institutional checks): What is judicial legislation, in one line?

**A.** Detailed court-made guidelines or rules that fill a legal vacuum, criticised as lawmaking by judges.

**Q.** Day 3, PH-003 (Central-force dynamics and effective potential): When is a circular orbit in a power-law force stable?

**A.** For attractive F = −K/rⁿ, when n < 3.

**Q.** Day 4, GS-189 (Governor and Chief Minister): What does Article 167 require of the Chief Minister?

**A.** To communicate Council decisions on administration and legislation, to furnish information the Governor calls for and to submit for the Council a matter the Governor requires.

**Q.** Day 4, GS-168 (Basic Structure doctrine): State one argument for and one against the doctrine.

**A.** For: protects the core from temporary majorities. Against: judicial overreach, as the phrase is not in the text.

**Q.** Day 4, PH-011 (CM and lab reference frame conversion): Why is the threshold energy larger than |Q|?

**A.** Only the CM-frame energy T m2/M is available; the rest is locked in the CM motion.

**Q.** Day 5, GS-197 (Local body finances and devolution): What are the five sources of municipal revenue?

**A.** Tax revenue, non-tax revenue, grants, devolution and loans.

**Q.** Day 5, GS-176 (Prime Minister and Council of Ministers): What did the 1998 appointment of Vajpayee show about the President's role?

**A.** In a hung House the President may ask for proof of support and set a time for a confidence vote (ten days in 1998).

**Q.** Day 5, PH-019 (Rigid molecular rotations (di/tri atomic)): Why does the rigid-rotor model overestimate the line frequencies at high J?

**A.** Centrifugal force stretches the bond, increasing I and lowering the level spacing.

**Q.** Day 6, GS-163 (Fundamental Rights articles 12–35): What can Parliament alone do under Articles 33 and 35?

**A.** Restrict FRs for armed and police forces (33); make certain laws that give effect to FRs and prescribe punishment for untouchability and forced labour (35).

**Q.** Day 6, GS-184 (Attorney General and CAG): State the qualifications for the AG.

**A.** Citizen; High Court judge for five years, or High Court advocate for ten years, or eminent jurist in the President's opinion.

**Q.** Day 6, PH-006 (Gravitational field and potential of spheres): Why does the cavity field not depend on the cavity radius?

**A.** The filler's field −(4πGρ/3)(r − d) differs from the full sphere's by a term that depends only on d.

**Q.** Day 7, GS-171 (Official language constitutional provisions): What does the Official Languages Act 1963 provide?

**A.** English may continue, in addition to Hindi, for all official purposes of the Union and for Parliament's business, indefinitely.

**Q.** Day 7, GS-192 (Centre-state fiscal relations): Why does a rising cess share hurt States even if the Finance Commission share is unchanged?

**A.** Cesses for specific purposes and surcharges are outside the shareable pool, so the pool shrinks and States receive a smaller real share.

**Q.** Day 7, PH-014 (Elastic and inelastic collisions): Why can the CM kinetic energy ½MV² never be lost in a collision?

**A.** Momentum conservation fixes V, so ½MV² is the same before and after; only ½μu² can change.

### Module 2: Error log and repair plan

*45 minutes · lesson*

Turn this week's misses into a short, scheduled repair list and an honest error log.

#### What goes in the error log

An error log is a record of mistakes, not a record of how bad the week was. Each entry has five parts: the question or topic, what you answered, what was correct, the cause category, and the repair you will do. Keep entries to two or three lines. Group repeated causes: if three misses are all 'confused two similar things', the repair is one comparison exercise, not three separate rereads. The log is useful only if you look at it, so review it at every checkpoint and before every mock in the later examination laboratory.

#### Building a repair plan you will keep

List your misses, rank them by how often the idea is likely to matter and how badly you missed it, and pick the top few. For each, write the smallest repair that fixes the cause: a five-minute recall card, a comparison table from memory, a single worked example, or one timed question of the same type. Assign each repair to a specific day this coming week and attach it to the revision queue rather than to your memory. Cut the rest on purpose, and note that you cut them; an honest cut list is better than a plan that quietly fails. An error is repaired only when a later retest succeeds, so plan the retest as well.

#### Checks

**Q.** What five parts does an error-log entry have?

**A.** The question or topic, what you answered, what was correct, the cause category, and the repair you will do.

**Q.** When is an error considered repaired?

**A.** Only when a later retest of the same idea or question type succeeds, not when you have reread the answer.

### Module 3: Backlog control and missed-day recovery

*30 minutes · lesson*

Learn the minimum viable session, how to cap backlog, and what to do after a missed or disrupted day.

#### Minimum viable session and backlog control

A minimum viable session is the smallest block of study that still counts: for example one lesson module, one recall prompt and one written line in the error log, about forty-five minutes in total. On a bad day, do that and stop without guilt. Backlog is controlled by choosing, not by moving everything forward: at each checkpoint decide which unfinished modules are core for the first pass, which are deep dives that can wait, and which can be cut for now. Core modules come first; deep-dive layers (master notes, full derivations, extra practice) are scheduled later through the revision queue.

#### After a missed or disrupted day

Do not try to compensate by doubling the next day. Open the roadmap, mark the missed day honestly, and run a minimum viable session on its most important unfinished topic. If you missed several days, take one checkpoint-style session first: retrieve what you can from the last topics you studied, log the gaps, and rebuild from there. Do not erase the missed days from the record; honest data is what lets the plan adapt. The system never penalises a gap with streak loss or false urgency, and your 12 to 13 hour window is a ceiling for good days, not a quota to be repaid.

#### Checks

**Q.** What is a minimum viable session, and when do you use it?

**A.** The smallest block that still counts, for example one lesson module, one recall prompt and one error-log line; use it on a bad or disrupted day.

**Q.** What should you do the day after a missed day?

**A.** Do not double up. Mark the day honestly and run a minimum viable session on the most important unfinished topic.
